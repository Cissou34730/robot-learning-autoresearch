R3 process lesson

The campaign ran with R3

R3 encouraged a capability and mechanism shaped inquiry. The inquiry asked whether a policy could reach and hold the target.

It also asked which mechanism limited that capability. Candidate mechanisms included braking, radial coverage, and branch geometry.

This structure helped the campaign collect evidence that could separate several explanations. It did not prove that R3 improved decisions.

Campaign sequence

What happened

  1. M1 tested physical mechanisms

    A prescribed pulse probe measured actuation, braking, joint margins, and Jacobian conditioning. It did not test learned policy behavior.

  2. E2 opened inquiry I1

    I1 connected the human goal to three main explanations. The explanations included braking, omitted radial coverage, and branch geometry.

  3. T1 supplied a learned baseline

    The strongest selected checkpoint completed 151 of 160 holds on a fixed development panel. This was 94.375 percent success.

  4. M3 and M4 localized the observed failures

    All nine failures occurred in one target-angle sector. The radial bins had similar results.

    The records linked all nine failures to an infeasible open branch. A feasible folded branch remained available.

  5. T2 tested continuous branch margins

    Its final measured checkpoint completed 28 of 160 holds. This was 17.5 percent success on the same panel.

  6. T3 tested binary branch indicators

    Training completed, but its final training success was 36 percent. The durable record contains no development measurement for T3.

  7. T4 did not start scientific training

    A validation failure stopped the operation. The failure came from an observation shape mismatch.

Useful result

Why this campaign matters

The evidence narrowed a broad capability gap to a local geometric pattern. Generic braking and radial coverage did not explain the nine T1 failures well.

The branch diagnostics gave a more specific mechanism. The policy approached an infeasible branch when another branch was feasible.

The later trials also gave a negative result. Adding branch metadata did not produce a better measured policy.

Later rule change

Why R3 was discarded

A broad question could keep an inquiry open without a clear end. More mechanism questions could always fit inside the same inquiry.

This made inquiry closure depend too much on the width of the question. The human goal could remain unmet for a long time.

Retained rule

An inquiry can close with a limited or inconclusive result while the human goal remains unmet.

Execution is not evidence

Operation failures have a separate meaning

T4 failed during validation before its requested training ran. The environment returned nine observation values to a buffer that expected eleven.

This observation shape mismatch is an operation failure. It is not evidence about policy learning or task capability.

The mismatch can support a claim about integration consistency. It cannot support a claim about whether the proposed representation can work.

Failed operations belong to execution history. Completed measurements and their artifacts provide scientific evidence.

Evidence boundary

What the record does not show

The 160-episode panels were development measurements with seed 4200. They were not official benchmark assessments.

The campaign did not show at least 196 successes in 200 official episodes. It did not assign a working, best-known, or retained model role.

T1 supplied a strong local result, but one panel cannot establish broad reliability. The branch pattern can guide another test, but it does not prove a general cause.

T2 performed worse after the observation changed. This does not prove that all branch-aware representations are ineffective.

T3 had no durable development evaluation. T4 had no scientific result because validation stopped it.
