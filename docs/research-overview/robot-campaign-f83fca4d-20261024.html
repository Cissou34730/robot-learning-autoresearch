3. What the arm had to do

A simulated two-joint planar arm had to reach targets 6 to 20 cm from its base, at any bearing. Its hand had to stay within 1 cm of the target for 100 uninterrupted control steps (two seconds). The official goal was at least 196 successes in 200 episodes, with a frozen learned policy.

The PI is the AI principal investigator. The analyst writes the scientific model before the PI starts. Runner executes operations, enforces boundaries and records results. Coder and reviewer agents implement and check scientific code. A panel is a set of test episodes. Research evaluation and the protected task reference are development evidence, not the official result.

4. The final controller: one learned choice per episode

Target and arm state: the PPO policy's first request in the episode selects a feasible IK endpoint (a joint posture).

Latch the endpoint for the episode

Episode latch (T4 runtime): the endpoint is held while the target is unchanged. Later policy requests cannot trigger branch replanning.

Move and hold analytically

Quintic reference

A rest-to-rest joint trajectory with bounded speed (1 rad/s) and acceleration (1 rad/s²) as Euclidean norms over both joints.

Computed-torque tracking

Model-based acceleration tracking with compiled-model torque compensation, at the 50 Hz control rate with ten 2-ms physics substeps.

This is an analytic controller with a learned posture selector, not pure PPO. The controller came from three diagnostics: M4 showed the fixed PD tracker of T2 did not converge on four feasible postures; M5 showed the quintic and computed-torque law reached and held all four; M10 and M11 located T3's in-hold branch replanning and showed the latch repairs it with unchanged T1 weights. [State]

What learning contributed is not measured. T4's training success proxy was 1.00 at all 24 checkpoints and its reward stayed between 105.97 and 106.47. On M12 the checkpoints at 5,120, 40,960 and 120,832 steps each scored 160/160, with zero discordant episodes between them. On M11, T1's unchanged weights scored 32/32 under the latch against 19/32 under T1's own runtime. No operation measured the same controller with a non-learned endpoint rule.

10. Maintainer analysis: the analytic shortcut

This section is the maintainer's analysis. It records concerns and options, not decisions.

Concern

In this task the simulator model is exact, there is no contact, no noise and no randomized dynamics. An IK, quintic and computed-torque controller can then solve the task almost alone. In a more complex scenario these conditions do not hold, and the analytic shortcut stops working.

Across 2d4fcc0e, faff0d94 and f83fca4d, the PI's habit when learning underperforms is to remove control from learning rather than to diagnose why learning fails. That habit would hit a wall where no exact model exists.

Mixing model-based and learned control is legitimate. The risk is a PI that never makes learning work.

Possible actions, not decided

  • At scenario level, define what counts as learned control in the human goal.
  • At scenario level, add evaluation variation (for example model mismatch or noise) so that a controller must cope with conditions an exact model does not describe.
  • For the maintainer only, report a learning-contribution metric in campaign reports, such as the candidate against the same runtime with a fixed non-learned choice. It would not be exposed to campaign agents.

Any scenario change is a human-owned contract change and would need its own approval and campaign.
