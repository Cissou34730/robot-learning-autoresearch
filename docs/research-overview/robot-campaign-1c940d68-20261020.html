2. The task

A two-joint planar arm in MuJoCo had an upper arm of 0.12 m and a forearm of 0.10 m. Each joint could turn from -170 to +170 degrees. Targets appeared 0.06 to 0.20 m from the base at any bearing. The hand had to stay within 1 cm of a target for 100 uninterrupted steps during a 500-step episode. The goal was at least 196 successes in the official 200-episode assessment. That assessment was terminal: it ended the campaign.

The PI was the AI principal investigator. The PI made scientific decisions. Runner was the harness software. It ran operations and recorded facts. Coder and reviewer were AI agents that changed and checked code for the PI. An inquiry was a bounded research question opened by the PI.

7. Scientific errors

The likely physical cause was not tested

The two-link arm has two possible elbow postures for many targets. The shoulder limit can make one posture impossible for some target directions.

On the task-reference panel, 65 of 200 targets were in the measured geometric zone: 31 on the positive-bearing side and 34 on the negative-bearing side. Every failure was among the 31 positive-side targets. The other 169 episodes had no failures. No failure occurred on the negative side of the zone.

T1 failed 10 of 10 times in this zone. T2 failed 6 of 6 times. T3 failed 6 of 6 times. T2 and T3 had the same six failed episodes. Their final hand distances were 1.1-2.5 cm from the target.

Hypothesis, not confirmed cause

Hypothesis: the policy always bends the elbow in the same direction. On the positive side, this posture may push the shoulder past its +170 degree limit. The hand then stops 1-2.5 cm short. The mirrored side may work because the same elbow posture is allowed there.

The pattern was strong. A Fisher-type test gave p below 0.00001. But the campaign has no joint-angle recordings to confirm the posture or the shoulder limit at failure.

The PI's scientific model already said that shoulder limits may block one elbow posture for some directions. It named joint-limit margins by bearing as useful evidence. The PI did not calculate them.

A repeat was treated as confirmation

M4 measured T2 on the same fixed panel as M3. The panel is deterministic. It returned the same 194/200 score and same six failed episodes.

The final decision cited 194/200 on both M3 and M4 as if they were two results. The second result added no new evidence.

One checkpoint per run was measured

Each run saved 24 checkpoints. The PI measured one checkpoint from each run. The PI chose checkpoints by the training proxy, not by a comparison across checkpoints.

A previous campaign had a high-proxy checkpoint at 85.5% and another checkpoint at 96%. This shows why proxy rank alone is weak evidence.

One panel was reused for selection

The PI chose T2 because it scored best on the same 200-episode practice panel. Its official score was 189/200.

If true success is near 97%, a new 200-episode test has about a 28% chance to reach 196 successes. The 95% interval for 194/200 is about 93.6-98.9%.

The official test came too early

The PI requested the terminal assessment at 194/200 on the practice panel. Thirteen of 15 inquiries were unused. The model's own joint-limit check was not tested.

The reason given was that no evidence-supported action was known. That reason was not correct. The model had already named a discriminating check.

T2 and T3 changed the training bearing mix. T3 did not change any of the six failures. This result should have led to a test of the suspected joint-limit cause, not another claim that extra focus had helped.

9. Proposed harness improvements

These proposals improve the process. They do not tell the PI what scientific answer to choose.

Proposal Problem it addresses Risk
P1. Balance the inquiry prompt. Remove the sentence that says measuring every checkpoint is not required, or restore the warning that proxy rank alone does not show which saved models deserve measurement. Keep the proxy caution beside the checkpoint sentence in the program contract. Only one checkpoint per run was measured, and the PI relied on the training proxy. Low.
P2. Restore “or develop the method” in the goal-review prompt's measurement sentence. The goal reviews asked for measurements only when they resolved uncertainty about the current choice. This may have reduced useful measurements. Low.
P3. Report deterministic repeats. If Runner sees the same saved-artifact fingerprint on the fixed practice panel, return the earlier result and say that this is a repeat, not a new sample. M4 repeated T2 on the same panel and was treated as separate confirmation. Low. This is a mechanical fact.
P4. Add uncertainty facts to success results. Report a 95% interval and the official threshold of 196/200. The PI selected a model from a reused panel and treated 194/200 as strong support for the official goal. Low. This adds facts, not advice.
P5. Improve the pre-assessment review. When the practice score is below the goal, state which discriminating evidence named in the scientific model remains unexamined. The PI requested the official test before testing the listed joint-limit check. Medium. It adds process text. Earlier reviews warned against forcing a measurement before assessment. Maintainer approval is needed.

Not proposed: tell the PI about joint limits or elbow branches. That is scientific work for the PI.

10. Limits of this analysis

  • This report covers one campaign. It cannot prove that the harness changes caused the differences.
  • The geometric pattern is strong, with a Fisher-type p below 0.00001. The campaign has no joint-angle recordings. The elbow-posture explanation remains a hypothesis.
  • The comparison campaigns used different random seeds and different PI choices.
  • The task-reference panel was fixed and reused. A repeated score on that panel is not an independent sample.
  • Campaign C stopped early. Its missing results cannot be compared with completed campaigns.
