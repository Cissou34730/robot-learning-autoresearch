Implemented Rewards

Each campaign replaced the empty reward with one formula. No comparison tested different reward formulas within the same campaign.

d
Current distance to the target, in metres. delta_d is the previous distance minus the current distance.
epsilon
The tolerance, 0.01 m. inside is 1 inside tolerance and 0 outside.
delta_h
The change in the consecutive hold count. Losing the hold gives a negative reset.
success
1 when the policy completes the required hold, and 0 otherwise.
sum_u2
The sum of the two squared applied motor commands. mean_u2 is half this sum.
Reward formulas used in training
Campaign Formula
1 20 * delta_d + 0.10 * inside
- 0.05 * inside * (d / epsilon)^2
+ 10 * success - w * sum_u2
2 delta_d / epsilon + 0.01 * inside
+ 10 * success - 0.001 * sum_u2
3 -2 * d + delta_h
+ 10 * success - 0.05 * mean_u2
4 20 * delta_d + 0.5 * delta_h
+ 30 * success - 0.02 * mean_u2
5 2 * delta_d / epsilon + inside
+ 10 * success - 0.02 * mean_u2

Campaign 1 uses w = 0.01 for the baseline and reduced-exploration designs. It uses w = 1 for the output-gain-0.1 design.

The larger coefficient compensates for squaring commands that are ten times smaller. This is a coefficient change, not a separate reward structure.

A Wrong Claim In Campaign 5

The analyst states that the dwell reward cannot reward repeated entry and exit. It also states that dwell reward is bounded by 100 steps per episode.

The hold counter resets when the robot leaves tolerance. A policy can collect more paid inside steps without completing 100 consecutive steps.

Discounted incentive check with campaign 5's discount of 0.99
Sequence Dwell and completion return
100 inside steps, then successful termination 67.09
Repeat 99 inside steps and one outside step, over 500 steps 98.76

This calculation isolates dwell and completion rewards. It omits movement costs and progress rewards. It does not show that the learned policy used this behavior.

The analyst's claimed protection is wrong. Correct mechanics did not prevent an error in reward reasoning.

What Worked

The Analysis Identified The Main Dynamics

All five reports identify a velocity response of about 20-24 ms and a full-command speed of about 10 rad/s.

The reports connect these dynamics to action scale, exploration and the 1 cm tolerance. Earlier Terra and Luna comparisons missed this mechanism.

The Prompt Repair Reached Every Analyst

The recorded system messages contain both paragraphs that the earlier DeepSeek attempt missed. This checks delivery, not scientific correctness.

The Comparisons Tested Consequential Choices

Campaigns compared control authority, exploration scale and action interfaces. These were more consequential than run 4's small parameter changes.

Campaign 1 found a useful gain change. Campaigns 4 and 5 kept direct output after feedback alternatives performed worse.

The PI Could Revise An Initial Explanation

Campaign 4 initially emphasized holding. Its measurements instead showed that most failures came before target acquisition.

The PI used this result to propose an observation study rather than continue developing the weaker feedback interface.

Training Logs Alone Were Not The Verdict

Campaign 4 logged zero training success, yet its direct-output policy achieved 50.25% in research evaluation.

Stochastic training behavior and evaluated policy behavior differed. A zero training statistic did not establish that the policy learned nothing.

What Needs Improvement

These are proposed maintainer changes. This report does not implement them.

1. Keep The First Reward Provisional

The analyst prompt gives one first reward a special status. The PI prompt warns against local adjustments of parameters or reward.

The PI receives full variant permissions. The analyst does not receive the training-variants contract directly in its system reference.

The analyst ranks variants, and startup asks the PI to select the leading scientific distinction. All five reports put action choices first.

Campaign 2 proposes a reward alternative at V7. The PI did not select it for its first comparison.

Needed: consistent information about full design freedom. Keep the first reward open to revision. Do not require a reward variant.

2. Remove Contradictory Role Text

The contracts say that the analyst selects no intervention. The launch prompt asks it to propose a reward and design variants.

Needed: distinguish a proposal from a PI decision. Give both roles the same account of this boundary.

3. Give Better Grounds For Training Length

The PI chose all lengths explicitly. There was no fixed 200,000-step requirement.

Reports treated roughly 100,000 steps as a screen and larger lengths as grounds for a stronger conclusion. Those thresholds lacked campaign evidence.

The trainer also contains a 100,000-step default. The maintainer identified this scaffold value as an anchor in the analyst's reasoning.

Needed: separate task limits, scaffold choices and evidence about learning. Relate the budget to the question and available learning signals.

Do not replace 100,000 with another fixed suggestion. A shorter run is not automatically more useful.

4. Improve Reward Reasoning

The campaign 5 error concerns the relation between termination, repeated entry and discounted return. A plausible term-by-term explanation did not catch it.

Needed: sound analysis of complete behavior and incentives. Do not treat the first proposed formula as validated.

5. Resolve The Execution Block

Campaign 2's training completed, but generated cache files triggered the ownership gate. There is no evaluated score for either variant.

Needed: investigate and repair this block in the main harness when authorized. Preserve the completed training evidence.

6. Record Comparable Cost

DeepSeek has token and time records but no AIU value. Copilot AIU does not include that external-provider analysis.

Needed: record an appropriate external-provider cost measure. Do not compare a missing analyst cost with Terra or Sol AIU.

Overall decision: DeepSeek remains a promising analyst candidate. It improved the physical model, but correct first-recipe construction is not yet reliable.

Campaign 1 is the strongest candidate for continuation. It still needs 25 more successes per 200 to reach the nominal goal.

Before another full five-campaign batch, address the instruction inconsistencies and the execution block. Do not alter the scientific evidence.

Earlier Runs

The following history comes from the maintainer's handover. This report did not repeat the earlier experiments.

  1. Run 1: two campaigns started from skeleton-v1. Training length was fixed at 120,000 steps. Both chose joint targets with feedback. Convergence failed. Later changes allowed free training length and added reward-term logs.
  2. Run 2: none of five full campaigns passed. The best reached 186/200 task-reference. Direct commands outperformed the repeated joint-target approach. Later changes added parallel variants, a richer analyst task and skeleton-v3.
  3. Run 3: Terra supplied the analysis. No PI used variants. The maintainer found useful insights but weak correctness. Both Terra and Luna missed the fast velocity dynamics.
  4. Run 4: Sol supplied the analysis. PIs used variants, but mostly changed small parameters. Examples and parameter-only variants constrained the choices. Reported analyst cost was 24-36 AIU per analysis.
  5. Earlier run 5 attempt: DeepSeek sessions stopped before completion. Copilot omitted two analyst paragraphs. The fixed system prompt repaired this before the current restart.

Direct inspection of the run 4 archive found no completed evaluation operations. Its training statistics cannot serve as measured research scores.

The supported improvement is clearer dynamics, broader design comparisons and useful evaluated outcomes. It is not a claimed numerical score gain over run 4.
