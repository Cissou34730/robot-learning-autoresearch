Final outcome

The best-known policy was T2:checkpoint-110592. It received an official assessment of 195 successes in 200 episodes, or 97.5%. The required threshold was 196/200, so the scientific goal was not met.

That benchmark failure does not negate the process improvement. OI-004 concerned how the PI nominates checkpoints, handles unavailable evidence, and chooses the next scientific direction. The campaign showed the intended behavior on all three points.

Supported conclusion: OI-004 brought clear value and is closed as effective. It improved the research process; it did not guarantee that this particular intervention would cross the task threshold.

What the comparison means

The intervention was not merely a higher aggregate score. It moved the mechanism that I1 identified: entry became earlier, difficult-radius entry coverage increased, all three failure categories improved, and hold interruptions fell sharply.

This supports the scientific value of the capture-focused route. It does not prove that reward shaping alone is sufficient for every residual failure or every future campaign.

Confidence and monitoring

An identical second campaign is not required

The claim is about intended harness behavior, not a population estimate of policy return. Fixed-context checks showed that the repaired instructions select non-terminal artifacts when warranted, preserve missing evidence as unresolved, and tie the next operation to the decision frontier. This live campaign exercised the same behaviors under real campaign conditions.

Repeating an identical campaign would add information about stochastic repeatability, but it is not required to close OI-004. Future campaigns should monitor for recurrence: automatic terminal-checkpoint preference, conclusions drawn from failed operations, or progression that ignores the current evidence frontier.
