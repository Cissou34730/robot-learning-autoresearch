2. What the arm had to do

A simulated two-joint arm had to reach targets 6 to 20 cm from its base, at any bearing. Its hand had to stay within 1 cm of the target for 100 uninterrupted control steps. Each episode allowed 500 control steps. The official goal was at least 196 successes in 200 episodes.

The PI was the AI principal investigator. It chose the scientific direction. Runner ran operations, enforced boundaries and recorded facts. Coder and reviewer agents changed and checked scientific code for the PI.

A checkpoint was a model saved during training. A panel was a set of test episodes. The protected task-reference panel supported development. A research panel used the PI-owned evaluator. Neither was the official assessment, which ended the campaign.

3. What the winning controller did

T6 was a hybrid controller. It combined a learned PPO policy with an analytic joint controller. The result does not show that the neural policy learned the whole task alone.

The saved runtime checked two possible elbow postures for the target. These are called elbow branches. In one region, the positive-elbow solution needed a shoulder angle outside the allowed range.

The analytic controller acted when that solution exceeded the shoulder limit by more than 6 degrees and the negative-elbow solution was feasible. Otherwise, the learned policy supplied the command through the existing action filter.

Target and joint state: check the two elbow solutions and the joint limits.

A PD controller combines a position correction with damping to reduce motion. These calculations belonged to the saved runtime used in evaluation.

T6 transferred from T2 and completed another 120,832 training steps. The final model was saved after 20,480 steps of that run. Selecting an early model did not reduce the training already performed or the campaign's total resource use.

6. Better decisions, with remaining weaknesses

What went well

  • The PI used geometry, trajectories and intervention tests. It did not stop at a plausible explanation.
  • It followed a selected-failure replay with a full paired panel. The latter was stronger evidence of a useful intervention.
  • It treated unsuccessful filter variants as limited results, not proof that every similar method would fail.
  • It promoted a net-better candidate despite one lost T2 success. Zero individual losses did not become a final veto.
  • It used already saved checkpoints instead of another training run near the end.
  • It requested the official assessment despite remaining causal questions and a task-reference failure.

What remained weak

  • Evidence labels drifted. In E11, a T1 score of 192/200 from M1 was attributed to T2.
  • M19 reused M15's seeds but was described as new, independent evidence.
  • Large reads remained. Evidence and training-log replies totaled about 2.11 million characters. Several replies were about 65-75k characters.
  • Core files were repeatedly opened. The harness had failed to include them in the first message, so this was not simply a PI reading failure.
  • A useful saved-runtime package could not enter protected evaluation through the available candidate workflow.

The PI called the pass-probability tool 21 times and the training-statistics tool six times. Those tools improved calculation and risk reporting. They did not check manually supplied candidate identities.

Reading became narrower near the finish: the largest evidence/log reply in the final six sessions was about 19k characters. The whole campaign still cannot be described as having solved large reading.

Judgment: the finish was less defensive. The PI accepted useful net gains and acted with uncertainty. That is behavior worth retaining. The unresolved causal questions do not justify requiring another round of ablations before acknowledging this success.

The tests did not isolate elbow-branch selection from PD stabilization, retraining or the learned policy's contribution. The official result established that the complete saved controller passed, not why each part was necessary.
