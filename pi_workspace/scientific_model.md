# Scientific model of the two-joint reach-and-hold system

## System boundary and success

The system is a planar, two-degree-of-freedom serial arm in a fixed
zero-gravity MuJoCo simulation. At each reset, a stationary target is sampled
uniformly in polar coordinates: radius 0.06-0.20 m and angle over the full
circle. The end effector must be within 0.01 m of that target for 100
consecutive control steps. Each control step is 0.02 s, so the uninterrupted
hold is 2 s. An episode has at most 500 control steps (10 s), and any excursion
outside the tolerance resets the current hold count to zero. The goal is
therefore not simply point reaching: it is reach, decelerate, converge, and
remain stable over a timed interval for every target geometry in the
distribution.

The following model distinguishes facts explicit in the human-authored
contracts and implementation from mechanical consequences inferred from them.

## Morphology, geometry, and kinematics

The shoulder is at the origin in the horizontal plane, with the arm plane at
z = 0.02 m. The upper arm length is 0.12 m and the forearm length is 0.10 m.
The shoulder and elbow are revolute joints about the z axis, with coordinates
q1 and q2 and independent ranges of -170 to 170 degrees. The end-effector
position is consequently

    p(q) = [0.12 cos(q1) + 0.10 cos(q1 + q2),
            0.12 sin(q1) + 0.10 sin(q1 + q2),
            0.02].

The zero configuration is fully extended along +x, placing the end effector at
(0.22, 0, 0.02) m. The arm has two joint degrees of freedom for two target
coordinates; z is fixed rather than controlled. The target is placed at the
same z coordinate as the end effector, so the distance used for success is
effectively the planar Euclidean distance.

Without joint limits, the radial workspace of this two-link geometry is
0.02-0.22 m. The official radial interval 0.06-0.20 m is inside that annulus.
For a target radius r, the two nominal inverse-kinematic branches satisfy

    cos(q2) = (r^2 - 0.12^2 - 0.10^2) / (2 * 0.12 * 0.10),

with q2 of opposite signs and q1 chosen to align the resulting link vector
with the target angle. These are the elbow-up and elbow-down configurations.
The joint limits remove configurations near the angular limits, but the
official radial range leaves at least one practical branch around the full
target circle; the tolerance also creates a small continuum of acceptable
configurations rather than one exact pose.

## Actuation, timing, and state transition

The policy emits two continuous action values, each clipped to [-1, 1]. The
values pass directly to two MuJoCo motor actuators, one per joint. Each motor
has gear 5, so the command produces a nominal gear-scaled joint effort up to
5 simulator torque units before the plant response and joint constraints. The
same command is held for 10 physics steps. The physics timestep is 0.002 s,
therefore the policy observes and acts at 50 Hz while the plant integrates at
500 Hz.

The explicit joint dynamics include damping 0.5 and armature 0.01 at each
joint. Gravity is zero. The arm and target have no task interaction: the target
is mocap-controlled and the ground plane has collision disabled, so success
cannot be obtained or lost through contact mechanics. The arm's masses and
body inertias are generated by MuJoCo from the capsule and cylinder geometry
and simulator defaults; they are not explicitly specified in the XML. The
resulting inertia, the armature, damping, gear ratio, and the coupled
configuration-dependent Jacobian jointly determine acceleration and stopping
distance. There is no explicit actuator dynamics, action smoothing, sensor
noise, target motion, or external disturbance in the authored model.

At reset, q = [0, 0], qdot = [0, 0], the model is forward-propagated, and only
then is the target sampled. Thus every episode starts from the same physical
arm state but a different target. The initial posture is interior to both joint
limits but is a kinematic singularity: with q2 = 0, the two-link Jacobian has
zero determinant. Small joint effort can move the tip radially immediately,
but lateral movement away from the initial arm direction requires first
creating elbow bend and therefore has weaker, coupled transient behavior.

## Sensing, policy interface, and information

The policy receives 11 values:

* q1 and q2;
* the two joint velocities;
* the three-dimensional end-effector-to-target displacement;
* four wrapped angular errors from the current pose to the two inverse-
  kinematic branches (open and folded elbow solutions).

The arm geometry and q determine the absolute end-effector position, so the
relative displacement plus q makes the target position observable in the
known planar model. The observation contains no measurement noise and no
unobserved target motion. It does not directly expose actuator effort after
clipping, acceleration, MuJoCo constraint forces, inferred mass properties,
or a separate contact state. In this task those omissions do not hide contact
events, but they do leave the policy to infer the plant's dynamic response
from qdot and temporal interaction. There is no observation history in the
scenario interface; the instantaneous physical state is sufficient for this
fixed, deterministic plant, provided the policy has learned the dynamics.

The action-to-outcome chain is therefore:

    observation(q, qdot, target relation)
        -> two joint effort commands
        -> 10-step MuJoCo transition
        -> new q and qdot
        -> geometric distance and hold-state update.

The environment increments the hold count only after the transitioned state is
within 1 cm. It terminates successfully on the 100th consecutive in-tolerance
control state. If a held state is followed by an out-of-tolerance state, the
count becomes zero; a near miss late in the interval is physically equivalent
to failing to stabilize for the complete hold.

## Coupled capabilities required for success

Success requires a policy to identify the target's radius and bearing from the
observation, select or approach a valid inverse-kinematic branch, and generate
a dynamically feasible trajectory from the extended singular start. It must
then reduce velocity before entering or while entering the 1 cm neighborhood:
position accuracy alone is insufficient if residual motion carries the tip
back outside the band. The final controller must maintain a stable equilibrium
for 2 s, using the absence of gravity and the joint damping to its advantage
without depending on damping alone. The 20 ms action interval means that
corrections are discrete and each command affects several substeps before a
new observation arrives.

Reach and hold are coupled through geometry and momentum. A branch that is
easy to approach may require a different braking trajectory than the other
branch. Near q2 = 0, the arm is poorly conditioned for tangential motion; near
the outer radial boundary, small angular errors demand tip motion that is
especially sensitive to configuration. Conversely, once a target pose is
settled, zero gravity means a low-effort equilibrium is physically available
and no persistent gravity compensation is required. The controller must still
avoid joint-limit behavior and avoid crossing a kinematic branch or singular
region unnecessarily.

The current training reward supplies distance progress, a closeness potential,
linear hold-progress credit, a completion bonus, and a small action cost. It
does not redefine the task outcome: the environment's consecutive counter is
authoritative. In particular, leaving the tolerance resets accumulated hold
progress, but the configured hold-exit forfeiture is zero and the outside-band
penalty is small. Reward optimization can therefore favor entering and
re-entering the band without by itself proving complete uninterrupted holds.
Training samples radii 0.14-0.20 m, whereas the official distribution includes
0.06-0.20 m; this is a real geometry-dependent distribution shift, not a
change to the official task.

## Scientifically meaningful quantities

Understanding complete behavior requires more than the binary episode outcome.
For each target and trajectory, the meaningful physical record includes target
radius and angle; q, qdot, commanded action, and end-effector position at every
control step; distance to target; radial and tangential position error; the
Jacobian determinant or conditioning; and distance to each joint limit. The
meaningful temporal quantities are time to first enter tolerance, peak and
terminal joint and tip velocity, stopping distance, time spent in tolerance,
maximum uninterrupted hold, number and timing of hold interruptions, and
whether termination was success or horizon truncation. Grouping these by
target geometry and apparent IK branch separates geometric reach failures from
transit, braking, conditioning, and stabilization failures.

These measurements define distinct physical behavior classes: failure to
approach a reachable target; approach through a poorly conditioned or
limit-near configuration; overshoot caused by insufficient braking; a
near-stationary but tolerance-borderline trajectory; and a stable reach that
still loses the hold through a transient excursion. They also expose whether
the same observed position error hides materially different velocities or
control authority, which is central to explaining hold reliability.

## Established facts

1. The physical plant is a two-link planar arm with lengths 0.12 m and 0.10 m,
   two z-axis hinge joints, independent +/-170 degree limits, zero gravity,
   timestep 0.002 s, damping 0.5, armature 0.01, and gear-5 motors.
2. The policy controls both joints with clipped continuous commands held for
   10 physics steps, giving 0.02 s control intervals.
3. The reset state is q = qdot = 0; the target is stationary, coplanar with
   the arm, and sampled uniformly by radius 0.06-0.20 m and full angle.
4. The task uses a 0.01 m distance threshold, 100 consecutive in-tolerance
   control steps, and a 500-step episode limit.
5. Observations contain q, qdot, end-effector-to-target displacement, and both
   branch-relative IK angle pairs; actions are passed through unchanged apart
   from environment clipping.
6. The target and plane do not provide collision or contact dynamics, and the
   implementation has no authored noise or moving disturbance.
7. There are two nominal IK elbow branches for reachable target positions, and
   the training radius range is narrower than the official radius range.

## Physical consequences

1. The initial fully extended posture is a Jacobian singularity, so targets
   away from the initial radial line require coordinated elbow creation before
   efficient lateral tip motion is possible.
2. The official target annulus is geometrically reachable, but the 1 cm band
   is only 5-17% of the target radius and demands substantially finer
   terminal control than coarse reaching.
3. The same action is applied for 20 ms, so braking and stabilization are
   sampled-data problems; a policy cannot correct continuously within that
   interval.
4. Damping and zero gravity make stationary joint configurations natural
   equilibria, but inertia, armature, and configuration-dependent leverage
   determine whether the tip enters the tolerance with enough residual velocity
   to escape.
5. A late one-step excursion is a complete episode failure, so high success
   requires low probability of transient escape throughout the entire hold,
   not merely low final position error.
6. Branch choice, approach direction, and braking are physically coupled:
   two poses with similar position error can have different velocity,
   conditioning, and future hold robustness.
7. The narrower training radius interval can leave the inner part of the
   official distribution underrepresented, where the inverse-kinematic elbow
   angles and approach dynamics differ materially.
8. The reward provides useful shaping toward proximity and hold duration but
   does not make uninterrupted completion equivalent to accumulating local
   reward; evaluation of the consecutive hold remains necessary.

## Unknowns

1. The exact masses, center-of-mass locations, full inertia tensors, and
   configuration-dependent effective inertia are simulator-derived rather than
   explicitly authored here. Their numerical values determine acceleration and
   braking margins.
2. The realized torque-to-motion response, including any MuJoCo actuator,
   integrator, and joint-limit behavior not specified in the authored XML, is
   not established analytically from the contracts alone.
3. The minimum robust stopping distance and settling time across target angle,
   radius, branch, and approach velocity are unknown. These quantities decide
   how much of the 500-step horizon is available for the 100-step hold.
4. It is unknown whether a single smooth policy will reliably use both IK
   branches or will select one branch and whether that choice remains robust
   near the initial singularity and joint limits.
5. The sensitivity of hold success to action quantization at 50 Hz, residual
   joint velocity, observation normalization, and policy inference variability
   has not been measured.
6. The extent to which reward shaping and the 0.14-0.20 m training distribution
   produce policies that reach but do not stabilize, or that fail on inner
   targets, is unresolved before campaign evidence.
7. No pre-campaign evidence establishes the distribution of failure modes:
   unreachable geometry, branch selection, slow transit, overshoot, joint-limit
   interaction, or hold interruption. These must remain hypotheses rather than
   being treated as observed causes.
