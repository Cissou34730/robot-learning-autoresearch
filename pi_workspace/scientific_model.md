## Established facts

### Embodiment and task frame

- **[Repository fact]** `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml` defines a world-fixed base, a shoulder hinge about world/local `z`, and an elbow hinge about `z` at the end of the upper arm. The links lie along local `+x`: the upper-arm capsule spans 0.12 m and the forearm spans 0.10 m; the end-effector site is at the forearm tip. `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.py` declares the same lengths. Both hinge ranges are `[-170°, 170°]`; there are no other robot joints.
- **[Repository fact]** The articulated plane is at `z=0.02 m` (the upper-arm body offset); the target is a mocap body. The target sphere, base geom, and floor plane have collision disabled. The target sphere and end-effector site have visual sizes, but success is based on distance between the site position and target center; neither the official task nor the visible research check specifies an end-effector orientation, grasp, or contact-force requirement. Sources: [`contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml`](../contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml), [`contracts/scenario.md`](../contracts/scenario.md#official-task), [`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/environment.py).

### Simulator, actuation, and control timing

- **[Repository fact]** The MuJoCo XML sets gravity to zero and the physics timestep to 0.002 s. Each hinge has damping 0.5 and armature 0.01. Each joint has one `motor` actuator with `ctrlrange="-1 1"` and `gear="5"`. Sources: [`contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml`](../contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml).
- **[Repository fact]** The task constants set `FRAME_SKIP=10`, a 0.01 m success threshold, a 2.0 s hold, a 500-control-step horizon, and target radii 0.06–0.20 m. The visible research environment applies one action over ten `mj_step` calls and uses `model.opt.timestep * frame_skip` as its control interval: 0.020 s, or 50 control updates/s. Sources: [`contracts/task_spec.py`](../contracts/task_spec.py), [`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/environment.py).
- **[Repository fact]** The visible research action space has two values in `[-1,1]`; its default action mapping is identity, then the environment clips to the action bounds and writes `data.ctrl`. Protected evaluation instead executes the saved policy runtime's observation and action callables, then validates/clips the action before applying it. Sources: [`robot_learning/scenario/policy_io.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/policy_io.py), [`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/environment.py), [`contracts/instruments.md`](../contracts/instruments.md#saved-controller-execution).

### Reset, observation, and evaluation surfaces

- **[Repository fact]** The visible research reset sets both joint positions and velocities to zero, forwards the model, and samples a target angle uniformly over `[-π,π]` and radius uniformly over the configured range. It sets target `z` equal to the end-effector `z`; the target is not resampled during a visible episode. Sources: [`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/environment.py).
- **[Repository fact]** The default 11-value observation contains two joint positions, two joint velocities, the three-dimensional end-effector-minus-target vector, and four wrapped joint-angle offsets to the two inverse-kinematic branches. The builder reads simulator state directly; it does not use the overhead camera image or a modeled noisy sensor. The saved runtime can preserve a different observation/action callable and can carry policy recurrence. Sources: [`robot_learning/scenario/observations.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/observations.py), [`robot_learning/scenario/policy_io.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/policy_io.py), [`contracts/policy_runtime.py`](../contracts/policy_runtime.py).
- **[Repository fact]** The visible research environment checks distance after each ten-substep action block. It increments the held-step counter when distance is at most 0.01 m and resets it to zero on an outside sample; 100 counted steps terminate successfully. Its evaluation artifact records per-episode target radius/angle, minimum and final distance, first in-tolerance step, maximum held streak, total in-tolerance steps, and hold interruptions. Sources: [`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/environment.py), [`robot_learning/scenario/evaluation.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/evaluation.py).

### Official task and baseline training exposure

- **[Repository fact]** The official contract requires entering a 1 cm tolerance and remaining continuously within it for 2 s; at current timing this is 100 consecutive control steps. Episodes allow at most 500 steps. The protected final assessment uses 200 episodes and reaches the stated 98% objective at 196 successes. Its task distribution spans radii 6–20 cm and the full angular range. Source: [`contracts/scenario.md`](../contracts/scenario.md#official-task).
- **[Repository fact]** The visible baseline training environment samples only radii 0.14–0.20 m, whereas the visible research evaluation range is 0.06–0.20 m. This is a training-configuration fact, not a restriction on future research. Sources: [`robot_learning/training/environment.py`](../robot_learning/training/environment.py), [`contracts/task_spec.py`](../contracts/task_spec.py).

## Physical consequences

### Workspace and inverse-kinematic alternatives

- **[Reasoned implication]** With link lengths \(L_1=0.12\) m and \(L_2=0.10\) m, the planar endpoint is
  \(x=L_1\cos q_s+L_2\cos(q_s+q_e)\),
  \(y=L_1\sin q_s+L_2\sin(q_s+q_e)\), at fixed `z`. The unconstrained radial workspace is 0.02–0.22 m. For target radii 0.06–0.20 m, the cosine rule gives \(q_e=\pm\arccos((r^2-L_1^2-L_2^2)/(2L_1L_2))\), with \(|q_e|\) about 49.5°–150.1°. For each branch, \(q_s=\theta-\operatorname{atan2}(L_2\sin q_e,L_1+L_2\cos q_e)\), where \(\theta\) is target azimuth. Both elbow signs satisfy the elbow limits. The shoulder alternatives differ by at least about 44°; since its excluded angular sector is only 20°, at least one shoulder solution remains within limits for each target azimuth. The task annulus is therefore kinematically reachable under the authored joint limits, although the two branches are not both feasible at every azimuth.
- **[Reasoned implication] Research decisions:** This can change whether the first explanation for failures is inadequate workspace, branch/limit selection, or trajectory control. The geometry does not justify treating any official target radius as unreachable.
- **[Unresolved quantity] Assumptions:** This is a kinematic reachability result. A collision-free path additionally assumes compiled contact filtering permits the adjacent capsule geoms to occupy their hinged poses; the link collision masks are not explicitly authored.
- **[Repository fact] Source references:** [`contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml`](../contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml), [`contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.py`](../contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.py), [`contracts/task_spec.py`](../contracts/task_spec.py), [`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/environment.py).
- **[Unresolved quantity] Discriminating evidence:** For target samples spanning radius and azimuth, compare measured endpoint positions and joint angles with both analytic IK branches and their joint-limit margins. A target with no valid branch, an unexpected collision, or a mismatch between predicted and observed endpoint geometry would revise this reachability conclusion.

### Singular reset and trajectory requirement

- **[Reasoned implication]** In the visible research reset, \(q_s=q_e=0\), so the links are fully extended and the endpoint is at radius 0.22 m. The planar Jacobian determinant is \(L_1L_2\sin q_e\), hence zero at reset: first-order joint motion cannot produce radial endpoint motion there. Every official target radius is below 0.22 m; the minimum distance over the stated target annulus is 0.02 m at radius 0.20 m along `+x`, outside the 0.01 m tolerance. The arm must first bend away from this singular configuration; target configurations themselves have nonsingular elbow angles.
- **[Reasoned implication] Research decisions:** This can change whether early progress is interpreted from Cartesian distance alone or also from joint motion, branch choice, and escape from the straight-arm singularity. If the protected reset matches the research reset, a 500-step episode that starts outside tolerance must first enter it by step 401 at the latest to leave 100 consecutive samples for the hold.
- **[Unresolved quantity] Assumptions:** The singular-start result is established for the visible research reset; the task contract does not specify the protected evaluator's initial joint state. The step-401 bound assumes its hold counter starts at the first in-band control sample and uses the stated 100-step criterion.
- **[Repository fact] Source references:** [`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/environment.py), [`contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml`](../contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml), [`contracts/task_spec.py`](../contracts/task_spec.py), [`contracts/scenario.md`](../contracts/scenario.md#official-task).
- **[Unresolved quantity] Discriminating evidence:** Initial joint state and target-relative error in the relevant environment, followed by joint/endpoint trajectories and first-reach steps across target angles and radii, would support or falsify the singular-start explanation for observed slow reaches.

### Torque control, transients, and stabilization

- **[Reasoned implication]** Under standard MuJoCo motor semantics, the authored gear and control bounds produce nominal joint torque \(\tau\approx5u\) per hinge for \(u\in[-1,1]\); the policy commands torque, not a joint position or velocity. Schematically, the zero-gravity dynamics are \((M_{\rm links}(q)+\operatorname{diag}(0.01))\ddot q+c(q,\dot q)+\operatorname{diag}(0.5)\dot q=5u\), where \(c\) denotes Coriolis/centrifugal terms. One command is held for 20 ms while the simulator advances in 2 ms steps. In the modeled no-load, zero-gravity plant, a stationary pose with zero velocity needs no gravity-compensating torque; damping dissipates motion but does not stop it instantaneously.
- **[Reasoned implication] Research decisions:** This can change whether control work prioritizes acceleration, braking, and settling versus only reducing position error. Reaching the tolerance while moving is insufficient: residual joint velocity maps through the Jacobian to endpoint motion and can break the hold. The no-gravity, no-contact hold is not evidence of robustness to payloads or disturbances.
- **[Unresolved quantity] Assumptions:** The nominal torque mapping assumes the compiled motor uses the standard direct motor transmission and that no default force limit changes it. Acceleration and stopping distance depend on compiled link inertia and configuration; those values are not explicit in the XML.
- **[Repository fact] Source references:** [`contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml`](../contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml), [`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/environment.py), [`contracts/task_spec.py`](../contracts/task_spec.py).
- **[Unresolved quantity] Discriminating evidence:** Compiled masses/inertias and actuator limits, together with per-step actions, joint velocities, endpoint velocity/error, and hold interruptions, would distinguish inadequate torque authority from inertia-driven overshoot, poor braking, or an inaccurate dynamic assumption.

### State feedback, hidden task state, and behavior diagnostics

- **[Reasoned implication]** The default observation is privileged simulator-state feedback, not visual reaching: it supplies joint position/velocity and a signed endpoint-target vector. Given the known forward kinematics, that vector and joint positions also determine the target coordinates. The four IK offsets are derived features for both elbow branches, wrapped at \(\pm\pi\); they are not independent sensors. The current observation does not include the held-step counter, episode-step/deadline, previous action, or a camera image. Thus the physical state relevant to the visible plant is largely exposed, but the success automaton and episode horizon are not fully Markov in a memoryless observation.
- **[Reasoned implication] Research decisions:** This can change whether an observation or controller limitation is attributed to perception, state estimation, branch representation, or missing dwell/history information. A stable policy can succeed without knowing the exact remaining hold time by staying inside tolerance; the hidden counter or clock matters if behavior depends on hold progress or the deadline.
- **[Unresolved quantity] Assumptions:** This conclusion describes the default observation builder. Protected evaluation executes the saved runtime, so a particular candidate may use different transforms, normalization, or recurrent state.
- **[Repository fact] Source references:** [`robot_learning/scenario/observations.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/observations.py), [`robot_learning/scenario/policy_io.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/policy_io.py), [`contracts/policy_runtime.py`](../contracts/policy_runtime.py), [`contracts/instruments.md`](../contracts/instruments.md#saved-controller-execution).
- **[Unresolved quantity] Discriminating evidence:** The candidate's saved observation/action runtime and its actual observation/action traces would establish what information and commands the evaluated policy receives and emits. Comparison with simulator state would reveal any unmodeled transform or information loss.

### Reach, convergence, and uninterrupted hold

- **[Reasoned implication]** Success is a conjunction: reach the 1 cm band, converge sufficiently to remain there, then complete an uninterrupted 2 s hold (represented by 100 consecutive 50 Hz control steps in the contract). A brief touch, a low mean distance, or many fragmented in-band steps is not success. In the visible research implementation, distance is checked only after each 20 ms action block; an excursion between those checks does not reset its counter. The official contract's continuous-hold requirement remains authoritative, so this visible sampling detail must not be assumed to describe protected checking.
- **[Reasoned implication] Research decisions:** This can change whether the leading failure mode is reaching, convergence, or stabilization, and whether aggregate reward or distance is an adequate proxy for the task. The full behavior is scientifically described by target radius/angle and initial error; time/steps to first reach; minimum and final error; joint position, velocity and acceleration; action/torque and actuator work; maximum uninterrupted hold and interruptions; endpoint speed/path; Jacobian conditioning and joint-limit margin. Existing research evaluation records several distance, timing, target, and hold-streak measures, but not the complete joint/action trajectory.
- **[Unresolved quantity] Assumptions:** Research diagnostics transfer only to the extent that their reset, control cadence, and hold-check semantics match protected evaluation. The official success definition, rather than a shaping reward or diagnostic proxy, determines the outcome.
- **[Repository fact] Source references:** [`contracts/scenario.md`](../contracts/scenario.md#success-criterion), [`contracts/task_spec.py`](../contracts/task_spec.py), [`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/environment.py), [`robot_learning/scenario/evaluation.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/evaluation.py).
- **[Unresolved quantity] Discriminating evidence:** Per-episode first-reach step, longest uninterrupted streak, interruptions, target geometry, and success separate no-reach from reach-then-loss failures. Joint/action traces and within-control-step distance extrema would distinguish endpoint-sampled stability from a physically uninterrupted hold.

## Unknowns

### Compiled numerical dynamics and effective authority

- **[Unresolved quantity]** The XML gives geometry, damping, armature, timestep, and motor control range, but no explicit body masses/inertias, geom density, integrator/solver choice, separate actuator force range, or velocity limit. The control bound and gear imply nominal \(\pm5\) joint torque under standard motor semantics; exact acceleration, configuration-dependent coupling, stopping distance, and any additional compiled limiting or solver effects require the compiled model and its defaults.
- **[Reasoned implication] Research decisions:** These quantities could change conclusions about action scaling, expected reach/settling time, and whether a failure is control-limited or inertia/solver-limited.
- **[Unresolved quantity] Assumptions:** Any numerical estimate must use the actual MuJoCo version and its compilation defaults, not infer physical mass from capsule dimensions alone.
- **[Repository fact] Source references:** [`contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml`](../contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml), [`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/environment.py), and the locked simulator version described in [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md).
- **[Unresolved quantity] Discriminating evidence:** The compiled model's body mass/inertia, actuator transmission/force limits, and observed acceleration and velocity decay under known controls would support, weaken, or revise the dynamic predictions.

### Arm-geom self-contact and path constraints

- **[Unresolved quantity]** The target, base, and floor geoms explicitly disable contact, but the upper-arm and forearm capsules do not specify contact masks or an explicit exclusion. Their capsules meet at the elbow and overlap by their radii; whether compiled parent-child contact filtering excludes this pair is not stated in the robot XML.
- **[Reasoned implication] Research decisions:** If this pair generates contacts, contact forces could alter motion or constrain trajectories despite the kinematic inverse solutions. That would change whether a failure is attributed to policy control or contact mechanics.
- **[Unresolved quantity] Assumptions:** The uncertainty depends on the compiled MuJoCo collision-filter defaults and whether contacts are active for these connected bodies.
- **[Repository fact] Source references:** [`contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml`](../contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml), [`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/environment.py).
- **[Unresolved quantity] Discriminating evidence:** Compiled geom contact masks and contact-pair/force records at reset and representative folded configurations would establish whether self-contact occurs and whether it changes the reachable path or dynamics.

## Decision-relevant synthesis

### Reachability, singular start, and target coverage

- **[Reasoned implication] Decision relevance:** The official annulus is kinematically reachable under joint limits, but the visible reset is fully extended and singular; the visible baseline training range also omits the official inner 6–14 cm band, which requires more folded elbow solutions. Compiled link self-contact could still constrain paths. These facts can change whether early work focuses on initialization/trajectory behavior, branch handling, target coverage, or contact mechanics; they do not establish a preferred intervention.
- **[Unresolved quantity] Assumptions:** The protected task shares the visible reset and kinematics, the official sampling law gives meaningful weight to the omitted inner band, and compiled link-contact filtering does not add an unexpected constraint.
- **[Repository fact] Sources:** [`contracts/scenario.md`](../contracts/scenario.md#official-task), [`contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml`](../contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml), [`contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.py`](../contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.py), [`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/environment.py), [`robot_learning/training/environment.py`](../robot_learning/training/environment.py).
- **[Unresolved quantity] Discriminating evidence:** Initial joint/target state and first-reach/hold outcomes stratified by radius and azimuth, alongside IK branch and joint-limit margins and compiled contact records, would distinguish coverage, singular escape, contact constraints, and subsequent control.

### Torque transients versus hold stability

- **[Reasoned implication] Decision relevance:** Success requires stopping and staying within 1 cm for an uninterrupted 2 s (100 consecutive control steps at current timing), not merely reaching. Direct torque control, damping, and zero gravity make stationary holding a zero-load equilibrium, but do not eliminate momentum-driven overshoot; this can change which part of behavior a first campaign comparison needs to explain.
- **[Unresolved quantity] Assumptions:** The nominal gear-to-torque mapping and visible 50 Hz hold checks represent the compiled protected plant; compiled inertia and between-sample enforcement remain unverified.
- **[Repository fact] Sources:** [`contracts/task_spec.py`](../contracts/task_spec.py), [`contracts/scenario.md`](../contracts/scenario.md#success-criterion), [`contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml`](../contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml), [`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`](../robot_learning/scenario/environment.py).
- **[Unresolved quantity] Discriminating evidence:** Compiled inertias/actuator limits plus per-episode first reach, longest held streak, interruptions, endpoint/joint velocities, actions, and within-block distance extrema would distinguish slow reach from overshoot or a hold-check mismatch.

### Privileged observation and protected-runtime fidelity

- **[Reasoned implication] Decision relevance:** The default policy receives simulator-state feedback and target-relative geometry, not camera perception, while the dwell counter and episode clock are absent from its instantaneous observation. This can change how policy limitations are interpreted and whether research-environment conclusions transfer to the saved policy used by protected evaluation.
- **[Unresolved quantity] Assumptions:** A candidate preserves the default observation/action mapping and any protected transforms do not change what state reaches the policy.
