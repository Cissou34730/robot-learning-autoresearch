## Established facts

### Robot morphology, coordinates, and simulator

- [Repository fact] The robot is a planar two-link arm with two hinge degrees of freedom: shoulder angle `q1` and elbow angle `q2`, both rotating about the world z axis. The upper arm length is 0.12 m and the forearm length is 0.10 m. Source: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:12-21`; `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.py:3-7`.
- [Repository fact] The arm plane is z = 0.02 m. The end-effector site is 0.10 m along the forearm, so its base-relative planar position is
  `p(q) = [0.12*cos(q1) + 0.10*cos(q1+q2), 0.12*sin(q1) + 0.10*sin(q1+q2), 0.02]`.
  Source: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:12-20`.
- [Repository fact] Each joint is specified with range -170 to 170 degrees, damping 0.5, and armature 0.01. The XML specifies zero gravity and a MuJoCo physics timestep of 0.002 s. Source: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:1-3,13-18`.
- [Repository fact] The visual plane, arm geoms, and target geom have collision disabled (`contype="0"` and `conaffinity="0"` where present); no task contact or grasp interaction is defined. The target is a mocap sphere. Source: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:6,10-11,25-27`.
- [Repository fact] The two actuators are MuJoCo motors attached directly to the shoulder and elbow, each with control range [-1, 1] and gear 5. Source: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:30-33`.

### Reset, task geometry, and timing

- [Repository fact] Reset sets both joint positions and velocities to zero, forwards the model, then samples target angle uniformly over [-pi, pi] and target radius uniformly over [0.06, 0.20] m. The target z coordinate is set to the current end-effector z coordinate. Source: `contracts/task_spec.py:6-12`; `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:83-120`.
- [Repository fact] The initial end effector is at (0.22, 0, 0.02) m. Source: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:12-20`; `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:109-114`.
- [Repository fact] Official success requires distance to the target to be at most 0.01 m for 2.0 s without interruption. With frame skip 10 and timestep 0.002 s, one policy action spans 0.020 s and the environment requires 100 consecutive in-tolerance control steps. An episode truncates at 500 control steps. Source: `contracts/task_spec.py:8-12`; `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:52-68,122-168`.
- [Repository fact] The official final assessment uses 200 fixed-panel episodes and requires at least 196 successes for the 98% objective. Source: `contracts/scenario.md:16-31`.

### Actuation, observations, and outcome bookkeeping

- [Repository fact] An action is converted to a two-element physical action, clipped to [-1, 1], written to `data.ctrl`, and held constant for 10 MuJoCo steps before distance and success state are evaluated. Source: `robot_learning/scenario/policy_io.py:11-16`; `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:122-168`.
- [Repository fact] The 11-element observation contains two joint positions, two joint velocities, the three-dimensional end-effector-to-target displacement, and four wrapped residuals to the open-elbow and folded-elbow inverse-kinematic solutions. Source: `robot_learning/scenario/observations.py:11-49`.
- [Repository fact] Joint state, target displacement, and the two analytic IK solutions are computed without sensor noise or an explicit observation delay. The hold counter, episode step count, previous action, and actuator/internal solver state are not included in the observation. Source: `robot_learning/scenario/observations.py:14-49`; `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:70-80,116-120`.
- [Repository fact] A distance sample inside tolerance increments the hold counter; one outside sample resets it to zero. Termination is based on the counter, not on reward. Current research evaluation records first reach, minimum and final distance, maximum held steps, in-tolerance steps, and hold interruptions. Source: `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:134-168`; `robot_learning/scenario/evaluation.py:51-105`.
- [Repository fact] The current training constructor samples radii only from [0.14, 0.20] m, while the official evaluation range is [0.06, 0.20] m. Training distribution is therefore a research choice, not the immutable official task definition. Source: `robot_learning/training/environment.py:14-19`; `contracts/task_spec.py:6-12`; `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:171-178`.

## Physical consequences

### Kinematic reachability and alternative configurations

- [Reasoned implication] With link lengths `l1=0.12` and `l2=0.10`, the unconstrained planar workspace is the annulus 0.02 m <= radius <= 0.22 m. The official radii 0.06-0.20 m lie strictly inside that annulus, so every official target has two nonsingular geometric IK branches before joint limits are applied:
  `q2 = +/- acos((r^2-l1^2-l2^2)/(2*l1*l2))`, with `q1` determined by target angle and the chosen branch. Decision relevance: a policy can solve the task by selecting either elbow posture or by moving between them; branch-aware representations may affect early learning and failure diagnosis. Assumptions: planar forward kinematics and the stated link lengths are exact, with no unmodeled geometry. Source references: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:12-20`; `robot_learning/scenario/observations.py:18-35`. Discriminating evidence: measured joint trajectories and branch residuals showing which branch is used and whether branch changes precede failures.
- [Reasoned implication] The joint limits do not remove the entire official task: at the smallest official radius, the required elbow magnitude is about 150.3 degrees, below the 170-degree limit, and at the largest it is about 49.5 degrees. Depending on target angle, one branch can nevertheless be outside the shoulder limit, so the legal solution set can change from two branches to one. Decision relevance: target-angle-dependent success gaps may be kinematic rather than optimization noise. Assumptions: the XML joint ranges are active MuJoCo limits. Source references: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:13-18`; `contracts/task_spec.py:6`; `robot_learning/scenario/observations.py:29-35`. Discriminating evidence: target-angle-binned success and minimum joint-limit margins, together with confirmed runtime limit activation.
- [Reasoned implication] The four IK residuals expose proximity to both alternatives but do not impose a branch. Since joint positions are also observed, the current geometric branch is inferable from the full observation; the redundancy is a control choice, not a perceptual ambiguity. Decision relevance: a controller can prefer the branch with larger limit margin or shorter motion without needing hidden target information. Assumptions: the observation functions and forward kinematics remain as implemented. Source references: `robot_learning/scenario/observations.py:27-49`. Discriminating evidence: compare residual-to-branch, joint-limit margin, path length, and success for each target sector.

### Dynamics, actuation, and control authority

- [Reasoned implication] The commanded generalized actuator effort is nominally proportional to `5 * ctrl` at each joint, with `ctrl` in [-1, 1], while viscous joint damping contributes a resisting torque proportional to 0.5 times joint velocity and armature adds 0.01 to effective joint inertia. Thus actions control acceleration rather than position directly, and a policy must brake as it approaches a target. Decision relevance: action scaling, action smoothness, and braking behavior can change reach time and hold reliability. Assumptions: MuJoCo motor transmission uses the stated scalar gear as the generalized force multiplier and no additional actuator dynamics or force limit is active. Source references: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:13-18,30-33`; `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:125-132`. Discriminating evidence: logged action, qvel, qacc, and distance traces, especially saturation and overshoot frequency.
- [Reasoned implication] The 10-physics-step zero-order hold makes the closed loop relatively coarse: the policy cannot correct within the 0.020 s action interval, and the target is judged only after that interval. A trajectory that crosses the tolerance boundary between evaluations can still count as inside, while a brief boundary crossing at an evaluation instant resets the complete hold. Decision relevance: temporal resolution and braking margin may matter more than nominal endpoint accuracy. Assumptions: success samples are exactly the post-frame-skip states shown in `step`. Source references: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:2`; `contracts/task_spec.py:8-12`; `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:56-58,131-159`. Discriminating evidence: compare per-physics-step distance traces with the recorded control-step outcomes and identify boundary crossings between samples.
- [Reasoned implication] Because gravity is zero and there are no enabled contacts, a perfectly reached target with zero velocity requires no steady holding torque in the modeled world. The hard part is therefore convergence and suppression of residual motion, not supporting link weight or resisting environmental contact. Decision relevance: a controller that learns aggressive reaching plus stable settling may be sufficient; gravity compensation and contact-force reasoning are not justified by this task. Assumptions: no hidden disturbance, contact, friction, or actuator dynamics materially enters the model. Source references: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:1-3,6,10-11,25-27`. Discriminating evidence: observe whether a stationary in-tolerance configuration remains stationary under zero action and whether failures occur only during approach or also after settling.
- [Reasoned implication] The model is deterministic conditional on initial target and action history, so repeated failures with the same seed and policy should be attributable to the closed-loop trajectory rather than observation noise. Decision relevance: reproducible failure classes can support causal diagnosis from a small panel. Assumptions: the simulator, policy runtime, and random seed are held fixed. Source references: `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:102-120`; `robot_learning/scenario/evaluation.py:34-44`. Discriminating evidence: repeated seeded rollouts with identical target and state traces.

### Initial state and target geometry

- [Reasoned implication] The reset posture is fully extended along +x. Initial target distance depends strongly on target angle: it can be as small as 0.02 m for radius 0.20 along +x and as large as 0.42 m for radius 0.20 along -x. The controller therefore faces both near-settling cases and long reorientation/reach cases at reset. Decision relevance: first-action behavior and time-to-first-reach should be separated from hold failures. Assumptions: the end-effector position follows the stated two-link geometry exactly. Source references: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:12-20`; `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:109-117`; `contracts/task_spec.py:6`. Discriminating evidence: first-reach step and initial-distance distributions stratified by target angle and radius.
- [Reasoned implication] Uniform radius sampling is not uniform area sampling: inner radii receive the same radial probability as outer radii despite representing less Cartesian area. The official distribution therefore gives substantial weight to central targets, including the [0.06, 0.14) region omitted by the current training constructor. Decision relevance: training on [0.14, 0.20] can produce a policy with good reported training behavior but an untested kinematic regime under the official distribution. Assumptions: the contract's phrase "sample a target uniformly from 6-20 cm" is implemented by the shown uniform radius and angle draws. Source references: `contracts/scenario.md:7-13`; `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:83-97`; `robot_learning/training/environment.py:14-19`. Discriminating evidence: official-range evaluation broken down by radius, especially 6-14 cm versus 14-20 cm.

### Observation, control, and task outcome

- [Reasoned implication] Under the current noiseless model, the physical task state needed for feedback is effectively observable: q and qdot are direct, end-effector-target displacement is supplied, and target position can be reconstructed from forward kinematics and that displacement. The missing hold counter does not prevent holding because the required action after reaching is continued stabilization, but it prevents a memoryless policy from knowing how much uninterrupted time remains. Decision relevance: policies may need only state feedback for physical control, while recurrent memory or a timer could affect termination-aware behavior and reward use. Assumptions: no hidden actuator state, disturbance, or observation normalization artifact changes the runtime information. Source references: `robot_learning/scenario/observations.py:27-49`; `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:134-159`. Discriminating evidence: compare success and interruptions for policies with and without memory while holding q, qvel, and target fixed.
- [Reasoned implication] The official outcome is a conjunction of reachability and temporal invariance: entering the 1 cm ball is necessary but insufficient; any sampled exit restarts the 100-step run. Consequently, a policy can have low minimum distance yet fail almost every episode through late overshoot, and fast reaching cannot compensate for unstable holding. Decision relevance: model selection must separate first reach, continuous hold, and hold interruption rate rather than rely on aggregate reward or minimum distance. Assumptions: the environment's counter reset is the authoritative success mechanism. Source references: `contracts/scenario.md:16-20`; `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:134-168`; `robot_learning/scenario/evaluation.py:90-105`. Discriminating evidence: episode diagnostics for first reach, maximum held steps, interruptions, final distance, and total success.
- [Reasoned implication] Current reward terms provide dense distance progress and hold-progress credit, plus action cost and a completion bonus, but reward is not the task definition. Reward can therefore favor fast approach or partial holding even when the official binary outcome fails. Decision relevance: training diagnostics and reward curves should not be treated as evidence of 98% official success. Assumptions: the current reward remains the training objective. Source references: `robot_learning/training/reward.py:16-25,47-107`; `contracts/scenario.md:16-31`. Discriminating evidence: paired reward and binary-outcome traces, particularly episodes with high reward and interrupted holds.

### Behavior and failure classes

- [Reasoned implication] The dominant physical failure classes are: unreachable or limit-constrained posture selection, slow or saturated approach, overshoot caused by insufficient braking, residual oscillation at the tolerance boundary, and a late hold interruption. Since all official targets are geometrically within the unconstrained workspace, persistent failures in the middle of the range would more likely indicate control or learning limitations than raw reach impossibility. Decision relevance: the first research decision should depend on which class dominates, not only on aggregate success. Assumptions: the XML is the complete physical model and target sampling is as specified. Source references: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:1-33`; `contracts/task_spec.py:6-12`; `robot_learning/scenario/evaluation.py:90-105`. Discriminating evidence: classify each failure using target geometry, joint-limit margin, action saturation, first-reach step, minimum distance, and hold interruptions.
- [Reasoned implication] Scientifically meaningful trajectory quantities include target radius and angle, q and qdot, actuator command and saturation, end-effector distance and velocity, first-reach time, tolerance-boundary margin, uninterrupted hold length, interruption count, and joint-limit margin. These quantities connect morphology, control authority, and the binary task outcome across the complete behavior. Decision relevance: they distinguish a changed mechanism from a changed aggregate score. Assumptions: the quantities are logged at adequate temporal resolution or can be reconstructed without aliasing. Source references: `robot_learning/scenario/evaluation.py:51-105`; `robot_learning/scenario/observations.py:27-49`. Discriminating evidence: synchronized trajectory logs containing these quantities, rather than success percentage alone.

## Unknowns

### Compiled inertial and numerical dynamics

- [Unresolved quantity] The XML does not explicitly state body masses, inertial tensors, geom density, solver/integrator settings, or any numerical friction parameters. MuJoCo therefore supplies or derives some runtime quantities that cannot be assigned exact values from the visible XML alone. Decision relevance: these quantities determine acceleration, stopping distance, and whether the available torque can settle within the 1 cm band. Assumptions: compiler-derived inertial values and defaults are materially different from any informal nominal model. Source references: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:1-33`. Discriminating evidence: inspect the compiled `MjModel` mass/inertia, actuator transmission and limits, integrator, solver settings, then compare predicted and observed qacc and stopping distance.

### Joint-limit and actuator-edge behavior

- [Unresolved quantity] The source specifies joint ranges and motor control ranges but does not, by itself, establish the exact runtime limit enforcement impulses, actuator force saturation after transmission, or behavior at the range boundary. Decision relevance: edge-angle failures could be caused by legal-branch loss, limit impulses, or control saturation, changing whether branch selection or dynamic control is the relevant research direction. Assumptions: MuJoCo default `limited` and actuator semantics are relied upon without explicit XML attributes. Source references: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:13-18,30-33`; `robot_learning/scenario/observations.py:29-35`. Discriminating evidence: compiled model flags and force ranges, plus q/qvel/action traces for targets near the angular sectors where one IK branch approaches a limit.

### Effective stabilization margin

- [Unresolved quantity] The definitions establish damping, armature, timestep, and action hold, but do not establish the resulting closed-loop settling time or the maximum residual velocity compatible with remaining inside a 1 cm ball for 2 s. Decision relevance: this determines whether the first campaign emphasis should be reach trajectory shaping, low-gain settling, explicit hold-aware control, or another mechanism. Assumptions: the policy can exploit qvel and target error but cannot react faster than every 0.020 s. Source references: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:1-3,13-18`; `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:56-58,125-159`. Discriminating evidence: time histories of qvel, end-effector velocity, distance margin, and action saturation from first entry through 100 successful samples.

### Observation sufficiency at runtime

- [Unresolved quantity] The nominal observation is noiseless and geometrically rich, but it is not yet established whether the exported policy runtime applies normalization, action transformation, or recurrent state in a way that changes the information and command semantics seen at deployment. Decision relevance: a mismatch could look like a physical-control failure while actually being an observation or runtime-interface failure. Assumptions: the exported runtime uses the same `PolicyIO` mapping and observation ordering as training and evaluation. Source references: `robot_learning/scenario/policy_io.py:7-16`; `robot_learning/scenario/observations.py:11-49`; `robot_learning/scenario/evaluation.py:37-60`. Discriminating evidence: runtime inspection and a one-step equivalence trace comparing raw environment observation, policy input, predicted action, and applied `data.ctrl`.

### Distributional coverage and geometry-dependent reliability

- [Unresolved quantity] No pre-campaign evidence establishes whether a policy trained on the current [0.14, 0.20] m range transfers to the official [0.06, 0.20] m range, or whether success varies by target angle, branch availability, or radius. Decision relevance: this can change the first campaign decision between preserving the current recipe and changing exposure to the official geometry. Assumptions: target geometry is the primary distribution shift and the policy is otherwise unchanged. Source references: `robot_learning/training/environment.py:14-19`; `contracts/task_spec.py:6-12`; `robot_learning/scenario/evaluation.py:90-105`. Discriminating evidence: matched-seed success and failure diagnostics binned by radius, angle, selected IK branch, and joint-limit margin.

## Decision-relevant synthesis

### Official coverage versus current training exposure

- [Reasoned implication] Decision relevance: the current training range omits official 6-14 cm targets, while the official metric weights those targets; early decisions may therefore need to distinguish a reach/hold method failure from a coverage failure.
- [Reasoned implication] Assumptions: the current training constructor is the recipe used for the first candidate and the target sampler is the contract-defined uniform radius/angle sampler.
- [Repository fact] Source references: `robot_learning/training/environment.py:14-19`; `contracts/task_spec.py:6-12`; `contracts/scenario.md:7-20`.
- [Unresolved quantity] Discriminating evidence: radius- and angle-binned official-range outcomes, with first-reach, maximum-held-steps, and interruption diagnostics.

### Hold stability is the success bottleneck, not merely reaching

- [Reasoned implication] Decision relevance: success requires 100 consecutive post-action samples, so a candidate with excellent reach accuracy but late exits is not close to the goal in the relevant physical sense.
- [Reasoned implication] Assumptions: the zero-gravity, no-contact model makes steady holding possible, and the environment's sampled counter is authoritative.
- [Repository fact] Source references: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:1-3,6,25-27`; `contracts/task_spec.py:8-12`; `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:134-168`.
- [Unresolved quantity] Discriminating evidence: synchronized distance, end-effector velocity, qvel, action, and hold-counter traces from first entry through completion or interruption.

### Transient authority and compiled dynamics remain decision-critical

- [Reasoned implication] Decision relevance: direct motor commands, damping, armature, and 0.020 s action holding create a braking and settling problem whose scale cannot be inferred from link lengths alone.
- [Reasoned implication] Assumptions: compiled inertia and default solver behavior materially govern the observed transient, while gravity and contacts do not.
- [Repository fact] Source references: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:1-3,13-18,30-33`; `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:125-132`.
- [Unresolved quantity] Discriminating evidence: compiled model parameters and measured action-to-qacc response, saturation, overshoot, and settling time.

### IK branch and limit structure can create angle-specific failures

- [Reasoned implication] Decision relevance: official targets have two geometric branches before limits, but some target angles may leave only one legal branch; branch-aware failure analysis can prevent treating a kinematic-sector problem as generic learning noise.
- [Reasoned implication] Assumptions: the analytic IK in the observation is consistent with the MuJoCo joint convention and the specified ranges are active.
- [Repository fact] Source references: `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:13-18`; `robot_learning/scenario/observations.py:18-49`.

### Official success must remain the selection criterion

- [Reasoned implication] Decision relevance: reward, minimum distance, or training success can diagnose mechanisms but cannot establish the campaign objective; candidate selection must preserve the distinction between partial reach and complete uninterrupted hold.
- [Reasoned implication] Assumptions: the protected 200-episode assessment and 196-success threshold remain the authoritative commitment test.
