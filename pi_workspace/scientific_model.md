# Scientific model: two-joint arm reach-and-hold

This model describes the embodied system before campaign evidence exists. The
task is a planar regulation problem embedded in the MuJoCo world: a policy must
move a two-link arm from a fixed rest state to a randomly placed stationary
target, then keep the end effector inside a small positional tolerance without
an interruption.

## Established facts

### Robot, coordinates, and reachable geometry

The robot has two revolute degrees of freedom about the vertical world axis.
The shoulder is at `(0, 0, 0.02)` m, the upper arm is 0.12 m long, and the
forearm is 0.10 m long. Both joints rotate in the horizontal `x-y` plane, so
the end-effector height is fixed at 0.02 m. With shoulder angle `q1` and
relative elbow angle `q2`, its planar position is

```text
x = 0.12 cos(q1) + 0.10 cos(q1 + q2)
y = 0.12 sin(q1) + 0.10 sin(q1 + q2).
```

The nominal radial workspace of an unconstrained two-link chain is 0.02 to
0.22 m. Both joints have a specified range of -170 to +170 degrees. The
official target radius is 0.06 to 0.20 m and its angle covers the full
circle; the implementation samples the radius and angle independently and
uniformly. Targets are placed at the arm's fixed height, so the relevant
three-dimensional distance is exactly the planar distance apart from numerical
effects.

For a target `(r, theta)`, the two nominal inverse-kinematic branches are

```text
q2 = +/- acos((r^2 - 0.12^2 - 0.10^2) / (2 * 0.12 * 0.10)),
q1 = theta - atan2(0.10 sin(q2), 0.12 + 0.10 cos(q2)).
```

The observation code computes these open- and folded-elbow branches and wraps
their angular residuals to `[-pi, pi]`. The target annulus is inside the
unconstrained workspace; the joint limits, rather than radial reach, are the
remaining kinematic qualification on each branch.

### Actuation, simulation, and initial state

Each joint is driven by a MuJoCo motor with normalized control in `[-1, 1]`
and gear 5, giving a nominal control-to-joint-torque scale of up to 5 in the
model's torque units. The policy action is passed through unchanged and then
clipped to that range. There is no additional command filter or actuator
state, so the selected command is held constant while the simulator advances.

The physics timestep is 0.002 s and each policy action is applied for 10
simulation steps. The policy therefore acts at 0.02 s (50 Hz), with a
zero-order-held command. The joints have damping 0.5 and armature 0.01. Gravity
is disabled. Link inertias and masses are generated by MuJoCo from the capsule
geometries and their model defaults; no explicit masses or inertias are
specified in the robot description. The plane and target have collisions
disabled, and the target is a kinematic mocap body rather than a contactable
object.

Reset sets both joint positions and velocities to zero, performs forward
kinematics, and then samples a stationary target. Thus the arm initially points
along positive `x` with the end effector at radius 0.22 m, and every episode
starts from the same physical arm state while the target changes. There is no
initial momentum or target motion.

### Task outcome and observable state

After each 0.02 s control interval, success distance is measured from the
end-effector site to the target. A control step counts toward the hold exactly
when that distance is at most 0.01 m. Any outside step resets the consecutive
counter. The episode terminates at 100 consecutive in-tolerance control steps,
which is 2 s, and truncates at 500 control steps, which is 10 s. Therefore a
near miss, a transient entry, or a hold with even one outside sample is a task
failure rather than partial success.

The policy receives 11 values: the two joint positions, two joint velocities,
the three-component end-effector-to-target vector, and four wrapped angular
residuals to the two analytical IK branches. The vertical component of the
position error is structurally zero. The target is not given as a separate
coordinate, but with joint positions and the forward kinematics the
end-effector position and error identify its planar position. Consequently the
physical target state is observable in this ideal simulator, although the
representation presents it through redundant current-error and IK features.

## Physical consequences

The arm must solve a coupled reach-and-regulate problem, not merely choose a
reachable pose. Reaching requires selecting a joint trajectory that reduces
the two-dimensional error from the common start pose. Convergence requires
reducing both position error and residual joint velocity; because the target
does not move, velocity is not needed for task motion once the target is
reached and instead becomes a source of hold failure. Stabilization therefore
requires torque that opposes motion and compensates for coupled joint
dynamics, while avoiding an oscillation whose excursions exceed 1 cm.

The two joints are dynamically coupled even though each motor is assigned to
one joint: changing the elbow changes the forearm's contribution and its
inertia about the shoulder, and changing the shoulder moves the whole distal
chain. Near a target, the local mapping from joint motion to Cartesian motion
is governed by the planar Jacobian. It becomes poorly conditioned near
straight or folded configurations, so small joint errors can produce different
Cartesian sensitivities depending on the selected IK branch. The initial
straight configuration is near the outer workspace boundary, while targets
are at smaller radii; trajectories that first fold the elbow and trajectories
that reorient around the shoulder are physically distinct.

The full angular target distribution makes the shoulder wrap and the joint
limits relevant. A target can have two nominal elbow postures, but one branch
may be preferable or necessary when a shoulder angle approaches its limit.
Crossing the wrapped angle representation is not itself a physical
discontinuity; the actual bounded joint and end-effector motion are continuous.
The policy must nevertheless produce a consistent torque sequence when the
observation changes representation around `-pi`/`pi`.

The direct motor action gives immediate control authority but also exposes the
policy to the consequences of high-speed motion. The 50 Hz decision interval
is long relative to the 2 ms integrator step: ten physics updates occur before
the policy can correct a command. A command that enters the tolerance ball
with appreciable velocity can leave it on the next observation, so an
apparently accurate pose is not sufficient evidence of a successful hold.
Damping dissipates motion, but without gravity there is no passive downward or
other directional settling; stabilization is principally active damping and
position regulation.

The 1 cm tolerance is a Cartesian ball, not separate coordinate tolerances.
The target itself is visually represented by a sphere, but collision and
contact play no role in success. All failures are consequently attributable to
kinematic branch/limit choices, finite-time motion, torque and damping
dynamics, observation-to-action behavior, or the strict uninterrupted timing
rather than to target interaction mechanics.

The shaped training reward contains progress and closeness terms, a linear
hold-progress signal, a small action cost, an outside-band penalty after an
interrupted hold, and a completion bonus. These signals can make approach and
first entry easier to learn, but the only physically authoritative objective is
the binary uninterrupted 2 s outcome. In particular, accumulating time inside
the tolerance is not interchangeable with completing the hold if the trajectory
contains an interruption.

Meaningful measurements of the complete behavior are therefore target radius
and angle; chosen IK branch and joint-limit margin; joint positions, velocities,
accelerations and commanded torques; action saturation and variation;
end-effector position and Cartesian error (including radial and tangential
components); first-entry time; overshoot and peak speed; the duration and
number of in-tolerance intervals; the largest deviation during a nominal hold;
and whether failure occurred before entry, during convergence, or during the
hold. These quantities connect the observed policy output to the mechanical
cause of an episode outcome.

## Unknowns

- The exact masses, center-of-mass locations, and joint-space inertia matrix are
  not specified explicitly. They must be obtained from the instantiated
  MuJoCo model before making quantitative claims about acceleration, peak
  torque demand, or settling time.
- The effective torque and acceleration response under the full MuJoCo actuator
  and joint-limit semantics is not established by the XML alone. In particular,
  the practical effect of the gear, damping, armature, and any limit contact
  near +/-170 degrees needs measurement rather than inference from the nominal
  5-unit control scale.
- It is not yet known how often the official target distribution drives the
  arm near a kinematic singularity or a joint limit, nor whether one IK branch
  is systematically safer across radius and angle.
- The relative importance of first-entry speed, residual velocity, and active
  corrective torque during the 100-step hold is unknown. A policy may fail
  despite small instantaneous distance if its closed-loop damping is
  insufficient.
- The coupled closed-loop behavior of the learned policy is unknown: reach-time
  distribution, branch switching, action saturation, oscillation frequency,
  and sensitivity to the 0.02 s action hold have no pre-campaign evidence.
- The observation is physically sufficient under the exact simulator state, but
  it is unresolved whether the learned function uses the redundant error and IK
  features consistently across angular wrapping and both elbow configurations.
- Training samples use radii 0.14 to 0.20 m, whereas official assessment uses
  0.06 to 0.20 m. Before evidence, it is unknown whether the inner-radius
  targets require qualitatively different folding, dynamics, or branch choices
  that the learned policy will not extrapolate to.
- The pre-campaign model does not establish an episode success probability.
  The 98% objective requires evidence on the fixed 200-episode official panel,
  with at least 196 complete uninterrupted holds; development or shaped-reward
  performance cannot substitute for that result.
