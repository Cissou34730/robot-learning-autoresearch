# Scientific model: two-joint planar reach-and-hold

This model treats the arm, its controller interface, the MuJoCo update loop,
and the hold criterion as one closed-loop physical system. It distinguishes
facts fixed by the implementation from implications derived from those facts
and from quantities that cannot be established without measurements. There is
no campaign evidence yet; the evidence described below is therefore
discriminating evidence for future decisions, not a reported result.

## Established facts

### Morphology and kinematics

The robot is a planar serial arm with two revolute degrees of freedom. The
shoulder joint is at the base and rotates about the world z axis; the upper arm
has length 0.12 m and the forearm has length 0.10 m. The end-effector site is
at the forearm tip. The arm plane is z = 0.02 m, because the upper-arm body is
mounted at that height. With joint coordinates \(q=(q_1,q_2)\), measured from
the zero configuration shown in the XML, its planar forward kinematics are

\[
x = 0.12\cos q_1 + 0.10\cos(q_1+q_2),\qquad
y = 0.12\sin q_1 + 0.10\sin(q_1+q_2).
\]

At reset, both joint positions and velocities are zero, so the end effector
starts at (0.22, 0, 0.02) m with the arm fully extended in the +x direction.
The shoulder and elbow ranges are each -170 to +170 degrees. The unconstrained
radial workspace is the annulus from 0.02 m to 0.22 m; the official target
radii, 0.06 to 0.20 m, lie strictly inside that annulus. The target is always
placed at the end-effector height, so the nominal task is genuinely
two-dimensional even though the distance calculation is three-dimensional.

For target polar coordinates \((r,\phi)\), inverse kinematics has, when
reachable, the two elbow branches

\[
q_2 = \mathord{+/-}\arccos\left(
 \frac{r^2-0.12^2-0.10^2}{2(0.12)(0.10)}
\right),
\]

with

\[
q_1 = \phi-\operatorname{atan2}
 \left(0.10\sin q_2,\ 0.12+0.10\cos q_2\right).
\]

Thus the same target generally has an elbow-open and an elbow-folded
configuration. At the official radial endpoints the elbow magnitudes are
approximately 150 degrees at 0.06 m and 49 degrees at 0.20 m. Both branches
are inside the stated elbow limits, and the shoulder can select a valid
equivalent angle for every target direction in the official range. The
branches are not physically interchangeable: they have different joint
velocities, torque coupling, and paths from the reset posture.

**Sources:** `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:9-23`,
`contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.py:5-7`, and
`contracts/scenario.md:9-12`.

### Actuation, timing, and physical dynamics

Each joint is driven by a MuJoCo motor with command range [-1, 1] and gear 5.
The scenario passes the policy's two action values directly to `data.ctrl`
after clipping to this range; there is no action remapping, rate limiter, or
command interpolation in the scenario code. The motor command is held while
MuJoCo advances ten simulation steps. The simulator timestep is 0.002 s, so
the policy acts at 0.020 s intervals, or 50 control decisions per second.

Gravity is explicitly zero. The XML specifies joint damping 0.5 and armature
0.01 for both joints. The plane and target geoms are explicitly non-contact,
and the target is a kinematic mocap body rather than a dynamic object; no task
object supplies an external contact reaction. Any internal arm-geom contact
behavior is not established by the visible contract. Consequently, absent
policy action, the reset state is a rest state; motion is generated by motor
torque and opposed primarily by damping and inertial effects, not by gravity
or target reaction.
Joint inertia and the resulting acceleration are not stated directly in the
XML: they depend on MuJoCo's compiled geom mass and inertia defaults and the
configuration-dependent two-link inertia.

The local environment measures distance after the ten substeps, increments
the hold counter once per control step inside the tolerance, and resets the
counter on a sampled violation. The required 2.0 s therefore becomes 100
post-control-step successes. An episode is truncated after 500 control steps,
which is 10 s of simulated control time if it does not terminate earlier.

**Sources:** `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:2, 12-20, 30-33`,
`contracts/task_spec.py:6-11`, and
`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:52-58, 122-168`.

### Task outcome and distribution

At reset, radius is sampled uniformly in [0.06, 0.20] m and angle uniformly
over [-pi, pi]. Uniform radius is not uniform area: it gives equal probability
to equal radial intervals and consequently more probability density per unit
area to the inner part of the workspace. The target is static during an
episode. Success is not merely reaching: the end effector must remain within
0.01 m for the complete 100-step hold. A single sampled exit returns the hold
counter to zero, and success is only reported when the counter reaches 100.
The official result is at least 196 successes in a fixed 200-episode panel.

The current training environment samples only radii [0.14, 0.20] m, while the
evaluation environment uses the full official interval. This is a scientific
fact about the current baseline, not an assumption that training and
assessment distributions coincide.

**Sources:** `contracts/scenario.md:9-31`,
`contracts/task_spec.py:6-11`, and
`robot_learning/training/environment.py:14-19`.

### Observation, action, and outcome relationship

The policy receives eleven values: the two joint positions, two joint
velocities, the three-dimensional end-effector-to-target vector, and four
wrapped joint errors to the elbow-open and elbow-folded inverse-kinematic
solutions. The target is not given as a separate polar coordinate, but the
relative vector plus the joint state carries the target geometry: forward
kinematics reconstructs the end-effector position, and subtracting the
relative vector reconstructs target position. The relative z component is
always zero under the reset and target construction. The target has no
velocity or episode-time signal.

The action is applied as joint motor control, causing a state transition over
the next 20 ms. Distance is then computed from the updated site position to
the mocap target. That distance both determines the hold state and contributes
to the current reward. The active reward also contains distance progress,
exponential closeness, linear hold-progress, a completion bonus, a small
quadratic action cost, and an outside-band penalty after a hold interruption.
The reward is a learning signal; the binary episode outcome remains the
distance-and-duration criterion.

Training uses PPO with a feed-forward MLP policy and observation
normalization. The saved runtime applies the same observation transform and
the direct action mapping during evaluation. There is no observed force,
acceleration, actuator state, contact state, target identity, or explicit
hold-counter state.

## Physical consequences

### 1. Reachability is broad, but radial and branch geometry are not uniform

**Consequence.** The official task is kinematically feasible throughout its
declared radius range, but targets near 0.06 m require a deeply folded
configuration and targets near 0.20 m require a relatively open one. Every
direction is available through at least one shoulder representation, while
the two inverse-kinematic branches offer different trajectories and dynamic
conditioning. The start posture is already close to a target near 0.20 m at
angle zero but is far from most other target geometries.

**Decision relevance.** This can change whether the first campaign decision
should treat failures as a single reach problem or separate inner-radius,
outer-radius, and angular/branch regimes. It can also change whether a policy
representation is judged by branch consistency rather than only endpoint
distance.

**Assumptions.** The analytic kinematics use the XML body offsets and site
positions, target height is exactly the reset end-effector height, and the
joint limits are the only kinematic limits. They do not assume a particular
learned behavior.

**Sources.** `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:12-20`,
`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:83-97`, and
`robot_learning/scenario/observations.py:18-47`.

**Discriminating evidence.** For each radius-angle bin, solve or measure the
closest attainable distance on both branches and record the executed branch,
joint-limit proximity, and endpoint error. Kinematic feasibility is weakened
by a bin with persistent nonzero minimum error or an active limit; it is
supported by both branches reaching tolerance across angles.

### 2. The initial condition makes path and convergence part of the task

**Consequence.** The arm always begins at zero velocity and maximum extension,
not at an inverse-kinematic solution. The policy must first generate a
coordinated rotation, then remove joint velocity and residual positional error
before it can accumulate hold time. A successful endpoint with appreciable
velocity is physically different from a converged endpoint because the same
inertia can carry the end effector out of the 1 cm ball after entry.

**Decision relevance.** This can change the first decision about whether the
dominant limitation is geometric reach, transient settling, or steady
stabilization, and whether time-to-first-entry is a meaningful bottleneck
relative to hold interruptions.

**Assumptions.** There are no unmodeled disturbances or contacts and the
reset really sets all qpos and qvel entries to zero before the first action.

**Sources.** `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:102-120` and
`contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:2, 9-20`.

**Discriminating evidence.** Measure joint velocity, end-effector velocity,
distance, and acceleration from first entry through all 100 hold steps.
Large velocity or overshoot at entry followed by exits supports a transient
or damping explanation; low velocity with exits localized to particular
angles or branches weakens it and points toward geometric or control
conditioning.

### 3. The actuator and sample rate impose a stabilization bandwidth

**Consequence.** A command affects ten 2 ms integration steps before the
policy can issue its next command. With direct saturated motor commands, the
policy has strong authority for gross motion but must regulate at a 20 ms
zero-order-hold update period at the scale of the hold tolerance. Damping
removes energy, but the unknown compiled inertia determines how much torque
produces acceleration and
how much residual motion remains after an action reversal. The two joints are
dynamically coupled even though each has its own motor.

**Decision relevance.** This can change decisions about whether a failure is
an authority/saturation problem, a sampling-bandwidth problem, or a policy
precision problem. The distinction matters more for hold success than for
first reach.

**Assumptions.** MuJoCo's motor gear and default actuator semantics are the
ones used by the compiled XML; no external force or noise is injected.

**Sources.** `contracts/robots/two_joint_arm.xml:2, 13-18, 30-33`,
`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:125-133`, and
`contracts/task_spec.py:8`.

**Discriminating evidence.** Identify the compiled body/joint inertias and
measure step responses at representative configurations, including peak
velocity, settling time, action saturation, and residual distance oscillation.
Configuration-dependent acceleration or saturation supports an inertia/torque
explanation; nearly identical fast responses across configurations shifts
attention toward policy learning and observation use.

### 4. The observation is close to Markov for this simulator, but the hold
state is external to it

**Consequence.** Joint position and velocity plus target-relative geometry are
enough in principle to determine the deterministic mechanical state relevant
to the next transition. The four branch errors make both endpoint solutions
explicit. However, the observation omits the environment's held-step counter
and elapsed time. A feed-forward policy therefore cannot condition its action
on how much of the hold has already been accumulated; it must maintain a
stable feedback state that works for the whole interval. The omitted counter
does not prevent success if the physical state is stationary, but it makes
near-boundary behavior history-blind.

**Decision relevance.** This can change whether the first campaign decision
concerns physical-state feedback and branch selection or an apparent need for
memory/time information. It also determines whether reward trends are trusted
as evidence of true hold competence.

**Assumptions.** The target remains fixed, observations are noiseless, and
there are no hidden actuator states beyond the MuJoCo state represented by
qpos and qvel. The runtime uses the saved normalization statistics.

**Sources.** `robot_learning/scenario/observations.py:27-49`,
`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:134-168`,
`robot_learning/train.py:103-136`, and
`contracts/policy_runtime.py:162-177`.

**Discriminating evidence.** Compare repeated actions and outcomes for the
same physical observation at different hold ages, and inspect whether
failure probability changes with held-step count while qpos, qvel, and error
are similar. No such dependence supports the Markov/stationary account;
hold-age-dependent outcomes would expose an unmodeled history or runtime
effect.

### 5. The local hold test is sampled at control boundaries

**Consequence.** The scenario implementation checks distance after each
20 ms control interval, not after each internal 2 ms substep, and resets the
counter only when a sampled post-interval distance is outside tolerance.
Fast excursions that leave and re-enter between checks would not be visible to
this local counter. The human contract describes an uninterrupted two-second
hold, so the exact relationship between this local sampling and the protected
official evaluator is consequential.

**Decision relevance.** This can change whether a measured hold failure is
interpreted as physical instability and whether control-step diagnostics are
sufficient evidence for the official criterion. It is a semantic boundary,
not a proposed change to the task.

**Assumptions.** The protected benchmark's success implementation is not
inferred from the research environment; only the visible local semantics are
asserted here.

**Sources.** `contracts/scenario.md:9-20`,
`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:131-159`, and
`contracts/task_spec.py:8-11`.

**Discriminating evidence.** Compare distance continuously over the ten
substeps with the post-step distance used by the counter for trajectories
near tolerance. Agreement supports the sampled counter as an adequate proxy;
between-check exits weaken that proxy and require official-semantics evidence
before interpreting hold statistics.

### 6. Training exposure does not cover the official radial distribution

**Consequence.** The baseline policy is trained only on 0.14-0.20 m targets
but assessed on 0.06-0.20 m targets. The inner 0.06-0.14 m region is both
unseen by this training constructor and dynamically more folded. A high
training success rate can therefore coexist with systematic official failures
without any contradiction in the robot mechanics.

**Decision relevance.** This can change the first campaign decision from
optimizing generic learning behavior to diagnosing distributional and
kinematic coverage. It also makes aggregate success insufficient to localize
the cause of failure.

**Assumptions.** The current training constructor and current active
configuration are the ones used for the first campaign; no hidden curriculum
or target wrapper changes the stated interval.

**Sources.** `robot_learning/training/environment.py:14-19`,
`contracts/scenario.md:9-12`, and
`robot_learning/scenario/evaluation.py:90-105`.

**Discriminating evidence.** Report success, first-entry time, minimum
distance, maximum held steps, and interruption count by radius and angle.
An inner-radius deficit with otherwise stable outer performance supports the
distribution/branch explanation; angle-only or all-radius failures weaken it.

### 7. Reward progress can be decoupled from uninterrupted success

**Consequence.** The reward pays for decreasing distance and for each
increment of hold progress, while the environment resets hold progress on an
exit and gives no hold-exit forfeiture under the current coefficient. A policy
can therefore receive useful reward for approaching or briefly entering the
band without completing the episode. Conversely, a small action cost and
closeness potential can favor low-motion behavior even when that behavior is
not robust to residual velocity. Reward is not evidence of the 98% outcome.

**Decision relevance.** This can change whether the first campaign decision
uses training reward as a proxy for competence or instead prioritizes
episode-level success and hold-specific diagnostics.

**Assumptions.** The visible reward implementation is the active learning
signal and PPO optimizes only its scalar total, not the diagnostic components.

**Sources.** `robot_learning/training/reward.py:36-107` and
`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py:145-168`.

**Discriminating evidence.** Compare reward components with binary success,
first entry, maximum held steps, and interruptions over the same episodes.
If reward rises while completion and uninterrupted hold do not, the proxy
decoupling is supported; close alignment weakens it for the observed regime.

## Unknowns

1. **Compiled inertial and actuator response quantities.** The XML gives
   damping, armature, gear, and control limits but not the compiled mass
   matrix, effective motor torque in the relevant coordinates, or the
   configuration-dependent acceleration and settling time. These quantities
   decide whether precise holding is authority-limited or bandwidth-limited.
   They are supported by the explicit XML only at the parameter level and
   require compiled-model inspection and step-response measurements to
   resolve. Relevant sources: `two_joint_arm.xml:12-18,30-33`.

2. **Actual branch usage and branch-switching risk.** Both IK solutions are
   represented in the observation, but no implementation fact says which
   branch a learned policy will choose or whether it can cross between them.
   Branch switching could create large transient motion even while endpoint
   error is small. Joint trajectories, the four IK errors, and target-binned
   path traces would support or revise this explanation. Relevant sources:
   `observations.py:18-47` and `two_joint_arm.py:5-7`.

3. **The limiting phase of behavior.** Before evidence, it is unknown whether
   the 98% gap, if present, will be dominated by failure to enter tolerance,
   overshoot at first entry, slow convergence near joint limits, or exits
   during an otherwise successful hold. The needed quantities are first-entry
   step, distance and velocity at entry, maximum held steps, interruption
   count, and action saturation. Relevant sources:
   `scenario/evaluation.py:51-105` and
   `scenario/environment.py:134-159`.

4. **Numerical adequacy of sampled hold semantics for official success.**
   The visible environment does not expose continuous within-substep distance,
   and the protected evaluator is the authority for the official result.
   A substep trajectory audit and comparison with the official evaluator's
   outcome are needed before treating sampled hold diagnostics as physically
   complete. Relevant sources: `scenario.md:11-20` and
   `scenario/environment.py:131-159`.

5. **Generalization over the target distribution.** It is unknown whether
   the current PPO policy, if trained, will extrapolate from the outer
   training annulus to the inner official annulus and whether angle or branch
   asymmetries appear despite rotationally symmetric target sampling. This
   requires stratified, seed-controlled evaluation rather than a single
   aggregate. Relevant sources: `training/environment.py:14-19`,
   `scenario/environment.py:83-97`, and
   `scenario/evaluation.py:90-105`.

6. **The effective precision of the learned closed loop.** The observation
   contains sufficient ideal state variables for deterministic control, but
   it is unknown whether normalization, policy approximation, action
   discretization at 50 Hz, or residual joint velocity leaves a stable
   invariant region smaller than the 1 cm task ball. Repeated local
   perturbation and hold trajectories can distinguish numerical/policy
   precision from kinematic infeasibility. Relevant sources:
   `observations.py:27-49`, `policy_runtime.py:167-177`, and
   `task_spec.py:8-11`.

## Scientifically meaningful quantities

The complete behavior should be characterized in physical units and in task
units: target radius and angle; both joint positions and velocities; end
effector position, velocity, and distance to target; distance and velocity at
first entry; joint-limit margin; action and implied motor-command saturation;
settling time; maximum held steps; total in-tolerance steps; interruption
count; minimum and final distance; and whether the executed trajectory stays
on one IK branch. These quantities separate geometry, actuation, convergence,
and stabilization, which the binary success metric necessarily combines.
They should be examined by radius, angle, and branch, because aggregate
performance can hide a physically coherent failure class.

## Decision-relevant synthesis

1. **The first physical split is reach versus hold.** The official annulus is
   analytically inside the two-link workspace and has two nominal IK branches,
   but reset begins at full extension and success requires 100 consecutive
   control samples. This changes whether early campaign evidence should be
   interpreted through minimum distance or through entry velocity, settling,
   and interruptions. Assumes the XML geometry and reset are authoritative.
   Sources: `two_joint_arm.xml:12-20`,
   `scenario/environment.py:102-159`. Discriminate with radius/angle-binned
   trajectories and hold-phase velocity/distance traces.

2. **Dynamic authority and 50 Hz control may set the precision ceiling.**
   Direct [-1,1] motor commands, ten-substep zero-order hold, explicit damping,
   and unknown compiled inertia jointly determine whether the end effector can
   remain inside 1 cm. This changes the first choice between diagnosing
   actuator/bandwidth limits and diagnosing policy approximation. Assumes no
   unmodeled disturbance or contact. Sources: `two_joint_arm.xml:2,13-18,30-33`,
   `scenario/environment.py:125-133`. Discriminate with compiled inertias,
   step responses, action saturation, and residual oscillation.

3. **Branch and distribution coverage are coupled risks.** Inner targets are
   absent from the current 0.14-0.20 m training interval and require a more
   folded configuration; the observation exposes both IK branches but does
   not force a consistent one. This changes whether official failures are
   attributed to generic learning or to an unseen geometric/dynamic regime.
   Assumes the current training constructor is active. Sources:
   `training/environment.py:14-19`, `observations.py:18-47`,
   `scenario.md:9-12`. Discriminate with target-radius/angle success,
   branch traces, joint-limit margins, and first-entry diagnostics.

4. **The official objective must remain the primary scientific endpoint.**
   The current reward contains useful progress and hold signals but is not the
   success definition, and the local hold counter samples at 20 ms boundaries.
   This changes how much confidence can be placed in reward curves or local
   hold statistics before they are tied to uninterrupted official outcomes.
   Assumes the protected benchmark remains authoritative. Sources:
   `reward.py:47-107`, `scenario.md:17-31`, and
   `scenario/environment.py:134-168`. Discriminate by pairing reward
   components with 200-episode success and by auditing within-interval
   distance excursions.
