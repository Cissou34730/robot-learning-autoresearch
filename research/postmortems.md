# Research postmortems

## dd6d401e-1c3b-46d0-8ecf-3b9304fbff2a / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The baseline learned the reach-and-hold behavior strongly enough to make `checkpoint-100352` the current working and best-known candidate, but the human objective is not established. It achieved 195/200 (97.5%) on the disjoint research panel and 196/200 (98.0%) on the permanently reused task-reference panel. The final checkpoint was slightly worse on both panels, so additional baseline training past the proxy peak is not supported as a selection rule.

**Lessons and limits:** Measured task success, rather than training reward or training success, supports the lineage choice. The three candidates were nearly tied on the research panel, with only 1-3 discordant episodes in each paired comparison. On the fixed task-reference panel, all four failures of `checkpoint-100352` were short-radius targets below 10 cm with negative angles between -128 and -116 degrees; this is a localized residual failure pattern, not evidence that the complete task is solved. The 98.0% task-reference score is not independent confirmation because that panel is permanently reused. The disjoint research result is below the 98% objective, and neither development instrument is the official assessment.

**Open questions:** Whether the localized short-radius, negative-angle failures persist on a new target panel and whether a changed training recipe can improve them without degrading the broad reach-and-hold behavior remain unresolved. The current baseline measurements do not expose enough trajectory detail to separate all reach, settling, and hold-margin mechanisms.

## dd6d401e-1c3b-46d0-8ecf-3b9304fbff2a / Experiment 1

**Result:** The baseline produced a useful but not yet human-objective-confirmed policy. `checkpoint-100352` is selected as the working and best-known lineage.

**Observed behavior:** The 100352-step checkpoint achieved 195/200 successes (97.5%) on the disjoint `research_evaluation` panel and 196/200 (98.0%) on `task_reference`. The 95232-step checkpoint also achieved 195/200 on the research panel but only 192/200 on task-reference; the 120832-step checkpoint achieved 194/200 and 194/200. The 100352-step candidate had five research-panel failures, while the task-reference failures were all truncated at 500 steps and concentrated at radii below 10 cm with angles from about -128 to -116 degrees. Training success and reward peaked around 100352 steps and were lower at 120832 steps. Paired research-panel comparisons had only 1-3 discordant episodes, so the candidate ranking is useful but not strongly separated.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline established high task performance and the proxy peak corresponded to the strongest protected-panel result, but the independent research panel remained at 97.5%, and the reusable task-reference panel cannot independently confirm the 98.0% result. The evidence supports selecting the 100352-step artifact over the pre-peak and final alternatives, while leaving official objective attainment inconclusive.

**Interpretation:** The frozen scientific model predicts that success requires both inverse-kinematic reaching and a 100-step stable hold under bounded torque. The measured completion times and truncated failures are consistent with a policy that usually reaches and stabilizes, with a residual geometry-specific failure mode rather than broad inability to hold. The late checkpoint does not improve that behavior and appears mildly degraded, so the proxy peak is the most useful saved policy for subsequent work.
