## 6a67124b-e88d-49da-aebe-ed9ee362dec4 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The unchanged PPO baseline learned a strong reach-and-hold policy, with `checkpoint-100352` scoring 195/200 and 196/200 on two research panels. On the newer disjoint panel, the retained working lineage scored 191/200 and the targeted transfer checkpoints scored 189/200, 190/200, and 187/200. The tested radius and hard-angle coverage intervention therefore did not improve the selected policy, and development measurements do not establish the official objective.

**Lessons and limits:** Training proxies tracked useful behavior but did not identify the best late checkpoint reliably; continuing beyond `checkpoint-100352` degraded measured success. Every measured transfer checkpoint was below the same-panel working control. This weakens that tested transfer recipe without ruling out other interventions or training variance. The fixed task-reference panel is reused, all development panels are distinct from the official assessment, and the campaign has only one fresh baseline run.

**Open questions:** Whether the selected policy reaches at least 98% on the official final panel remains unresolved. It is also unresolved whether unchanged learning from a fresh seed or a different scientific intervention can improve the residual generalization gap without degrading broad task performance.

The selected checkpoint therefore meets the 98% count on the disjoint development panel, while the official result remains unmeasured.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline hypothesis is supported in the limited sense that the unchanged method learned substantial task competence and approached the human objective. The late proxy improvement corresponded to genuine task progress, but the final training interval weakened measured performance.

**Interpretation:** Select `checkpoint-100352` because it is consistently stronger than the final checkpoint across both research panels and on the fixed reference panel, with independent disjoint-panel confirmation of its near-threshold performance. Keep the baseline scientific recipe unchanged for lineage restoration. No weaker late checkpoint is retained because it adds no demonstrated advantage or independent capability; future training can proceed from the selected lineage and address the remaining uncertainty.

## 6a67124b-e88d-49da-aebe-ed9ee362dec4 / Experiment 2

**Result:** Transfer fine-tuning with broader radius coverage and extra sampling of the observed hard-angle sector did not improve task success. Keep the existing `checkpoint-100352` working lineage, restore its baseline recipe, and do not retain an experiment-2 candidate.

**Observed behavior:** On the disjoint research panel covering episodes 2400-2599, the working control achieved 191/200 (95.5%). `checkpoint-55296` achieved 189/200 (94.5%), `checkpoint-65536` achieved 190/200 (95.0%), and the final `checkpoint-120832` achieved 187/200 (93.5%). Paired comparisons recorded working-policy wins over each candidate of 2, 1, and 4 discordant episodes, with no candidate wins. The transfer run's highest training-success proxy was `checkpoint-65536`, but its measured task result remained below the working control; the final checkpoint was weaker still.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis is contradicted within the tested transfer recipe and measured checkpoints. It predicted at least 196/200 on a disjoint research panel while reducing the known hard-sector failures without broad regression; none of the measured transfer checkpoints reached 196/200, and all underperformed the same-panel control. This conclusion is limited to the one transfer run, its measured checkpoints, and the researcher-owned development panel; it does not establish that every possible radius or angle intervention would fail.

**Interpretation:** The experiment-2 changes provide no evidence for replacing the baseline lineage and add evidence that proxy-peak selection is unsafe for this transfer run. Reverting the changed training-environment recipe preserves the strongest measured policy. The retained policy remains near the human objective but is not shown to satisfy it: its pooled earlier research-panel result was 391/400, while the new disjoint control result was 191/200, and none of these development panels is the official assessment. Further training, if pursued, should be a separate next experiment rather than an extension of this unsuccessful transfer recipe.
