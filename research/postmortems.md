## 9f0c1a9e-23c2-4c48-85a6-09cb6d902999 / Scientific strategy

**Direction:** Retain the experiment-2 full-radius working lineage as the strongest measured recipe. Unchanged continuation did not improve it, experiment 5's explicit inverse-kinematic joint-limit margins produced a severe broad regression, and experiment 6's measured negative-angle oversampling did not repair the recurring failures. The next useful investigation should target action/control or observation-contract behavior while preserving the official task distribution; the tested angular oversampling recipe should not be repeated unchanged.

**Lessons and limits:** Experiment 2's full-radius transfer produced 197/200 (98.5%) on the fixed task-reference panel at checkpoints 100352 and 120832, versus 196/200 (98.0%) for the selected baseline. At checkpoint 100352, sub-14 cm success improved from 101/105 to 103/105, while above-14 cm success fell from 95/95 to 94/95; the distinct 200-episode research panel tied the baseline at 98.5% with zero discordant outcomes. This partially supports broader radius coverage for particular small-radius cases, but not a robust gain or a causal claim. Experiment 3 trained the proposed negative-angle bias for 120,832 steps and produced 24 checkpoints, but none had a task-reference or research-evaluation artifact; its proxy and reward trajectory were training diagnostics only, so its hypothesis was inconclusive. Experiment 4 measured three continued checkpoints on the same development panels: task-reference success was 98.5% (20480), 95.0% (95232), and 97.5% (120832), while research-evaluation success was 98.0%, 97.0%, and 96.5%. The best continuation tied, rather than exceeded, the parent's 98.5% task-reference result, and the training proxy did not track later task regressions. This weakens the insufficient-training explanation on that trajectory, but one continuation from one parent and one seed cannot prove a general PPO plateau or identify a causal mechanism. Experiment 5's 13-value observation with branch-level joint-limit margins collapsed broadly: both measured task-reference checkpoints achieved 23/200 (11.5%), versus 197/200 for working, and research-panel success was 22/200 (11.0%) and 19/200 (9.5%). Its paired comparisons had no candidate wins, so the tested intervention was deprioritized; fresh initialization and one seed prevent attributing the collapse to feature semantics alone. Experiment 6 then measured the angular-coverage hypothesis directly. On the fixed task-reference panel, checkpoint-100352 scored 193/200 (96.5%) and checkpoint-105472 scored 195/200 (97.5%), versus 197/200 (98.5%) for working. The recurring failures at seeds 7384 and 7402 and the high-radius failure at seed 7475 persisted at both checkpoints. The candidate had no paired wins and lost four episodes at 100352 and two at 105472; at 100352 its negative-sub-14 cm group was 52/57 versus 55/57 for working and its positive-sub-14 cm group was 47/48 versus 48/48, while the other radius/angle groups tied. On the research panel, the candidates scored 196/200 (98.0%) and 195/200 (97.5%), below working's 197/200 (98.5%), with one and two paired losses respectively and no wins. Training diagnostics were non-monotonic: proxy success was 0.974 at 5120, fell to 0.85 at 41984, reached 0.99 at 105472, and ended at 0.94; reward peaked at 121.9 around 60416 and ended at 99.9. These results contradict the expected measured improvement under the tested transfer run and show that this angular sampling recipe did not resolve the failure cluster. They do not prove that all angular curricula fail or that action/control is causal. The development panels are repeated evidence, the experiment-6 candidates use one training seed, and no official benchmark result is available.

**Open questions:** Are the recurring failures near 9-10 cm and -123 to -128 degrees caused primarily by action/control limits, policy I/O or normalization behavior, or another representation issue rather than angular coverage? How much of checkpoint variation persists under another seed or distinct panel? Can a targeted intervention improve those cases without creating positive-angle or high-radius regressions, and can any candidate exceed 98% across more than one development panel before official assessment? The experiment-5 collapse remains unresolved at the component level because feature semantics, scaling, initialization, and optimization were not separated.

**Conditional next steps:** Restore the experiment-2 working lineage and complete recipe; this is a measured practical choice, not proof that every fresh augmented run would fail. Do not reuse the experiment-5 joint-limit-margin or experiment-6 negative-angle-oversampling interventions unchanged. Prefer a measured action/control or observation-contract diagnostic, with feature scaling and normalization explicit if the observation changes. Retain a direction only if task measurements improve the recurring cases without positive- or high-radius tradeoffs on a distinct panel; broad regression or training-only improvement should deprioritize it. Do not request the official benchmark until development evidence is stronger than the current threshold-level result.
