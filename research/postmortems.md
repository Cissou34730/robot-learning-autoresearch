## 5d4e159f-7fae-4a25-b885-b127042e894e / Scientific strategy

**Direction:** Establish a reliable baseline for the reach-and-hold objective, then improve robustness from the strongest measured checkpoint by addressing the directional and near-base weaknesses with mechanisms that do not broaden failures. Target-radius coverage, redundant direction features, a lower learning rate, and stronger action regularization have all failed as standalone interventions. The next useful training question should therefore use a distinct, carefully isolated control or representation mechanism rather than assume that more coverage, smaller optimization steps, stronger action penalties, or more training will help.

**Lessons and limits:** The fresh baseline learned the task late in training, reaching 97.0% on the research panel and 98.0% on the fixed task-reference panel at 100,352 steps. The later 120,832-step checkpoint declined to 96.5% and 97.0% on those same panels, so the measured peak is preferable to the final checkpoint. Expanding training radii from 14-20 cm to the full 6-20 cm range did not improve the paired result: the transferred experiment-2 checkpoint reached 97.0% on the research panel and 98.0% on the task-reference panel, exactly matching the unchanged working policy. The same six research-panel failure identities remained, with negative angles from about -122 to -145 degrees; on the task-reference panel the candidate replaced one failure with another but kept four failures, including the near-base cluster. Experiment 3 contradicted the smooth target-direction encoding hypothesis: its 120,832-step candidate reached only 41.5% on the research panel and 46.5% on task-reference, versus the incumbent's 97.0% and 98.0%. The candidate lost 111 net episodes in the paired research comparison, with 91 of 117 failures never reaching tolerance and the remaining 26 failing to hold; failures were distributed across the full angular range rather than remaining a small negative-angle cluster. Experiment 4 also contradicted the lower-learning-rate hypothesis: the transferred candidate reached 95.0% on research and 91.0% on task-reference, versus 97.0% and 98.0% for the incumbent, and won no discordant research or task-reference episode (4 and 14 losses, respectively). It retained all four incumbent task-reference failures and added 14 failures, including failures across angles outside the original negative-angle cluster. Experiment 5 contradicted the action-regularization hypothesis: increasing the action-cost coefficient from 0.01 to 0.05 produced 91.0% on research and 93.0% on task-reference, versus 97.0% and 98.0% for the incumbent. It retained all six incumbent research failures and added 12; on task-reference it retained three of four incumbent failures, fixed one, and added 11. The task-reference failures were mostly large-reach failures rather than only the original near-base hold failures, so the stronger penalty did not isolate the suspected control weakness. The changed reward also gives the research evaluation a different context identity, so its result is not a formal paired comparison with earlier research measurements. Training reward and success remain process signals, not substitutes for fixed-panel task evaluation. These interventions are insufficient standalone solutions, while the development panels and single training realizations do not establish official success or learning-process reproducibility.

**Open questions:** Whether the 100,352-step checkpoint reaches the 98% objective on the official panel remains unknown. It is unresolved whether the original negative-angle failures arise from control limits, a representation detail, or stochastic training variation. Experiment 3 does not prove that every directional encoding is harmful, but it strongly weakens the specific redundant cosine/sine augmentation under the tested PPO recipe. Experiment 4 rejects the tested learning-rate reduction as a standalone fix, but one transferred realization does not establish that all optimizer changes are ineffective. Experiment 5 weakens the excessive-command explanation under the tested action-cost change, but does not distinguish whether the penalty harmed reachability, optimization, or policy stochasticity. The development panels do not resolve stochastic training variation, and the reward change prevents a formal research-panel paired comparison across experiment 5 and the incumbent.

**Conditional next steps:** Keep checkpoint 100,352 as the working and best-known development lineage and restore the baseline scientific code. A future training proposal may investigate a control mechanism or a more carefully isolated representation change, with fixed-panel measurements and intermediate-checkpoint selection; it should not repeat the tested radius, redundant direction-feature, lower-rate, or stronger action-cost changes without a new mechanism. Replication or paired measurement is justified only when it can change a training-process or lineage decision. The incumbent still requires the official benchmark for an objective verdict.

## 5d4e159f-7fae-4a25-b885-b127042e894e / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh baseline produced a strong but not yet officially validated policy. Checkpoint 100,352 is the measured peak and is selected for continued work.

**Observed behavior:** Training success rose from 0 through the first 70,656 steps, reached 0.97 at 100,352 steps, and was 0.95 at 120,832 steps. On the same 200-episode research panel, checkpoint 100,352 succeeded on 194 episodes (97.0%) and checkpoint 120,832 on 193 (96.5%); the later checkpoint added one failure and did not recover any of the earlier failures. On the fixed task-reference panel, the checkpoints achieved 98.0% and 97.0%, respectively. The earlier checkpoint had four failures, all truncated at 500 steps with target angles between -116 and -128 degrees and target radii between 6.7 and 9.9 cm. The later checkpoint retained those four failures and added two more. Neither result is the official benchmark.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline expectation of establishing a competent initial policy was supported: learning was substantial and the best checkpoint approached the objective. The contradicting observation is that the completed 120,000-step run did not improve monotonically and both development results remained below the objective on at least one panel. Because this was a baseline with no intervention, the evidence does not attribute the outcome to a method change or establish reproducibility.

**Interpretation:** The decline after the 100,352-step peak makes checkpoint selection consequential and argues against continuing from the final checkpoint by default. The paired research-panel comparison favors 100,352 by one episode, while the task-reference comparison favors it by two; together these are sufficient to choose the earlier policy for lineage, but not to claim generalization or explain causality. The persistent task-reference failures suggest a targeted angular/near-base robustness question for future training.

## 5d4e159f-7fae-4a25-b885-b127042e894e / Experiment 2

**Result:** Expanding the training target-radius distribution did not improve measured development performance. The unchanged experiment-1 checkpoint remains the working and best-known lineage.

**Observed behavior:** The measured experiment-2 checkpoint-105472 policy scored 97.0% on the research panel and 98.0% on the task-reference panel. The paired unchanged working policy scored 97.0% and 98.0%, respectively, on the same settings. The research-panel failures had the same six episode identities for both policies and remained concentrated at negative target angles; the candidate's diagnostics changed distances and hold behavior but did not turn any of these episodes into successes. On the task-reference panel, both policies had four failures. The candidate failed episodes with radii/angles approximately (6.7 cm, -116 degrees), (9.9 cm, -123 degrees), (9.4 cm, -128 degrees), and (18.2 cm, -155 degrees), while the incumbent instead failed a 7.2 cm, -125 degree episode rather than the last candidate failure. Training success was 1.0 at 105,472 steps but declined to 0.94 by 120,832 steps.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis that full target-radius coverage would improve near-base reach-and-hold robustness while preserving baseline performance is contradicted on the measured panels: baseline performance was preserved, but not improved, and the negative-angle failure pattern persisted. This conclusion is limited to the transferred run and the paired 200-episode development measurements; it does not establish that the intervention never helps or determine the official benchmark result.

**Interpretation:** The expected observation was a higher success rate or fewer near-base failures for the full-range candidate, while the contradicting observation was an unchanged rate with persistent failures. The paired results provide enough evidence to reject radius coverage as the next working intervention and to avoid retaining the candidate. The unchanged failure geometry points toward a directional control or representation issue, but the measurement does not distinguish those mechanisms. The late-training decline also argues against selecting the final checkpoint merely because it was trained longer.

## 5d4e159f-7fae-4a25-b885-b127042e894e / Experiment 5

**Result:** Increasing the action-cost coefficient during transfer contradicted the control-regularization hypothesis. The candidate was materially worse on both development panels, so the experiment-1 checkpoint remains the working and best-known lineage and the action-cost change is reverted.

**Observed behavior:** The transferred checkpoint-100352 candidate used the baseline PPO learning rate and increased the action-cost coefficient from 0.01 to 0.05. Its training success was 0.98 at 100,352 steps and 0.97 at 120,832 steps, but the fixed research evaluation reached only 91.0% (18 failures) versus the incumbent's 97.0% (6 failures). All six incumbent research failures remained and 12 additional episodes failed. On the task-reference panel, the candidate reached 93.0% (14 failures) versus the incumbent's 98.0% (4 failures): it fixed one incumbent failure, retained three, and added 11. The candidate's added task-reference failures included targets across the difficult negative-angle region and one positive-angle target, with most ending far from the target. The experiment-5 research evaluation used semantics `aa9405516642` rather than the incumbent's `d72ec900e7de`, so no formal paired research comparison was recorded; the task-reference panel was the same fixed panel.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis that stronger action regularization would reduce excessive torque changes near difficult negative-angle targets while preserving incumbent success is contradicted. It neither preserved the incumbent's success nor improved the localized failure pattern: all incumbent research failures persisted and the task-reference candidate added 11 failures despite fixing one. This conclusion is limited to the tested coefficient 0.05, transferred initialization, PPO recipe, single training realization, and development panels; it does not establish that every control-aware reward or action strategy is ineffective, nor does it determine the official benchmark result.

**Interpretation:** The larger action penalty changed behavior broadly rather than repairing the suspected localized control problem. It may have discouraged commands needed to reach some targets, but the measurements cannot separate that mechanism from altered optimization or stochastic policy variation. Because the reward changed, training rewards and cross-context research-panel comparisons are not causal or formally paired evidence. The task-reference result is nevertheless sufficient to reject this candidate for the lineage decision; another measurement of it is unlikely to reverse the clear deficit.
