# Research postmortems

## abd93958-a0b2-4d26-b212-96ebeb401d22 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 98% success on the unchanged official two-joint-arm task. The fresh PPO baseline in experiment 1 reached 97.0% on the researcher panel and 98.0% on the protected task-reference panel at checkpoint-100352, while its endpoint regressed on both panels. Experiment 2 transferred that checkpoint and expanded training coverage to the official 6–20 cm radius range; checkpoint-100352 and its endpoint remained at 97.0% on the researcher panel and reached 98.5% on the task-reference panel. Experiment 3 added joint-configuration progress shaping but its measured mid-run checkpoints matched experiment 2's 97.0%/98.5% results and retained the same task-reference failures, while its endpoint regressed to 96.5%/97.0%. The current evidence therefore describes a near-objective policy with a fixed-panel gain but no robust cross-panel attainment, and the experiment-2 checkpoint-100352 lineage remains the strongest measured development policy.

**Lessons and limits:** Measured task success, rather than logged reward or training success, is the policy-progress signal. All three experiments identify checkpoint selection as important: later training reduced logged dynamics and did not improve measured success. Experiment 2 reduced the task-reference failures from four to three, recovering two short-radius negative-angle cases, but left failures at 9.4–9.9 cm and about -123 to -128 degrees plus one 18.2 cm case at -154.8 degrees. Experiment 3's 80,896- and 100,352-step candidates preserved exactly those three task-reference failures and the same six researcher-panel failures; the 120,832-step endpoint added three further task-reference failures. Thus, under this transferred run, joint-configuration shaping did not measurably resolve the targeted cases, although the fixed panels cannot isolate why. The current observation representation includes errors to both valid inverse-kinematics branches, whereas the reward directly shapes Cartesian progress and hold completion; angular control remains a plausible but weakened explanation rather than an established cause. Transfer compatibility is supported by the unchanged robot, action mapping, target geometry, observation semantics and network shape, but fixed 200-episode panels remain development evidence and are not the official 200-episode assessment.

**Open questions:** It remains unresolved whether the residual negative-angle failures reflect insufficient joint-configuration shaping, PPO optimization dynamics, or a limitation of the transferred policy. It is also unknown whether any development-panel gain generalizes to the official final assessment.

## abd93958-a0b2-4d26-b212-96ebeb401d22 / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh baseline learned a useful near-objective policy. Checkpoint-100352 is selected as the working and best-known policy; the scientific recipe is kept, and terminal assessment is deferred.

**Observed behavior:** The run completed 120,832 steps against a requested 120,000. The training log's proxy success was 0% through 70,656 steps, rose to 98% at 99,328 steps, was 97% at 100,352, and was 95% at the endpoint; logged episode reward likewise peaked earlier and was lower at the endpoint. Research evaluation measured checkpoint-86016 at 93.5%, checkpoint-100352 at 97.0% (194/200), and checkpoint-120832 at 96.5% (193/200). The protected task-reference panel measured the same checkpoints at 94.0% (188/200), 98.0% (196/200), and 97.0% (194/200). On that shared protected panel, the endpoint lost both discordant episodes against checkpoint-100352 and had no wins. The four checkpoint-100352 protected-panel failures all had target radii below 10 cm and angles between -116 and -128 degrees; all four episodes ran to 500 steps, with final distances from 0.986 to 1.325 cm. Research-evaluation failures for checkpoint-100352 also ran to 500 steps, and at least one failed endpoint episode retained a high positive reward, showing a proxy/task mismatch.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline established strong learned task behavior and identified a measured late checkpoint near the objective, but it did not demonstrate reliable attainment of the human objective: one distinct development panel was 2 percentage points below 98%, and the endpoint regressed on both panels. The baseline result supports checkpoint selection and further development, while the limited panels do not support a terminal readiness claim.

**Interpretation:** Training improved the learned policy substantially, but continuing to the saved endpoint was not beneficial for measured task success. The agreement between the two panels that checkpoint-100352 is better than the endpoint supports selecting 100352, while the panel disagreement about whether it reaches 98% leaves meaningful uncertainty. The concentrated protected-panel failures suggest a useful next investigation around short-radius targets in the negative-angle region, without establishing that this is the sole cause of all failures.

## abd93958-a0b2-4d26-b212-96ebeb401d22 / Experiment 2

**Result:** Partially supported. Full-radius transfer training produced a useful candidate and a modest fixed task-reference improvement, but did not resolve the researcher-panel gap or establish terminal readiness. Checkpoint-100352 is selected as the working and best-known policy; the expanded-radius recipe is kept and terminal assessment is deferred.

**Observed behavior:** The run completed 120,832 steps against a requested 120,000. The raw training log's proxy success and episode reward were 0.99 and 114 at checkpoint-100352, then 0.94 and 111 at the endpoint. Research evaluation measured checkpoint-25600, checkpoint-100352, and checkpoint-120832 at 97.0% (194/200) each. Their six researcher-panel failures were the same episode seeds (11, 25, 111, 167, 171, and 188), and each ran to 500 steps. The task-reference panel measured checkpoint-25600 at 98.0% (196/200) and both checkpoint-100352 and checkpoint-120832 at 98.5% (197/200). Relative to the experiment-1 retained checkpoint's four task-reference failures, the later experiment-2 checkpoints succeeded on the 6.7 cm, -116.4 degree case and the 7.2 cm, -125.4 degree case, but still failed at 9.4 cm/-127.9 degrees, 9.9 cm/-122.9 degrees, and 18.2 cm/-154.8 degrees.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The predicted reduction in short-radius failures occurred on the protected development panel and broad success was preserved or modestly improved there. However, the researcher-panel success did not improve, two short-radius negative-angle failures remained, and the endpoint did not improve on the selected checkpoint. This supports radius coverage as a useful but incomplete contributor under this transferred run; it does not establish that radius coverage caused the improvement or that the result generalizes beyond the development panels.

**Interpretation:** The measured policy remains near the human objective but is not robustly demonstrated at 98% across development evidence. The fixed task-reference gain justifies retaining the full-radius training recipe and selecting checkpoint-100352, while the unchanged researcher-panel result and concentrated negative-angle failures leave angular control or optimization as live explanations. Since later training degraded logged dynamics without changing measured success, the earlier checkpoint is preferable to the endpoint. No additional measurement is needed to resolve the lineage decision, and terminal assessment is not requested.

## abd93958-a0b2-4d26-b212-96ebeb401d22 / Experiment 3

**Result:** The joint-configuration shaping hypothesis was weakened. The transferred intervention produced no measured improvement on the targeted residual failures, and the endpoint regressed; the experiment-2 working and best-known lineage remains selected, with the experiment-3 recipe reverted and no terminal assessment requested.

**Observed behavior:** Training completed 120,832 steps against a requested 120,000. The logged proxy success reached 1.00 at checkpoint-80,896, was 0.98 at checkpoint-100,352, and was 0.96 at the endpoint; logged episode reward was 109.98, 104.72, and 108.44 at those checkpoints respectively. Research evaluation measured checkpoint-80,896 and checkpoint-100,352 at 97.0% (194/200), with the same six failures on episode seeds 11, 25, 111, 167, 171, and 188, all truncated at 500 steps. The endpoint measured 96.5% (193/200) and retained those failures while adding seed 76. The task-reference panel measured 98.5% (197/200) at both checkpoint-80,896 and checkpoint-100,352, with the same failures at 9.9 cm/-122.9 degrees, 9.4 cm/-127.9 degrees, and 18.2 cm/-154.8 degrees; the endpoint fell to 97.0% (194/200) and added failures at 19.5 cm/-170.1 degrees, 19.5 cm/136.2 degrees, and 18.4 cm/-165.7 degrees. All task-reference failures truncated at 500 steps.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Weakened. The proposal expected joint-configuration progress to reduce the persistent negative-angle failures while preserving broad task behavior. The measured mid-run candidates preserved broad task-reference success but preserved exactly the targeted failures and did not improve the researcher-panel result; the endpoint also lost success on both panels. This is evidence against useful improvement from this shaping intervention under the tested transferred recipe, but does not isolate reward shaping from transfer or PPO optimization dynamics.

**Interpretation:** The intervention did not provide measured progress toward the at-least-98% objective beyond the existing experiment-2 policy. The agreement between the two mid-run checkpoints and experiment 2's task-reference failure geometry makes the targeted angular explanation less persuasive for this implementation. Training metrics again identified a preferable earlier checkpoint but did not establish task success, so no experiment-3 candidate is retained.
