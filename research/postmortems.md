## ad87dedc-d374-4fe4-a4fc-7cfea548ef31 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The unchanged PPO baseline learned the reach-and-hold task to near-objective development performance. Checkpoint 100352 is the strongest measured policy: it achieved 98.0% on the task-reference panel and 98.5% on the research panel, while later checkpoints achieved lower success on both panels. The measurements support selecting the earlier checkpoint rather than the completed-training checkpoint, but they remain development-panel evidence rather than the official result.

**Lessons and limits:** Measured task success, not training reward or training success, supports policy progress. The two panels agree on checkpoint ordering but differ by one episode for the selected checkpoint. Four task-reference episodes fail for every measured checkpoint, with targets concentrated in a negative-angle region and final distances just outside the tolerance; this identifies a residual behavioral limitation without establishing its cause or distribution-wide frequency. The fixed development panels do not establish official 200-episode performance.

**Open questions:** Whether checkpoint 100352 reaches at least 98% on the distinct official panel remains unresolved. The source of the persistent negative-angle failures and whether they can be removed without harming already successful targets are also unresolved.

## ad87dedc-d374-4fe4-a4fc-7cfea548ef31 / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh PPO baseline learned a policy with near-objective measured task performance. Checkpoint 100352 is selected as the working and best-known candidate for official assessment.

**Observed behavior:** Training reward increased from -24.48 at checkpoint 5120 to 163.85 at checkpoint 86016, then was 117.32 at checkpoint 100352 and 112.02 at checkpoint 120832. Training success rose from 0 through checkpoint 70656 to 0.97 at checkpoint 100352, then was 0.94, 0.93, 0.95 and 0.95 at the later checkpoints. On the 200-episode research panel, checkpoints 100352, 115712 and 120832 achieved 98.5%, 97.0% and 97.5%. On the independent 200-episode task-reference panel, they achieved 98.0%, 96.5% and 97.0%. The task-reference panel has four failures shared by all three checkpoints: seeds 7300, 7310, 7384 and 7402, with target angles from -116.4 to -127.9 degrees and final distances from 0.986 to 1.325 cm after 500 steps. Later checkpoints add or change failures, including seeds 7386, 7453 and 7464 at checkpoint 115712 and seeds 7421 and 7452 at checkpoint 120832. Paired comparisons on the shared research episodes favored checkpoint 100352 over checkpoint 115712 by 3 to 0 wins across 3 discordant episodes, and over checkpoint 120832 by 2 to 0 across 2 discordant episodes.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline hypothesis is supported as an initial learning result: the unchanged method produced a policy with 96.5% to 98.5% measured success and a checkpoint at the 98% task-reference level. It is only partially supported as evidence toward the human objective because the panels are development measurements, the later checkpoints are weaker, and persistent failures remain. Training reward and training success improved substantially but did not monotonically track measured task success; they are useful diagnostics, not acceptance evidence. The paired comparisons have few discordant episodes and do not establish a statistically strong checkpoint superiority claim.

**Interpretation:** The policy made substantial task progress, and the consistent cross-panel ordering makes checkpoint 100352 the most useful measured artifact. The lower later scores are temporally associated with continued training under this recipe, but this single run does not identify overtraining or another cause. The repeated negative-angle failures suggest a systematic residual reach-and-hold limitation under the development reference panel, while their near-tolerance final distances indicate that the failures are not wholesale inability to control the arm. Official readiness is therefore a decision about assessing this best measured candidate, not a claim that the development evidence already proves the 98% objective.
