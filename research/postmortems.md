## f3356be0-50ce-45d0-a495-b909a50b054c / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 98% success, or 196/200 episodes, on the official reach-and-hold panel. Across experiments 1-8, the fresh baseline was about 51% on development panels; reward-credit shaping reached 224/400 versus 206/400 for its unchanged control; and the transferred positive-extreme coverage recipe reached 296/400 versus 215/400 for its contemporaneous control. Experiment 9's second fresh replication reached 387/400 versus 303/400 for matched working controls across two disjoint panels, with full positive-extreme recovery and a reduced but persistent negative-extreme deficit. The earlier fresh replication reached only 265/400 versus 288/400 for working, so the aggregate recipe benefit is supported by experiment 9 but not uniform across fresh trajectories. Explicit target-angle/radius features produced mixed sector shifts without durable aggregate improvement: 289/400 versus 288/400 for working at the best representation checkpoint. Symmetric coverage and unchanged continuation did not improve measured behavior. The current evidence describes a strong but variable reward-plus-positive-extreme recipe, with experiment-9's intermediate checkpoint the strongest measured policy and the official objective still unmet.

**Lessons and limits:** Experiment 1 established a learned but inadequate baseline. Experiment 2's aggressive symmetric sampler and experiment 6's gentler balanced sampler reduced aggregate behavior and did not resolve the negative-extreme deficit, weakening simple coverage-only explanations. Experiment 3's unchanged continuation was near-balanced with its parent and left the positive-extreme sector at zero. Experiment 4's reward change improved complete success and mean maximum held duration but increased interruptions and traded angular-sector performance. Experiment 5's positive-extreme focus improved the previously failing sector and measured mid sectors, while negative-extreme success remained 14/69 and training proxies selected different checkpoints. Experiment 7 reproduced positive-extreme recovery from fresh initialization on two disjoint panels (31/31 and 34/36 versus 12/31 and 17/36 for working), but its pooled aggregate was lower than working and paired outcomes favored working 94-71. Experiment 8 showed a partial, inconsistent angular signal: explicit geometry changed extreme-angle counts but traded mid-sector outcomes and did not improve aggregate success or hold stability. Experiment 9's selected 110592-step checkpoint achieved 192/200 and 195/200 on two new disjoint panels, versus 153/200 and 150/200 for working; its 84-0 pooled paired advantage and 67/67 positive-extreme success versus 10/62 for working support measured policy progress, but do not isolate a causal coverage mechanism or overcome the experiment-7 variance signal. Raw logs show the divergence is already present during learning: experiment 9 reaches 0.71-0.82 training success and positive reward late in the run, whereas experiment 7 remains near 0.03-0.05 training success with negative reward. The experiment-9 endpoint had the strongest training proxies but lower task success than the intermediate checkpoint, preserving the proxy/task discrepancy. The current 14-value observation remains semantically compatible with the retained policy: it contains joint state, end-effector target error and velocity, plus both inverse-kinematics configurations; policy I/O maps those observations directly to the unchanged physical actions. Complete uninterrupted hold success is authoritative; proximity, partial hold time, interruptions, and training reward are diagnostic only. The task-reference panel is reused and selection-contaminated, and development panels are not the official verdict.

**Open questions:** The stability of the experiment-9 aggregate gain across fresh trajectories remains uncertain because experiment 7 underperformed despite recovering the focused sector, and the two runs also diverged in their training dynamics. The negative-extreme deficit, hold interruption variability, and the source of proxy/task disagreement remain unresolved. Experiment 8 weakens the claim that appended polar geometry alone resolves those deficits but does not distinguish control, reward, optimization, or interaction effects. No development measurement establishes readiness for terminal assessment.
