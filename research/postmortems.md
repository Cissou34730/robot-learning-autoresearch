# Research postmortems

## 052b98fa-ee96-41c6-ae7e-1c2ad99cfc0a / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 98% success on the official two-joint-arm reach-and-hold task. The unchanged PPO recipe learned substantial task behavior in experiment 1 and reached 97.5% (195/200) on the development task-reference panel at checkpoint-105472, but later checkpoints fell to 95.5% and 92.0% while training proxies improved. Transfers that changed learning rate or entropy regularization did not preserve the parent peak: experiment 2 reached at most 96.5% and experiment 3 at most 97.0%. The fresh unchanged replication in experiment 4 rose from 89.5% to 94.0% on its measured late checkpoints, while the direct unchanged continuation in experiment 5 fell from 94.5% to 89.0-90.0%. Increasing the completion bonus threefold in experiment 6 produced 91.0%, 96.0%, and 94.5% on its measured checkpoints, below the parent peak, while proxies rose to 0.99. Thus the late trajectory is not fixed by the unchanged recipe or by this tested completion-bonus intervention. The campaign has one near-objective measured policy, no measured policy at the objective, and a persistent gap between training proxies and protected task-reference behavior.

**Lessons and limits:** Measured task behavior, not training reward or proxy success, governs policy progress. High proxies repeatedly coexisted with lower task-reference success: experiment 2 had 0.99 training success and 152.588 mean reward at checkpoint-90112 but 94.0% measured success; experiment 3 reached 1.00 and about 154 reward near 100352-105472 steps but measured 97.0% and 95.5%; experiment 4 reached 0.95 and 135.373 at 115712 but measured 92.5%; experiment 5 reached 1.00 and 153.642 at 105472 but measured 89.5%; experiment 6 reached 0.99 and 345.128 after the completion-bonus increase but measured 96.0% at the same late peak-reward checkpoint. The current reward combines distance shaping, linear hold-progress potential, a full hold-progress forfeiture on exit, and a one-shot completion bonus; increasing that bonus from 100 to 300 did not improve or preserve the parent on the sampled checkpoints, but this single transfer run does not identify whether reward incentives caused the discrepancy. In experiment 5, all measured failures truncated at 500 steps, and the failure set changed substantially from the parent's five failures; experiment 6 likewise had only two failures overlapping the parent at each measured checkpoint. These outcomes characterize degradation and failure-set changes without identifying a mechanism. The retained parent and all tested runs share the observation, action, task, and 64x64 tanh policy semantics, making transfer and replication comparisons relevant; however, two fresh runs do not estimate run-to-run variation. The task-reference panel is repeated development evidence rather than the official panel, and unmeasured checkpoints remain unmeasured. Sources: `research/scenario.md`, `research/brief.md`, `research/research_state.json`, `research/results.jsonl`, `research/postmortems.md`, the experiment-1 through experiment-6 inventories and parameters, the six training-log queries, `robot_learning/scenario/reward.py`, and the twenty-three task-reference artifacts under `research/evaluations/052b98fa-ee96-41c6-ae7e-1c2ad99cfc0a/`.

**Open questions:** It remains unknown whether the persistent proxy/reference gap reflects reward incentives, update dynamics, target-distribution effects, or run-specific variation; the tested threefold completion bonus weakens but does not isolate the reward explanation. It is also unknown whether another reward formulation or a different intervention can improve protected task-reference behavior without sacrificing the parent policy's learned reach representation. The unmeasured checkpoints leave any brief recovery unsampled, and the parent policy's five residual failures and the relationship between development-panel performance and the official assessment remain uncharacterized.

## 052b98fa-ee96-41c6-ae7e-1c2ad99cfc0a / Experiment 1

**Result:** The baseline produced a useful learned policy, with checkpoint-105472 the best measured candidate at 97.5% task-reference success, but no measured candidate reached the 98% objective.

**Observed behavior:** Training proxies improved throughout the run: proxy success increased from 0.00 at 5,120 steps to 0.53 at 120,832 steps, while mean episode reward increased from -2014.4 to 24.4 and mean episode length fell from 500 to 343 steps. On the shared 200-episode task-reference panel, checkpoint-105472 succeeded on 195 episodes (97.5%), checkpoint-115712 on 191 (95.5%), and checkpoint-120832 on 184 (92.0%). The five failures of checkpoint-105472 were episodes 52, 83, 84, 100, and 102; all measured failures truncated at 500 steps. The later checkpoints lost more shared-panel successes than they recovered relative to checkpoint-105472.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline established that the unchanged recipe can learn high task performance and provided a policy close to, but below, the human objective. It did not establish objective attainment, and the higher late training proxies did not correspond to higher task-reference success. This conclusion is limited to the one fresh run and the fixed development panel; unmeasured checkpoints remain unmeasured.

**Interpretation:** Measured task behavior, rather than the training proxies, supports selecting checkpoint-105472 for continued development. The decline across later checkpoints makes checkpoint-120832 unsuitable as the working or best-known policy despite its strongest training-time metrics. The result indicates a proxy/evaluation mismatch or late-policy degradation, but does not identify a causal mechanism. No official benchmark is requested because the best measured policy remains below the objective on the development panel.

## 052b98fa-ee96-41c6-ae7e-1c2ad99cfc0a / Experiment 4

**Result:** The fresh replication did not reproduce the experiment-1 near-objective peak. Its best measured candidate reached 94.0% on the task-reference panel, below both the retained parent's 97.5% and the 98% campaign objective.

**Observed behavior:** The unchanged fresh run completed 120832 steps against a 120000-step budget. Training success rose from 0.00 at 5120 steps to 0.90 at 100352, dipped to 0.89 at 105472, then rose to 0.95 at 115712 and remained 0.95 at 120832. Mean training reward rose from -1965.107 at 5120 to 122.229 at 100352, 118.318 at 105472, 135.373 at 115712, and 129.364 at 120832. On the shared 200-episode task-reference panel, checkpoint-100352 succeeded on 179 episodes (89.5%), checkpoint-105472 on 180 (90.0%), checkpoint-115712 on 185 (92.5%), and checkpoint-120832 on 188 (94.0%). All 21, 20, 15, and 12 failures respectively truncated at 500 steps. The measured fresh-run task success improved across these late checkpoints, although the final training reward fell from its 115712 value. The other 20 of 24 experiment-4 checkpoints were not measured.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted for this tested fresh run and fixed development panel. The proposal expected at least one checkpoint at or above experiment 1's 97.5% result; none did, and the maximum was 94.0%. The run also did not broadly reproduce experiment 1's late degradation pattern: measured task success increased from 89.5% to 94.0% over the sampled late checkpoints rather than falling from its 105472 peak. This supports the alternative that the experiment-1 peak and trajectory were run-specific under these conditions, but one replication cannot estimate the frequency of that variation or identify its cause.

**Interpretation:** Experiment 4 provides a useful learned policy but no candidate that improves or displaces the retained experiment-1 parent. The repeated high training proxies alongside lower task-reference success reinforce that proxies are orthogonal to the task claim. The differing late trajectories show that late degradation is not established as a deterministic consequence of unchanged training, without distinguishing stochastic variation from other mechanisms. Keep the existing working and best-known lineage and its unchanged recipe; do not request the official benchmark because the best measured policy remains below the objective on development evidence.

**Result:** Increasing the one-shot completion bonus from 100 to 300 did not produce a replacement for the retained parent. The best measured experiment-6 checkpoint reached 96.0% on the task-reference panel, below the parent's 97.5%, so the parent remains working and best-known.

**Observed behavior:** Experiment 6 transferred from the retained checkpoint-105472 and changed only `HOLD_COMPLETE_BONUS`. **Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted for this tested completion-bonus transfer and sampled checkpoints. The exploratory question sought evidence that emphasizing the terminal uninterrupted hold could improve protected task-reference success while preserving the parent's learned reach behavior. **Interpretation:** The reward intervention is not useful for the current working lineage under the tested conditions. Restore the retained parent's complete scientific recipe and preserve its 97.5% measured policy as best-known.
