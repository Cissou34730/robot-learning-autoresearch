# Research postmortems

## f0cf745a-e772-45c6-9002-6f8f0f0c8595 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** PPO learned broad reach-and-hold behavior, but the working lineage remains below the campaign objective at 624/640 successes (97.5%) across four disjoint research panels. Its newest panel result was 158/160, with the same two short negative-angle one-step interruptions. The experiment-3 hold-stability reward intervention regressed broadly: the matched checkpoint scored 138/160 and its proxy-peak checkpoint 145/160, both below the parent’s 158/160. The fixed task-reference result of 98% is selection evidence rather than independent confirmation.

**Lessons and limits:** Transfer from `100352` preserves broad behavior better than unchanged continuation to `120832`, which regressed slightly and added an isolated positive-angle hold failure. Full-radius exposure did not improve complete-task success, and the residual pair spans both an inner target and 17.85 cm, weakening radius coverage as a sufficient explanation. The reward intervention produced many short-radius non-reaches and repeated interruptions, and lost 20 and 13 paired episodes to the parent at its two measured checkpoints. This weakens the hypothesis that stronger interruption penalties improve holding without sacrificing reach behavior; the single new panel for the parent and all development panels remain non-official.

**Open questions:** Are the working lineage’s remaining failures primarily ineffective branch selection, excessive torque-driven motion, or inadequate dissipation after first reach? Can a future intervention target those mechanisms without reproducing the broad reach regression seen here? Is the isolated positive-angle failure of `120832` evidence of late-training regression or sampling variation?

**Observed behavior:** Training success rose sharply late in the run, reaching a proxy peak of 97% at 100352 and ending at 95% at 120832. On the disjoint second panel, `100352` scored 157/160 (98.125%) and `120832` scored 155/160 (96.875%); pooled over the two panels they scored 308/320 and 306/320 respectively. **Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline expectation that ordinary PPO training would learn the reach-and-hold task is partially supported: success improved from near zero to roughly 96% on pooled disjoint development panels. The stronger claim that the late training peak represented a policy satisfying the human 98% objective is weakened by the pooled result below 98% and by the repeated angle-specific failures. This conclusion is limited to the measured checkpoints and development panels; the official result remains unmeasured.

**Interpretation:** The policy has learned coordinated reaching and stable holding for most of the target distribution, consistent with the model's prediction that the task requires both dynamic convergence and a 100-step hold. Its remaining failures are structured around a difficult negative-angle region, where the policy either selects an ineffective trajectory or cannot dissipate/stabilize sufficiently. Continuing the unchanged recipe to 120832 did not improve that region and introduced one additional instability, so the unchanged recipe should be preserved while a future experiment targets the residual behavior.

## f0cf745a-e772-45c6-9002-6f8f0f0c8595 / Experiment 2

**Result:** Full-radius continued training did not improve the targeted negative-angle reach-and-hold failures. The transferred `working` parent remains the working and best-known lineage; the experiment's changed training recipe is reverted at closure, and no terminal benchmark is requested.

**Observed behavior:** On the disjoint research panel covering episodes 6200-6359, `working`, `checkpoint-100352`, and `checkpoint-105472` each achieved 158/160 successes (98.75%), with zero discordant paired outcomes. All three had the same two failed episode seeds: 6245, a 17.85 cm target at -145.65 degrees with a one-step hold interruption, and 6306, an 11.22 cm target at -130.05 degrees. The parent never reached tolerance on seed 6306, while both intervention checkpoints reached at step 17 but held for only one step before interruption. The complete success vectors remained identical, so this diagnostic shift did not change task success.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The preservation component was supported on this panel: full-radius training did not reduce the transferred policy's measured success and retained the same binary outcomes as the parent. The central prediction of improved short negative-angle complete-task success was not supported; the intervention checkpoints retained both failures, and the additional failure above 14 cm shows that omitted short-radius coverage was not sufficient to explain the residual weakness. The result is limited to the measured candidates and one disjoint development panel.

**Interpretation:** Full-radius exposure changed the trajectory of one short negative-angle case from non-reach to a brief, unstable hold, but did not provide the uninterrupted 100-step stabilization required by the scientific model or improve the policy's binary outcome. Because neither the matched-step checkpoint nor the training-proxy peak outperformed the retained parent, the intervention does not justify replacing the parent recipe or lineage. The remaining failures keep branch selection and torque-driven dissipation as unresolved, more useful directions than radius coverage alone.

## f0cf745a-e772-45c6-9002-6f8f0f0c8595 / Experiment 3

**Result:** The hold-stability reward intervention was contradicted by the measurements. The existing `working` and `best_known` lineage remains the reusable policy; the experiment-3 reward change is reverted, challenger artifacts are not retained, and no terminal benchmark is requested.

**Observed behavior:** On the disjoint episodes 7200-7359, the working parent achieved 158/160 successes (98.75%), while matched checkpoint `100352` achieved 138/160 (86.25%) and proxy-peak checkpoint `105472` achieved 145/160 (90.625%). Paired comparisons favored the parent by 20 and 13 discordant episodes, respectively, with no paired wins for either intervention. The parent’s two failures were short negative-angle targets with one-step holds. The matched intervention had 22 failures, including 14 non-reaches; the proxy peak had 15 failures, including 10 non-reaches. Both intervention checkpoints therefore regressed beyond the original hold interruptions, especially on short targets.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted on the measured panel. The prediction was that forfeiting accumulated hold-progress after an interruption and increasing the outside-tolerance penalty would improve uninterrupted holding without sacrificing broad reach behavior. Neither measured intervention improved complete-task success or the parent’s residual pair, and both caused a large reach-and-hold regression. This conclusion is limited to the two measured checkpoints, one disjoint development panel, and the changed reward recipe; training-time logs and the official benchmark were not evidence for this assessment.

**Interpretation:** The intervention appears to have changed credit assignment in a way that harmed robust approach behavior rather than isolating the residual holding problem. The result does not identify whether branch selection, torque-driven overshoot, or insufficient dissipation is the primary cause of the parent’s two failures, but it rejects this reward modification as a safe way to address them. The parent’s 624/640 pooled development success remains promising but below the 98% objective, so the evidence does not justify terminal assessment.
