## 8c345590-23d1-446d-b3de-14fca7c9df5d / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The tanh [64, 64] PPO `best_known` remains the strongest established lineage, with 386/400 (96.5%) on the latest disjoint panel and 5640/5800 (97.2%) across 14 distinct research panels; this development evidence does not establish the official 98% result. Its 14 latest failures are all at negative angles: six never entered tolerance, while the others entered but held for at most six steps. Lowering command authority changed speed and saturation without repairing the shared failures, the added target-direction features performed poorly, and the lower-learning-rate, zero-entropy continuation added broad non-entry failures. The widened-network run remains behaviorally unmeasured and therefore does not replace the established lineage.

**Lessons and limits:** Training reward and proxy success do not establish the uninterrupted 100-step hold. The frozen model establishes that official targets lie inside the annular workspace, while bounded 50 Hz zero-order-held control must coordinate motion and sustain a 100-step, 1 cm hold; it does not identify the learned branch, braking, or stabilization decision. The trajectory comparison weakens saturation as a sufficient explanation: the parent saturated for 496-499 control steps in its failures, whereas the scaled policy had no saturated steps and still shared the failure set. The current observation already exposes joint velocity, Cartesian error, and both IK-branch errors, so the model offers no established sensing deficit to correct. Reward, radius/angle sampling, action authority, observation changes, and the tested PPO continuation recipe have not produced a measured replacement; capacity has only proxy evidence because its candidates were not measured.

**Open questions:** It remains unresolved whether the residual negative-angle failures arise primarily from branch choice, braking, sampled stabilization, policy capacity, or another learning-process limitation. In particular, the campaign has not established whether rollout/update diversity can improve both non-entry and brief-hold failures without trading them for broad regressions.
