## e9b968c4-3137-4bd2-ac09-05422a64bcc2 / Scientific strategy

**Direction:** Improve the highest-priority measured gap of incumbent `checkpoint-100352`: six lower-left failures at target angles from about -122 to -145 degrees. Radial expansion, focused-angle oversampling, the tested explicit target-geometry representation, the tested command-magnitude response, and the tested branch-progress shaping intervention have not removed the gap; the next causal question is whether control timing or actuator/physics dynamics, rather than the tested branch-directed training signal, limits the incumbent.

**Lessons and limits:** Experiment 2 established that expanding training radii from 14-20 cm to 6-20 cm did not remove the six lower-left failures under its tested transfer run and budget. Experiment 3 then transferred the incumbent while sampling the -150 to -115 degree band with probability 0.5 and measured 85.0% at 100,352 steps, versus 97.0% for the incumbent on the identical 200-episode panel. The focused-angle candidate retained all six lower-left failures and added 24 other failures, so the exact angular-oversampling intervention was contradicted and is not a viable remedy under those conditions. Experiment 4 freshly appended normalized target direction and radius to the observation, but measured 59.0% at 100,352 steps and 70.0% at 120,832 steps; all six incumbent lower-left targets still failed and the candidates incurred broad additional failures. Thus the exact representation intervention was contradicted under its tested recipe and budget, while the broader representation mechanism remains open because one encoding and training run do not rule out other representations or training designs. Experiment 5 transferred the incumbent with the bounded monotonic response `sign(action) * abs(action)^0.75`; its measured candidate reached only 91.5%, retained all six lower-left failures, and added 11 other failures relative to the incumbent on the paired panel. The exact command-magnitude intervention therefore failed and broadly regressed, weakening underpowered command magnitude as the explanation under these conditions. Experiment 6 transferred the incumbent with nearest joint-limit-safe IK branch selection and potential-based branch-distance shaping. Its candidates measured 85.0% at 35,840 steps and 82.0% at 100,352 steps; the later candidate retained all six lower-left failures and added 30 failures elsewhere. The exact branch-shaping intervention therefore failed and broadly regressed under this recipe, weakening the branch-selection explanation as tested. It does not resolve the broader control/dynamics mechanism: actual branch execution, control timing, actuator dynamics, alternate representations or training designs, and stochastic effects remain open. Training reward and proxy success do not override measured policy behavior; experiment 6's proxy success was 0.99 at both measured checkpoints while measured success was 85.0% and 82.0%, and no causal link makes that proxy evidence against the lower-left gap.

**Open questions:** Whether the lower-left failures are caused by actual inverse-kinematics branch execution, control timing or another angularly asymmetric dynamics interaction, a different useful target representation or training design, or stochastic variation remains unresolved. Experiment 6 rejects the tested reset branch choice plus potential-based branch-distance shaping, not every branch-aware intervention or the broader control/dynamics mechanism. Experiment 4's additional small-radius and positive-angle failures, experiment 5's additional failures, and experiment 6's broad failures are secondary evidence from regressed policies, not replacements for the incumbent's lower-left gap. Positive-angle hold stability remains open unless it becomes the largest measured deficit.

**Conditional next steps:** Restore `checkpoint-100352` and, in the next experiment, test a concrete control-timing or actuator/dynamics intervention while keeping the official task and the incumbent's measured lower-left gap unchanged; do not repeat branch shaping or command amplification without a new causal rationale. If a timing/dynamics intervention reduces the six failures without broad regression, prioritize that mechanism; if it does not, revisit alternate representation or optimization designs while retaining branch selection as an unresolved possibility. Make positive-angle hold stability primary only if it becomes the largest measured gap. Further development is higher value than terminal assessment while the measured best-known policy remains below 98%.
