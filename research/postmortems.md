# Research postmortems

## 052b98fa-ee96-41c6-ae7e-1c2ad99cfc0a / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 98% success on the official two-joint-arm reach-and-hold task. The unchanged PPO recipe learned substantial task behavior in experiment 1 and reached 97.5% (195/200) on the development task-reference panel at checkpoint-105472, but later checkpoints fell to 95.5% and 92.0% while training proxies improved. Experiments 2 and 3 transferred from that measured peak and changed learning rate and entropy regularization; neither preserved the parent peak, with experiment 3 reaching 97.0%, 95.5%, and 94.0%. Experiment 4 independently initialized the unchanged recipe and measured 89.5%, 90.0%, 92.5%, and 94.0% at 100352 through 120832 steps, so this run did not reproduce the 97.5% peak or reach the objective. Its measured late trajectory improved, unlike experiment 1's late decline, which is evidence of run-specific behavior but not a characterization of its frequency or cause. The campaign has demonstrated a near-objective policy but not objective attainment or terminal readiness.

**Lessons and limits:** Measured task behavior, not training reward or proxy success, governs policy progress. Experiment 2 reached 0.99 training success and mean reward 152.588 at checkpoint-90112 but measured 94.0%; experiment 3 reached 1.00 and about 154 reward near 100352-105472 steps but measured 97.0% and 95.5%; experiment 4 reached 0.95 training success and 135.373 mean reward at 115712 but measured 92.5%. The changing failure sets and 500-step truncations characterize behavior without identifying a mechanism. The retained parent uses the same observation, action, task, and 64x64 tanh policy semantics as all four experiments, so the transfer and replication comparisons are relevant, but two fresh runs do not establish the distribution of run-to-run variation. The task-reference panel is a repeated development panel, not the official panel, and experiment 4's four sampled checkpoints leave its other 20 candidates unmeasured. Sources: `research/scenario.md`, `research/brief.md`, `research/research_state.json`, `research/results.jsonl`, `research/postmortems.md`, the experiment-1 through experiment-4 inventories and parameters, the four training logs, and the fourteen task-reference artifacts under `research/evaluations/052b98fa-ee96-41c6-ae7e-1c2ad99cfc0a/`.

**Open questions:** It remains unknown whether another initialization or a targeted change can improve the retained 97.5% parent to the 98% objective. The causes of differing late trajectories remain unresolved among update dynamics, reward or termination incentives, target-distribution effects, and stochastic variation. The parent policy's five residual failures and the relationship between development-panel performance and the official assessment also remain uncharacterized.

## 052b98fa-ee96-41c6-ae7e-1c2ad99cfc0a / Experiment 1

**Result:** The baseline produced a useful learned policy, with checkpoint-105472 the best measured candidate at 97.5% task-reference success, but no measured candidate reached the 98% objective.

**Observed behavior:** Training proxies improved throughout the run: proxy success increased from 0.00 at 5,120 steps to 0.53 at 120,832 steps, while mean episode reward increased from -2014.4 to 24.4 and mean episode length fell from 500 to 343 steps. On the shared 200-episode task-reference panel, checkpoint-105472 succeeded on 195 episodes (97.5%), checkpoint-115712 on 191 (95.5%), and checkpoint-120832 on 184 (92.0%). The five failures of checkpoint-105472 were episodes 52, 83, 84, 100, and 102; all measured failures truncated at 500 steps. The later checkpoints lost more shared-panel successes than they recovered relative to checkpoint-105472.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline established that the unchanged recipe can learn high task performance and provided a policy close to, but below, the human objective. It did not establish objective attainment, and the higher late training proxies did not correspond to higher task-reference success. This conclusion is limited to the one fresh run and the fixed development panel; unmeasured checkpoints remain unmeasured.

**Interpretation:** Measured task behavior, rather than the training proxies, supports selecting checkpoint-105472 for continued development. The decline across later checkpoints makes checkpoint-120832 unsuitable as the working or best-known policy despite its strongest training-time metrics. The result indicates a proxy/evaluation mismatch or late-policy degradation, but does not identify a causal mechanism. No official benchmark is requested because the best measured policy remains below the objective on the development panel.

## 052b98fa-ee96-41c6-ae7e-1c2ad99cfc0a / Experiment 2

**Result:** The lower-learning-rate transfer did not improve or preserve the parent policy. The experiment-1 checkpoint-105472 policy remains the strongest measured candidate at 97.5% task-reference success, while the best experiment-2 checkpoint reached 96.5%.

**Observed behavior:** Experiment 2 transferred from checkpoint-105472 and changed only PPO learning rate from 0.0003 to 0.0001. The four measured checkpoints scored 94.0% (checkpoint-90112), 96.0% (checkpoint-105472), 95.0% (checkpoint-115712), and 96.5% (checkpoint-120832) on the same 200-episode task-reference panel. Their training proxies were high at the selected late points: checkpoint-90112 had 0.99 training success and 152.588 mean reward, checkpoint-115712 had 0.99 and 152.464, and checkpoint-120832 had 0.98 and 149.238. Every failure in these four artifacts truncated at 500 steps. The 120832 lower-rate policy did exceed the original run's 92.0% at the same checkpoint position, but it remained one percentage point below the parent peak; the measured failure sets also changed across checkpoints.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted for this tested transfer intervention. The proposal expected a lower-rate continuation to preserve or improve the parent's 97.5% task-reference success, whereas all four measured experiment-2 checkpoints fell below it, and the proxies again improved without corresponding task success. The 120832 improvement over the original late checkpoint is a partial signal compatible with reduced late degradation, but the single transfer run does not establish that update aggressiveness caused either result and does not rule out reward, termination, target-distribution, or run-variation explanations.

**Interpretation:** The intervention did not provide a sufficiently robust policy or a reliable route to the objective, so the parent lineage and its 0.0003 recipe should remain active. The development-panel result supports continued development rather than terminal assessment: the parent is close to, but below, 98%, and the panel is not the official 200-episode assessment.

## 052b98fa-ee96-41c6-ae7e-1c2ad99cfc0a / Experiment 4

**Result:** The fresh replication did not reproduce the experiment-1 near-objective peak. Its best measured candidate reached 94.0% on the task-reference panel, below both the retained parent's 97.5% and the 98% campaign objective.

**Observed behavior:** The unchanged fresh run completed 120832 steps against a 120000-step budget. Training success rose from 0.00 at 5120 steps to 0.90 at 100352, dipped to 0.89 at 105472, then rose to 0.95 at 115712 and remained 0.95 at 120832. Mean training reward rose from -1965.107 at 5120 to 122.229 at 100352, 118.318 at 105472, 135.373 at 115712, and 129.364 at 120832. On the shared 200-episode task-reference panel, checkpoint-100352 succeeded on 179 episodes (89.5%), checkpoint-105472 on 180 (90.0%), checkpoint-115712 on 185 (92.5%), and checkpoint-120832 on 188 (94.0%). All 21, 20, 15, and 12 failures respectively truncated at 500 steps. The measured fresh-run task success improved across these late checkpoints, although the final training reward fell from its 115712 value. The other 20 of 24 experiment-4 checkpoints were not measured.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted for this tested fresh run and fixed development panel. The proposal expected at least one checkpoint at or above experiment 1's 97.5% result; none did, and the maximum was 94.0%. The run also did not broadly reproduce experiment 1's late degradation pattern: measured task success increased from 89.5% to 94.0% over the sampled late checkpoints rather than falling from its 105472 peak. This supports the alternative that the experiment-1 peak and trajectory were run-specific under these conditions, but one replication cannot estimate the frequency of that variation or identify its cause.

**Interpretation:** Experiment 4 provides a useful learned policy but no candidate that improves or displaces the retained experiment-1 parent. The repeated high training proxies alongside lower task-reference success reinforce that proxies are orthogonal to the task claim. The differing late trajectories show that late degradation is not established as a deterministic consequence of unchanged training, without distinguishing stochastic variation from other mechanisms. Keep the existing working and best-known lineage and its unchanged recipe; do not request the official benchmark because the best measured policy remains below the objective on development evidence.

## 052b98fa-ee96-41c6-ae7e-1c2ad99cfc0a / Experiment 3

**Result:** Removing PPO entropy regularization did not preserve or improve the measured parent policy. The experiment-1 checkpoint-105472 remains the strongest measured candidate at 97.5% task-reference success, while experiment-3 checkpoints reached 97.0%, 95.5%, and 94.0%.

**Observed behavior:** Experiment 3 transferred from checkpoint-105472 and changed only `ppo.ent_coef` from 0.01 to 0.0. Training success reached 1.00 from 98,304 through 112,640 steps, with mean reward about 154-155; it then declined to 0.96 and about 140 at 120,832 steps. On the same 200-episode task-reference panel, checkpoint-100352 succeeded on 194 episodes (97.0%), checkpoint-105472 on 191 (95.5%), and checkpoint-120832 on 188 (94.0%). All six, nine, and twelve failures respectively truncated at 500 steps. Failure sets changed with training: the early candidate shared three failures with the parent and the late candidates shared two, so the intervention did not simply preserve the parent failure pattern.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted for this tested transfer intervention. The proposal expected a zero-entropy transfer to retain at least the parent's 97.5% task-reference success at a later checkpoint or improve it while proxies remained high. None of the measured checkpoints reached the parent result; the matched 105472-step checkpoint was 95.5%, and the late checkpoint was 94.0% despite the preceding 1.00 training-success proxy. This weakens the explanation that residual entropy pressure was the main source of late policy drift under these tested conditions. It does not distinguish reward, termination, target-distribution, update dynamics, or run-specific explanations, and unmeasured checkpoints remain unmeasured.

**Interpretation:** The entropy intervention did not provide a policy suitable for working or best-known status. The evidence supports restoring the parent lineage and its `ent_coef: 0.01` recipe. The parent remains near but below the campaign objective on a development panel, so this closure does not justify the official benchmark; further training remains a separate next experiment.
