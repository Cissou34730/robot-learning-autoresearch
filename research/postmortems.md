# Research postmortems

## 285f0407-8cc3-401d-bdcd-2af73de9a61c / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 98% success on the official 6–20 cm, full-angle reach-and-hold distribution. Across experiments 1–5, PPO learned strong reach-and-hold behavior, but measured performance is panel- and checkpoint-dependent. The strongest development artifact is the experiment-2 working lineage: 98.0% on its research panel and 98.5% on the protected task-reference panel. Full-radius training produced that small protected-panel gain; focused negative-angle sampling changed some researcher-panel outcomes without correcting the protected residual cases; hold-exit shaping preserved high performance but did not improve those cases; and the experiment-5 explicit target-geometry representation caused broad fresh-training regressions to 25% on the research panel and 31%/28% on the task-reference panel. These are development measurements, not the official assessment.

**Lessons and limits:** Measured task success, rather than training reward or the training success proxy, is the relevant progress signal (`research/evaluations/` and `research/results.jsonl`). The baseline and experiment 2 showed that later checkpoints can match or lose earlier task performance, so reward and proxy peaks do not establish a better saved policy. On the protected panel, experiment-2 checkpoint-100352 failed three negative-angle episodes near -123 to -155 degrees, while the experiment-1 baseline failed four negative-angle episodes; experiment 3 retained the experiment-2 residual identities and experiment 4 repeated them despite hold-exit shaping. Experiment 5 adds a training/evaluation discrepancy: its proxy stayed at 0.0 while mean reward rose from negative values to 22.84, yet measured success remained 25%, and no raw log artifact is available for further diagnosis. Its augmented policies failed on all three working failures plus 135 or 141 additional episodes across both angle signs. The observation change is therefore not supported as a useful replacement under fresh training, but that run does not isolate representation from fresh-training variance or their interaction. Development panels are not the official assessment, and repeated episode identities are not new coverage.

**Open questions:** The cause of the recurring negative-angle failures remains unresolved among control, target geometry, and optimization/checkpoint effects. It is also unknown whether unchanged optimization from the retained policy can correct those cases or merely reproduce the late-training plateau and degradation seen elsewhere in the campaign. The official success rate remains unknown because no development panel substitutes for the fixed official assessment.

The training-proxy peak and later training did not provide evidence that the final checkpoint was better, so the result does not support treating continued unchanged training as an improvement.

**Interpretation:** The policy has learned the core reach-and-hold behavior, but the repeated negative-angle failures indicate a residual distributional weakness in the measured task behavior. ## 285f0407-8cc3-401d-bdcd-2af73de9a61c / Experiment 2

**Result:** Full-radius training produced a useful candidate and a small improvement on the protected development panel, but did not resolve the residual failure pattern; terminal assessment remains deferred.

**Observed behavior:** Experiment 2 trained the transferred PPO policy on 6-20 cm targets for 120,832 completed steps. The training success proxy reached 1.00 at checkpoint-105472, then was 0.94 at checkpoint-120832; episode reward was highest among the measured late checkpoints at checkpoint-100352. The research-evaluation context changed between experiments, so its 98.0% result is interpreted within experiment 2 rather than as a cross-experiment score comparison. No raw training-log artifact was available beyond the recorded proxy and reward summaries.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis is **partially supported**. It is not fully supported because two near-base failures remained, a new long-radius negative-angle failure appeared, and the three experiment-2 research measurements were behaviorally unchanged. These measurements show a modest task-performance improvement under the protected panel, but do not establish that radius coverage alone caused it or that the policy meets the official objective.

**Interpretation:** Full-radius training is a reasonable recipe to retain, since it preserves strong reach-and-hold behavior and gives the best observed protected-panel result in this experiment. The unchanged research-panel failure identities and the persistent negative-angle concentration indicate that radius coverage is not a complete solution; angular/control or hold-stability limitations remain plausible. The equivalent protected-panel outcomes of checkpoints 100352 and 120832 make the earlier checkpoint preferable as working policy because it reaches the same measured task performance before the later proxy decline, while the final checkpoint remains useful as a distinct late-training control. Development evidence is not the official 200-episode assessment.

## 285f0407-8cc3-401d-bdcd-2af73de9a61c / Experiment 5

**Result:** Explicit target-radius and target-angle features did not improve the learned policy under the tested fresh recipe; both measured candidates were substantially worse than the working policy. Close with `working`, revert the observation change, and defer terminal assessment.

**Observed behavior:** Experiment 5 trained from fresh initialization for 120,832 completed steps with the observation expanded from 11 to 14 values. The recorded training proxy was 0.0 for every checkpoint, while mean episode reward rose from -15.79 at step 25,600 to 22.84 at step 120,832. On the matched 200-episode research evaluation, checkpoint-100352 and checkpoint-120832 each achieved 25% (50/200), versus 98% (196/200) for the measured working reference. Paired comparisons reported zero candidate wins and 146 working-policy wins for each candidate over 146 discordant episodes. On the distinct task-reference-v1 panel, checkpoint-100352 achieved 31% (62/200) and checkpoint-120832 achieved 28% (56/200), versus 98.5% (197/200) for working. The mid and final augmented policies included all three working task-reference failures and added 135 and 141 failures respectively; their failures covered both negative and nonnegative target angles and all listed failures truncated at 500 steps. The final checkpoint therefore did not recover the mid-checkpoint result despite its higher reward.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis is **contradicted under the tested fresh-training conditions**. The expected pattern—fewer negative-angle failures while retaining broad reach-and-hold success—was absent: the augmented policies had widespread failures, no measured task-level improvement, and no protected-panel preservation. This result supports the stated alternative more than the proposition, but one fresh run does not separate the changed representation from fresh-training variance or an interaction with the new observation contract; it also does not establish an official success rate.

**Interpretation:** The explicit geometry representation is not a useful replacement for the current recipe on this evidence, and the large broad regression makes the augmented candidates unsuitable working or best-known policies. The rising reward alongside a zero training proxy and poor measured success is an orthogonal training/evaluation discrepancy, not evidence of policy progress. Reverting to the working recipe preserves the strongest measured development behavior; the official objective remains unassessed.
