**Direction:** Improve the highest-priority measured gap of incumbent `checkpoint-100352`: six lower-left failures at target angles from about -122 to -145 degrees. After radial expansion and focused-angle oversampling both failed, the next causal question is whether a researcher-controlled representation or action/control intervention can address this persistent gap without changing the official task mechanics.

**Lessons and limits:** Experiment 2 established that expanding training radii from 14-20 cm to 6-20 cm did not remove the six lower-left failures under its tested transfer run and budget. The broader causal mechanism remains open: these interventions weaken radial coverage and this particular angular-exposure schedule as sufficient explanations, but do not distinguish control or dynamics limits, representation or action mapping, alternate training designs, and stochastic or transfer effects. Training proxy and reward changes do not override the measured policy behavior.

**Open questions:** Whether the lower-left failures are caused by a representation or action/control limitation, an angularly asymmetric dynamics interaction, or an untested training/optimization effect remains unresolved. The focused candidate's additional small-radius and positive-angle failures are secondary evidence from a broadly regressed policy, not a replacement for the incumbent's lower-left gap. Positive-angle hold stability also remains open unless it becomes the largest measured deficit.

**Conditional next steps:** Continue from the measured 97% incumbent and test a concrete researcher-owned representation or action/control intervention targeted at the -122 to -145 degree lower-left gap, while evaluating the unchanged official task. If the six failures improve, continue that mechanism; if they persist, prioritize the remaining control/dynamics or representation distinction rather than treating training proxies as official evidence. Make positive-angle hold stability primary only if it becomes the largest measured gap. Further development is higher value than terminal assessment while the measured policy remains below 98%.

The exact intervention therefore failed to remove the gap under this transfer run and budget. The broader causal mechanism remains unresolved: this result weakens radial coverage as the sole explanation but does not rule out other radial training designs or distinguish angular asymmetry, control/dynamics limits, representation, and stochastic effects.

Revert to the incumbent’s complete scientific recipe and use the measured lower-left behavior to direct the next experiment toward angular/control or dynamics asymmetry.
