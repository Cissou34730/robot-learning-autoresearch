## 800411fd-f254-463e-9a99-6a3020988477 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 196 successes on the official 200-episode panel. Measured reach-and-hold success, rather than training reward or logged success, is the relevant progress signal. The fresh baseline reached 195/200 on the protected development panel, while experiment 2 checkpoint-95232 reached 197/200 on both that panel and a fresh 200-episode all-angle research panel. Experiment 3's unchanged continuation produced 191-194/200 on its measured checkpoints, so checkpoint-95232 remains the strongest measured policy. These are development results, not the official objective verdict.

**Lessons and limits:** In experiment 1, the final checkpoint fell from 95.625% to 92.5% on the paired research panel and from 97.5% to 92.0% on the protected panel while logged success and mean reward increased. In experiment 2, the highest logged reward and success proxy occurred at step 90112; checkpoint-95232 remained at proxy 0.95, and later checkpoints declined to 0.91. Checkpoint-95232 reached 153/160 on the shared research panel, exactly matching the working baseline, and both reached 197/200 on the fresh 200-episode panel with the same three failures. Its protected-panel failures include targets at -122.9°, -127.9° and -154.8°. In experiment 3, the proxy peak at checkpoint-25600 (0.97 success proxy, reward 333.503) measured 193/200 on both panels, the late proxy rebound at checkpoint-105472 measured 191/200 on both, and the endpoint's proxy decline to 0.90 and reward 279.860 still measured 194/200 on the research panel and 192/200 on the protected panel. These observations show proxy/behavior disagreement and a concentrated but not exclusive measured failure pattern, not their causes. Development panels are not the official verdict, and unmeasured checkpoints remain unmeasured.

**Open questions:** Whether the 197/200 development behavior persists on the independent official panel, whether the remaining negative-angle failures are reducible without trading away other targets, and whether a changed recipe can improve them without repeating continuation degradation. The relationship between late training proxies and saved-policy behavior remains unresolved, as does the official frozen-policy result and terminal-readiness evidence for the current best-known designation.

## 800411fd-f254-463e-9a99-6a3020988477 / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh PPO baseline produced a learned policy with substantial task performance but did not establish the 98% objective. Checkpoint-115712 is selected as the working and best-known policy; the unchanged scientific recipe is kept for closure and no final benchmark is requested.

**Observed behavior:** Training-time proxies improved across the late run: logged success rose from 0.01 at 90,112 steps to 0.30 at checkpoint-120832, while mean episode reward rose from -387.43 to 24.03. On the same 160-episode research panel, checkpoint-115712 achieved 153/160 (95.625%) and checkpoint-120832 achieved 148/160 (92.5%); all five discordant episodes were wins for checkpoint-115712. On the fixed task-reference development panel, checkpoint-115712 achieved 195/200 (97.5%) and checkpoint-120832 achieved 184/200 (92.0%). The paired task-reference outcomes had 11 earlier-only wins, no final-only wins, and five shared failures. The five checkpoint-115712 failures all ran to 500 steps and had target angles from -125.4 to -154.8 degrees; checkpoint-120832 shared those failures and added eleven failures across additional angles, including several targets with radii near 6–10 cm.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. As an automatic baseline, the proposal only claimed to establish an initial baseline and had no type-specific confirmatory or exploratory prediction. That baseline claim is supported: the run learned a policy reaching 97.5% on the protected development panel. It is not evidence that the human objective has been reached. The round-1 question's possibility that the late increase in training proxies represented improved task success was contradicted on the paired research panel and protected panel: the endpoint's proxies were highest, but its measured task success was lower. This conclusion is scoped to the measured artifacts and panels; it does not identify a causal mechanism or imply that every further training step will degrade performance.

**Interpretation:** Checkpoint-115712 is the strongest measured candidate because it dominates checkpoint-120832 on both measured task surfaces, while the final proxy improvement is not reliable evidence of policy progress. Keeping the baseline recipe preserves the measured policy contract and provides a working parent for a subsequent experiment. The remaining gap to 98% and the concentrated failure pattern make further development more useful than terminal assessment at this point.

## 800411fd-f254-463e-9a99-6a3020988477 / Experiment 2

**Result:** The targeted angular-coverage intervention produced a useful checkpoint, but did not establish a broad improvement over the retained policy. Checkpoint-95232 is selected as the working and best-known policy for continued development.

**Observed behavior:** The experiment-2 training log reached its highest recorded mean reward, 319.01, and tied highest logged success proxy, 0.95, at checkpoint-90112; checkpoint-95232 had reward 315.91 and proxy 0.95, while later checkpoints declined to a proxy of 0.91 at step 120832. Measured task success for checkpoint-90112 was 153/160 on the research panel and 194/200 on the task-reference panel. Checkpoint-95232 reached 153/160 on the same research panel, 197/200 on the task-reference panel, and 197/200 on the fresh 200-episode research panel. The working baseline reached the same 153/160 and 197/200 on those two research panels. The paired comparisons recorded zero discordant wins in 360 shared research episodes. On the task-reference panel, checkpoint-95232's failures were at target angles -122.9, -127.9 and -154.8 degrees; the baseline's five failures were at -125.4, -147.2, -127.9, -154.8 and -128.7 degrees.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The exploratory question asked whether extra coverage of the observed negative-angle failure sector could improve reach-and-hold success without sacrificing behavior elsewhere. The protected-panel result improved from 195/200 for the retained policy to 197/200 for checkpoint-95232, and the candidate also reached 197/200 on a fresh all-angle research panel, so the measured behavior is consistent with useful progress and no aggregate sacrifice in these panels. However, checkpoint-95232 had exactly the same outcomes as the baseline on both paired research panels, including the same three fresh-panel failures. Therefore these measurements do not isolate targeted coverage as the cause of the protected-panel gain or show a general advantage; the conclusion is limited to the measured panels and saved policies.

**Interpretation:** Checkpoint-95232 is the strongest measured candidate because it reaches 98.5% on both a protected development panel and a fresh all-angle panel, while checkpoint-90112 is lower and later proxy increases do not identify a better policy. The fresh-panel tie means the result should be treated as a useful selection decision rather than proof that the intervention improved the policy distribution-wide. The official 200-episode assessment remains the only decisive test of the human objective.

## 800411fd-f254-463e-9a99-6a3020988477 / Experiment 3

**Result:** Unchanged continuation from checkpoint-95232 did not improve measured reach-and-hold behavior. Checkpoint-95232 remains the working and best-known lineage; the unchanged recipe is kept and the official benchmark is deferred.

**Observed behavior:** The continuation log was non-monotonic. Checkpoint-25600 had the highest logged success proxy and mean reward (0.97 and 333.503), checkpoint-105472 had a late proxy rebound (0.96 and 320.692), and checkpoint-120832 ended at 0.90 and 279.860. On the fresh 200-episode research panel, these checkpoints achieved 193/200 (96.5%), 191/200 (95.5%), and 194/200 (97.0%), respectively. On the fixed task-reference panel they achieved 193/200 (96.5%), 191/200 (95.5%), and 192/200 (96.0%). The parent checkpoint-95232 had previously achieved 197/200 on both development surfaces. The continuation measurements therefore did not exceed the parent; the endpoint's lower logged proxies did not make it the lowest measured research result, while its protected-panel result remained below the early checkpoint. Failures still included the previously observed negative-angle sector, alongside failures at other angles; every listed task-reference failure used the 500-step episode limit. Within-continuation paired comparisons found 7 versus 5 discordant wins for checkpoint-25600 versus checkpoint-105472, and 1 versus 4 for checkpoint-105472 versus checkpoint-120832.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted for the tested continuation snapshots. The expected observation was a later checkpoint exceeding checkpoint-95232, ideally by removing residual negative-angle failures without new failures elsewhere; none of the three measured checkpoints did so on either panel. The alternative that the policy had plateaued or that further updates would degrade measured behavior is supported in these tested snapshots, including despite fluctuating or improving training proxies. This conclusion is limited to the measured checkpoints and panels and does not establish that every future update would fail or identify a causal mechanism.

**Interpretation:** Additional unchanged updates are not supported as a route to measured progress by this experiment. The earlier parent remains the strongest measured saved policy and is useful for further development, but its 197/200 development results remain selection evidence rather than an official verdict or stopping-validation result. Keeping the unchanged recipe preserves the compatible policy contract while leaving the campaign open for a later, deliberately changed experiment aimed at residual failures.
