# Research postmortems

## d1897119-e054-4e2a-a2fd-06958f8ce0bd / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 98% success on the official 6–20 cm reach-and-hold distribution. Experiment 1 established that the PPO recipe learns the task, but its best measured checkpoint reached only 51.0% on the protected 200-episode development panel. The training proxy peaked near 0.95 while protected-task success remained near one half, so the proxy does not identify an acceptable policy. The 100352-step checkpoint is the current working and best-known candidate because it has the highest measured protected-task success, not because its one-episode margin is robust.

**Lessons and limits:** The baseline sampled training targets only from 14–20 cm, while the protected panel covers 6–20 cm. On that panel, the working checkpoint succeeded on 3/57 (5.3%) targets at 6–10 cm, 32/59 (54.2%) at 10–15 cm, and 67/84 (79.8%) at 15–20 cm; angle sectors were comparatively balanced. The raw log shows learnability and late proxy saturation, but does not establish why short-radius behavior is weak. The three measured checkpoints differ by at most seven episodes overall and were all measured on the same development panel, so checkpoint ranking and intervention effects remain uncertain. The task-reference panel is not the official final assessment.

**Open questions:** It is unresolved whether the training-distribution mismatch is a material cause of short-radius failures, whether the existing representation transfers across the full radius range, and whether improved short-radius behavior can coexist with the established long-radius behavior. The amount of checkpoint-to-checkpoint and panel-to-panel variation also remains unknown.

## d1897119-e054-4e2a-a2fd-06958f8ce0bd / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh baseline produced a usable but insufficient learned policy. The best measured checkpoint achieved 51.0% success on the protected task-reference panel; the campaign objective is at least 98%.

**Observed behavior:** Training completed 120832 steps with 24 saved candidates. The raw log's training proxy rose from 0 at the early checkpoints to 0.95 around 108544-110592 steps, then was 0.94 at 120832 steps. On the same 200-episode task-reference panel, candidate-cdb684be at 100352 steps succeeded on 102 episodes (51.0%), candidate-ab1b5d07 at 110592 steps on 101 (50.5%), and candidate-519172ed at 120832 steps on 95 (47.5%). For cdb684be, success was 3/57 at 6-<10 cm, 32/59 at 10-<15 cm, and 67/84 at 15-20 cm; angle sectors ranged from 20/37 to 28/53. All failures were truncated at 500 steps. The cdb684be-versus-ab1b5d07 paired outcomes were 31 episodes successful only for cdb684be, 30 only for ab1b5d07, and 139 equal.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported for the baseline's limited purpose of establishing that the unchanged method can learn the task: the training proxy and protected-task success both improve markedly from the early run. The evidence does not support treating the baseline as close to satisfying the human objective, and it weakens any inference that the late proxy peak identifies the best protected-task policy. This conclusion is limited to the tested recipe and task-reference panel; unmeasured checkpoints remain unmeasured.

**Interpretation:** Measured task behavior shows real but insufficient progress, with the clearest residual deficit at short target radii. The 51.0% checkpoint is selected for working and best-known lineage because it is the highest observed protected-task result, not because the one-episode margin over the proxy-peak checkpoint establishes robust superiority. The baseline recipe should be retained for provenance while development continues; an official benchmark is not warranted from this evidence.

## d1897119-e054-4e2a-a2fd-06958f8ce0bd / Experiment 2

**Result:** Expanding training targets to the full official radius range produced substantial but insufficient measured progress. The horizon-matched checkpoint candidate-32a2902d reached 82.5% (165/200) on the protected task-reference panel, up from the parent working policy's 51.0% (102/200), but remains below the 98% campaign objective.

**Observed behavior:** Training completed 120832 steps. The inventory training-success proxy reached 1.00 at 30720 steps, stayed around 0.99 through much of the late run, and was 0.97 at the final checkpoint; the corresponding task-reference results were 78.5% at 30720 steps, 82.5% at 100352 steps, and 80.5% at 120832 steps. On the same panel, the selected checkpoint improved radius-stratified success from the parent comparison of 3/57 to 50/57 at 6-10 cm and from 32/59 to 54/59 at 10-15 cm, while 15-20 cm changed from 67/84 to 61/84. Angle-sector success increased in all four sectors for the selected checkpoint: 27/52 to 42/52, 27/58 to 40/58, 20/37 to 32/37, and 28/53 to 51/53. The parent-selected paired outcomes were 85 episodes successful only for the selected checkpoint, 22 only for the parent, 80 for both, and 13 for neither. All three experiment-2 measurements used task-reference-v1 with seed 7300; this is development evidence, not the official final assessment.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The result supports the exploratory expectation that full-radius training can improve the short-radius deficit and materially raise aggregate protected-task success: the selected checkpoint's 85 parent-exclusive successes versus 22 parent-exclusive successes are consistent with meaningful behavioral progress. The preservation clause is not fully supported because the horizon-matched checkpoint lost six successes in the 15-20 cm band, and the endpoint's 80.5% result does not show monotonic late improvement. The intervention was transferred from the parent and trained further rather than compared with a contemporaneous unchanged control, so these measurements do not isolate radius coverage as the sole cause. The high training proxy also remains an orthogonal, non-acceptance signal.

**Interpretation:** Matching the training radius support to the official support is a promising route for the dominant short-radius failure mode, and candidate-32a2902d is a materially better measured policy than the prior working lineage. The evidence does not support official assessment yet: the best development result is 31.5 percentage points below the objective, uses one repeated development panel, and leaves a long-radius tradeoff unresolved. The selected horizon-matched checkpoint should become working and best-known; the endpoint is worth retaining as a complementary late-training alternative because it has one more short-radius success despite lower aggregate performance.
