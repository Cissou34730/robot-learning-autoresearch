# Research postmortems

## a82df388-883d-4362-b081-0c03c09f4dc5 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 98% complete reach-and-hold success on the unchanged official task. The fresh unchanged PPO baseline reached about 52% task-reference success, while transferring that learned policy into experiment 3's focused training sampler produced a selected checkpoint at 94.0% on the protected task-reference panel and 92.0% on the researcher panel. This is substantial progress toward the objective, but it remains below the 196/200 development-equivalent success level and is not terminal evidence.

**Lessons and limits:** Measured task outcomes, rather than training reward or the callback success proxy, must govern policy selection. In experiment 3, the training proxy reached 0.98 by 40,960 steps and 0.99 at both measured late checkpoints, while the protected task-reference result was 93.0% at step 115,712 and 94.0% at step 120,832. Relative to the transferred parent measured in the same round, the protected result rose from 52.0% to 93.0-94.0%; the -90 to 0 degree sector rose from 3/58 to 58/58, and the other sectors were retained or improved. The intervention therefore supports training coverage as a useful contributor under these tested conditions, but does not establish that coverage alone caused the change or rule out interactions with transfer and continued training. The selected late checkpoint still had 12/200 protected-panel failures, all in the -180 to -90 degree sector, with no failures in the other three angle bins; failures were also concentrated at shorter radii. The two late candidates scored 92.5% and 92.0% on the researcher panel, so their small difference does not provide a broad cross-panel superiority claim. The transferred parent scored 58.0% on that researcher measurement versus 61.0% in experiment 2, while its protected result reproduced 52.0%; this context/run discrepancy limits direct use of the researcher-panel change as an absolute estimate. Development panels remain distinct from the final benchmark.

The current evidence is consistent with targeted coverage being useful but does not isolate it from transfer or continued training. The selected policy's remaining failures are localized enough to support sector-specific analysis, while the observed radius pattern and the absence of fresh-run replication leave the mechanism and generality uncertain.

**Open questions:** It remains unresolved whether the remaining -180 to -90 degree and short-radius failures respond to targeted coverage, require a different curriculum, or arise from representation, reward, or control learning. It is also unresolved whether the 92-94% development performance is reproducible across fresh training runs and whether additional training can improve the residual failures without regression. The development measurements do not determine which explanation accounts for the residual failures or whether the selected policy would meet the official 98% result.

## a82df388-883d-4362-b081-0c03c09f4dc5 / Experiment 2

**Result:** The fresh unchanged baseline produced a partially successful learned policy. Checkpoint-115712 is selected as working and best known; the 98% objective is not supported by the available development measurements.

**Observed behavior:** Training metrics rose from zero success through 120,832 completed steps, reaching a callback success proxy of 0.62 at step 115,712 and 0.63 at step 120,832; the associated training reward peaked at 100.12 and then fell to 99.53. These are training signals, not task acceptance measurements. On 200 researcher-evaluation episodes, checkpoint-115712 succeeded on 122 episodes (61.0%) and checkpoint-120832 on 116 (58.0%). The paired comparison on their shared episodes had 10 wins for checkpoint-115712, 4 for checkpoint-120832, 14 discordant episodes, and a -3 percentage-point final-checkpoint delta. On the independent protected task-reference panel, the candidates succeeded on 104 (52.0%) and 103 (51.5%) episodes respectively. For checkpoint-120832, task-reference success was 0/58 in the -90 to 0 degree angle bin, compared with 27/52 in -180 to -90, 23/37 in 0 to 90, and 53/53 in 90 to 180; checkpoint-115712 showed the same qualitative concentration, including 3/58 in -90 to 0. The radius-bin success for checkpoint-120832 was 25/57 (43.9%), 29/59 (49.2%), and 49/84 (58.3%) for 6-10, 10-15, and 15-20 cm. On the researcher diagnostics, checkpoint-115712 reached tolerance in 127/200 episodes and succeeded in 122; its failures had mean maximum hold of 1.41 steps, while successful episodes completed the 100-step hold.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline hypothesis was to establish whether the unchanged method could learn useful behavior for the human-defined task. It is partially supported: learning progress is visible in the training metrics and the saved policies achieve well above chance partial task behavior. It is not evidence that the human objective is met: both measured candidates remain roughly half-successful on development panels, and the slight late-training proxy increase coincides with lower measured task success. Because this was a baseline rather than a confirmatory intervention, the evidence does not attribute the residual failures to a particular component or prove that the late decline is general.

**Interpretation:** The policy has learned a nontrivial reach-and-hold strategy for part of the target distribution, but the measured behavior is dominated by residual geometry-dependent reach failures and is not ready for terminal assessment. The protected-panel pattern identifies a useful next scientific question, but it does not by itself establish whether observation semantics, action control, reward shaping, or training coverage is responsible.

## a82df388-883d-4362-b081-0c03c09f4dc5 / Experiment 3

**Result:** The focused target sampler substantially improved measured performance on the unchanged task. Checkpoint-120832 is selected as working and best known; the 98% objective is not established, so terminal assessment is not requested.

**Observed behavior:** The transferred parent measured 58.0% on the researcher panel and 52.0% on the protected task-reference panel. The focused-sampler checkpoints succeeded on 185/200 (92.5%) and 184/200 (92.0%) researcher episodes at steps 115,712 and 120,832, respectively, and on 186/200 (93.0%) and 188/200 (94.0%) protected task-reference episodes. On the protected panel, checkpoint-115712 succeeded in 58/58 episodes in the -90 to 0 degree bin, 37/37 in 0 to 90, 52/53 in 90 to 180, and 39/52 in -180 to -90. Checkpoint-120832 succeeded in 58/58, 37/37, 53/53, and 40/52 in those bins. Its radius-bin results were 49/57, 55/59, and 84/84 for 6-10, 10-15, and 15-20 cm. Training metrics reached a 0.99 callback success proxy at both measured checkpoints; the training reward peaked at 120.15 around step 30,720 and was 105.85 at the final checkpoint. Twenty-two of 24 checkpoints were not measured.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The exploratory question is partially supported, with the tested behavioral prediction strongly observed: extra exposure to the -90 to 0 degree sector coincided with its improvement from 3/58 to 58/58 while preserving or improving the other protected-panel sectors. The conclusion is limited to this transferred PPO run and the development panels. It does not prove that sampler coverage alone caused the gain, establish reproducibility, or show that the remaining 6% failure rate can be removed by coverage alone.

**Interpretation:** The intervention produced meaningful policy progress toward the human objective and is preferable to the prior baseline on measured task behavior. The protected-panel result remains eight successes short of the 196/200 episodes corresponding to 98%, and the residual failures are concentrated in the opposite angle sector and shorter radii. The small late-checkpoint difference is instrument-dependent, with checkpoint-120832 higher on the protected panel by one percentage point but lower on the researcher panel by 0.5 points; the later checkpoint is selected because the protected task-reference panel is the closer development instrument for the unchanged official task, not because the training proxy increased.
