# Research postmortems

## f0cf745a-e772-45c6-9002-6f8f0f0c8595 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** PPO learned broad reach-and-hold behavior, but checkpoint `100352` remains below the campaign objective at 466/480 successes (97.1%) across three disjoint research panels. Full-radius continued training preserved the parent at 158/160 on a new panel and produced no paired wins; the same two negative-angle failures remained. One short-target non-reach became a one-step hold before interruption, while the 17.85 cm case stayed a one-step hold failure, so neither changed the complete-task outcome. The fixed task-reference result of 98% is selection evidence rather than independent confirmation.

**Lessons and limits:** Transfer from `100352` preserves broad behavior better than unchanged continuation to `120832`, which regressed slightly and added an isolated positive-angle hold failure. Full-radius exposure did not improve complete-task success, and the residual pair spans both an inner target and 17.85 cm, weakening radius coverage as a sufficient explanation. The one-step interruptions show that entering tolerance is not enough; branch selection, torque-driven overshoot, and insufficient dissipation remain competing explanations. Development panels remain non-official.

**Open questions:** Does stronger credit assignment for uninterrupted holding improve the persistent negative-angle cases without sacrificing the broad policy? Are the remaining failures primarily ineffective branch selection, excessive torque-driven motion, or inadequate dissipation after first reach? Is the isolated positive-angle failure of `120832` evidence of late-training regression or sampling variation?

## f0cf745a-e772-45c6-9002-6f8f0f0c8595 / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh PPO baseline produced a useful but sub-target policy. Checkpoint `100352` is selected as the working and best-known lineage; no terminal benchmark is requested.

**Observed behavior:** Training success rose sharply late in the run, reaching a proxy peak of 97% at 100352 and ending at 95% at 120832. On the first research panel, both late candidates scored 151/160 (94.375%). On the disjoint second panel, `100352` scored 157/160 (98.125%) and `120832` scored 155/160 (96.875%); pooled over the two panels they scored 308/320 and 306/320 respectively. The paired comparison favored `100352` by two discordant wins and showed no wins for `120832`. The 12 failures of `100352` were all at target angles approximately -119 to -154 degrees: most never reached tolerance, while the remaining cases reached briefly but held only 1-2 steps before interruption. The final checkpoint had the same negative-angle failures and additionally showed an interrupted hold at a positive angle on the disjoint panel.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline expectation that ordinary PPO training would learn the reach-and-hold task is partially supported: success improved from near zero to roughly 96% on pooled disjoint development panels. The stronger claim that the late training peak represented a policy satisfying the human 98% objective is weakened by the pooled result below 98% and by the repeated angle-specific failures. This conclusion is limited to the measured checkpoints and development panels; the official result remains unmeasured.

**Interpretation:** The policy has learned coordinated reaching and stable holding for most of the target distribution, consistent with the model's prediction that the task requires both dynamic convergence and a 100-step hold. Its remaining failures are structured around a difficult negative-angle region, where the policy either selects an ineffective trajectory or cannot dissipate/stabilize sufficiently. Continuing the unchanged recipe to 120832 did not improve that region and introduced one additional instability, so the unchanged recipe should be preserved while a future experiment targets the residual behavior.

## f0cf745a-e772-45c6-9002-6f8f0f0c8595 / Experiment 2

**Result:** Full-radius continued training did not improve the targeted negative-angle reach-and-hold failures. The transferred `working` parent remains the working and best-known lineage; the experiment's changed training recipe is reverted at closure, and no terminal benchmark is requested.

**Observed behavior:** On the disjoint research panel covering episodes 6200-6359, `working`, `checkpoint-100352`, and `checkpoint-105472` each achieved 158/160 successes (98.75%), with zero discordant paired outcomes. All three had the same two failed episode seeds: 6245, a 17.85 cm target at -145.65 degrees with a one-step hold interruption, and 6306, an 11.22 cm target at -130.05 degrees. The parent never reached tolerance on seed 6306, while both intervention checkpoints reached at step 17 but held for only one step before interruption. The complete success vectors remained identical, so this diagnostic shift did not change task success.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The preservation component was supported on this panel: full-radius training did not reduce the transferred policy's measured success and retained the same binary outcomes as the parent. The central prediction of improved short negative-angle complete-task success was not supported; the intervention checkpoints retained both failures, and the additional failure above 14 cm shows that omitted short-radius coverage was not sufficient to explain the residual weakness. The result is limited to the measured candidates and one disjoint development panel.

**Interpretation:** Full-radius exposure changed the trajectory of one short negative-angle case from non-reach to a brief, unstable hold, but did not provide the uninterrupted 100-step stabilization required by the scientific model or improve the policy's binary outcome. Because neither the matched-step checkpoint nor the training-proxy peak outperformed the retained parent, the intervention does not justify replacing the parent recipe or lineage. The remaining failures keep branch selection and torque-driven dissipation as unresolved, more useful directions than radius coverage alone.
