## f3356be0-50ce-45d0-a495-b909a50b054c / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 98% success, or 196/200 episodes, on the official reach-and-hold panel. The fresh baseline reached about 51% development success; reward-credit shaping reached 224/400 pooled success versus 206/400 for its unchanged control; and the transferred positive-extreme coverage recipe reached 296/400 across two disjoint panels versus 215/400 for its contemporaneous working control. A fresh replication reached 265/400 versus 288/400 for matched working controls, while recovering the positive-extreme sector on both panels. Experiment 8's explicit target-angle/radius features produced mixed sector shifts but no durable aggregate gain: across its two panels, checkpoint-115712 reached 289/400, the unchanged working comparator 288/400, and checkpoint-120832 285/400. Every measured policy remains far below the official objective. The current evidence supports preserving the reward-plus-positive-extreme recipe as a useful tested configuration while leaving its causal contribution, representation dependence, residual negative-angle failures, and hold stability unresolved.

**Lessons and limits:** Experiment 3's unchanged continuation was near-balanced with its parent and left the positive-extreme sector at zero. Experiment 2's aggressive symmetric sampler reduced overall success and degraded the strong negative-mid sector; experiment 6's gentler balanced sampler also underperformed its parent on a fresh panel and did not improve the negative-extreme sector. These results weaken coverage-only explanations without identifying whether control, representation, hold stability, or optimization is limiting. Experiment 4's reward change improved complete success and mean maximum held duration but increased interruptions and traded angular-sector performance. Experiment 5's positive-extreme focus improved the previously failing sector and measured mid sectors, while negative-extreme success remained 14/69 and its training proxies selected different checkpoints. Experiment 7 reproduced positive-extreme recovery from fresh initialization on two disjoint panels (31/31 and 34/36 for the fresh checkpoint versus 12/31 and 17/36 for working), but its pooled aggregate was lower than working (265/400 versus 288/400) and paired outcomes favored working 94-71. Experiment 8 showed a partial and inconsistent angular signal: explicit geometry improved some extreme-angle counts on each panel, but the sector shifts traded across mid sectors and did not produce a durable aggregate advantage. The endpoint's stronger training proxies again failed to identify the most useful task checkpoint and it recorded 1,244 interruptions across its two panels versus 891 for checkpoint-115712 and 1,107 for working. These proxies and partial-hold signals are checkpoint-selection diagnostics, not acceptance evidence. Complete uninterrupted hold success is authoritative; proximity, partial hold time, and training reward are incomplete signals. The task-reference panel is reused and selection-contaminated, and development panels are not the official verdict.

**Open questions:** The positive-extreme coverage gain now has fresh-run evidence, but the aggregate benefit over the unchanged working lineage is not reproducible under this replication. Experiment 8 weakens the claim that appending continuous target polar geometry alone resolves the remaining deficits, but does not distinguish control, reward direction, optimization, or interactions among them. The source of proxy/task disagreement, the extent of checkpoint-selection variance, and the generality of fresh versus transferred behavior also remain uncertain. No development measurement establishes readiness for terminal assessment.
