# Research postmortems

## 4b17531f-ff44-4b08-997f-e703fc28eb8e / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The PPO baseline learned competent reach-and-hold behavior, but the best characterized checkpoint remains below the 98% human objective: working checkpoint-100352 achieved 609/640 pooled successes across four disjoint research panels, including 152/160 on the latest. Residual failures include repeated inner-radius no-reach cases and some hold interruptions. The reduced-learning-rate continuation and the full-radius transfer both underperformed the parent, so the parent remains the strongest available policy.

**Lessons and limits:** Training success and reward are imperfect proxies for the required uninterrupted hold. The full 6-20 cm sampling intervention produced 151/160 versus the parent's 152/160 on its disjoint panel, weakening a coverage-only explanation without ruling out other curricula. The lower learning rate produced 151/160 at its proxy peak and 147/160 at its final checkpoint, with paired comparisons favoring the parent. The current reward awards partial hold progress but applies no forfeiture on a hold exit; whether that shaping affects measured behavior is unresolved. Development measurements are not the official 200-episode assessment.

**Open questions:** It remains unresolved whether stronger feedback against exiting a partial hold improves uninterrupted hold success without sacrificing reach, and whether the remaining inner-radius failures require a different curriculum or another scientific intervention. The cause of late-training decline and a recipe for crossing the objective are also unresolved.
