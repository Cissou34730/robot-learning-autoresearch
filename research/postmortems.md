## 5d4e159f-7fae-4a25-b885-b127042e894e / Scientific strategy

**Direction:** Establish a reliable baseline for the reach-and-hold objective, then improve robustness from the strongest measured checkpoint by addressing the directional and near-base weaknesses that remain after testing target-radius coverage.

**Lessons and limits:** The fresh baseline learned the task late in training, reaching 97.0% on the research panel and 98.0% on the fixed task-reference panel at 100,352 steps. The later 120,832-step checkpoint declined to 96.5% and 97.0% on those same panels, so the measured peak is preferable to the final checkpoint. Expanding training radii from 14-20 cm to the full 6-20 cm range did not improve the paired result: the transferred experiment-2 checkpoint reached 97.0% on the research panel and 98.0% on the task-reference panel, exactly matching the unchanged working policy. The same six research-panel failure identities remained, with negative angles from about -122 to -145 degrees; on the task-reference panel the candidate replaced one failure with another but kept four failures, including the near-base cluster. This makes radius coverage insufficient as a standalone intervention, while the one paired 200-episode comparison per panel cannot establish learning-process reproducibility. Both panels are development evidence rather than the official verdict.

**Open questions:** Whether the 100,352-step checkpoint reaches the 98% objective on the official panel remains unknown. It is unresolved whether the persistent negative-angle failures arise from control or representation limitations rather than target-radius coverage, and whether a different intervention can improve them without repeating the late-training decline. The development panels do not resolve stochastic training variation.

**Conditional next steps:** Keep checkpoint 100,352 as the working and best-known development lineage. The next intervention tests whether explicit smooth target-direction features let a freshly trained policy resolve the persistent negative-angle failures; measurements should retain the fixed panels and inspect failure geometry. If the failures persist, treat the representation hypothesis as weakened and investigate control or optimization limits rather than assuming that more training or broader radius sampling will help. Additional replication or paired measurement is justified only if it can change the intervention or lineage decision.

## 5d4e159f-7fae-4a25-b885-b127042e894e / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh baseline produced a strong but not yet officially validated policy. Checkpoint 100,352 is the measured peak and is selected for continued work.

**Observed behavior:** Training success rose from 0 through the first 70,656 steps, reached 0.97 at 100,352 steps, and was 0.95 at 120,832 steps. On the same 200-episode research panel, checkpoint 100,352 succeeded on 194 episodes (97.0%) and checkpoint 120,832 on 193 (96.5%); the later checkpoint added one failure and did not recover any of the earlier failures. On the fixed task-reference panel, the checkpoints achieved 98.0% and 97.0%, respectively. The earlier checkpoint had four failures, all truncated at 500 steps with target angles between -116 and -128 degrees and target radii between 6.7 and 9.9 cm. The later checkpoint retained those four failures and added two more. Neither result is the official benchmark.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline expectation of establishing a competent initial policy was supported: learning was substantial and the best checkpoint approached the objective. The contradicting observation is that the completed 120,000-step run did not improve monotonically and both development results remained below the objective on at least one panel. Because this was a baseline with no intervention, the evidence does not attribute the outcome to a method change or establish reproducibility.

**Interpretation:** The decline after the 100,352-step peak makes checkpoint selection consequential and argues against continuing from the final checkpoint by default. The paired research-panel comparison favors 100,352 by one episode, while the task-reference comparison favors it by two; together these are sufficient to choose the earlier policy for lineage, but not to claim generalization or explain causality. The persistent task-reference failures suggest a targeted angular/near-base robustness question for future training.

## 5d4e159f-7fae-4a25-b885-b127042e894e / Experiment 2

**Result:** Expanding the training target-radius distribution did not improve measured development performance. The unchanged experiment-1 checkpoint remains the working and best-known lineage.

**Observed behavior:** The measured experiment-2 checkpoint-105472 policy scored 97.0% on the research panel and 98.0% on the task-reference panel. The paired unchanged working policy scored 97.0% and 98.0%, respectively, on the same settings. The research-panel failures had the same six episode identities for both policies and remained concentrated at negative target angles; the candidate's diagnostics changed distances and hold behavior but did not turn any of these episodes into successes. On the task-reference panel, both policies had four failures. The candidate failed episodes with radii/angles approximately (6.7 cm, -116 degrees), (9.9 cm, -123 degrees), (9.4 cm, -128 degrees), and (18.2 cm, -155 degrees), while the incumbent instead failed a 7.2 cm, -125 degree episode rather than the last candidate failure. Training success was 1.0 at 105,472 steps but declined to 0.94 by 120,832 steps.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis that full target-radius coverage would improve near-base reach-and-hold robustness while preserving baseline performance is contradicted on the measured panels: baseline performance was preserved, but not improved, and the negative-angle failure pattern persisted. This conclusion is limited to the transferred run and the paired 200-episode development measurements; it does not establish that the intervention never helps or determine the official benchmark result.

**Interpretation:** The expected observation was a higher success rate or fewer near-base failures for the full-range candidate, while the contradicting observation was an unchanged rate with persistent failures. The paired results provide enough evidence to reject radius coverage as the next working intervention and to avoid retaining the candidate. The unchanged failure geometry points toward a directional control or representation issue, but the measurement does not distinguish those mechanisms. The late-training decline also argues against selecting the final checkpoint merely because it was trained longer.
