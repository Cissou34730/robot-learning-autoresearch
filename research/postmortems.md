## 8c345590-23d1-446d-b3de-14fca7c9df5d / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The tanh [64, 64] PPO recipe remains the strongest measured policy: `best_known` scored 777/800 (97.125%) on the latest disjoint panel and 4082/4200 (97.2%) across ten distinct research panels. Its 23 latest failures all target negative angles from about -156 to -120 degrees; 13 never entered tolerance and 10 held only briefly, with 497-500 saturated action steps in every failure. The open IK branch was consistently closer than the folded branch in those episodes, but this does not establish the learned failure mechanism. Experiment 6 widened the fresh PPO MLP to tanh [128, 128], but its 24 checkpoints remain unmeasured and its training proxy peaked at 0.45 success, which does not establish task performance. The retained experiment-2 lineage scored 722/800 (90.25%) on the same fresh panel and lost 110 of 111 discordant episodes to `best_known`; all development measurements remain distinct from the official assessment.

**Lessons and limits:** Training reward and proxy success do not establish the uninterrupted 100-step hold. The latest diagnostics show persistent command saturation and high joint speed alongside both non-entry and brief-hold failures, while the nearer open branch suggests that gross selection of the folded IK branch is not established as their common explanation. The retained inferior lineage fails more broadly, mostly without entering tolerance, so the paired evidence supports selecting `best_known` but does not identify whether its residual failures arise from sampled-control stabilization, command authority, trajectory choice, learned conditioning, or capacity. The frozen model establishes feasible annular geometry, bounded 50 Hz inertial control, and the need for a 2-second hold; it does not identify the mechanism of a learned failure. Prior reward and target-sampling changes did not repair the residual subgroup, and experiment 6 does not provide measured evidence about widened capacity.

**Open questions:** It remains unknown whether reducing effective command authority can let the learned controller brake and stabilize the near-open-branch trajectories without sacrificing the reorientation needed elsewhere, or whether the failures instead reflect representation, branch/trajectory conditioning, or learning-process limitations. The 98% objective has not been established across the campaign, and the physical model distinguishes the need for sampled stabilization from the unresolved behavior of the learned policy.

Because no trajectory diagnostics were recorded, branch selection, braking, overshoot, and sustained regulation remain competing explanations. The disjoint comparison is sufficient to select checkpoint-100352 over checkpoint-120832 for closure, while the sub-objective success rates argue against requesting the official benchmark now.

This does not prove that radius coverage can never help, because only two checkpoints and one disjoint panel were measured, but it provides no evidence to replace the established parent or continue this recipe.

**Interpretation:** The unchanged parent is the useful saved policy for subsequent development. The frozen model establishes that the newly covered radii are physically feasible and that success requires sampled inertial stabilization, while the measurements show only that this transfer under the changed sampler did not improve the realized policy. The failure exchange between one inner-radius episode and one outer-radius episode, together with persistent saturation and closest-branch diagnostics, does not identify branch choice or stabilization as the cause; another training intervention should therefore be chosen only after a decision-changing measurement or with its competing explanations stated.
