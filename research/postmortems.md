## 5d4e159f-7fae-4a25-b885-b127042e894e / Scientific strategy

**Direction:** Establish a reliable baseline for the reach-and-hold objective, then improve robustness from the strongest measured checkpoint by addressing the directional and near-base weaknesses that remain after testing target-radius coverage. The first explicit direction-feature intervention failed broadly, so the next useful training question should distinguish control or optimization limits from representation choices rather than assume that more target coverage or more training will help.

**Lessons and limits:** The fresh baseline learned the task late in training, reaching 97.0% on the research panel and 98.0% on the fixed task-reference panel at 100,352 steps. The later 120,832-step checkpoint declined to 96.5% and 97.0% on those same panels, so the measured peak is preferable to the final checkpoint. Expanding training radii from 14-20 cm to the full 6-20 cm range did not improve the paired result: the transferred experiment-2 checkpoint reached 97.0% on the research panel and 98.0% on the task-reference panel, exactly matching the unchanged working policy. The same six research-panel failure identities remained, with negative angles from about -122 to -145 degrees; on the task-reference panel the candidate replaced one failure with another but kept four failures, including the near-base cluster. Experiment 3 contradicted the smooth target-direction encoding hypothesis: its 120,832-step candidate reached only 41.5% on the research panel and 46.5% on task-reference, versus the incumbent's 97.0% research result and prior 98.0% task-reference result. The candidate lost 111 net episodes in the paired research comparison, with 91 of 117 failures never reaching tolerance and the remaining 26 failing to hold; failures were distributed across the full angular range rather than remaining a small negative-angle cluster. Its increasing training reward and zero training success do not substitute for task evaluation. Radius coverage and this representation change are therefore insufficient standalone interventions, while the development panels and single training realization do not establish official success or learning-process reproducibility.

**Open questions:** Whether the 100,352-step checkpoint reaches the 98% objective on the official panel remains unknown. It is unresolved whether the original negative-angle failures arise from control or optimization limits, or from a representation detail that can be changed without destabilizing the policy. Experiment 3 does not prove that every directional encoding is harmful, but it strongly weakens the specific redundant cosine/sine augmentation under the tested PPO recipe. The development panels do not resolve stochastic training variation.

**Conditional next steps:** Keep checkpoint 100,352 as the working and best-known development lineage and revert experiment 3's observation change. A future training proposal may investigate control or optimization mechanisms, or a more carefully justified representation change, with fixed-panel measurements and intermediate-checkpoint selection. Additional replication or paired measurement is justified only if it can change that intervention or lineage decision; repeating measurements of this clearly inferior candidate cannot.

## 5d4e159f-7fae-4a25-b885-b127042e894e / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh baseline produced a strong but not yet officially validated policy. Checkpoint 100,352 is the measured peak and is selected for continued work.

**Observed behavior:** Training success rose from 0 through the first 70,656 steps, reached 0.97 at 100,352 steps, and was 0.95 at 120,832 steps. On the same 200-episode research panel, checkpoint 100,352 succeeded on 194 episodes (97.0%) and checkpoint 120,832 on 193 (96.5%); the later checkpoint added one failure and did not recover any of the earlier failures. On the fixed task-reference panel, the checkpoints achieved 98.0% and 97.0%, respectively. The earlier checkpoint had four failures, all truncated at 500 steps with target angles between -116 and -128 degrees and target radii between 6.7 and 9.9 cm. The later checkpoint retained those four failures and added two more. Neither result is the official benchmark.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline expectation of establishing a competent initial policy was supported: learning was substantial and the best checkpoint approached the objective. The contradicting observation is that the completed 120,000-step run did not improve monotonically and both development results remained below the objective on at least one panel. Because this was a baseline with no intervention, the evidence does not attribute the outcome to a method change or establish reproducibility.

**Interpretation:** The decline after the 100,352-step peak makes checkpoint selection consequential and argues against continuing from the final checkpoint by default. The paired research-panel comparison favors 100,352 by one episode, while the task-reference comparison favors it by two; together these are sufficient to choose the earlier policy for lineage, but not to claim generalization or explain causality. The persistent task-reference failures suggest a targeted angular/near-base robustness question for future training.

## 5d4e159f-7fae-4a25-b885-b127042e894e / Experiment 2

**Result:** Expanding the training target-radius distribution did not improve measured development performance. The unchanged experiment-1 checkpoint remains the working and best-known lineage.

**Observed behavior:** The measured experiment-2 checkpoint-105472 policy scored 97.0% on the research panel and 98.0% on the task-reference panel. The paired unchanged working policy scored 97.0% and 98.0%, respectively, on the same settings. The research-panel failures had the same six episode identities for both policies and remained concentrated at negative target angles; the candidate's diagnostics changed distances and hold behavior but did not turn any of these episodes into successes. On the task-reference panel, both policies had four failures. The candidate failed episodes with radii/angles approximately (6.7 cm, -116 degrees), (9.9 cm, -123 degrees), (9.4 cm, -128 degrees), and (18.2 cm, -155 degrees), while the incumbent instead failed a 7.2 cm, -125 degree episode rather than the last candidate failure. Training success was 1.0 at 105,472 steps but declined to 0.94 by 120,832 steps.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis that full target-radius coverage would improve near-base reach-and-hold robustness while preserving baseline performance is contradicted on the measured panels: baseline performance was preserved, but not improved, and the negative-angle failure pattern persisted. This conclusion is limited to the transferred run and the paired 200-episode development measurements; it does not establish that the intervention never helps or determine the official benchmark result.

**Interpretation:** The expected observation was a higher success rate or fewer near-base failures for the full-range candidate, while the contradicting observation was an unchanged rate with persistent failures. The paired results provide enough evidence to reject radius coverage as the next working intervention and to avoid retaining the candidate. The unchanged failure geometry points toward a directional control or representation issue, but the measurement does not distinguish those mechanisms. The late-training decline also argues against selecting the final checkpoint merely because it was trained longer.

## 5d4e159f-7fae-4a25-b885-b127042e894e / Experiment 3

**Result:** Adding explicit cosine and sine target-direction features substantially degraded the fresh policy on both development panels. The incumbent remains the working and best-known lineage.

**Observed behavior:** The 120,832-step candidate scored 41.5% on the 200-episode research evaluation and 46.5% on the fixed task-reference panel. The unchanged working policy scored 97.0% on the same research evaluation; its compatible task-reference measurement scored 98.0%. In the paired research comparison, the incumbent won 113 episodes to 2, with 115 discordant episodes and a net candidate loss of 111. Among the candidate's 117 research failures, 91 never reached tolerance and 26 reached it but failed the continuous hold; all had fewer than 100 held steps. Candidate failures covered angles from -178.5 to 179.1 degrees, unlike the incumbent's six failures concentrated from -145.4 to -122.1 degrees. The candidate's training reward rose to 72.08 at 120,832 steps, but recorded training success remained zero.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis that smooth global target-direction features would reduce the persistent negative-angle failures and improve fixed-panel success is contradicted. The candidate performed far worse, with failures across the full angular range and a large loss on both panels. This supports rejecting this specific observation augmentation and its fresh PPO result; it does not establish that all representation changes are harmful, nor does it identify whether the degradation came from representation interaction, optimization, or control behavior.

**Interpretation:** The expected observation was a higher success rate with fewer negative-angle failures and no compensating losses elsewhere. Instead, the candidate's broad failure pattern and 55.5 percentage-point research deficit make another measurement of this candidate unable to change the lineage decision. The result also reinforces that training reward and late training progress are not reliable substitutes for fixed-panel task success. The development panels remain non-official evidence, so the incumbent's official objective status is still unknown.
