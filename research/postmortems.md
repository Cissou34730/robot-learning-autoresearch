**Direction:** The observation-representation and conservative-optimization investigations are resolved for the tested transfers. The retained baseline checkpoint-100352 remains the model to pursue: lowering PPO learning rate did not reduce its localized negative-angle no-reach gap at the early peak and caused broader reach-and-hold degradation by the comparable-horizon checkpoint. The next experiment should test whether policy capacity, rather than update size, limits the gap by changing the network capacity while transferring the retained baseline; it should keep the fixed target distribution and matched research evaluation unchanged.

**Lessons and limits:** The baseline checkpoint-100352 achieved 97.75% pooled research success across two matched panels, with nine failures concentrated around -121 to -150 degrees, establishing a concrete robustness gap (`research/brief.md`; `research/evaluations/10469e2d-31ee-4afe-a37b-5a24da0d14bb`). On the compatible seed-2000 panel, it had 5 failures, all in the focus sector. Experiment 2 rejected focused exposure after 8 focus-sector and 3 outside-sector failures at 15,360 steps and 9 focus-sector and 9 outside-sector failures at 120,832 steps. Experiment 3 established that fresh PPO realization variance can produce a globally failing policy, so transfer remains appropriate when testing a mechanism. Experiment 4 rejected a stability bonus: its later checkpoints reached only 81.5%, with 37 failures and 21 to 28 no-reach failures spanning the angular range. Experiment 5 reached 0.97 training success at 100,352 steps and 0.96 at 120,832, but the matched research evaluation reached only 66.5%; the candidate had 67 failures, including 43 no-reach and 24 reached-but-not-held episodes, versus the baseline's 5 failures, including 3 no-reach and 2 reached-but-not-held. Failures broadened to 54 outside-sector episodes, while the baseline had none. Experiment 6 reached a 0.98 training-success plateau near 27,648-35,840 steps but declined to 0.97 by 120,832; its 35,840-step evaluation matched the baseline's 5 failures, including 3 no-reach and 2 hold-limited failures all in the focus sector, while its 100,352-step evaluation fell to 94.5% with 11 failures, including 3 no-reach, 8 hold-interrupted, and 6 outside-sector episodes. Training success and reward therefore did not predict fixed-panel robustness. These measurements reject focused exposure, the stability bonus, the circular residual representation, and the tested lower learning rate for the current recipe. All measurements are development evidence, not the official objective verdict.

**Open questions:** It remains uncertain whether the baseline's localized no-reach gap is caused by policy capacity or stochastic realization rather than the rejected reward, observation, and learning-rate mechanisms. Experiment 6 resolves the tested lower-rate transfer, but not the general effect of optimization or the frequency of successful intervention realizations. Seed robustness should be revisited only if it changes the capacity decision or is needed to interpret a capacity result.

**Conditional next steps:** The lower-rate transfer did not reduce the negative-angle gap at its early peak and broadened failures later, so reject this intervention and restore the baseline recipe. A capacity experiment should transfer the retained checkpoint-100352 with a larger policy and evaluate the same matched panel; improvement localized to the focus sector would support capacity as the actionable limit, while unchanged or broader failures would leave stochasticity as the remaining decision-relevant alternative. Do not repeat the tested sampler, stability reward, circular-residual transfer, or lower-rate intervention.
