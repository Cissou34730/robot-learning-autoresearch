# Research postmortems

## 488237cb-f9aa-4aa1-af1f-25083833e753 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The unchanged PPO baseline learned a strong reach-and-hold policy late in training. The reward peak at `checkpoint-90112` did not identify the best measured behavior: it achieved 91.875% on the research panel and 93.0% on the fixed task-reference panel. `checkpoint-100352` is the best-supported candidate, reaching 98.0% (196/200) on the fixed task-reference panel and 99.5% (199/200) on the disjoint research panel. The latter confirms that the task-level result is not confined to the panel used for the initial selection, although development measurements do not replace the official assessment.

**Lessons and limits:** Training reward and training success were useful for locating late checkpoints but were not reliable policy rankings by themselves. The task-reference panel is permanently reused, so its 98.0% result is selection-panel evidence rather than independent confirmation; the disjoint 200-episode research panel provides that independent development check. The saved policy still has measured failures, and the available records do not identify whether they arise from reaching, convergence, or the uninterrupted hold, so no mechanism claim is made. The robot and task model establishes that success requires entering the 1 cm disk and remaining there for 100 consecutive control checks, but observed policy performance is determined by the measurement artifacts.

**Open questions:** The official 200-episode benchmark result for `checkpoint-100352` remains unknown. The exact physical causes of its residual development-panel failures are also unresolved, but they do not change the current lineage choice or justify another development measurement before the terminal assessment.

## 488237cb-f9aa-4aa1-af1f-25083833e753 / Experiment 1

**Result:** The baseline produced a useful learned policy, with `checkpoint-100352` selected over the later final checkpoint for closure and final assessment.

**Observed behavior:** The run completed 120,832 steps with the unchanged PPO recipe: tanh `[64, 64]` policy, learning rate `0.0003`, `n_steps=1024`, batch size `64`, `gamma=0.99`, `gae_lambda=0.95`, entropy coefficient `0.01`, and one environment. `checkpoint-90112` measured 147/160 (91.875%) on the first research panel and 93.0% on the fixed task-reference panel. `checkpoint-100352` measured 151/160 (94.375%) on that first research panel, 196/200 (98.0%) on the fixed task-reference panel, and 199/200 (99.5%) on the disjoint research panel. `checkpoint-120832` measured 151/160 (94.375%), 194/200 (97.0%), and 198/200 (99.0%) on the corresponding panels. The second-round paired comparison favored `checkpoint-100352` by one discordant episode over 360 accumulated comparable episodes; the shared task-reference panel remains reused evidence.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline hypothesis is supported for producing a policy with strong complete reach-and-hold behavior, but the evidence does not establish the official 98% objective because all measurements so far are development measurements. The planned expectation that the late proxy-success peak would be a strong checkpoint was supported: `checkpoint-100352` outperformed the reward-peak checkpoint and matched or exceeded the later checkpoint on every relevant panel. The conclusion is limited to this single training run and the recorded evaluation settings; it does not establish training reproducibility or explain the remaining failures.

**Interpretation:** `checkpoint-100352` is the scientifically justified working and best-known lineage. Continuing the unchanged baseline past this point did not improve protected-task performance, while the disjoint panel confirmed that the selected policy remains above the objective threshold in development evaluation. The evidence is sufficient to close this experiment and submit the selected artifact to the official benchmark; only that benchmark can determine whether the human objective is reached.
