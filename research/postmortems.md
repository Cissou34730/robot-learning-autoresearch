# Research postmortems

## e396d2fc-96c5-4690-8031-b9a7969d0c0c / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 196 successes in the official 200-episode panel for the unchanged two-joint-arm reach-and-hold task. The unchanged PPO baseline learned effective reaching behavior, but its strongest measured checkpoint, `100352`, reached 97.0% on the independent research panel and 98.0% on the task-reference panel; this does not establish the official objective. Later checkpoints were no better on the independent panel, the transferred hold-forfeit reward in experiment 2 did not improve the residual failures before late regression, and the fresh experiment-3 replication reached only 47.5% on its best research-panel measurement. The current evidence therefore describes a capable but not yet robust policy with substantial fresh-run variability, with the experiment-1 `checkpoint-100352` lineage remaining the strongest measured candidate.

**Lessons and limits:** Training success and reward are useful for locating candidate checkpoints but are not task-performance claims. In experiment 1, logged success rose to 0.98 around 99,328 steps, then declined to 0.95 by 120,832 steps; independent research evaluation likewise declined from 97.0% at 100,352 to 96.0% at 120,832. The task-reference panel is development evidence rather than the official assessment. Its four failures at checkpoints `100352` and `110592` were the same fixed negative-angle cases, from about -116 to -128 degrees and radii from about 6.7 to 9.9 cm, with final distances around 0.99 to 1.33 cm. The independent panel had six failures for checkpoint `100352`, all truncated, and the matching experiment-2 checkpoint had the same six episode outcomes in the paired comparison. Experiment 2's later checkpoint instead had broad regression, so the changed reward did not isolate or repair the residual pattern. Experiment 3 provides direct replication evidence under the same evaluation semantics: its research-panel measurements rose from 30.0% to 47.5% and its task-reference measurements from 32.0% to 49.5%, while all three measured research checkpoints remained far below experiment 1. This establishes variability for the tested fresh seed, not its population frequency or a causal mechanism. These remain single-panel development measurements and do not establish the official result.

**Open questions:** The prevalence of the negative-angle residual region on the official target distribution, the contribution of hold-continuity learning versus geometry-specific control limitations, and the frequency of near-98% results across training seeds remain unresolved. Experiment 3 shows that the unchanged recipe can also produce a much weaker fresh run, but one replication does not estimate the full seed distribution or explain the source of the variability. No next action is prescribed by this memory.

## e396d2fc-96c5-4690-8031-b9a7969d0c0c / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh baseline produced a useful but not yet official-objective-ready policy. `checkpoint-100352` is selected as both working and best-known lineage.

**Observed behavior:** Training logs show no successes through 70,656 steps, then a rapid rise in logged success from 0.06 at 75,776 to 0.97 at 100,352. Logged success subsequently fluctuated between 0.93 and 0.96 and ended at 0.95, while mean episode reward peaked at 163.85 around 86,016 and fell to 112.02 by 120,832. On the independent 200-episode research panel, checkpoints `100352`, `110592`, and `120832` achieved 97.0%, 96.5%, and 96.0%, respectively. On the fixed 200-episode task-reference panel they achieved 98.0%, 98.0%, and 97.0%, respectively. The two strongest task-reference checkpoints shared four failures at negative angles between -116.4 and -127.9 degrees; the final checkpoint added two failures. All task-reference failures ran to the 500-step limit.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. As an automatic fresh baseline, the experiment established that the unchanged method can learn a policy with high measured task performance and identified a useful checkpoint near 100,352 steps. It did not establish that the learned policy reliably satisfies the 98% objective: the strongest independent measurement was 97.0%, and the task-reference result is development evidence on a distinct fixed panel. The non-monotonic late training metrics and the lower independent-panel results weaken any inference that continuing this baseline recipe to the final checkpoint improves the task policy. These conclusions concern the saved checkpoints and measured panels, not unmeasured checkpoints or the official final panel.

**Interpretation:** The baseline made substantial progress toward the human objective, but residual failures and late-training degradation make `checkpoint-100352` preferable to the later saved checkpoints. The recipe itself is unchanged and should be kept for provenance; no alternative artifact is retained because the later candidates have weaker independent measurements and no distinct demonstrated advantage.

## e396d2fc-96c5-4690-8031-b9a7969d0c0c / Experiment 2

**Result:** The transferred hold-forfeit reward did not improve the measured policy over the existing working lineage. `checkpoint-95232` reached 97.0% on the independent 200-episode panel, tying the parent, and 97.5% on the task-reference panel, below the parent's 98.0%. `checkpoint-105472` reached 90.5% and 89.0% on those panels, respectively. The working and best-known lineage remain the experiment-1 `checkpoint-100352`.

**Observed behavior:** The experiment-2 logs began with high training-proxy success, reached 100% at 35,840 steps, and remained around 0.98-0.99 near checkpoints 90,112-109,568, while mean reward was about 103 at 35,840 and 105,472. Both proxies declined late, ending at 0.95 success and about 98 mean reward at 120,832. These are training signals, not task-performance claims. On the independent panel, `checkpoint-95232` had six failures, all truncated at 500 steps, matching the parent's six failures in the paired comparison with no discordant outcomes. On task-reference, `checkpoint-95232` had five truncated failures: the four previously observed negative-angle cases persisted, and one additional failure occurred at about -66 degrees. The later `checkpoint-105472` had 19 independent-panel failures and 22 task-reference failures, all truncated; its paired independent comparison lost 13 of 13 discordant episodes to the parent. The fixed task-reference panel is development evidence and neither panel is the official final assessment.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Weakened. The expected improvement in independent-panel success and reduction of negative-angle failures did not occur at the strongest measured experiment-2 checkpoint, although that checkpoint preserved the parent's independent-panel outcomes and most of the previously successful task-reference behavior. The later checkpoint showed broad regression rather than a focused hold improvement. This weakens the hold-continuity explanation under the tested transferred recipe and leaves geometry-specific control or representation limits as plausible alternatives; it does not identify a causal mechanism because this was one changed-reward training run and checkpoints also differ in training time.

**Interpretation:** The intervention supplied no measured progress toward the 98% objective and did not resolve the residual failure pattern. The high training proxies are orthogonal evidence that the transferred run learned and retained a capable policy, but they do not justify selecting its artifacts. Restoring the parent recipe preserves the strongest measured candidate while leaving future work free to address the persistent geometry and hold failures with a different investigation.

## e396d2fc-96c5-4690-8031-b9a7969d0c0c / Experiment 3

**Result:** The fresh unchanged PPO replication did not reproduce the experiment-1 near-threshold policy. Its best measured challenger reached 47.5% on the independent research panel and 49.5% on the task-reference panel, so the experiment-1 `checkpoint-100352` remains the working and best-known lineage.

**Observed behavior:** The raw experiment-3 log reported zero training-proxy success through 93,184 steps, 0.04 at 100,352, 0.08 at 110,592, and 0.13 at 120,832. Mean episode reward rose from -17.94 at 5,120 steps, reached 107.41 at 120,832, and was accompanied by improving episode lengths; these are training signals rather than task-performance claims. On the 200-episode independent research panel, the measured checkpoints `100352`, `110592`, and `120832` achieved 30.0%, 38.0%, and 47.5%, respectively, compared with experiment 1's 97.0%, 96.5%, and 96.0% at the same checkpoints. On the fixed task-reference panel, the corresponding experiment-3 results were 32.0%, 42.5%, and 49.5%, compared with 98.0%, 98.0%, and 97.0% for experiment 1. All 105 independent-panel failures at `120832` were truncated at 500 steps; only one failure first reached tolerance and the maximum held count among failures was three steps. The final task-reference measurement had 101 truncated failures spanning both angular signs and target radii from 6.07 to 19.53 cm, unlike experiment 1's six matched independent-panel failures, all at negative angles. Within experiment 3, the final checkpoint beat `100352` on all 35 discordant paired episodes, showing partial learning progression without producing a useful policy.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted under the tested fresh seed and measured checkpoints. The expected near-threshold result at a checkpoint near 100,000 steps did not occur on either development panel; the observed lower performance supports the proposal's alternative that the experiment-1 result can be seed- or checkpoint-specific. The assessment is limited to one replication seed and the three measured checkpoints: the 21 unmeasured checkpoints remain unmeasured, and this result does not estimate population-level seed variance.

**Interpretation:** Measured task behavior, rather than the rising reward or proxy success, shows that experiment 3 made partial but inadequate progress toward the human objective. The large matched-panel gap from experiment 1 is evidence of learning-process variability under the unchanged recipe, but this single fresh run cannot identify its cause. The replication made no researcher-owned code or parameter changes, so keeping the scientific recipe preserves the comparison while the previously measured experiment-1 candidate remains the only justified working lineage.
