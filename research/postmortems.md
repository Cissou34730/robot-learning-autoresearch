## 6dab7993-582d-4243-b0a2-f935e51c2728 / Experiment 2

**Result:** Full-radius transfer training did not improve measured task performance. The existing working lineage remains the strongest policy, and the full-radius final checkpoint is retained as a reusable alternative.

**Observed behavior:** On the disjoint research panel covering episodes 4520-4679, the working control, checkpoint-105472, and checkpoint-120832 each achieved 155/160 (96.875%). Both paired comparisons had zero candidate wins, zero reference wins, and zero discordant episodes, indicating identical success outcomes on this panel. The five shared failures occurred at episode seeds 4556, 4565, 4583, 4593, and 4625, with target radii approximately 6.29, 16.72, 7.30, 19.93, and 10.69 cm and target angles approximately -117.0, -139.5, -118.6, -161.6, and -126.0 degrees. The 105472 checkpoint reached a training-success proxy of 1.0, but that proxy did not translate into measured improvement.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Weakened. The prediction that uniform training over 6-20 cm would reduce the residual failures and improve independent success was not observed: both measured transfer checkpoints tied the parent below the 98% objective, and the shared failures included covered long radii as well as short radii and were concentrated in a directional sector. This is evidence against radius coverage as a sufficient explanation under this transfer run, not proof that radius coverage can never help.

**Interpretation:** The intervention preserved the learned reach-and-hold behavior but did not address the dominant residual failure pattern. The parent working lineage should remain active, while the current full-radius source change should be reverted before a different intervention is prepared. Retaining the full-radius endpoint preserves a measured transfer trajectory for later comparison without promoting it.
