# Research postmortems

## 999bd25e-73f3-4cc7-9b9f-de2d67fcf678 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The baseline learned a strong but not yet officially verified reach-and-hold policy. `checkpoint-100352` is the strongest measured candidate: it achieved 199/200 (99.5%) on the disjoint research panel and 196/200 (98.0%) on the fixed task-reference panel. The latter was used in selection and is therefore not independent confirmation. The final checkpoint, `checkpoint-120832`, was comparable on the first research panel but lower on the task-reference panel and slightly lower on the disjoint panel, so continued baseline training did not show a behavioral gain.

**Lessons and limits:** Training reward and the reward-peak checkpoint at 86016 did not identify the best complete task behavior; 86016 reached 93.125% on the first research panel and 94.0% on the task-reference panel. For 100352, the two research panels pooled to 350/360 (97.22%), so development evidence does not establish uniform 98% performance. The task-reference failures for 100352 all occurred at radii from 6 to 10 cm and timed out near the tolerance boundary, consistent with the known difference between the 14-20 cm training-radius range and the official 6-20 cm range, but not proving a single cause. Research diagnostics also show failures that either never entered tolerance or entered briefly and lost the hold, preserving the coupling between approach, tolerance entry, settling, and sustained completion. The available evidence is sufficient to select a best-known lineage, not to replace the official benchmark.

**Open questions:** Whether the residual failures in the negative-angle and inner-radius regions arise mainly from approach geometry, inverse-kinematic branch or joint-limit interactions, tolerance entry, or sustained stabilization remains unresolved. It is also unresolved whether the small transient-hold difference between the two late checkpoints is behavioral or panel variation, and how these coupled failure modes will distribute on the official fixed panel.

## 999bd25e-73f3-4cc7-9b9f-de2d67fcf678 / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh baseline produced a near-threshold policy, with `checkpoint-100352` selected as the best-known candidate for final assessment.

**Observed behavior:** The reward peak at `checkpoint-86016` was not the strongest measured policy. `checkpoint-100352` scored 151/160 (94.375%) on the first research panel and 199/200 (99.5%) on the disjoint panel, for 350/360 pooled research-panel successes. It scored 196/200 (98.0%) on the fixed task-reference panel. Its four task-reference failures all targeted 6-10 cm radii and timed out after 500 steps. On the first research panel, failures included episodes with no tolerance entry and episodes with only brief in-tolerance intervals. `checkpoint-120832` scored 151/160 and 198/200 on the two research panels and 97.0% on the task-reference panel, with no measured advantage over 100352.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline established substantial learned reach-and-hold capability and produced a candidate meeting the threshold on the fixed development panel, while the disjoint panel was above threshold. However, the pooled research evidence was 97.22%, and the fixed panel was involved in candidate selection, so the baseline did not by itself establish robust achievement of the human objective.

**Interpretation:** `checkpoint-100352` is preferable to the reward-peak and final checkpoints because it combines the strongest task-reference result with the strongest disjoint-panel result and no observed complete-task benefit from later training. The forced forecast for the official benchmark is `goal_reached`, based chiefly on 199/200 disjoint-panel success and 196/200 task-reference success, while recognizing the pooled-panel shortfall and the coupled residual failure modes.
