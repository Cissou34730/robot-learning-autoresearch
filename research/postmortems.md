# Research postmortems

## b62e8ccc-3c2e-4a3f-9477-2aa04cacd0d2 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The PPO baseline learned strong reach-and-hold behavior, but its best measured checkpoint remains near rather than securely above the 98% objective. `checkpoint-95232` is strongest on the research panels at 391/400 pooled successes, while its fixed task-reference result is 96.0%. Its eight task-reference failures were all at target radii from 6.03 to 9.91 cm, whereas the baseline trained only on 14 to 20 cm targets, making short-radius coverage a supported limitation but not yet a proven cause.

**Lessons and limits:** Training proxies do not rank task behavior reliably: `checkpoint-86016` had the highest listed training reward among measured checkpoints yet reached only 96.0% on the research panel and 94.0% on task-reference. `checkpoint-95232` led `checkpoint-120832` on the disjoint research comparison (196/200 versus 194/200), but `checkpoint-120832` reached 97.0% on task-reference. Research and task-reference measurements are development evidence, not the official 200-episode verdict; the fixed task-reference panel is reused and is not independent confirmation.

**Open questions:** Whether exposing training to the full 6 to 20 cm target-radius distribution improves short-radius reach-and-hold behavior without sacrificing outer-radius performance remains unresolved. The current evidence does not separate target-coverage effects from other causes of the residual failures.

## b62e8ccc-3c2e-4a3f-9477-2aa04cacd0d2 / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh PPO baseline produced a useful near-threshold policy, with `checkpoint-95232` selected as the strongest measured candidate; the human objective is not established by the available development evidence.

**Observed behavior:** `checkpoint-95232` achieved 195/200 (97.5%) on research episodes 2000-2199 and 196/200 (98.0%) on the disjoint episodes 2200-2399, for 391/400 pooled successes. `checkpoint-120832` achieved 194/200 (97.0%) on each research panel. On the fixed task-reference panel, `checkpoint-95232` achieved 96.0% and `checkpoint-120832` achieved 97.0%; `checkpoint-86016` achieved 94.0%. The paired research comparison over both panels favored `checkpoint-95232` by 4 wins to 1 among five discordant episodes. Training proxies rose late in the run, but the measured ranking did not simply follow the training-reward peak.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline established substantial learned reach-and-hold performance and produced a candidate that reaches the 98% threshold on one disjoint 200-episode development panel. However, its pooled research result is 97.75% and its fixed task-reference result is 96.0%, so the evidence does not support claiming reliable attainment of the human objective or requesting the official benchmark yet. This conclusion is limited to the single trained recipe and measured checkpoints.

**Interpretation:** `checkpoint-95232` is the best current working and best-known policy because it leads the independent-panel comparison and is slightly better than the final checkpoint on pooled research coverage. The unchanged scientific recipe should be preserved for lineage provenance. `checkpoint-120832` is worth retaining as a close alternative because it is competitive on research panels and stronger on the reused task-reference panel; further training remains an ordinary next experiment.
