# Research postmortems

## e0fec0e7-77ed-4433-97cd-1944e1b15eca / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The unchanged PPO baseline (1 environment, 1024-step rollouts, tanh [64, 64] policy, 0.0003 learning rate, entropy coefficient 0.01) learned a strong but not reliably objective-satisfying policy. `checkpoint-100352` achieved 99/100 on the first research panel, 195/200 on a disjoint panel, and 196/200 on the reused task-reference panel; the disjoint result is below the 98% objective and is development evidence, not an official result. Its failures are spatially concentrated in negative angles, with both no-entry and brief-hold failures, while later unchanged training reached only 97/100 on the first panel.

**Lessons and limits:** Training-time reward and success located the late-training transition but did not guarantee complete 100-step holds. Successful episodes terminate after roughly 104--120 control steps, whereas failed episodes run to the 500-step horizon. The frozen model establishes multiple inverse-kinematic branches, folded inner targets, coupled direct-torque dynamics, and possible joint-limit sensitivity; it does not establish which mechanism the policy uses. The current evidence supports `checkpoint-100352` as best known while leaving official 98% performance unresolved.

**Open questions:** The no-entry versus brief-hold failure pattern distinguishes reach from stabilization symptoms, but whether the negative-angle blind spot is caused primarily by elbow-branch selection or angle wrapping, proximity to a joint limit, inadequate braking and stabilization, or insufficient exposure to inner-radius targets remains unknown. Joint-limit, branch, velocity, and boundary-margin traces would discriminate those causes; the current artifacts do not contain them. It is also unresolved whether reducing this localized failure mode would preserve the broad success learned elsewhere. The official benchmark remains unrequested because current development evidence does not support expecting a `goal_reached` verdict.

## e0fec0e7-77ed-4433-97cd-1944e1b15eca / Experiment 1

**Result:** The baseline produced a useful near-objective policy, with `checkpoint-100352` selected as both working and best known, but the experiment did not establish reliable achievement of the 98% objective.

**Observed behavior:** The run used the unchanged PPO recipe and completed 120,832 steps. Training success increased from 0 at 70,656 steps to 0.42 at 86,016, peaked at 0.97 at 100,352, and ended at 0.95. On the first research panel, `checkpoint-86016`, `checkpoint-100352`, and `checkpoint-120832` scored 92%, 99%, and 97%; on the fixed task-reference panel they scored 94%, 98%, and 97%. The disjoint 200-episode research panel for `checkpoint-100352` scored 97.5% (195/200). Its five failures were at target angles from -149.7 to -128.1 degrees and radii from 10.3 to 17.3 cm; three had no tolerance entry, while two entered for at most three steps before failing. The fixed-panel failures for this checkpoint were also concentrated at negative angles (-127.9 to -116.4 degrees) and radii 6.7--9.9 cm. Successful episodes generally completed in 104--120 control steps, while failures truncated at 500 steps.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline established a high-performing learned policy and the late proxy peak identified the strongest measured checkpoint, but proxy improvement and continued training did not establish reliable complete-hold performance at or above 98% on fresh episodes. The comparison is limited to this single training run and the available development panels; the fixed task-reference panel is reused and was part of model selection.

**Interpretation:** The baseline learned reach-and-hold behavior for most targets, but its residual failures are structured by target geometry. The frozen scientific model makes branch choice, folded-posture sensitivity, joint limits, and configuration-dependent direct-torque stabilization plausible distinctions; the measurements show the spatial concentration and reach-versus-hold symptoms but cannot attribute the cause among them. The current checkpoint is therefore worth extending in a later experiment, while the final benchmark should not yet be requested.
