## 7a8a9e3a-e0cc-4940-a747-cb7529b2da8c / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The unchanged PPO baseline remains the strongest saved policy, with 582/600 successes across three distinct research panels. It reached 196/200 on the experiment-2 panel but 191/200 on the latest experiment-3 panel, while the partial hold-exit forfeiture continuation fell to 170/200 and 165/200 at its measured checkpoints. The campaign has strong development progress but no official result.

**Lessons and limits:** Training success and reward identify useful checkpoints but do not replace task-success measurement. Both full and partial hold-exit forfeiture interventions regressed substantially in the measured continued-training checkpoints, showing that these exit penalties are disruptive but not ruling out gentler hold-credit shaping. Incumbent failures include both episodes that never reach tolerance and episodes that interrupt a hold, and the research panels plus reused task-reference panel remain development evidence rather than official confirmation.

**Open questions:** It remains unresolved whether emphasizing later hold steps can improve stability without sacrificing reach reliability. The official result for the selected policy is also unresolved; development panels do not declare the objective reached.

**Result:** The full hold-exit forfeiture intervention did not improve the learned policy. The saved parent remains the working and best-known policy, and the intervention recipe should be reverted.

**Observed behavior:** On the new disjoint research panel covering episodes 2200-2399, the parent `working` policy achieved 196/200 successes (98.0%). The measured intervention checkpoints achieved 177/200 (88.5%) at `checkpoint-105472` and 167/200 (83.5%) at `checkpoint-120832`. Paired comparisons favored `working` over those checkpoints by 19 and 29 discordant episodes, respectively; the earlier parent-selection panel is not counted as independent confirmation of this new result. The measured candidates therefore fail the 98% development threshold by 19 and 29 episodes, while 22 other experiment-2 candidates remain unmeasured.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis that setting `HOLD_EXIT_FORFEIT_FRACTION` to 1.0 would improve hold completion and persist through continued training is contradicted under the tested continuation and checkpoints. This conclusion is limited to the tested intervention, training trajectory, and evaluation panel; it does not disprove every possible hold-robustness method. The parent result is useful saved-policy evidence, but development measurements do not declare the human objective reached.

**Interpretation:** The intervention should not replace the parent lineage. The independent 196/200 parent result justifies retaining the unchanged recipe as the current working and best-known lineage, while the large regression at both measured intervention checkpoints makes another measurement of the same experiment unlikely to change the lineage decision. No terminal benchmark is requested in this closure because the development evidence is at the threshold on one new panel but remains 391/400 (97.8%) across the two research panels, so a goal-reached verdict is not yet expected with sufficient confidence.

## 7a8a9e3a-e0cc-4940-a747-cb7529b2da8c / Experiment 3

**Result:** The 50% hold-exit forfeiture intervention did not improve the learned policy. The unchanged incumbent remains the working and best-known lineage, and the experiment recipe should be reverted.

**Observed behavior:** On the disjoint research panel covering episodes 2400-2599, the incumbent achieved 191/200 successes (95.5%). The measured intervention checkpoints achieved 170/200 (85.0%) at `checkpoint-105472` and 165/200 (82.5%) at `checkpoint-120832`. Paired comparisons favored the incumbent over those checkpoints by 21 and 26 discordant episodes, respectively. The later intervention checkpoint also trailed the earlier one by 5 percentage points. The remaining experiment-3 checkpoints are unmeasured and are not treated as failed policies.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis that partial forfeiture would improve uninterrupted-hold reliability without the reach regression caused by full forfeiture is contradicted for the tested continuation and measured checkpoints: both candidates were materially worse than the unchanged parent on the disjoint task panel. This conclusion is limited to the tested 50% intervention, training trajectory, and panel; it does not disprove other hold-robustness methods. The development measurements do not establish the official objective.

**Interpretation:** The intervention should not replace the parent lineage. No measured candidate justifies retention, and another measurement of this same changed recipe is unlikely to alter the working-lineage decision after the large paired regressions. The incumbent is useful saved-policy evidence but remains below the 196/200 development threshold on the latest panel, so no final benchmark is requested in this closure.
