# Research postmortems

## 10469e2d-31ee-4afe-a37b-5a24da0d14bb / Scientific strategy

**Direction:** The baseline investigation is resolved for checkpoint selection: PPO learns a near-threshold reach-and-hold policy, but continued training after the peak reduces robustness. The next investigation should target robustness across target geometry, especially the recurring negative-angle failure sector, rather than assume that a later checkpoint is better.

**Lessons and limits:** Training success increased from 0 to 0.98 by 99,328 steps and then declined to 0.95 at 120,832 steps, with episode length increasing again after the peak (`research/results.jsonl`, experiment 1 training record; `research/query_training_log.py` output for steps 75,776-120,832). The 100,352-step checkpoint achieved 97.75% pooled research success (97.5% and 98.0% across two 200-episode panels), while the 120,832-step endpoint achieved 97.0% on both panels. Existing paired evidence favored the peak on 3 episodes with no endpoint wins, but only 3 episodes were discordant (`research/results.jsonl`). The peak's 9 failures across both panels were concentrated at angles from about -121 to -150 degrees; 6 were reach failures and 3 were hold failures. These are development measurements, not the official 200-episode verdict, and one training run cannot establish whether the late regression is caused by PPO drift or stochastic variation.

**Open questions:** Can a targeted intervention improve the difficult negative-angle sector without sacrificing the rest of the angular range or the 2-second hold? Is the late-training regression a repeatable learning-process effect or a single-run fluctuation?

**Conditional next steps:** Close this baseline with the peak checkpoint. In the next experiment, test a concrete robustness intervention aimed at angular coverage or target-conditioned control and evaluate the selected peak against its later endpoint on matched panels. If the intervention removes the negative-angle failures without reducing hold reliability, continue that direction; if it does not, use a mechanism-focused measurement or a fresh replication to distinguish representation/reward limitations from training variance.

## 10469e2d-31ee-4afe-a37b-5a24da0d14bb / Experiment 1

**Result:** The 100,352-step baseline checkpoint is selected as the working and best-known lineage; the experiment remains below the human objective on development evidence, so the campaign should continue without requesting the terminal benchmark.

**Observed behavior:** Training success rose from 0 at 70,656 steps to 0.97 at 96,256 steps and 0.98 at 99,328 steps, then declined to 0.95 by 120,832 steps. The selected checkpoint scored 97.5% and 98.0% on the two 200-episode research panels, for 97.75% pooled success. The endpoint scored 97.0% on both panels. The paired comparison recorded 3 peak wins, 0 endpoint wins, 3 discordant episodes, and a two-sided exact p-value of 0.25. Existing episode diagnostics show that peak failures cluster in the negative-angle sector, with both reach and hold failures present.

**Hypothesis assessment:** This was a fresh baseline, whose recorded hypothesis was only to establish an initial reference for the human-defined objective; the proposal recorded no intervention-specific expected or contradicting observation. That baseline expectation was met: the policy learned substantially and supplied a comparable near-threshold reference. The evidence does not establish objective success, does not prove the late regression is causal, and does not justify selecting the endpoint over the earlier peak. The current investigation is resolved for lineage choice, while the scientific direction changes to improving geometric robustness.

**Interpretation:** The peak is the strongest available candidate because it is better on both measured panels and the only candidate with a 98.0% panel result, whereas later training appears to trade away robustness. The recurring negative-angle failures identify a concrete next problem, but their persistence across two panels is not enough to determine whether reward/observation design or PPO stochasticity is responsible. No additional measurement is needed before closure because the existing matched panels and diagnostics already change the lineage decision and define a non-arbitrary next direction.
