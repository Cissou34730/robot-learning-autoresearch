# Research postmortems

## 1a939de2-8d9d-42c0-a475-dc59a9a5e55c / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The unchanged PPO baseline learned a competent reach-and-hold controller, but its complete-hold performance is not yet established as robustly at the human objective. Training proxies rose sharply late in the run, while all three measured late checkpoints achieved the same 151/160 (94.375%) on the first disjoint research panel. The selected working checkpoint then achieved 159/160 (99.375%) on a second disjoint panel with substep diagnostics, while the retained final checkpoint achieved 158/160 (98.75%). These are encouraging development results, not evidence that the official objective is reached.

**Lessons and limits:** Late training success and reward are informative about learning progress but do not rank complete task behavior: the proxy peak, plateau entry, and final checkpoint were indistinguishable on paired research outcomes, with zero discordant episodes in every comparison. In the diagnostic panel, both policies shared one failure at angle -142.15 degrees and radius 16.37 cm; neither entered tolerance even at a simulator substep, with minimum substep distance about 1.24 cm for working and 1.22 cm for the retained final. This identifies a pre-entry reach failure rather than a sampled-hold interruption. The retained final also had one distinct +100.49 degree, 16.38 cm failure that entered tolerance at step 8 with 86.7 cm/s tip speed, held only five sampled steps, and repeatedly interrupted, while working succeeded on that episode. The new evidence therefore supports a dominant shared reach/coverage limitation plus a secondary checkpoint-specific regulation failure. The 200-episode task-reference result remains development evidence on a fixed panel, not the official assessment.

**Competing explanations:** The shared -142.15 degree miss is consistent with incomplete coverage of an inverse-kinematic branch or a difficult approach path from the reset singularity, and is also consistent with insufficient exposure to the official inner-radius distribution because baseline training used only radii 14–20 cm. The evidence weakens a purely post-entry regulation account for that shared failure. The retained-final-only +100.49 degree failure supports a separate dynamic regulation or checkpoint-variance explanation, but one episode does not establish its prevalence. A representation limitation remains possible because the observation exposes branch errors but not action history or substep state.

**Decision frontier:** The remaining decision is whether restoring training coverage over the full official radius range reduces the shared pre-entry basin without degrading complete-hold regulation. The next evidence must compare the transferred policy with the saved working reference on a new research panel, using the same substep diagnostics and task-reference outcome; improvement specifically in pre-entry misses, with stable success outside the basin, would support the coverage explanation, while unchanged misses would redirect investigation toward branch representation or approach control.

## 1a939de2-8d9d-42c0-a475-dc59a9a5e55c / Experiment 1

**Result:** The baseline learned a policy that completed 151/160 research-panel holds and 196/200 protected task-reference holds at its best measured checkpoint, but no evidence justifies claiming the official 98% objective. Checkpoint-100352 is selected as both working and best-known; checkpoint-120832 is retained as a close alternative.

**Observed behavior:** Training proxies reached 0.97 success at checkpoint-100352 and 0.95 at checkpoint-120832, but checkpoint-95232, checkpoint-100352, and checkpoint-120832 each scored 151/160 on the same research panel. Their paired comparisons had zero discordant wins. The research failures were the same nine episode identities for all three checkpoints, centered on target angles roughly -119 to -150 degrees and commonly radii from 7 to 19 cm. Five to seven of those episodes never entered tolerance depending on checkpoint; the remaining failures entered for at most two control samples and then left. On the protected task-reference panel, the checkpoints scored 192/200, 196/200, and 194/200 respectively.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline hypothesis was only partially supported. Learning clearly produced substantial reach-and-hold capability and late proxy gains transferred to strong task-reference performance, but the proxy peak did not improve the disjoint complete-hold result over the neighboring checkpoints, and the shared directional failure basin remains. The 98.0% task-reference result is encouraging but is one development panel and cannot establish the official result or generalize beyond the measured target set.

**Interpretation:** The policy has acquired most of the task but exhibits a reproducible blind region that is more scientifically important than small differences among late checkpoints. The evidence favors retaining the proxy-peak checkpoint as the current working baseline because it is best on the protected development panel, while preserving the final checkpoint for later transfer or comparison. Further progress should address the causal distinction between directional reach/branch coverage and dynamic settling rather than simply selecting a later checkpoint.
