# Research postmortems

## 1a939de2-8d9d-42c0-a475-dc59a9a5e55c / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The unchanged PPO baseline learned a competent reach-and-hold controller, but its complete-hold performance is not yet established as robustly at the human objective. Training proxies rose sharply late in the run, while all three measured late checkpoints achieved the same 151/160 (94.375%) on the disjoint research panel. The protected task-reference panel favored checkpoint-100352 at 196/200 (98.0%), ahead of checkpoint-120832 at 194/200 (97.0%) and checkpoint-95232 at 192/200 (96.0%). The selected checkpoint is therefore a useful working baseline, not evidence that the campaign objective is reached.

**Lessons and limits:** Late training success and reward are informative about learning progress but do not rank complete task behavior: the proxy peak, plateau entry, and final checkpoint were indistinguishable on paired research outcomes, with zero discordant episodes in every comparison. The nine research-panel failures were shared across all three policies and clustered at negative target angles, mostly with radii below 18 cm; several approached tolerance briefly but did not sustain it, while others never entered tolerance. This supports a systematic directional/geometry limitation, but the 50 Hz diagnostics do not reveal branch choice, velocity at entry, substep excursions, or action saturation. The 200-episode task-reference result is development evidence on a fixed panel, not the official assessment.

**Competing explanations:** The failure basin may reflect incomplete coverage of an inverse-kinematic branch or an approach path from the reset singularity that is difficult for a subset of target bearings. Alternatively, the policy may reach those configurations but fail to dissipate or regulate motion inside the tolerance disk; the small differences in maximum held steps and interruptions across checkpoints are consistent with this but do not identify it. A third possibility is a training-distribution or representation limitation that makes the same directional region hard to learn, rather than a purely physical control limitation. The current evidence cannot separate these explanations.

**Decision frontier:** The unresolved distinction is whether the systematic directional failures are primarily geometric/branch-selection errors before tolerance entry or dynamic settling/regulation errors after entry. Evidence that separates them must jointly resolve target geometry, approach and hold timing, and sufficiently fine motion or control state to distinguish missed reach from overshoot and interrupted regulation; it should also test whether the failure basin persists after a scientific intervention.

## 1a939de2-8d9d-42c0-a475-dc59a9a5e55c / Experiment 1

**Result:** The baseline learned a policy that completed 151/160 research-panel holds and 196/200 protected task-reference holds at its best measured checkpoint, but no evidence justifies claiming the official 98% objective. Checkpoint-100352 is selected as both working and best-known; checkpoint-120832 is retained as a close alternative.

**Observed behavior:** Training proxies reached 0.97 success at checkpoint-100352 and 0.95 at checkpoint-120832, but checkpoint-95232, checkpoint-100352, and checkpoint-120832 each scored 151/160 on the same research panel. Their paired comparisons had zero discordant wins. The research failures were the same nine episode identities for all three checkpoints, centered on target angles roughly -119 to -150 degrees and commonly radii from 7 to 19 cm. Five to seven of those episodes never entered tolerance depending on checkpoint; the remaining failures entered for at most two control samples and then left. On the protected task-reference panel, the checkpoints scored 192/200, 196/200, and 194/200 respectively.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline hypothesis was only partially supported. Learning clearly produced substantial reach-and-hold capability and late proxy gains transferred to strong task-reference performance, but the proxy peak did not improve the disjoint complete-hold result over the neighboring checkpoints, and the shared directional failure basin remains. The 98.0% task-reference result is encouraging but is one development panel and cannot establish the official result or generalize beyond the measured target set.

**Interpretation:** The policy has acquired most of the task but exhibits a reproducible blind region that is more scientifically important than small differences among late checkpoints. The evidence favors retaining the proxy-peak checkpoint as the current working baseline because it is best on the protected development panel, while preserving the final checkpoint for later transfer or comparison. Further progress should address the causal distinction between directional reach/branch coverage and dynamic settling rather than simply selecting a later checkpoint.
