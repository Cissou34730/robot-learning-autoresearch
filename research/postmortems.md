# Research postmortems

## 76a0e44c-15e1-4f0a-aad3-e75ef2d20c09 / Scientific strategy

**Direction:** Improve official-task generalization from the near-threshold baseline by addressing the lower-left negative-angle failures and interrupted holds through observation, control, or hold-objective changes rather than further radius-coverage changes.

**Lessons and limits:** The fresh PPO baseline trained on radii 14-20 cm, while the official task spans 6-20 cm (`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`, `research/scenario.md`). The transferred full-coverage recipe reached 98% on each of three 200-episode research panels, 97.4% on a 500-episode panel, and 98.5% on the 200-episode task-reference panel. On the paired 500-episode panel, it had the same 13 failures as the baseline parent, so the coverage intervention did not demonstrate a reproducible task-performance gain. On the task-reference panel it replaced two baseline failures with one different failure (98.0% to 98.5%), while the 100,352- and 120,832-step challengers had identical three-failure sets. Failures remain concentrated at negative angles around -117 to -157 degrees and include both failure to reach tolerance and interrupted holds. Training success and reward are proxies, and all panels are development measurements rather than the official benchmark; these results do not prove that radius coverage is irrelevant or identify the cause of the residual failures.

**Open questions:** Whether the persistent negative-angle failures arise from observation/representation limits, control precision, or the hold objective; whether a targeted intervention can reduce interrupted holds without harming far-target behavior; and how the selected policy performs on the official benchmark.

**Conditional next steps:** Continue from the baseline `working` lineage and investigate one targeted observation/control or hold-objective change. Prefer a future measurement that separates reach failures from hold interruptions; revise this direction if a new intervention improves the lower-left failure cluster without sacrificing overall success.

## 76a0e44c-15e1-4f0a-aad3-e75ef2d20c09 / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh baseline produced a strong but not officially validated policy. `checkpoint-100352` achieved 97% on research evaluation and 98% on the independent task-reference development panel; `checkpoint-120832` achieved 97% on both.

**Observed behavior:** This was a fresh baseline with the default PPO recipe, not a changed recipe, continuation, or replication. The training proxy rose from 0% to 97% by 97,280 steps, then remained around 93-97% through 120,832 steps while proxy reward declined from its peak. On the identical 200-episode research panel, the two measured checkpoints both had 6 failures and the paired comparison had one win each with two discordant episodes. On the protected task-reference panel, the 100,352-step checkpoint had 4 failures (98%) and the final checkpoint had 6 failures (97%). The late-checkpoint task-reference failures were concentrated at radii 6.7-9.9 cm and angles -116 to -128 degrees. Research-evaluation failures similarly concentrated at negative angles; diagnostics showed both episodes that never reached tolerance and episodes that reached it but repeatedly interrupted the hold. The task-reference evaluator is independent of the researcher-owned environment and reward, but both panels are fixed development measurements.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline hypothesis was to establish an initial reference for the human objective, and it is supported as a useful near-threshold reference. It does not establish that the objective is reached: the 98% task-reference result is one development panel, the research panel is 97%, and no official benchmark was run. The late checkpoint is preferred over the final checkpoint on the available task-reference evidence, but the difference is uncertain and does not support a causal claim about training duration.

**Interpretation:** The default recipe learns the task substantially, but its restricted 14-20 cm training distribution leaves residual generalization and hold-stability failures on the official 6-20 cm task. This pattern motivates changing training coverage; it is an observed association rather than proof that radius coverage caused the failures.

## 76a0e44c-15e1-4f0a-aad3-e75ef2d20c09 / Experiment 2

**Result:** Broadening transferred-policy training from radii 14-20 cm to the full official 6-20 cm range produced a near-threshold challenger but did not establish an improvement over the baseline parent. The baseline recipe remains the working and best-known lineage.

**Observed behavior:** This was a changed-recipe transfer experiment, not a continuation or replication. The training proxy rose from 96.6% at 5,120 steps to 100% at 24,576 and 105,472 steps, then ended at 94% at 120,832; these are training facts, not evaluation results. The 100,352-step challenger scored 98.0% on the 200-episode research panel, 97.4% on the 500-episode research panel, and 98.5% on the task-reference panel. The 105,472-step challenger scored 98.0% on both 200-episode panels, and the final 120,832-step challenger scored 98.0% on research evaluation and 98.5% on task reference. On the identical 500-episode panel, the challenger and the baseline parent both scored 97.4% with no discordant outcomes. The task-reference panel changed from baseline failures at seeds 7300, 7310, 7384, and 7402 to challenger failures at 7384, 7402, and 7475; the 100,352- and 120,832-step challengers had the same three failures. Detailed research diagnostics still place failures at negative lower-left angles, with short-radius and farther-radius cases and both no-reach and interrupted-hold outcomes.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The coverage hypothesis is weakened under the tested transfer, budget, and PPO conditions. The expected consistent reduction in short-radius/lower-left failures without sacrificing other behavior was not observed: the larger paired panel was exactly tied, while the fixed task-reference panel showed only a one-episode net improvement and a changed failure identity. The intervention may still alter failure trajectories, but the evidence supports no reliable task-performance gain and cannot establish that coverage caused either the small panel difference or the unchanged paired result.

**Interpretation:** The persistent failure geometry after adding short-radius training examples shifts the useful next investigation toward observation/representation, control precision, or hold stability. The 200-episode research results and 98.5% task-reference result make the challenger viable, but their panel-specific variation is insufficient to replace the established parent. No further measurement round is needed for this lineage decision; repeated development panels would not resolve the remaining causal uncertainty, and the campaign still has a scientifically useful next intervention.
