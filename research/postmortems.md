## f0cf745a-e772-45c6-9002-6f8f0f0c8595 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** PPO learned broad reach-and-hold behavior, but the working lineage remains below the campaign objective at 1863/1920 successes (97.0%) across twelve distinct research panels; its newest panel was 155/160 (96.875%). Its residual failures are concentrated in negative-angle trajectories, spanning non-reach and brief or unstable holds. Experiment 10's 0.65 effort-envelope candidates scored 141/160 and 120/160 on that same fresh panel, with no paired wins against working, so the unchanged `100352` lineage remains the strongest reusable policy. The fixed task-reference result of 98% is selection evidence, not independent confirmation.

**Lessons and limits:** Transfer from `100352` preserves broad behavior better than later unchanged learning, while full-radius exposure, difficult-angle oversampling, hold-progress changes, action smoothing, polar encoding, endpoint-speed reward shaping, signed branch-residual margins, explicit branch joint-limit clearance, and the tested 0.65 effort envelope did not produce a measured complete-task gain. In experiment 10, the matched effort-scaled candidate had 19 failures and the final candidate had 40, including broad reach regressions; reducing the effort envelope alone therefore did not preserve learned coordination. Effort, shoulder-limit proximity, velocity, branch choice, approach timing, and dissipation remain observationally confounded by the available measurements. Training proxies remain unreliable for complete-task success, and all development panels remain non-official.

**Open questions:** It remains unresolved whether a less disruptive learning update can refine the residual negative-angle behavior without eroding the retained policy's broad reach and 100-step hold. Branch selection, approach dynamics, and hold dissipation remain competing explanations, and the official task result remains unmeasured.
