# Research postmortems

## f3356be0-50ce-45d0-a495-b909a50b054c / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 98% success, or 196/200 episodes, on the official reach-and-hold panel. Experiment 4's transferred reward-credit intervention is the strongest measured policy on two disjoint development panels: checkpoint-115712 reached 220/400 pooled success and checkpoint-120832 reached 224/400, versus 206/400 for the unchanged working control. This is useful but modest progress, still far from the objective. The intervention improved complete holds and mean maximum hold duration in aggregate, while the positive-extreme sector remained at zero for every compared policy. The evidence supports preserving the reward direction but does not isolate its terms or explain the residual angular failure.

**Lessons and limits:** The fresh baseline improved from 44% at checkpoint-65536 to 51% at checkpoint-120832, with training reward improving from -2591.08 to -598.35, but training success remained zero and later unchanged training has not been shown to improve. Experiment 3's continuation was near-balanced with its parent and left the positive-extreme sector at zero, so proxy improvement and extra unchanged steps are not sufficient progress signals. Experiment 2's symmetric extreme-angle sampler was contradicted: overall success fell and the strong negative-mid sector degraded, but that single recipe does not establish that every one-sided coverage schedule fails. Across measured failures, many policies entered tolerance or accumulated partial hold time without completing the uninterrupted 100-step hold, making reward credit assignment a plausible but unproven explanation for the hold gap. Experiment 4 changed only reward credit assignment and raised pooled complete success from 51.5% for working to 55.0% at checkpoint-115712 and 56.0% at checkpoint-120832, with pooled mean maximum held duration about 56.0 and 57.5 steps versus about 53.0 for working. Its interruption averages increased, positive-mid success improved while negative-mid performance traded off, and positive-extreme success remained 0/65 for all compared policies. These observations support useful policy progress and a reward-direction signal, not causal decomposition or generalization to the official panel. Complete uninterrupted hold success is authoritative; proximity, partial hold time, and training reward are incomplete signals. The task-reference panel is reused and selection-contaminated, the experiment-2 through experiment-4 panels are development evidence, and the unchanged 14-value observation and 2-value action semantics make transfer between these recipes meaningful.

**Open questions:** It is unknown whether the experiment-4 gain is durable beyond these two panels or reflects a tradeoff among angular sectors, and whether complete hold success is limited mainly by late-hold credit, physical control, observation representation, or training dynamics. It remains unresolved whether the persistent positive-extreme failures reflect insufficient training coverage, a distinct reach/control limitation, or a broader inability to sustain tolerance, and whether correcting that sector trades away established negative-mid behavior. No development measurement establishes readiness for terminal assessment.

## f3356be0-50ce-45d0-a495-b909a50b054c / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh baseline produced a usable but substantially sub-target policy. `checkpoint-120832` is the best measured lineage candidate, with 51% success on the fresh research panel and 50.5% on the fixed task-reference panel.

**Observed behavior:** Training reward improved from -2591.08 at checkpoint-5120 to -598.35 at checkpoint-120832, while training success was 0 at every recorded checkpoint. On the shared 100-episode research panel, `checkpoint-65536`, `checkpoint-110592`, and `checkpoint-120832` achieved 44%, 45%, and 51% success respectively. Paired comparisons favored `checkpoint-120832` over `checkpoint-110592` by 7 wins to 1 and over `checkpoint-65536` by 9 wins to 2, among discordant episodes. The terminal policy achieved 50.5% on 200 episodes of the fixed task-reference panel. Within that panel it succeeded on 76/110 negative-angle targets and 25/90 nonnegative-angle targets; it succeeded on 4/74 targets with absolute angle at least 120 degrees. Successful episodes averaged about 117 control steps, while failures generally ran to the 500-step limit.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline hypothesis, to establish an initial baseline for the human-defined objective, is supported as a characterization of learned behavior but not as evidence that the objective has been met. The improving training proxy translated into partial measured task success, so proxy-only improvement is weakened as the complete explanation, but the persistent zero training-success metric and roughly 50% measured success show that the translation is incomplete. The paired and reference measurements support selecting the terminal checkpoint over the two measured alternatives, while the reused reference panel is not independent confirmation and no causal claim about reward or angular coverage is warranted.

**Interpretation:** `checkpoint-120832` is the strongest available saved policy for the next experiment because it has the highest comparable fresh-panel success and a consistent, though modest, paired advantage. Its angular asymmetry and large failure rate indicate that the baseline recipe has learned partial task behavior rather than a policy ready for the 98% objective. The complete baseline recipe should be kept with this lineage so a subsequent experiment can deliberately address the observed residual failures.

## f3356be0-50ce-45d0-a495-b909a50b054c / Experiment 2

**Result:** The symmetric extreme-angle coverage intervention did not improve the learned reach-and-hold policy. The unchanged `working` lineage remains the best available measured policy, and the adapted recipe should not be carried forward.

**Observed behavior:** Experiment 2 recorded training success of 0.02 from checkpoint-90112 onward. Its best late training reward was -917.31 at checkpoint-115712, compared with -969.58 at checkpoint-120832. On the same 200-episode research panel, checkpoint-115712 achieved 38.5% success and checkpoint-120832 achieved 35.5%, while `working` achieved 52.5%. Paired comparisons favored `working` over checkpoint-115712 by 37 to 9 discordant episodes and over checkpoint-120832 by 40 to 6. By angle sector, checkpoint-115712 achieved 3/34 in [-180, -120), 40/61 in [-120, 0), 34/67 in [0, 120), and 0/38 in [120, 180]; checkpoint-120832 achieved 1/34, 36/61, 34/67, and 0/38; `working` achieved 6/34, 58/61, 41/67, and 0/38. Some episodes entered tolerance or accumulated partial hold time, but that did not establish the required uninterrupted 100-step hold.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted under the tested transferred policy, symmetric sampling intervention, and training budget. The expected hard-sector gains were absent, while the strong [-120, 0) sector degraded and overall success fell. This weakens target coverage as an adequate explanation for the observed angular failures in this setting, but does not establish that every alternative coverage schedule would fail or identify the causal contribution of control, reward, or representation.

**Interpretation:** The measured task behavior supports reverting to the unchanged baseline recipe and retaining its existing best-known designation. The adapted checkpoints are not useful lineage candidates despite their nonzero training proxy and partial proximity signals. The selected policy remains far below the 98% objective, so this closure should allow ordinary further development rather than request the official benchmark.

## f3356be0-50ce-45d0-a495-b909a50b054c / Experiment 3

**Result:** Unchanged continuation did not establish improvement over the working parent. The existing `working` and `best_known` lineage remains the appropriate selection, and the experiment is closed for ordinary further development rather than terminal assessment.

**Observed behavior:** The training log's proxy success rose from 0 at 5,120 steps to 0.29 at checkpoint-105472, briefly reached 0.30 between saved checkpoints, and declined to 0.27 at checkpoint-120832. The checkpoint reward proxy was -375.80 at 105472 and -430.61 at 120832. On the same disjoint 200-episode research panel, checkpoint-105472 achieved 98/200 (49.0%), checkpoint-120832 achieved 96/200 (48.0%), and `working` achieved 97/200 (48.5%). Paired comparisons were close: 16-15 discordant wins for checkpoint-105472 versus `working`, and 19-20 for checkpoint-120832 versus `working`. The 105472 and 120832 sector results were 2/37, 48/62, 48/62, 0/39 and 2/37, 45/62, 49/62, 0/39 across [-180, -120), [-120, 0), [0, 120), and [120, 180), while `working` achieved 2/37, 61/62, 34/62, 0/39. Mean maximum held steps were 50.3, 50.0, and 50.0, respectively. The continuation policies reached tolerance in some failures and had fewer recorded hold interruptions, but neither converted that partial behavior into a higher complete-hold rate.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Inconclusive with respect to a small continuation benefit, but the proposed improvement was not established. The proxy-peak checkpoint was only 0.5 percentage points above the parent and its paired result was near-balanced; the continuation endpoint was 0.5 points below the parent and also near-balanced. These measurements do not support a durable gain or show that the late proxy peak is a reliable selection signal. They also do not prove that all further unchanged training would fail, because only this continuation, its saved checkpoints, and this panel were examined.

**Interpretation:** Measured complete reach-and-hold behavior supports retaining the unchanged working lineage, not promoting either continuation checkpoint. The continuation preserved partial learned behavior and slightly shifted the balance between negative-mid and positive-mid sectors, but it left the positive-extreme sector at zero and did not improve aggregate or hold-duration evidence. Training proxies were useful for selecting measurement points but were not sufficient to select a better policy. At roughly 48-49% on a development panel, the selected policy is far below the 98% objective; requesting the official benchmark would not serve the current scientific question.

## f3356be0-50ce-45d0-a495-b909a50b054c / Experiment 4

**Result:** The reward-credit intervention produced a modest, repeatable development improvement but did not approach the 98% objective. `checkpoint-120832` is the strongest current candidate, with 56.0% pooled success over two disjoint 200-episode panels.

**Observed behavior:** Training completed 120,832 steps against a 120,000-step request. The training log's success proxy rose from 0 at 5,120 steps to 0.12 at checkpoints 115,712 and 120,832; episode reward was -484.379 at 115,712 and -504.957 at 120,832, with the best logged late reward at 115,712. On episodes 400000-400199, checkpoint-115712, checkpoint-120832, and `working` achieved 121/200, 120/200, and 108/200 success. On the disjoint episodes 500000-500199, they achieved 99/200, 104/200, and 98/200. The pooled rates were therefore 55.0%, 56.0%, and 51.5%. Paired outcomes favored checkpoint-115712 over `working` 62-35 and checkpoint-120832 over `working` 72-42 across the two panels; the two intervention checkpoints were near-balanced at 42-45. Mean maximum held steps were approximately 56.0, 57.5, and 53.0 in the same pooled order. The modified policies improved the positive-mid sector on these panels but traded off some negative-mid performance; all three policies achieved 0/65 in the positive-extreme sector. Modified policies also recorded more hold interruptions on average, so the expected interruption reduction was not observed.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported under the tested transferred policy, reward intervention, training budget, evaluator, and two disjoint development panels. The predicted improvement in complete uninterrupted success and longer maximum holds was observed relative to the contemporaneous working control. The predicted reduction in interruptions was not observed, the endpoint was only marginally better than 115712, and the positive-extreme failure mode persisted. Because this is one transferred training run with a changed reward recipe, the result supports the intervention as a useful direction but does not isolate which reward term caused the gain or establish that it generalizes to the official panel.

**Interpretation:** The measured complete-hold behavior supports selecting `checkpoint-120832` for the working and best-known lineages and keeping the modified reward recipe. Its advantage is sufficiently consistent across independent panels to preserve for ordinary further development, while the unchanged baseline should remain available as a measured control. The candidate remains far below 196/200 official success, so this closure does not request terminal assessment.
