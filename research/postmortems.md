# Research postmortems

## e9b968c4-3137-4bd2-ac09-05422a64bcc2 / Scientific strategy

**Direction:** Improve the highest-priority measured gap of `checkpoint-100352`: failures on lower-left targets, concentrated at target angles from about -122 to -145 degrees. The next causal question is whether an angularly asymmetric reach/control or dynamics limitation, rather than radial training coverage, causes this persistent blind spot.

**Lessons and limits:** The exact experiment-2 intervention was transfer from `best_known` with training target-radius sampling expanded from 14-20 cm to 6-20 cm, while preserving full angular sampling and official evaluation. Both measured checkpoints reached 97.0% on the 200-episode panel, below the 98% objective. They retained the same six lower-left failure episodes as the baseline: three targets in the newly covered 6-14 cm band and three in the already covered 14-20 cm band. One newly covered-band failure briefly reached tolerance, but held for only one step and still failed. Thus the intervention established that this radial expansion did not remove the measured gap under the tested transfer run and budget; it did not establish a general causal effect for radial coverage. The broader mechanism remains open: persistence weakens radial coverage as the sole explanation, but does not distinguish angular asymmetry, control or dynamics limits, representation, or stochastic training effects. Training proxy values as high as 0.99 are not used to override the measured policy behavior.

**Open questions:** Whether the lower-left failures are caused by angular coverage or representation, a control/dynamics limitation, or residual radial-support effects under another training design remains unresolved. The additional positive-angle hold-stability behavior remains secondary because the radius intervention neither improved the primary reach gap nor made that finding the dominant measured deficit.

**Conditional next steps:** Continue from the measured 97% incumbent and test an angular/control or dynamics intervention targeted at the -122 to -145 degree lower-left gap, while evaluating the unchanged official task. If those failures improve, continue that direction; if they persist, investigate control/dynamics limits or representation rather than treating the training proxy as evidence of official success. Make positive-angle hold stability primary only if it becomes the largest measured gap. Further development is higher value than terminal assessment while the measured policy remains below 98%.

**Interpretation:** Select `checkpoint-100352` as both working and best-known because it has the strongest compatible measured outcome. The measured lower-left reach gap is the primary next scientific problem; the isolated late positive-angle hold failure remains open and secondary.

## e9b968c4-3137-4bd2-ac09-05422a64bcc2 / Experiment 2

**Result:** Expanding training target-radius sampling from 14-20 cm to the official 6-20 cm range did not improve measured official-task behavior: both measured checkpoints achieved 97.0%, below the 98% objective.

**Observed behavior:** The 100,352-step and 120,832-step experiment-2 policies failed on the same six deterministic panel episodes (11, 25, 111, 167, 171, and 188). Their targets were at radii 12.99, 6.00, 8.37, 15.09, 16.63, and 16.07 cm, respectively, all at angles from -122.1 to -145.4 degrees. Four never reached tolerance; episode 111 reached tolerance for one step in experiment 2 but still failed, and episode 188 held for only two steps. The training proxy reached 0.99 at several checkpoints, including 100,352 steps, while measured success remained 97.0%.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The proposal expected fewer lower-left failures, especially in the newly covered 6-14 cm band, with a possible increase toward or above 98%. That expectation was contradicted: failures persisted in both the newly covered and previously covered radial bands, with no overall success improvement. The exact intervention therefore failed to remove the gap under this transfer run and budget. The broader causal mechanism remains unresolved: this result weakens radial coverage as the sole explanation but does not rule out other radial training designs or distinguish angular asymmetry, control/dynamics limits, representation, and stochastic effects.

**Interpretation:** Keep `checkpoint-100352` as the best-known lineage because the experiment-2 candidate ties its 97.0% single-panel result but has no compatible evidence that warrants replacing the incumbent’s two-panel designation. Revert to the incumbent’s complete scientific recipe and use the measured lower-left behavior to direct the next experiment toward angular/control or dynamics asymmetry. The training proxy is not evidence against that measured direction.
