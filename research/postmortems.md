# Research postmortems

## 9f0c1a9e-23c2-4c48-85a6-09cb6d902999 / Scientific strategy

**Direction:** Treat full-radius training as a promising but incomplete coverage intervention, and prioritize the residual negative-angle failures and checkpoint variability. The next useful investigation should test whether angular/geometry-focused coverage can reduce those failures while preserving the high-radius behavior that the full-radius run traded away.

**Lessons and limits:** Experiment 2's full-radius transfer produced 197/200 (98.5%) on the fixed task-reference panel at checkpoints 100352 and 120832, versus 196/200 (98.0%) for the selected baseline. At checkpoint 100352, the sub-14 cm result improved from 101/105 to 103/105, but the above-14 cm result fell from 95/95 to 94/95: two baseline small-radius failures were repaired, while a new 18.24 cm, -154.8 degree failure appeared. The 105472 checkpoint scored 98.0% and restored one small-radius failure, so the improvement was not monotonic. On the distinct 200-episode research panel, the challenger and baseline both scored 98.5% with zero discordant success outcomes in the paired comparison. These observations partially support the prediction that broader radius coverage can help some small-radius cases, but they do not establish a robust task-progress gain or prove coverage caused it. The repeated task-reference panel is development evidence, not independent confirmation; the distinct-panel comparison used one seed and tied on episode success. Training proxy success reached 0.99 at checkpoint 100352, peaked at 1.00 at 105472, and ended at 0.94, while measured task success was 98.5%, 98.0%, and 98.5% respectively; proxy and reward remain diagnostic rather than evidence of task progress. The persistent failures near 9.4-9.9 cm and -123 to -128 degrees, plus the newly observed high-radius negative-angle failure, leave angular geometry, policy variability, and optimization effects unresolved.

**Open questions:** Can targeted negative-angle or geometry coverage reduce the recurring 6-14 cm failures without introducing high-radius regressions? Is the checkpoint-to-checkpoint variation in which episodes fail ordinary PPO variability, policy degradation, or a representation/control limitation? Does a candidate exceed 98% across more than one distinct evaluation panel, and does the selected policy meet the official benchmark?

**Conditional next steps:** Continue from experiment 2 checkpoint-100352, retaining the full-radius recipe because it is the strongest measured candidate and repaired part of the observed small-radius deficit. If a future angular/geometry-focused intervention improves the recurring negative-angle cases on a distinct panel without reducing above-14 cm success, it becomes the preferred route; if it only swaps failures between sectors or remains tied with the baseline, deprioritize coverage-only changes and investigate representation and PPO variability. Do not request the official benchmark until evidence is more robust than the current threshold-level development measurements.

## 9f0c1a9e-23c2-4c48-85a6-09cb6d902999 / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh automatic baseline produced a viable policy and reached 98% on the task-reference panel at checkpoint-100352, but its final checkpoint measured 97%. The experiment is closed with checkpoint-100352 selected as working and best-known; no official benchmark is requested yet.

**Observed behavior:** Training completed 120,832 steps. The training log's proxy success increased from 0.06 at 75,776 steps to 0.97 at 97,280-100,352 steps, then stayed between 0.93 and 0.96 through the final checkpoint; mean training reward peaked at 135 around 80,896 and was 117 at 100,352 and 112 at 120,832. On the fixed 200-episode task-reference panel (seed 7300), checkpoint-100352 succeeded on 196 episodes (98.0%) and checkpoint-120832 on 194 (97.0%). The earlier checkpoint had four failures, at episode seeds 7300, 7310, 7384, and 7402; the final checkpoint failed on those plus seeds 7421 and 7452. The recurring failures were near 6.7-9.9 cm and approximately -116 to -128 degrees, with the final checkpoint's additional failures at approximately -132 degrees and 169 degrees.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline hypothesis was only to establish an initial baseline, and that objective was met: the recipe learned a policy with 98% measured task-reference success. The measurement does not show robust attainment of the human objective because the final checkpoint scored 97%, the best score is exactly at the threshold, and both measurements use the same development panel. Since this was not an intervention, the result does not support a causal claim about PPO settings, reward terms, or the training-range mismatch.

**Interpretation:** The selected checkpoint is preferable to the final checkpoint on measured task behavior, despite the final checkpoint's still-high training proxy. The baseline makes the current method a useful starting point for targeted improvement, while the persistent failure cluster and the 14-20 cm training range motivate coverage-focused investigation. The evidence does not distinguish coverage mismatch from policy optimization variability or another representation issue.

## 9f0c1a9e-23c2-4c48-85a6-09cb6d902999 / Experiment 2

**Result:** Partially supported. Full-radius transfer reached 98.5% on the task-reference panel at checkpoints 100352 and 120832, one episode above the 98.0% baseline, but the gain was accompanied by a high-radius failure tradeoff and did not improve paired success on the distinct research panel. Checkpoint-100352 is selected as working and best-known; the experiment-2 recipe is kept and no official benchmark is requested.

**Observed behavior:** The intervention trained target radii over 6-20 cm instead of 14-20 cm while preserving the task, observations, actions, reward, and hold semantics. On the fixed 200-episode task-reference panel, the experiment-2 checkpoints scored 98.5% (100352), 98.0% (105472), and 98.5% (120832). At 100352, sub-14 cm success was 103/105 versus 101/105 for the experiment-1 selected checkpoint, while above-14 cm success was 94/95 versus 95/95. The baseline failures at seeds 7300 and 7310 were no longer failures, the failures at 7384 and 7402 persisted, and seed 7475 (18.24 cm, -154.8 degrees) newly failed. The distinct research panel measured both the challenger and `working` at 98.5%, with zero discordant episode outcomes and a paired success delta of 0.0 percentage points. Training proxy success was 0.99 at 100352, 1.00 at 105472, and 0.94 at 120832; these are training facts, not task-evaluation results.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported under the tested panels and checkpoints. The expected increase above 98% and reduction in some sub-14 cm failures occurred at the selected checkpoint, but the no-regression condition was not met because above-14 cm success declined by one episode. The paired distinct-panel comparison found no task-success advantage, and the reused task-reference panel is not independent confirmation. The result supports broader radius coverage as a plausible contributor to particular small-radius failures, not as a demonstrated sole cause or robust policy improvement.

**Interpretation:** Full-radius exposure appears useful enough to retain, but the unchanged total of three failures on the distinct panel and the shifted failure set on the reference panel indicate that coverage alone did not resolve the residual deficit. The recurring failures around 9.4-9.9 cm and -123 to -128 degrees remain consistent with an angular geometry or control issue, while the different outcomes across checkpoints leave PPO variability and degradation as viable alternatives. No causal attribution beyond the tested transfer run is warranted.
