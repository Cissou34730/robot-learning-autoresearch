# Research postmortems

## 9f0c1a9e-23c2-4c48-85a6-09cb6d902999 / Scientific strategy

**Direction:** Retain the experiment-2 full-radius working lineage as the strongest measured recipe. Unchanged continuation did not improve it, and experiment 5's explicit inverse-kinematic joint-limit margins produced a severe task-performance regression rather than resolving the geometry cluster. Negative-angle oversampling remains unvalidated. The next useful investigation should use measured task behavior to distinguish angular coverage from action/control or observation-contract issues while preserving the official task distribution.

**Lessons and limits:** Experiment 2's full-radius transfer produced 197/200 (98.5%) on the fixed task-reference panel at checkpoints 100352 and 120832, versus 196/200 (98.0%) for the selected baseline. At checkpoint 100352, sub-14 cm success improved from 101/105 to 103/105, while above-14 cm success fell from 95/95 to 94/95; the distinct 200-episode research panel tied the baseline at 98.5% with zero discordant outcomes. This partially supports broader radius coverage for particular small-radius cases, but not a robust gain or a causal claim. Experiment 3 trained the proposed negative-angle bias for 120,832 steps and produced 24 checkpoints, but none has a task-reference or research-evaluation artifact. Its training proxy varied from 0.974 at 5,120 steps to 0.85 at 41,984, recovered to 0.99 at 105,472, and ended at 0.94; mean training reward peaked at 121.9 around 60,416 steps and ended at 99.9. These are training diagnostics only: they neither show that negative-angle failures improved nor establish regressions on positive or high-radius targets. The experiment-3 hypothesis is therefore inconclusive, and the absence of task measurements prevents causal attribution to angular sampling. Experiment 4 measured three continued checkpoints on the same development panels: task-reference success was 98.5% (20480), 95.0% (95232), and 97.5% (120832), while research-evaluation success was 98.0%, 97.0%, and 96.5%. The best continuation therefore tied, rather than exceeded, the parent's 98.5% task-reference result; later checkpoints added failures across small, large, negative-angle, and positive-angle geometries, including a high-radius failure at 120832. The training proxy reached 1.0 near 20,480 steps and ended at 0.96, so the proxy did not track the later task regressions. These continuation results weaken the insufficient-training explanation under this trajectory, but one continuation from one parent and one seed cannot prove a general PPO plateau or identify a causal representation/control mechanism. Experiment 5 trained the 13-value observation from scratch with open- and folded-branch joint-limit margins. On the fixed task-reference panel, both measured checkpoints achieved only 23/200 (11.5%), versus 197/200 (98.5%) for working; on the research panel they achieved 22/200 (11.0%) and 19/200 (9.5%). Paired comparisons showed no candidate wins: the 100352 task-reference comparison had 174 discordant episodes, all losses, and the research comparison had 175; the 120832 comparison had 174 task-reference losses and 178 research-panel losses. The degradation was broad rather than confined to the proposed cluster: at 100352 the candidate succeeded on 21/110 negative-angle and 2/90 positive-angle targets, 20/105 sub-14 cm and 3/95 at-or-above-14 cm targets, compared with working's 107/110, 90/90, 103/105, and 94/95. The training log reported proxy success 0 throughout while mean reward rose from -13.9 at 10,240 steps to 14.1 at 120,832, so the reward trajectory did not establish task progress. These measurements strongly contradict the expected improvement under this fresh run and justify abandoning this intervention; they do not by themselves prove that joint-limit information can never help, because fresh initialization and one seed confound component-level causality. The repeated development panels remain non-independent, and no official benchmark result is available.

**Open questions:** Does the experiment-3 angular bias improve the recurring negative-angle cases, or merely alter training dynamics without improving official-task success? Was experiment 5's collapse caused by the added feature semantics or scaling/normalization interaction, fresh-run variance, or a broader learning/control limitation? Can a targeted action/control or angular-coverage change reduce the recurring failures near 9-10 cm and about -123 to -128 degrees without creating high-radius regressions? How much of checkpoint variation persists under another seed or distinct panel, and can any candidate exceed 98% across more than one development panel before official assessment?

**Conditional next steps:** Restore the experiment-2 working lineage and its complete recipe; this is a measured practical choice, not proof that every fresh augmented run would fail. For a later experiment, do not reuse the joint-limit-margin intervention unchanged. Prefer a measured angular-coverage or action/control investigation, and if another observation change is attempted, first make its feature-scale/normalization behavior an explicit diagnostic rather than inferring progress from reward. Retain a direction only if it improves the recurring negative-angle cases without positive- or high-radius tradeoffs on a distinct panel; if it produces another broad regression or only changes training diagnostics, deprioritize it. Do not request the official benchmark until development evidence is stronger than the current threshold-level result.

## 9f0c1a9e-23c2-4c48-85a6-09cb6d902999 / Experiment 5

**Result:** Contradicted under the tested fresh run. Adding two branch-level joint-limit margins did not reduce the recurring failures or preserve task performance; both measured checkpoints collapsed to 11.5% on the task-reference panel and 11.0%/9.5% on the research panel. The experiment is closed by restoring the experiment-2 working lineage, with no official benchmark requested.

**Observed behavior:** The intervention changed the observation from 11 to 13 values while preserving the task, action mapping, reward, and hold semantics. Training completed 120,832 steps. The training log reported success rate 0 at every logged step; mean reward increased from -13.89 at 10,240 steps to 14.14 at 120,832. On the fixed 200-episode task-reference panel, checkpoint-100352 and checkpoint-120832 each succeeded on 23 episodes (11.5%), while the working comparator succeeded on 197 (98.5%). On the research panel, the same checkpoints succeeded on 22 (11.0%) and 19 (9.5%), versus 197 for working. The task-reference paired comparisons had zero candidate gains and 174 candidate losses at each checkpoint. At 100352, candidate success was 21/110 for negative-angle targets, 2/90 for positive-angle targets, 20/105 below 14 cm, and 3/95 at or above 14 cm; working achieved 107/110, 90/90, 103/105, and 94/95 in those groups. The unmeasured checkpoints remain unmeasured.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted under the tested training seed and development panels. The proposal expected fewer negative-angle/joint-limit failures with no high-radius or positive-angle regression; instead, the candidate lost nearly all successes across every reported geometry group, and the research result declined between the measured checkpoints. This is measured task-performance evidence against the intervention as a useful direct representation change in this run. The zero training proxy alongside rising reward is an unexpected discrepancy and does not rescue the policy or identify the cause. Because the policy was trained from scratch with one seed, the result does not prove that joint-limit margins are intrinsically harmful or separate feature semantics from optimization and initialization effects.

**Interpretation:** The broad collapse, zero paired gains, and poor performance on both independent evaluator code and the protected task-reference evaluator make another measurement of the same candidates unnecessary for the lineage decision. The added margins should be deprioritized in their current form. Restoring working is based on its 98.5% measured task success, not on a claim that the experiment-5 code was mechanically invalid or that another representation/control design cannot help. A future representation study should explicitly investigate observation scaling/normalization and use task measurements; otherwise action/control or angular coverage is the more useful next direction.
