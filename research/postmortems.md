# Research postmortems

## 8b90fea8-b848-4455-9c37-b4b50e0123a4 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The fresh PPO baseline learned substantial but incomplete reach-and-hold behavior. Experiment 2's transferred `checkpoint-100352` improved to 88.0% on the research panel and 89.5% on the independent task-reference panel, but remains below the 98% objective. Its task-reference success improved over experiment 1 on far targets (65.6% to 78.1% for 18-20 cm) and positive-angle targets (45.9% to 67.6% for 0-90 degrees). The later experiment-2 checkpoint reached higher training proxies but fell to 75.5% and 78.0% on the two development panels, so task success from saved policies remains the selection signal.

**Lessons and limits:** Measured task success, rather than training success or episode reward, is the relevant progress signal. The focused sampler is consistent with useful early progress on the targeted regions and higher full-distribution success, but this is one transferred run and does not isolate angle exposure from radius exposure or establish causality. In experiment 2, all failures in the recorded research and task-reference artifacts truncated rather than terminated; those artifacts do not separately identify reach and hold failures, so preservation of hold behavior is not directly established. The within-run comparison favored `checkpoint-100352` over `checkpoint-120832` (29 wins to 4, 33 discordant episodes, success delta -12.5 percentage points for the later checkpoint), despite the later checkpoint's training proxy rising from 0.30 to 0.41.

**Open questions:** How to improve the remaining failures without the late-training degradation remains unresolved. The separate contributions of angle versus radius exposure, the cause of the later checkpoint collapse, and the balance between reach and hold failures require further investigation. The current best development policy is still below the official objective and has not received the final benchmark.

## 8b90fea8-b848-4455-9c37-b4b50e0123a4 / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh baseline produced a useful but insufficient policy. `checkpoint-100352` was the strongest measured candidate at 81.5% research-panel success and 83.0% task-reference success, well below the 98% objective; it is selected as both working and best-known lineage for continued development.

**Observed behavior:** The three research evaluations returned 81.5% at 100352 steps, 77.0% at 115712 steps, and 77.5% at 120832 steps. The corresponding task-reference results were 83.0%, 82.5%, and 77.5%. The inventory reports training-success proxy values of 0.64, 0.77, and 0.71 for these checkpoints, while episode reward peaked at 107.18 at the unmeasured 95232-step checkpoint and was 107.14, 104.99, and 100.91 at the measured checkpoints. In the 100352 research evaluation, 31 of 37 failures never reached the target and 6 reached it but held for fewer than 100 steps; the corresponding splits were 36/46 and 10/46 at 115712, and 37/45 and 8/45 at 120832. On the 100352 panel, success was 48.4% for radii 18-22 cm versus 100.0% for radii 6-10 cm, and 51.0% for angles 0 to 90 degrees versus 100.0% for angles -180 to -90 degrees.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline established measurable learned behavior and a reproducible development reference, but no measured checkpoint satisfies the human objective. The observed divergence between training proxies and task success weakens using those proxies alone to select a policy; it does not by itself identify a causal mechanism or disprove further improvement.

**Interpretation:** The strongest available policy makes substantial progress toward the task but primarily remains limited by target reach coverage, especially for farther and positive-angle targets. The protected task-reference trend corroborates the research-panel ordering of the measured checkpoints, while the common late decline is evidence about these saved policies, not proof that additional training caused degradation. Further training is warranted as a new experiment, with task success as the selection evidence.

## 8b90fea8-b848-4455-9c37-b4b50e0123a4 / Experiment 2

**Result:** The focused target-exposure intervention produced a useful but insufficient policy at `checkpoint-100352`: 88.0% research-panel success and 89.5% task-reference success. The later measured checkpoint was worse at 75.5% and 78.0%, so `checkpoint-100352` is selected as both working and best-known for continued development.

**Observed behavior:** The experiment produced 24 checkpoints, of which 22 remain unmeasured. At 100352 steps, the inventory reports episode reward 48.09 and training success 0.30; at 120832 steps, these proxies rose to 81.18 and 0.41. Saved-policy task success moved in the opposite direction: 88.0% to 75.5% on the research panel and 89.5% to 78.0% on the task-reference panel. On the common task-reference panel, the 100352-step challenger improved over experiment 1's 100352-step policy from 83.0% to 89.5%, including 65.6% to 78.1% for 18-20 cm targets and 45.9% to 67.6% for 0-90 degree targets. At 120832 steps, the corresponding target-reference rates were 84.4% for 18-20 cm and 13.5% for 0-90 degrees. The research and task-reference artifacts record every failure as truncated, with no separate reach-versus-hold diagnosis.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The early saved policy showed higher full-distribution success and higher success in the targeted far and positive-angle regions, consistent with the proposal that targeted exposure can reduce the observed deficit while retaining broad coverage. The intervention did not reach the 98% objective, and the later checkpoint degraded on both panels despite higher training proxies; the within-experiment paired comparison favored 100352 over 120832 (29 wins to 4, 33 discordant episodes, -12.5 percentage points for the later checkpoint). Because this was a single transfer run and the measurements do not separate reach from hold failures, the result supports usefulness of the recipe at an earlier checkpoint but does not establish the mechanism or hold preservation.

**Interpretation:** The intervention appears practically useful for selecting an improved intermediate policy, especially on the previously weak far and positive-angle regions. The late decline indicates that additional training under this recipe is not reliably beneficial and that proxy improvement is not a safe checkpoint-selection rule. The code is retained because the best measured artifact is better than the prior working policy on both development panels, while further training or refinement remains a separate experiment.
