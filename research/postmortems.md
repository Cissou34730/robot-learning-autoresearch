# Research postmortems

## 052b98fa-ee96-41c6-ae7e-1c2ad99cfc0a / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 98% success on the official two-joint-arm reach-and-hold task. The unchanged PPO recipe learned substantial task behavior in experiment 1 and reached 97.5% (195/200) on the development task-reference panel at checkpoint-105472, but its later checkpoints fell to 95.5% and 92.0% while training reward and proxy success continued to rise. Experiment 2 transferred from that measured peak and reduced the PPO learning rate to 0.0001; its four measured checkpoints reached 94.0%, 96.0%, 95.0%, and 96.5%, so the intervention did not preserve the parent peak. Checkpoint-105472 remains the strongest measured policy evidence, close to but below the objective, and the campaign has not established terminal readiness. The evidence therefore separates a demonstrated near-objective capability from an unresolved question about reproducibility and late-training robustness.

**Lessons and limits:** Training reward and proxy success are useful learning-dynamics signals but did not predict the ordering of the measured late checkpoints. In experiment 2, the lower-rate run reached 0.99 training success and mean reward 152.588 at checkpoint-90112, yet measured 94.0%; its later proxy values remained high while measured success stayed below the parent peak. The 120832 lower-rate checkpoint reached 96.5%, above the original run's 92.0% at the same position, but this is a mixed signal from one transfer run rather than causal evidence that the learning-rate change caused the recovery. All experiment-2 measured failures truncated at 500 steps. The shared development panel is distinct from the official panel, repeated use of it is not held-out confirmation, and the campaign has no independent replication of the fresh baseline or direct evidence isolating the mechanism of late degradation. The selected policy's observation construction, action mapping, tanh 64x64 policy and task semantics are a coherent learned reach-and-hold representation, but the transfer result does not show that changing update scale preserves its robustness. Sources: `research/scenario.md`, `research/brief.md`, `research/research_state.json`, `research/results.jsonl`, `research/postmortems.md`, `research/checkpoints/challengers/052b98fa-ee96-41c6-ae7e-1c2ad99cfc0a/experiment-2/inventory.json`, the experiment-1 and experiment-2 training logs, and the seven task-reference artifacts under `research/evaluations/052b98fa-ee96-41c6-ae7e-1c2ad99cfc0a/`.

**Open questions:** It remains unresolved whether the 97.5% peak is reproducible under a fresh run of the unchanged recipe or is substantially run-specific. It also remains unresolved whether late degradation reflects PPO update dynamics, reward or termination incentives, target-distribution differences, or another aspect of the scientific recipe; the lower-rate transfer neither preserved the parent policy nor isolated those alternatives. The parent policy's five residual failures and the relationship between development-panel performance and the official assessment remain uncharacterized.

## 052b98fa-ee96-41c6-ae7e-1c2ad99cfc0a / Experiment 1

**Result:** The baseline produced a useful learned policy, with checkpoint-105472 the best measured candidate at 97.5% task-reference success, but no measured candidate reached the 98% objective.

**Observed behavior:** Training proxies improved throughout the run: proxy success increased from 0.00 at 5,120 steps to 0.53 at 120,832 steps, while mean episode reward increased from -2014.4 to 24.4 and mean episode length fell from 500 to 343 steps. On the shared 200-episode task-reference panel, checkpoint-105472 succeeded on 195 episodes (97.5%), checkpoint-115712 on 191 (95.5%), and checkpoint-120832 on 184 (92.0%). The five failures of checkpoint-105472 were episodes 52, 83, 84, 100, and 102; all measured failures truncated at 500 steps. The later checkpoints lost more shared-panel successes than they recovered relative to checkpoint-105472.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline established that the unchanged recipe can learn high task performance and provided a policy close to, but below, the human objective. It did not establish objective attainment, and the higher late training proxies did not correspond to higher task-reference success. This conclusion is limited to the one fresh run and the fixed development panel; unmeasured checkpoints remain unmeasured.

**Interpretation:** Measured task behavior, rather than the training proxies, supports selecting checkpoint-105472 for continued development. The decline across later checkpoints makes checkpoint-120832 unsuitable as the working or best-known policy despite its strongest training-time metrics. The result indicates a proxy/evaluation mismatch or late-policy degradation, but does not identify a causal mechanism. No official benchmark is requested because the best measured policy remains below the objective on the development panel.

## 052b98fa-ee96-41c6-ae7e-1c2ad99cfc0a / Experiment 2

**Result:** The lower-learning-rate transfer did not improve or preserve the parent policy. The experiment-1 checkpoint-105472 policy remains the strongest measured candidate at 97.5% task-reference success, while the best experiment-2 checkpoint reached 96.5%.

**Observed behavior:** Experiment 2 transferred from checkpoint-105472 and changed only PPO learning rate from 0.0003 to 0.0001. The four measured checkpoints scored 94.0% (checkpoint-90112), 96.0% (checkpoint-105472), 95.0% (checkpoint-115712), and 96.5% (checkpoint-120832) on the same 200-episode task-reference panel. Their training proxies were high at the selected late points: checkpoint-90112 had 0.99 training success and 152.588 mean reward, checkpoint-115712 had 0.99 and 152.464, and checkpoint-120832 had 0.98 and 149.238. Every failure in these four artifacts truncated at 500 steps. The 120832 lower-rate policy did exceed the original run's 92.0% at the same checkpoint position, but it remained one percentage point below the parent peak; the measured failure sets also changed across checkpoints.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted for this tested transfer intervention. The proposal expected a lower-rate continuation to preserve or improve the parent's 97.5% task-reference success, whereas all four measured experiment-2 checkpoints fell below it, and the proxies again improved without corresponding task success. The 120832 improvement over the original late checkpoint is a partial signal compatible with reduced late degradation, but the single transfer run does not establish that update aggressiveness caused either result and does not rule out reward, termination, target-distribution, or run-variation explanations.

**Interpretation:** The intervention did not provide a sufficiently robust policy or a reliable route to the objective, so the parent lineage and its 0.0003 recipe should remain active. The development-panel result supports continued development rather than terminal assessment: the parent is close to, but below, 98%, and the panel is not the official 200-episode assessment.
