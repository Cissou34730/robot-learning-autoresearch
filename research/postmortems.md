## b62e8ccc-3c2e-4a3f-9477-2aa04cacd0d2 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The PPO baseline learned strong reach-and-hold behavior, but no measured policy is securely above the 98% objective. The full-radius continuation produced a useful intermediate `checkpoint-105472`: it achieved 191/200 on the new disjoint research panel versus 189/200 for its parent and 98.5% on the reused task-reference panel versus the parent's 96.0%. The prior `checkpoint-95232` remains best-known on pooled research evidence at 580/600 including the new control panel, while `checkpoint-105472` remains below the objective on independent development evidence.

**Lessons and limits:** Training proxies do not rank task behavior reliably: the experiment-2 checkpoint with 100% training success reached only 95.5% on the disjoint research panel, and the final checkpoint reached 92.0%. The intervention is partially supported as a way to improve the selected intermediate policy's transfer signal, but the 98.5% task-reference score is on a permanently reused panel and is not independent confirmation. Research and task-reference measurements are development evidence, not the official 200-episode verdict.

**Open questions:** Whether the apparent short-radius benefit of full-radius training generalizes beyond the measured panels, and what causes the remaining failures on the full official radius range, remain unresolved. The official objective has not been demonstrated.

This conclusion is limited to the single trained recipe and measured checkpoints.

**Interpretation:** `checkpoint-95232` is the best current working and best-known policy because it leads the independent-panel comparison and is slightly better than the final checkpoint on pooled research coverage. The unchanged scientific recipe should be preserved for lineage provenance. `checkpoint-120832` is worth retaining as a close alternative because it is competitive on research panels and stronger on the reused task-reference panel; further training remains an ordinary next experiment.

## b62e8ccc-3c2e-4a3f-9477-2aa04cacd0d2 / Experiment 2

**Result:** Full-radius target training produced a useful intermediate policy but did not establish the human objective or justify terminal assessment.

**Observed behavior:** `checkpoint-105472` achieved 191/200 (95.5%) on the new disjoint research panel, compared with 189/200 (94.5%) for `best_known`, and won both discordant paired episodes. On the reused task-reference panel it achieved 98.5% (197/200), compared with 96.0% (192/200) for the parent. The final `checkpoint-120832` degraded to 184/200 (92.0%) on the disjoint research panel and 96.5% on task-reference, losing its paired comparison with `checkpoint-105472` by 0-7.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The intervention improved the selected intermediate checkpoint relative to its matched parent on both the new disjoint panel and the fixed task-reference panel, consistent with a benefit from covering the short-radius training range. However, the independent result remained 2.5 percentage points below the 98% target, the task-reference panel is reused and cannot confirm the result independently, and continued training to the final checkpoint worsened measured behavior. These observations do not establish that target coverage alone solves the residual failures.

**Interpretation:** `checkpoint-105472` is the most useful working artifact from experiment 2 because it is the strongest measured experiment-2 checkpoint and improves on the matched new-panel control, while the prior `checkpoint-95232` remains best-known because its pooled research evidence is stronger. The changed recipe should be kept with the working lineage for possible future development; terminal assessment is not warranted from these development measurements.
