# Research postmortems

## 285f0407-8cc3-401d-bdcd-2af73de9a61c / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 98% success on the official 6–20 cm, full-angle reach-and-hold distribution. PPO learned strong behavior, but all development evidence is panel- and checkpoint-dependent: the best protected task-reference result is 98.5% from the experiment-2 working lineage, while experiment 4 reached 98.0% and retained the same recurring negative-angle failures. Across experiments, some residual cases never entered tolerance and others reached it only briefly; changing radius coverage, negative-angle exposure, or hold-exit forfeiture did not establish a robust improvement beyond the working lineage. These are development measurements, not the official assessment.

**Lessons and limits:** Measured task success, rather than training reward or the training success proxy, is the relevant progress signal (`research/evaluations/` and `research/results.jsonl`). The proxy and reward trajectory did not predict a monotonic task result: experiment 4's proxy was 1.00, 0.98, and 0.97 at steps 20,480, 100,352, and 120,832 while research success was 96.5%, 96.5%, and 98.0%. On the protected panel, experiment-2 checkpoint-100352 failed four negative-angle episodes; three of them had at most four in-tolerance steps and the fourth never entered tolerance (`research/evaluations/285f0407-8cc3-401d-bdcd-2af73de9a61c/task-reference-285f0407-8cc3-401d-bdcd-2af73de9a61c-experiment-2-checkpoint-100352-task-reference-v1.json`). Experiment 4's later checkpoints repeated those identities, so hold-exit shaping did not identify hold stability as the cause. Experiment 3's focused exposure changed some researcher-panel outcomes but left the protected residual cases. The current 11-value observation already contains target-relative Cartesian error and inverse-kinematic errors, but not an explicit base-frame target angle/radius encoding (`robot_learning/scenario/observations.py`); this makes representation and control plausible alternatives without proving either. Development panels are not the official assessment, and repeated episode identities are not new coverage.

**Open questions:** Whether the residual negative-angle failures are partly caused by the policy having to infer target geometry from relative and kinematic features, rather than by actuator/control or task geometry, remains unresolved. It is also unknown whether the experiment-3 research-panel improvement transfers beyond its panel and whether any development-panel result transfers to the official 200-episode assessment.

## 285f0407-8cc3-401d-bdcd-2af73de9a61c / Experiment 1

**Result:** The baseline produced a useful learned policy, with checkpoint-100352 selected as the working and best-known candidate for continued development; terminal assessment is deferred.

**Observed behavior:** Training success was 0 through step 70,656, rose to 0.93 at step 95,232 and 0.98 at step 99,328, then declined to 0.95 at the completed 120,832 steps; the recorded reward likewise peaked before the final checkpoint. On the 200-episode research panel, success was 95.5% at checkpoint-95232 and 97.0% at both checkpoint-100352 and checkpoint-120832. On the distinct 200-episode task-reference panel, the same checkpoints scored 96.0%, 98.0%, and 97.0%, respectively. The four failures for checkpoint-100352 all used target angles from -116.4 to -127.9 degrees and target radii from 6.7 to 9.9 cm; checkpoint-120832 retained those four failures and added failures at -132.4 degrees and 169.1 degrees. All failed episodes truncated at 500 steps.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline hypothesis to establish an initial learned policy is supported: learning produced high measured task performance and a useful candidate. Progress toward the human objective is only partial and panel-dependent, however; the best development result is 98.0% on one panel while the independent researcher panel is 97.0%, and neither is the official assessment. The training-proxy peak and later training did not provide evidence that the final checkpoint was better, so the result does not support treating continued unchanged training as an improvement.

**Interpretation:** The policy has learned the core reach-and-hold behavior, but the repeated negative-angle failures indicate a residual distributional weakness in the measured task behavior. The evidence identifies where the current candidate fails, not why; it does not establish a causal explanation or an official success rate.

## 285f0407-8cc3-401d-bdcd-2af73de9a61c / Experiment 2

**Result:** Full-radius training produced a useful candidate and a small improvement on the protected development panel, but did not resolve the residual failure pattern; terminal assessment remains deferred.

**Observed behavior:** Experiment 2 trained the transferred PPO policy on 6-20 cm targets for 120,832 completed steps. The training success proxy reached 1.00 at checkpoint-105472, then was 0.94 at checkpoint-120832; episode reward was highest among the measured late checkpoints at checkpoint-100352. On the same 200-episode research evaluation, checkpoints 100352, 105472, and 120832 each achieved 98.0% and failed on the same four episode identities, all truncated at 500 steps. On the protected task-reference-v1 panel, checkpoint-100352 achieved 98.5% (197/200), checkpoint-105472 achieved 98.0% (196/200), and checkpoint-120832 achieved 98.5% (197/200). For checkpoint-100352, the three failures were at 9.915 cm/-122.902 degrees, 9.355 cm/-127.912 degrees, and 18.240 cm/-154.787 degrees; the first two are in the previously observed near-base cluster and the third is a longer-radius negative-angle failure. Relative to experiment 1 checkpoint-100352 on this same protected panel, the 6.734 cm/-116.409 degrees failure became successful, while the 7.243 cm/-125.397 degrees failure became successful and a new 18.240 cm/-154.787 degrees failure appeared, reducing the failure count from four to three. The research-evaluation context changed between experiments, so its 98.0% result is interpreted within experiment 2 rather than as a cross-experiment score comparison. No raw training-log artifact was available beyond the recorded proxy and reward summaries.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis is **partially supported**. The protected panel improved from 98.0% to 98.5% for the selected checkpoint, and two short-radius failures from the prior selected policy were corrected, consistent with broader near-base exposure being useful. It is not fully supported because two near-base failures remained, a new long-radius negative-angle failure appeared, and the three experiment-2 research measurements were behaviorally unchanged. These measurements show a modest task-performance improvement under the protected panel, but do not establish that radius coverage alone caused it or that the policy meets the official objective.

**Interpretation:** Full-radius training is a reasonable recipe to retain, since it preserves strong reach-and-hold behavior and gives the best observed protected-panel result in this experiment. The unchanged research-panel failure identities and the persistent negative-angle concentration indicate that radius coverage is not a complete solution; angular/control or hold-stability limitations remain plausible. The equivalent protected-panel outcomes of checkpoints 100352 and 120832 make the earlier checkpoint preferable as working policy because it reaches the same measured task performance before the later proxy decline, while the final checkpoint remains useful as a distinct late-training control. Development evidence is not the official 200-episode assessment.

**Result:** Focused negative-angle exposure produced a useful intermediate challenger but not a robust improvement over the current working policy. Close the experiment with the experiment-2 working lineage and retain checkpoint-35840 as an alternative; terminal assessment remains deferred.

**Observed behavior:** Experiment 3 transferred the experiment-2 working policy and completed 120,832 steps with a 50% mixture focused on -165 to -90 degrees and a 50% full-angle branch over the unchanged 6-20 cm radius range. The training proxy was high early, declined through much of training, and was 0.89 at the final checkpoint; the final recorded episode reward was 104.53. On the experiment-3 200-episode research panel, the parent scored 96.5%, checkpoints 5,120 and 30,720 scored 97.0%, checkpoint-10,240 scored 96.5%, checkpoint-35,840 scored 97.5%, and checkpoint-120,832 scored 96.5%. The best intermediate checkpoint removed two parent-panel failures, but five failures remained at approximately 7.1 cm/-122.0 degrees, 12.4 cm/-135.9 degrees, 19.0 cm/-148.7 degrees, 7.6 cm/-123.5 degrees, and 13.8 cm/-139.6 degrees. On the distinct task-reference-v1 panel, the parent and checkpoints 5,120, 10,240, 30,720, and 35,840 all scored 98.5% (197/200); checkpoint-35,840 retained the same failures as the parent at 9.9 cm/-122.9 degrees, 9.4 cm/-127.9 degrees, and 18.2 cm/-154.8 degrees. The completed-budget checkpoint scored 97.0% (194/200), adding failures at approximately 19.5 cm/-175.4 degrees, 19.0 cm/175.7 degrees, and 18.5 cm/130.9 degrees. All listed failures truncated at 500 steps.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis is **partially supported** under the experiment-3 research evaluation: the 35,840-step checkpoint improved from 96.5% to 97.5% and removed two failures without reducing its task-reference result. It is not supported as a robust explanation of the protected residual behavior because the same three task-reference failures persisted at every measured focused-training checkpoint, and the completed-budget policy degraded to 97.0% with additional nonnegative-angle failures. The evidence is checkpoint- and panel-specific and does not establish that angular exposure caused the research-panel change; control or hold stability remains a plausible alternative.

**Interpretation:** Focused exposure may alter some behavior in the researcher-owned panel, but it did not resolve the recurring protected negative-angle cases or establish progress beyond the existing 98.5% task-reference result. Checkpoint-35,840 is retained because it is the strongest measured experiment-3 challenger, while the experiment-2 working recipe remains preferable for the active lineage. The development measurements do not establish the official success rate or justify terminal assessment.

## 285f0407-8cc3-401d-bdcd-2af73de9a61c / Experiment 4

**Result:** The hold-exit forfeiture produced a useful measured alternative but no robust improvement over the working lineage; close with `working`, restore its scientific recipe, and defer terminal assessment.

**Observed behavior:** Experiment 4 transferred the working checkpoint and set `HOLD_EXIT_FORFEIT_FRACTION` to 0.5 without changing the task distribution, interface, or PPO configuration. Training completed 120,832 steps. At the measured checkpoints, the training success proxy was 1.00 at step 20,480, 0.98 at step 100,352, and 0.97 at step 120,832; checkpoint episode reward was highest at step 100,352 (106.38) and lower at step 120,832 (104.64). Research evaluation success was 96.5% at checkpoints 20,480 and 100,352, then 98.0% at 120,832. The task-reference panel was 98.0% at all three checkpoints. The early task-reference failures included one positive-angle case, while checkpoints 100,352 and 120,832 failed the same four negative-angle identities as the parent: approximately 7.2 cm/-125.4 degrees, 9.9 cm/-122.9 degrees, 9.4 cm/-127.9 degrees, and 18.2 cm/-154.8 degrees. All listed failures truncated at 500 steps.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis is **weakened** under the measured conditions. The final candidate reached 98.0% on both development panels and the early positive-angle failure did not persist, but the protected panel did not improve over the parent's 98.5%, and the recurring negative-angle failures were unchanged. No trajectory measurement showed longer uninterrupted holds or fewer exits, so the evidence does not distinguish a hold-stability effect from ordinary checkpoint variation or control/geometry behavior. The result does not contradict the possibility that hold shaping helps other cases, but it weakens the proposed explanation for the residual deficit.

**Interpretation:** The intervention preserved strong general reach-and-hold behavior but did not supply evidence that hold-exit shaping resolves the campaign's recurring failures. The completed-budget candidate is worth retaining as a measured alternative because it reached 98.0% while ending with a lower training proxy and different checkpoint behavior, but the experiment-2 working policy remains the stronger development choice at 98.5% on the protected panel. The official success rate remains unknown, so terminal assessment is deferred.
