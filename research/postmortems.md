# Research postmortems

## a82df388-883d-4362-b081-0c03c09f4dc5 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 98% complete reach-and-hold success on the unchanged official task. The unchanged PPO baseline learned substantial partial behavior, but the best measured candidate, checkpoint-115712, reached only 52.0% on the protected task-reference panel and 61.0% on the researcher panel. The later checkpoint-120832 had a slightly higher training success proxy but lower measured success on both panels, so proxy improvement is not sufficient evidence of policy progress.

**Lessons and limits:** Measured task outcomes, rather than training reward or the callback success proxy, must govern policy selection. The raw experiment-2 log shows the training proxy at zero through 51,200 steps and rising to about 0.63 by the final checkpoints, while the development measurements remain near half-success. On the protected panel, failures are strongly concentrated in the -90 to 0 degree target quadrant, and researcher diagnostics show that most failures do not reach tolerance; these are behavioral correlations, not established causes. The current training target generator samples angles uniformly, while the 11-value observation includes target-relative position and inverse-kinematics error features and the action mapping is identity. Thus the policy representation is target-aware and reusable for a sampler change, but the evidence does not identify whether coverage, representation, reward, or control learning causes the quadrant deficit. The evidence covers two late checkpoints on one 200-episode researcher panel and one fixed 200-episode task-reference panel; neither development panel establishes the final objective.

**Open questions:** It remains unresolved whether greater training exposure to the -90 to 0 degree sector improves that sector under the unchanged official distribution while preserving performance elsewhere. It is also unresolved whether the late-checkpoint decline is repeatable or specific to this training run, and whether the remaining failures are primarily coverage-related or arise from representation, reward, or control-learning limitations.

## a82df388-883d-4362-b081-0c03c09f4dc5 / Experiment 2

**Result:** The fresh unchanged baseline produced a partially successful learned policy. Checkpoint-115712 is selected as working and best known; the 98% objective is not supported by the available development measurements.

**Observed behavior:** Training metrics rose from zero success through 120,832 completed steps, reaching a callback success proxy of 0.62 at step 115,712 and 0.63 at step 120,832; the associated training reward peaked at 100.12 and then fell to 99.53. These are training signals, not task acceptance measurements. On 200 researcher-evaluation episodes, checkpoint-115712 succeeded on 122 episodes (61.0%) and checkpoint-120832 on 116 (58.0%). The paired comparison on their shared episodes had 10 wins for checkpoint-115712, 4 for checkpoint-120832, 14 discordant episodes, and a -3 percentage-point final-checkpoint delta. On the independent protected task-reference panel, the candidates succeeded on 104 (52.0%) and 103 (51.5%) episodes respectively. For checkpoint-120832, task-reference success was 0/58 in the -90 to 0 degree angle bin, compared with 27/52 in -180 to -90, 23/37 in 0 to 90, and 53/53 in 90 to 180; checkpoint-115712 showed the same qualitative concentration, including 3/58 in -90 to 0. The radius-bin success for checkpoint-120832 was 25/57 (43.9%), 29/59 (49.2%), and 49/84 (58.3%) for 6-10, 10-15, and 15-20 cm. On the researcher diagnostics, checkpoint-115712 reached tolerance in 127/200 episodes and succeeded in 122; its failures had mean maximum hold of 1.41 steps, while successful episodes completed the 100-step hold.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline hypothesis was to establish whether the unchanged method could learn useful behavior for the human-defined task. It is partially supported: learning progress is visible in the training metrics and the saved policies achieve well above chance partial task behavior. It is not evidence that the human objective is met: both measured candidates remain roughly half-successful on development panels, and the slight late-training proxy increase coincides with lower measured task success. Because this was a baseline rather than a confirmatory intervention, the evidence does not attribute the residual failures to a particular component or prove that the late decline is general.

**Interpretation:** The policy has learned a nontrivial reach-and-hold strategy for part of the target distribution, but the measured behavior is dominated by residual geometry-dependent reach failures and is not ready for terminal assessment. The protected-panel pattern identifies a useful next scientific question, but it does not by itself establish whether observation semantics, action control, reward shaping, or training coverage is responsible.
