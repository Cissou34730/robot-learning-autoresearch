# Research postmortems

## 768a395f-05a2-43c3-b1ef-449056fbf8ed / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The fresh PPO baseline learned a near-objective reach-and-hold policy, but the final assessment of its frozen checkpoint was rejected by isolated evidence review and therefore did not establish the official objective. Development measurements favored checkpoint-100352: it reached 98% on the protected task-reference panel and 98% pooled success across two 200-episode researcher panels, while checkpoint-120832 reached 97% on the protected panel and had identical outcomes to checkpoint-100352 on their shared researcher episodes. The campaign therefore has a useful but not officially accepted policy, with official success unresolved.

**Lessons and limits:** Training proxy success rose to 0.98 near step 99,328 and then declined to 0.95 by step 120,832; reward and proxy metrics are not task-acceptance evidence. The training environment sampled only 14-20 cm although the official distribution is 6-20 cm. On the protected panel, checkpoint-100352's four failures were all 6.7-9.9 cm targets at -116 to -128 degrees; the endpoint retained those failures and added two others. Researcher panels showed eight negative outcomes for checkpoint-100352, but their diagnostic artifacts omit target geometry, so they cannot establish the same radial pattern. The evidence identifies a coverage mismatch and residual behavior, not a proven causal mechanism, and development panels remain distinct from the final benchmark. Sources: `research/results.jsonl`, `research/research_state.json`, the six detailed artifacts under `research/evaluations/768a395f-05a2-43c3-b1ef-449056fbf8ed/`, `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`, and the experiment 1 log query for steps 70656-120832.

**Open questions:** Whether exposure to the full official radius range improves small-radius reach-and-hold reliability without degrading the already strong outer-radius behavior is unresolved. It is also unknown whether the repeated negative-angle failures arise mainly from training coverage or from a directional control/representation limitation. The official 98% objective remains unresolved, and development evidence cannot substitute for the distinct final panel.

## 768a395f-05a2-43c3-b1ef-449056fbf8ed / Experiment 1

**Result:** The baseline produced a useful near-objective policy. Checkpoint-100352 is selected for closure because it achieved 98% on the protected task-reference panel; the final benchmark is requested to determine the official result.

**Observed behavior:** Training logs show proxy success increasing from 0.00 at step 70656 to 0.98 at step 99328, then declining to 0.95 at step 120832; mean reward similarly declined from 168 at step 87040 to 117 at step 100352 and 112 at step 120832. These are training signals, not task-acceptance measurements. On task-reference-v1, checkpoint-90112 scored 93% (186/200), checkpoint-100352 scored 98% (196/200), and checkpoint-120832 scored 97% (194/200). On researcher evaluation, checkpoint-100352 scored 97% on seeds 0-199 and 99% on seeds 200-399; checkpoint-120832 scored 99% on seeds 200-399. The paired comparison on seeds 200-399 found no discordant outcomes and no wins for either checkpoint. The four checkpoint-100352 task-reference failures were all small-radius targets (6.7-9.9 cm) at angles from -116.4 to -127.9 degrees; the endpoint retained those failures and added two failures, including one at +169.1 degrees. Twenty-two of the twenty-four saved checkpoints remain unmeasured.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Supported for the baseline's intended purpose of establishing a strong initial reference: the learned policy substantially improved measured task success and reached 98% on one independent development panel. This does not establish the official objective, and the endpoint's research-panel result does not overturn the protected-panel regression because the paired research outcomes were identical and the panels are distinct. The assessment is limited to this single training run and the measured checkpoints.

**Interpretation:** The saved policy demonstrates measured progress toward the human objective and is ready for the official frozen-policy assessment. Checkpoint-100352 is preferable to the endpoint on the protected development panel and has comparable independent research performance. The failure concentration suggests a residual directional/near-radius weakness, but the evidence does not justify attributing it to a particular reward, observation, or training mechanism.

## 768a395f-05a2-43c3-b1ef-449056fbf8ed / Experiment 2

**Result:** The full 6-20 cm training-radius intervention was partially supported as a task-level refinement but did not produce a measured aggregate improvement over the selected baseline policy. Checkpoint-100352 is selected as the working candidate; the experiment-1 best-known lineage remains unchanged.

**Observed behavior:** Experiment 2 was transferred from the experiment-1 best-known policy and trained for 120,832 local steps with the official 6-20 cm radius range. On the researcher evaluator, checkpoints 65,536, 100,352, and 120,832 each scored 99% on the same 200-episode seed-200 panel, with the same two failures (episodes 42 and 174). On task-reference-v1, all three checkpoints scored 98% (196/200). Checkpoint-100352 failed on episodes 10, 84, and 102 at 7.2-9.9 cm and negative angles, plus episode 175 at 18.2 cm and -154.8 degrees. Relative to experiment-1 checkpoint-100352, it removed the 6.7 cm failure at episode 0 but added the outer-radius episode-175 failure, leaving total success unchanged. The experiment-2 endpoint also scored 98%, compared with 97% for the experiment-1 endpoint, but its failures shifted to one small-radius case and three outer-radius cases rather than demonstrating a uniform improvement. Training proxy success reached 0.99 around steps 64,512-68,608 and was 0.97 at step 120,832; these proxy and reward signals were not used as task-acceptance evidence.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. Full-radius exposure removed one of the selected baseline's small-radius failures at checkpoint-100352 and preserved 98% task-reference success, while the endpoint recovered one percentage point relative to the experiment-1 endpoint. However, the selected candidate gained an outer-radius failure with no net success gain, the repeated negative-angle failure at episode 102 persisted, and researcher-evaluation outcomes were unchanged across measured checkpoints. The single transferred run and one fixed development panel do not establish that radius coverage caused the observed redistribution or that the intervention improves the official task distribution.

**Interpretation:** The intervention addresses a real training-distribution mismatch and is compatible with the learned policy, but the measurements do not show that radius coverage alone resolves the residual failures. Checkpoint-100352 is a reasonable working candidate because it matches the prior best-known task-reference score while avoiding the endpoint's measured task regression; the prior best-known remains the evidence-backed best-known designation because the new candidate has no superior comparable success result. The full-range recipe is kept as the working recipe for a later ordinary experiment, while the official objective remains unresolved and development panels remain distinct from the final assessment.
