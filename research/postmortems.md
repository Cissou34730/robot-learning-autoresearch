# Research postmortems

## 3f8899d3-51f3-4f34-8ace-9209912451c8 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The unchanged PPO baseline learned a useful reach-and-hold controller, with task success rising sharply late in training and checkpoint-100352 the strongest measured candidate. Across two disjoint 200-episode research panels it achieved 393/400 successes (98.25%), ahead of checkpoint-120832 at 391/400 and checkpoint-95232 at 389/400. The fixed task-reference panel also ranked checkpoint-100352 highest at 98%, but that panel is permanently reused and was part of the selection evidence, so it is not independent confirmation. The current evidence supports preserving checkpoint-100352 for further development, not terminal assessment: the disjoint panel measured it at 97%, below the 98% objective.

**Lessons and limits:** Training success and reward identified the late plateau but were only proxies; direct measurements were required to distinguish useful policies. The selected checkpoint usually completed the task, but research diagnostics show residual instability in the required hold: on the disjoint panel, four of its six failures reached the tolerance and held for only 10 or 1 control steps, while two did not reach tolerance. This is physically relevant because the planar arm must remain within the 1 cm region for 100 consecutive 0.020-second control intervals, not merely pass through it. The evidence supports checkpoint-100352 as the best available baseline lineage and partially supports the baseline hypothesis, while leaving the source of the residual failures unresolved. Development measurements are not the official 200-episode benchmark and do not establish that the human objective has been reached.

**Open questions:** Which training or control changes can improve the selected policy's generalization and sustained in-tolerance hold without sacrificing reach performance remains unresolved. The distribution of failures across target geometry, inverse-kinematic branch, approach dynamics, and late hold behavior also remains only partly characterized.

## 3f8899d3-51f3-4f34-8ace-9209912451c8 / Experiment 1

**Result:** The baseline produced a useful but not yet objective-level policy. Checkpoint-100352 is selected as both the working and best-known lineage for continued development; the unchanged scientific recipe is kept and no alternative candidate is retained.

**Observed behavior:** The 120,832-step baseline had a late training-success plateau, with measured candidates at 95.5% (checkpoint-120832), 97% (checkpoint-100352), and 93% (checkpoint-95232) training success. On research episodes 10000-10199, the candidates achieved 198/200, 199/200, and 197/200 successes respectively; on the disjoint episodes 10200-10399 they achieved 191/200, 194/200, and 194/200 respectively for checkpoint-95232, checkpoint-100352, and checkpoint-120832. The pooled disjoint research evidence therefore favors checkpoint-100352 at 393/400 (98.25%), versus 389/400 and 391/400 for the alternatives. The fixed task-reference panel recorded 96%, 98%, and 97% for checkpoint-95232, checkpoint-100352, and checkpoint-120832, respectively, but it is not independent confirmation. For checkpoint-100352, the seven research failures across both panels included five episodes that reached tolerance but failed to complete the 100-step hold and two that never reached tolerance; the disjoint-panel failures specifically included four maximum hold streaks of 10 or 1 step and two zero-step reaches.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline clearly learned the reach-and-hold task and produced a candidate near the 98% objective, while direct task measurements supported checkpoint-100352 over the sampled alternatives. However, its 97% result on the disjoint panel and residual reach/hold failures do not support concluding that the policy reliably satisfies the human objective. The result establishes useful progress and a defensible lineage choice, not official success or causal claims about the PPO recipe.

**Interpretation:** The policy can generally move the planar two-link arm into the small target region and complete the required hold, but performance varies across target episodes and remains sensitive to final stabilization. The measured ranking is sufficiently consistent across the two research panels to preserve checkpoint-100352, while continued training or a changed method should be treated as a new experiment aimed at the remaining generalization and hold-stability failures. No additional measurement is needed for the current lineage decision because all three sampled late candidates have already been compared on a disjoint panel.
