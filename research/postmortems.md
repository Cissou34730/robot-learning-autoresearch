# Research postmortems

## e0fec0e7-77ed-4433-97cd-1944e1b15eca / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The unchanged PPO baseline (1 environment, 1024-step rollouts, tanh [64, 64] policy, 0.0003 learning rate, entropy coefficient 0.01) remains the strongest learned policy. `checkpoint-100352` achieved 99/100 on the first research panel, 195/200 on a disjoint panel, 196/200 on the reused task-reference panel, and 197/200 on the new disjoint experiment-2 panel. The latest 98.5% result independently supports the parent as a candidate for terminal assessment, while its remaining failures are still concentrated in negative angles and are all no-entry cases on that panel. The focused target-sampler continuation did not preserve this behavior: its measured checkpoints achieved only 178/200 and 179/200.

**Lessons and limits:** Training-time reward and success did not predict the experiment-2 outcome: the focused sampler reached proxy success of 0.96 and 0.94 at its measured checkpoints, yet complete-hold success fell 9.5 and 9.0 percentage points below the parent on the same fresh panel. The challenger failures were broader than the parent's residual: 17/22 and 14/21 were outside the targeted -165 to -110 degree sector, and nearly all challenger failures entered the tolerance but failed to hold. The 1 cm Cartesian band and 100-step uninterrupted requirement make this a stabilization symptom, not proof of a particular physical cause. The frozen model establishes multiple inverse-kinematic branches, folded inner targets, coupled direct-torque dynamics, and possible joint-limit sensitivity; it does not establish which mechanism the policy uses. No experiment-2 challenger merits retention, and the parent recipe should be restored.

**Open questions:** Whether the parent's negative-angle no-entry failures arise primarily from elbow-branch selection or angle wrapping, proximity to a joint limit, or inadequate approach control remains unknown; the available evaluation diagnostics do not contain joint-limit, branch, velocity, or boundary-margin traces. The focused sampler result weakens target reweighting as a safe remedy because it caused broad hold regressions, but it does not identify the underlying mechanism. The parent now has a fresh disjoint 197/200 result, so the evidence supports requesting the official benchmark while keeping these diagnostic questions out of the terminal claim.

## e0fec0e7-77ed-4433-97cd-1944e1b15eca / Experiment 1

**Result:** The baseline produced a useful near-objective policy, with `checkpoint-100352` selected as both working and best known, but the experiment did not establish reliable achievement of the 98% objective.

**Observed behavior:** The run used the unchanged PPO recipe and completed 120,832 steps. Training success increased from 0 at 70,656 steps to 0.42 at 86,016, peaked at 0.97 at 100,352, and ended at 0.95. On the first research panel, `checkpoint-86016`, `checkpoint-100352`, and `checkpoint-120832` scored 92%, 99%, and 97%; on the fixed task-reference panel they scored 94%, 98%, and 97%. The disjoint 200-episode research panel for `checkpoint-100352` scored 97.5% (195/200). Its five failures were at target angles from -149.7 to -128.1 degrees and radii from 10.3 to 17.3 cm; three had no tolerance entry, while two entered for at most three steps before failing. The fixed-panel failures for this checkpoint were also concentrated at negative angles (-127.9 to -116.4 degrees) and radii 6.7--9.9 cm. Successful episodes generally completed in 104--120 control steps, while failures truncated at 500 steps.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline established a high-performing learned policy and the late proxy peak identified the strongest measured checkpoint, but proxy improvement and continued training did not establish reliable complete-hold performance at or above 98% on fresh episodes. The comparison is limited to this single training run and the available development panels; the fixed task-reference panel is reused and was part of model selection.

**Interpretation:** The baseline learned reach-and-hold behavior for most targets, but its residual failures are structured by target geometry. The frozen scientific model makes branch choice, folded-posture sensitivity, joint limits, and configuration-dependent direct-torque stabilization plausible distinctions; the measurements show the spatial concentration and reach-versus-hold symptoms but cannot attribute the cause among them. The current checkpoint is therefore worth extending in a later experiment, while the final benchmark should not yet be requested.

## e0fec0e7-77ed-4433-97cd-1944e1b15eca / Experiment 2

**Result:** The focused target-sampler continuation was contradicted by fresh complete-hold measurements. The transferred `best_known` parent scored 197/200 (98.5%), while `checkpoint-115712` scored 178/200 (89.0%) and `checkpoint-120832` scored 179/200 (89.5%) on the same disjoint panel.

**Observed behavior:** The experiment changed the training target sampler to cover the full 0.06--0.20 m radius range and sample the previously observed -165 to -110 degree sector with 50% probability. Training proxies reached 0.96 at checkpoint-115712 and 0.94 at checkpoint-120832. On the 200-episode panel, the parent had three failures, all no-entry cases in the targeted sector. The two challengers had 22 and 21 failures; 17 and 14 respectively were outside that sector, and 20 and 21 respectively entered the tolerance but failed the complete hold. Both challengers lost the paired comparison to the parent, by 19 and 18 net wins.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted under the tested transfer and 120,000-step continuation. The focused sampler did not improve the residual sector and caused a large broad-task regression relative to the parent on identical fresh episodes. This does not prove that target coverage can never help, nor does it identify whether the parent's residual is caused by branch selection, angle wrapping, joint-limit proximity, or torque stabilization; it does show that this intervention is not a safe route to the objective in the tested recipe.

**Interpretation:** Complete-hold measurements, rather than the proxy peak, identify the transferred parent as the useful policy. The challengers' frequent tolerance entry followed by hold interruption is consistent with the physical model's distinction between reach and stabilization under direct bounded torque, but the artifacts do not establish the trajectory-level cause. The parent is restored as both the working and best-known lineage, with no experiment-2 challenger retained. Its 197/200 result on a panel disjoint from the panels that selected it supports requesting the official benchmark; development measurements remain non-official.
