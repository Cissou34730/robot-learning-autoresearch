# Research postmortems

## 614b8ee6-6a63-4ee9-b948-d4fb9064d6be / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The baseline learned a useful but sub-objective reach-and-hold controller. Checkpoint-100352 remains the strongest measured lineage, with 728/760 successes across four disjoint research panels, including 197/200 on the latest panel. The full-target-radius intervention instead achieved 118/200 at both measured checkpoints. Baseline failures recur in a narrow lower-left target-angle region and include both failure to enter tolerance and brief entry followed by exit, so the evidence is consistent with coupled geometry, posture selection, approach, settling, and hold behavior without isolating one cause.

**Lessons and limits:** Training reward and training success tracked the emergence of competence but did not establish the human objective. The experiment-2 proxy rose from 0.49 at checkpoint-100352 to 0.60 at checkpoint-120832, while both complete-task measurements stayed at 59.0%; each lost all 79 discordant paired episodes to the baseline reference at 98.5%. Across the experiment-1 baseline diagnostics, failures repeatedly clustered around target angles roughly -117 to -162 degrees across several radii, while the diagnostics also contained both non-entry and interrupted-hold outcomes. The full-radius intervention therefore weakened a distribution-coverage-only explanation under its tested recipe, but its poor result does not identify the responsible behavior. The current instrumentation does not establish inverse-kinematic branch choice, joint-limit margin, velocity, or braking causality, and development evidence remains insufficient to declare the 98% objective.

**Open questions:** It remains unresolved how target radius and angle, the two joint-limit-constrained inverse-kinematic postures, direct-torque trajectory and velocity, settling, and sustained local regulation interact in the residual failures. It is also unresolved whether the recurring angle band reflects posture feasibility, learned representation, trajectory generation, or their interaction, and whether complete-success changes would come from fewer tolerance-entry failures, fewer hold interruptions, or a trade between them.

## 614b8ee6-6a63-4ee9-b948-d4fb9064d6be / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh baseline produced a useful learned policy but did not demonstrate the human objective. Checkpoint-100352 is selected as the working and best-known lineage for this closure.

**Observed behavior:** Checkpoint-100352 achieved 94.375%, 94.5%, and 95.5% on three disjoint research panels, totaling 531/560 successes. It achieved 98.0% on the reused fixed task-reference panel. Checkpoint-120832 achieved 94.375% and 93.5% on two disjoint panels, while checkpoint-110592 achieved 94.5% on the third panel and lost the paired comparison to checkpoint-100352 by two discordant episodes. The late checkpoints therefore remained below the 98% objective on fresh research panels despite high training-time proxy values.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline established substantial reach-and-hold competence and produced a measurable candidate for future work, but it did not establish a policy satisfying the 98% objective. The conclusion is limited to this training recipe, these saved checkpoints, and the recorded development panels; it does not explain the residual failures or rule out future improvements.

**Interpretation:** The policy commonly reaches and holds the target, but the complete behavior remains unreliable across the full target distribution. The failure diagnostics show both failure to enter the tolerance region and failure to sustain an entered tolerance region, consistent with the Scientific model's coupled approach, settling, and hold constraints. Since the additional late checkpoint did not improve the measured leader, the available evidence supports resolving lineage around checkpoint-100352 without treating its favorable fixed-panel score as independent confirmation.

## 614b8ee6-6a63-4ee9-b948-d4fb9064d6be / Experiment 2

**Result:** The full official target-radius training distribution did not improve complete reach-and-hold success and is closed without selecting either challenger.

**Observed behavior:** On the fresh research panel covering episode seeds 4800-4999, checkpoint-100352 and checkpoint-120832 each achieved 118/200 successes (59.0%). The retained baseline achieved 197/200 (98.5%) on the same panel; each challenger lost all 79 discordant paired episodes to that reference, and the challengers were tied on every paired outcome. Training proxies increased from 0.49 to 0.60 success between the measured checkpoints, but this did not correspond to improved complete-task behavior. The detailed challenger traces contain both episodes that never enter tolerance and episodes that enter without completing the uninterrupted hold.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted under the tested fresh initialization, training recipe, checkpoints, and panel. Training on the full official radius interval did not produce the predicted overall complete-success improvement, and the available result does not show an inner-radius benefit; both measured challengers were 39.5 percentage points below the baseline reference. The conclusion is bounded by the single fresh evaluation panel and does not identify which coupled physical behavior caused the regression or rule out a different intervention.

**Interpretation:** The unchanged baseline remains the useful working and best-known lineage, while the experiment-2 radius change is not supported as a path toward the human objective. The unchanged result between matched and final challenger checkpoints indicates that the higher training proxy and additional late training did not recover the measured policy behavior. Because success couples approach, tolerance entry, settling, and sustained regulation, the 59% outcome should not be assigned to radius coverage alone as a single isolated stage failure.
