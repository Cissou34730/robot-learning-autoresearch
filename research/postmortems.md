# Research postmortems

## dd6d401e-1c3b-46d0-8ecf-3b9304fbff2a / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The baseline learned strong reach-and-hold behavior, making `checkpoint-100352` the current working and best-known candidate, but the human objective remains unestablished. The experiment-2 transfer that expanded training radii from 14–20 cm to the full official 6–20 cm range did not improve the policy: the working lineage and both measured experiment-2 checkpoints each achieved 194/200 (97.0%) on the new disjoint panel. The earlier disjoint result was 195/200 (97.5%), while the permanently reused task-reference result was 196/200 (98.0%) and is not independent confirmation.

**Lessons and limits:** Measured task success, rather than training reward or training success, supports the lineage choice. On episodes 3000–3199, both experiment-2 candidates had zero discordant wins against the working lineage and shared its six failures. Three inner-radius targets around 7.4–7.7 cm were never reached; the remaining failures were negative-angle outer targets with either no reach or only a brief hold. The single-panel result is below the 98% objective, and the task-reference panel is permanently reused, so development evidence does not establish the official objective. Radius coverage alone did not resolve the residual behavior, whose cause remains underdetermined.

**Open questions:** It remains unresolved whether the recurring negative-angle failures arise from posture selection, settling, or hold-margin control, and whether a different intervention can address the inner-radius misses without sacrificing the established controller. The current evidence does not justify an official objective claim or a final benchmark request.

The late checkpoint does not improve that behavior and appears mildly degraded, so the proxy peak is the most useful saved policy for subsequent work.

## dd6d401e-1c3b-46d0-8ecf-3b9304fbff2a / Experiment 2

**Result:** The full-radius training intervention produced no measured improvement. `checkpoint-100352` remains the working and best-known lineage.

**Observed behavior:** The working lineage, `checkpoint-105472`, and `checkpoint-120832` each achieved 194/200 successes (97.0%) on the disjoint research panel covering episodes 3000–3199. Both candidates had zero discordant wins against working and shared the same six failed episodes. Three failures were inner-radius targets at about 7.4–7.7 cm with no recorded reach; the other three were negative-angle targets near 17–19 cm, including one missed reach and two brief holds. The experiment-2 training success proxy peaked at 1.0, but this did not translate into higher measured task success.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Weakened. Expanding target-radius exposure did not reduce the targeted inner-workspace failures, although it also did not materially change aggregate success on this panel. Because the conclusion rests on one disjoint panel and no official benchmark was run, the intervention is not conclusively ruled out as a source of behavior change, but it is not supported as a useful recipe for the selected lineage.

**Interpretation:** Coverage of the omitted inner radii was insufficient by itself to fix the residual failures. The shared failure geometry and the occasional short holds indicate that the remaining deficit may involve posture selection, settling, or hold-margin control in addition to reach coverage; the available measurements do not identify the mechanism. The experiment-2 recipe should therefore be reverted while preserving the previously selected policy.
