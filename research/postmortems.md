# Research postmortems

## e0fec0e7-77ed-4433-97cd-1944e1b15eca / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The unchanged PPO baseline (1 environment, 1024-step rollouts, tanh [64, 64] policy, 0.0003 learning rate, entropy coefficient 0.01) learned a strong but not yet reliably objective-satisfying policy. Training success rose from 0 through 70,656 steps to 0.97 at 97,280--100,352 steps, then remained between 0.93 and 0.96 through 120,832 steps. The best measured artifact is `checkpoint-100352`: 99/100 on the first research panel, 195/200 on a disjoint research panel, and 196/200 on the fixed task-reference panel. The disjoint result is below the 98% objective and the task-reference panel was used in selection, so this is comparative development evidence rather than independent confirmation or an official result. The five disjoint failures all lie in a negative-angle sector (approximately -150 to -130 degrees); three never entered tolerance and two entered only briefly, indicating a localized reach/stabilization weakness rather than a uniform loss of performance. Continued training to `checkpoint-120832` did not improve this behavior (97/100 on the first research panel and 97% on the task-reference panel).

**Lessons and limits:** Training-time reward and success were useful for locating the late-training transition but did not guarantee complete 100-step holds. The best checkpoint reaches and holds successfully in 195 of 200 fresh research episodes, with successful episodes terminating after roughly 104--120 control steps; failed episodes run to the 500-step horizon. The geometry and diagnostics are consistent with a direction- and posture-dependent problem: the frozen model establishes multiple inverse-kinematic branches, folded inner targets, coupled direct-torque dynamics, and possible joint-limit sensitivity, but does not identify which mechanism the policy uses or why it fails. The measurements therefore support retaining `checkpoint-100352` as the best known policy, while leaving its official 98% status unresolved.

**Open questions:** Whether the negative-angle blind spot is caused primarily by elbow-branch selection or angle wrapping, proximity to a joint limit, or inadequate braking and stabilization under configuration-dependent dynamics remains unknown. A future experiment should target this observed geometry-dependent residual rather than infer success from the training proxy; its result would determine whether the current lineage can be improved toward the objective. The official benchmark remains unrequested because current development evidence does not support expecting a `goal_reached` verdict.

## e0fec0e7-77ed-4433-97cd-1944e1b15eca / Experiment 1

**Result:** The baseline produced a useful near-objective policy, with `checkpoint-100352` selected as both working and best known, but the experiment did not establish reliable achievement of the 98% objective.

**Observed behavior:** The run used the unchanged PPO recipe and completed 120,832 steps. Training success increased from 0 at 70,656 steps to 0.42 at 86,016, peaked at 0.97 at 100,352, and ended at 0.95. On the first research panel, `checkpoint-86016`, `checkpoint-100352`, and `checkpoint-120832` scored 92%, 99%, and 97%; on the fixed task-reference panel they scored 94%, 98%, and 97%. The disjoint 200-episode research panel for `checkpoint-100352` scored 97.5% (195/200). Its five failures were at target angles from -149.7 to -128.1 degrees and radii from 10.3 to 17.3 cm; three had no tolerance entry, while two entered for at most three steps before failing. The fixed-panel failures for this checkpoint were also concentrated at negative angles (-127.9 to -116.4 degrees) and radii 6.7--9.9 cm. Successful episodes generally completed in 104--120 control steps, while failures truncated at 500 steps.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline established a high-performing learned policy and the late proxy peak identified the strongest measured checkpoint, but proxy improvement and continued training did not establish reliable complete-hold performance at or above 98% on fresh episodes. The comparison is limited to this single training run and the available development panels; the fixed task-reference panel is reused and was part of model selection.

**Interpretation:** The baseline learned reach-and-hold behavior for most targets, but its residual failures are structured by target geometry. The frozen scientific model makes branch choice, folded-posture sensitivity, joint limits, and configuration-dependent direct-torque stabilization plausible distinctions; the measurements show the spatial concentration and reach-versus-hold symptoms but cannot attribute the cause among them. The current checkpoint is therefore worth extending in a later experiment, while the final benchmark should not yet be requested.
