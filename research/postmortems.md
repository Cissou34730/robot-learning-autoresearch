# Research postmortems

## 1a939de2-8d9d-42c0-a475-dc59a9a5e55c / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The unchanged PPO baseline learned a competent reach-and-hold controller, but its complete-hold performance is not established as robustly at the human objective. Training proxies do not reliably rank complete-hold behavior: the selected working checkpoint reached 159/160 (99.375%) on the latest fresh panel, while the retained baseline final reached 158/160. Experiments 2 and 4 did not produce a fresh-panel improvement, and experiment 3 did not learn a competent policy. Experiment 5 then increased hold-exit forfeiture and outside-band penalty while preserving the original observation and task semantics. Its measured late and final candidates reached 146/160 (91.25%) and 135/160 (84.375%), versus 159/160 for working, with zero candidate wins in either paired comparison. The continuity-aware reward intervention therefore did not improve the residual failure basin and instead damaged the learned controller during this run. The next intervention preserves the established reward and observation recipe but applies a stateful first-order smoothing filter to successive physical actions, targeting transient post-entry excursions without changing the task evaluator.

**Lessons and limits:** Late training success and reward remain learning proxies rather than behavioral rankings. The experiment-5 fresh panel is paired and disjoint from the earlier preparation panel, and the working reference was measured at 159/160 under the same semantics. The late candidate lost 13 paired episodes and won none; the final candidate lost 24 and won none. The final also lost to the late candidate 13 times and won twice, consistent with late-run degradation rather than a selective continuity gain. These results strongly reject this reward configuration as a practical improvement, but they do not isolate reward credit from checkpoint-dependent optimization dynamics because only one changed-reward training run was performed. They also do not prove that post-entry regulation is irrelevant: the prior trajectory measurements still identify short holds and larger excursions in the residual failures. All development measurements remain distinct from the official assessment.

**Competing explanations:** Insufficient radius exposure and simple angular exposure are weakened by experiments 2 and 4. The trajectory panel weakens a simple excessive-entry-speed explanation and supports transient braking or post-entry feedback failure through larger excursions. Experiment 5 weakens the explanation that stronger continuity penalties alone will fix that phenotype: the changed reward did not preserve competence and produced no paired improvement. The remaining alternatives are branch selection and approach control from the reset singularity, transient state representation, hold regulation, and interactions between reward scale and PPO optimization. Experiment 3 also leaves open feature conditioning and sample-efficiency effects, because its observation change collapsed learning. The experiment-5 result cannot distinguish these alternatives, but it does establish that the tested reward change is not a safe route to the objective.

**Decision frontier:** The unresolved distinction is whether the residual negative-angle failures are caused primarily by branch/approach dynamics or by transient-state and hold-regulation limitations after a competent policy is learned. A transferred policy with action smoothing can discriminate whether reducing high-frequency torque changes improves post-entry excursions while preserving reach; unchanged negative-angle failures despite competent learning would redirect toward branch selection and approach-path design. The official assessment remains withheld because the independent development evidence does not yet support an expectation of at least 196/200.

## 1a939de2-8d9d-42c0-a475-dc59a9a5e55c / Experiment 1

**Result:** The baseline learned a policy that completed 151/160 research-panel holds and 196/200 protected task-reference holds at its best measured checkpoint, but no evidence justifies claiming the official 98% objective. Checkpoint-100352 is selected as both working and best-known; checkpoint-120832 is retained as a close alternative.

**Observed behavior:** Training proxies reached 0.97 success at checkpoint-100352 and 0.95 at checkpoint-120832, but checkpoint-95232, checkpoint-100352, and checkpoint-120832 each scored 151/160 on the same research panel. Their paired comparisons had zero discordant wins. The research failures were the same nine episode identities for all three checkpoints, centered on target angles roughly -119 to -150 degrees and commonly radii from 7 to 19 cm. Five to seven of those episodes never entered tolerance depending on checkpoint; the remaining failures entered for at most two control samples and then left. On the protected task-reference panel, the checkpoints scored 192/200, 196/200, and 194/200 respectively.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline hypothesis was only partially supported. Learning clearly produced substantial reach-and-hold capability and late proxy gains transferred to strong task-reference performance, but the proxy peak did not improve the disjoint complete-hold result over the neighboring checkpoints, and the shared directional failure basin remains. The 98.0% task-reference result is encouraging but is one development panel and cannot establish the official result or generalize beyond the measured target set.

**Interpretation:** The policy has acquired most of the task but exhibits a reproducible blind region that is more scientifically important than small differences among late checkpoints. The evidence favors retaining the proxy-peak checkpoint as the current working baseline because it is best on the protected development panel, while preserving the final checkpoint for later transfer or comparison. Further progress should address the causal distinction between directional reach/branch coverage and dynamic settling rather than simply selecting a later checkpoint.

## 1a939de2-8d9d-42c0-a475-dc59a9a5e55c / Experiment 2

**Result:** Full-range target exposure did not improve the dominant complete-hold failure basin on the fresh paired research panel. The exp2 proxy-peak and final candidates each achieved 155/160, exactly matching working; the final achieved 197/200 on the reused task-reference panel versus 196/200 for working.

**Observed behavior:** On research episodes 6000–6159, working, checkpoint-105472 and checkpoint-120832 failed on the same five episode identities. Their targets all had negative angles from -125.26 to -145.41 degrees and radii from 9.37 to 16.76 cm. Three failures never entered tolerance at any recorded substep; the other two had only 2 or 3 internal tolerance hits and no complete hold. The paired comparisons had zero discordant episodes. On the fixed task-reference panel, working and checkpoint-105472 each scored 196/200; checkpoint-120832 scored 197/200, but its failure set changed because the panel is reused development evidence.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis that extending target exposure from 14–20 cm to 6–20 cm would reduce the shared pre-entry reach failure while preserving regulation was weakened and contradicted as an explanation for the dominant basin. The predicted reduction was absent on a fresh paired panel, including failures at both inner and mid-range radii. The one-episode task-reference gain for the final candidate is insufficient to establish a reliable improvement, and the single changed-recipe run cannot exclude smaller effects outside these panels.

**Interpretation:** The intervention did not alter the reproducible negative-angle basin, so insufficient radius exposure is unlikely to be its primary cause. The evidence instead leaves branch selection, approach control from the reset singularity, and transient state representation as the leading causal alternatives. The working lineage is retained for continuation because it is tied with both exp2 candidates on the fresh panel and has the stronger established independent development history. The exp2 final is retained as a comparison lineage, not promoted as a superior policy.

## 1a939de2-8d9d-42c0-a475-dc59a9a5e55c / Experiment 3

**Result:** The explicit target-geometry observation did not yield a usable policy in the 120,832-step fresh run. The measured late and final candidates achieved 74/160 and 77/160 on the new research panel and 43% and 44% on the reused task-reference panel, while working achieved 158/160 and 98% on those same panels.

**Observed behavior:** Both representation candidates lost broadly to working on the paired fresh panel, with working winning 84 episodes against the late candidate and 81 against the final candidate. The candidates' training-success proxies were only 0.10 and 0.17, far below the learned baseline competence. The final candidate was only slightly better than the late candidate on the fresh panel and task-reference panel, so continued training within this run did not recover the deficit.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis that explicit radius and continuous bearing features would reduce the negative-angle pre-entry basin is inconclusive as a mechanistic representation test and strongly weakened as a practical intervention at this training budget. The candidates were not behaviorally competent enough to compare the narrow failure basin or sustained regulation fairly. The evidence does not establish that the representation is intrinsically incapable, because the fresh run also changed the observation contract and failed to reach baseline-level learning proxies.

**Interpretation:** The intervention exposed a major compatibility or sample-efficiency failure in fresh PPO learning. The consistent collapse on both a fresh research panel and the protected development panel rules out treating it as panel noise, but it cannot distinguish feature conditioning from optimization dynamics or a deeper representation limitation. No candidate provides a defensible replacement for the established working lineage, and another measurement of these undertrained candidates would not resolve that distinction.

## 1a939de2-8d9d-42c0-a475-dc59a9a5e55c / Experiment 4

**Result:** Target-angle oversampling did not improve complete-hold behavior on the disjoint research panel. The late candidate achieved 153/160 (95.625%), while the final candidate achieved 158/160 (98.75%), exactly matching `working`. The final candidate scored 197/200 (98.5%) on the reused task-reference panel versus 196/200 (98.0%) for `working`.

**Observed behavior:** The final candidate and `working` had zero discordant episodes on the fresh paired panel. On the reused task-reference panel, the final candidate succeeded on two episodes that `working` failed (-116.41 degrees at 6.73 cm and -125.40 degrees at 7.24 cm), but failed on one episode that `working` completed (-154.79 degrees at 18.24 cm). Both policies failed on the -122.90 degree, 9.91 cm and -127.91 degree, 9.36 cm episodes. The final candidate's three failures all ran to the 500-step limit, with final distances above 1 cm. The task-reference result is reused development evidence and does not independently confirm the one-episode net gain.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis that increased exposure to the broadened -155 to -110 degree sector would reduce the persistent negative-angle failure basin was weakened. The final candidate did not beat `working` on the fresh paired panel, and its task-reference improvement was accompanied by a changed failure set on a reused panel. The final checkpoint's recovery from the weaker late checkpoint shows checkpoint-dependent behavioral variation, but does not establish a causal benefit from the sampling intervention.

**Interpretation:** The experiment does not support selecting angular oversampling as the current recipe or promoting its policy. It weakens a simple exposure-shortage explanation for the residual basin, while leaving branch/approach selection, transient dynamics, and hold regulation unresolved. The final candidate is close enough to the working policy to preserve as a reusable alternative, but the established `working` lineage remains the scientifically safer continuation and best-known designation.

## 1a939de2-8d9d-42c0-a475-dc59a9a5e55c / Experiment 5

**Result:** The continuity-aware reward intervention failed to improve complete-hold behavior and degraded both measured candidates relative to `working`. The established `working` lineage remains selected; no experiment-5 candidate is retained or assigned as a developing method.

**Observed behavior:** On the fresh paired panel, `working` completed 159/160 episodes, checkpoint-75776 completed 146/160, and checkpoint-120832 completed 135/160. The late candidate lost 13 paired episodes and won none against `working`; the final candidate lost 24 and won none. The final candidate also lost 13 paired episodes to the late candidate and won 2. The run's late training proxy had already degraded at the final checkpoint. The measured candidates therefore showed broad loss of learned behavior rather than a reduction in the known narrow negative-angle failures.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis that increasing `HOLD_EXIT_FORFEIT_FRACTION` to 1.0 and `OUTSIDE_BAND_PENALTY` to 2.0 would improve post-entry regulation without degrading the established controller is contradicted for this training run. The fresh paired evidence gives no candidate improvement and a large performance loss. Because this is one changed-reward run with checkpoint-dependent degradation, it does not establish whether reward scale, credit assignment, or optimization dynamics caused the loss, and it does not rule out every continuity-oriented reward design.

**Interpretation:** Stronger continuity pressure was not a localized correction to the measured hold phenotype; it interacted with training strongly enough to erase competence before producing a reliable regulation gain. The reward intervention should not be carried into the scientific recipe or treated as evidence for final assessment. The next causal effort should return to the established recipe and separately resolve branch/approach dynamics versus transient-state and regulation limitations.
