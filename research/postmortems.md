## dd6d401e-1c3b-46d0-8ecf-3b9304fbff2a / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The baseline learned strong reach-and-hold behavior, making `checkpoint-100352` the current working and best-known candidate, but the human objective remains unestablished. Expanding training radii and then oversampling the observed negative-angle and inner-radius geometries did not improve the selected lineage: the working policy and both measured experiment-3 checkpoints each achieved 196/200 (98.0%) on a new disjoint panel, with identical binary failures and no paired wins. Earlier disjoint panels were 195/200 (97.5%) and 194/200 (97.0%); the permanently reused task-reference result was 196/200 (98.0%) and is not independent confirmation.

**Lessons and limits:** Measured task success, rather than training reward or training success, supports the lineage choice. On episodes 4000–4199, both measured experiment-3 candidates shared the working policy's four failures, all in a negative-angle sector, and neither had a discordant win. The geometry-mixture intervention therefore produced no observed binary improvement on the disjoint panel, although the result does not identify whether the residual is caused by reach selection, settling, or hold-margin control. Across three distinct research panels, the working lineage is 585/600 (97.5%); development measurements remain non-official and do not establish the human objective.

**Open questions:** It remains unresolved whether the recurring negative-angle failures require a change to posture selection, settling control, hold-margin behavior, or another policy capability rather than additional target-distribution coverage. The evidence supports preserving the measured working policy but does not justify an official objective claim or a final benchmark request.

## dd6d401e-1c3b-46d0-8ecf-3b9304fbff2a / Experiment 2

**Result:** The full-radius training intervention produced no measured improvement. `checkpoint-100352` remains the working and best-known lineage.

**Observed behavior:** The working lineage, `checkpoint-105472`, and `checkpoint-120832` each achieved 194/200 successes (97.0%) on the disjoint research panel covering episodes 3000–3199. Both candidates had zero discordant wins against working and shared the same six failed episodes. Three failures were inner-radius targets at about 7.4–7.7 cm with no recorded reach; the other three were negative-angle targets near 17–19 cm, including one missed reach and two brief holds. The experiment-2 training success proxy peaked at 1.0, but this did not translate into higher measured task success.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Weakened. Expanding target-radius exposure did not reduce the targeted inner-workspace failures, although it also did not materially change aggregate success on this panel. Because the conclusion rests on one disjoint panel and no official benchmark was run, the intervention is not conclusively ruled out as a source of behavior change, but it is not supported as a useful recipe for the selected lineage.

**Interpretation:** Coverage of the omitted inner radii was insufficient by itself to fix the residual failures. The shared failure geometry and the occasional short holds indicate that the remaining deficit may involve posture selection, settling, or hold-margin control in addition to reach coverage; the available measurements do not identify the mechanism. The experiment-2 recipe should therefore be reverted while preserving the previously selected policy.

**Interpretation:** The independent panel confirms that the selected policy is close to the human threshold under development measurement, but the earlier 97.0% and 97.5% disjoint results show that one 98.0% panel is insufficient to expect an official `goal_reached` verdict. The candidate checkpoints do not justify replacing the better-supported working lineage or retaining the geometry-mixture recipe. No final benchmark is requested.
