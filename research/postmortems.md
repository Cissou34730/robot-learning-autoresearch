## dd6d401e-1c3b-46d0-8ecf-3b9304fbff2a / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The baseline learned strong reach-and-hold behavior, making `checkpoint-100352` the current working and best-known candidate, but the human objective remains unestablished. It achieved 195/200 (97.5%) on the disjoint research panel and 196/200 (98.0%) on the permanently reused task-reference panel. The baseline training distribution omitted official targets below 14 cm, matching the four reference failures at 6.7–9.9 cm; research-panel failures also concentrated at negative angles and included both missed reaches and interrupted holds.

**Lessons and limits:** Measured task success, rather than training reward or training success, supports the lineage choice. The three candidates were nearly tied on the research panel, with only 1–3 discordant episodes in each paired comparison. The reference panel is permanently reused, and the disjoint research result is below the 98% objective, so neither development result is an official verdict. The evaluator diagnostics show that the residual pattern cannot yet be attributed solely to reaching or stabilization.

**Open questions:** Whether exposure to the complete 6–20 cm target-radius distribution improves inner-workspace reach while preserving outer-radius reach-and-hold performance remains unresolved. It is also unresolved whether the recurring negative-angle failures arise from target coverage, posture selection, settling, or hold-margin control.

**Observed behavior:** The 100352-step checkpoint achieved 195/200 successes (97.5%) on the disjoint `research_evaluation` panel and 196/200 (98.0%) on `task_reference`. The 100352-step candidate had five research-panel failures, while the task-reference failures were all truncated at 500 steps and concentrated at radii below 10 cm with angles from about -128 to -116 degrees. Training success and reward peaked around 100352 steps and were lower at 120832 steps. Paired research-panel comparisons had only 1-3 discordant episodes, so the candidate ranking is useful but not strongly separated.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline established high task performance and the proxy peak corresponded to the strongest protected-panel result, but the independent research panel remained at 97.5%, and the reusable task-reference panel cannot independently confirm the 98.0% result. The evidence supports selecting the 100352-step artifact over the pre-peak and final alternatives, while leaving official objective attainment inconclusive.

**Interpretation:** The frozen scientific model predicts that success requires both inverse-kinematic reaching and a 100-step stable hold under bounded torque. The measured completion times and truncated failures are consistent with a policy that usually reaches and stabilizes, with a residual geometry-specific failure mode rather than broad inability to hold. The late checkpoint does not improve that behavior and appears mildly degraded, so the proxy peak is the most useful saved policy for subsequent work.
