## df7588aa-bb83-4dcf-a7c3-300afb63ee97 / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 98% success on the complete two-second reach-and-hold task. The fresh PPO baseline learned broad competence, but checkpoint-100352 reached 94.5% on each of two disjoint 200-episode research panels. The transferred hold-stability reward change did not improve the policy on a third 200-episode panel: checkpoint-95232 reached 93.5% and checkpoint-120832 92.5%, while the unchanged working policy reached 95.5%. The repeated independent baseline result and the matched-panel control support retaining the unchanged recipe as the current best-known lineage; the reused task-reference result of 98.0% remains relevant but selection-contaminated and does not establish readiness for terminal assessment.

**Lessons and limits:** Training proxies are informative but not acceptance evidence: the modified run reached 0.99 training success and 112.056 reward at checkpoint-95232, then declined to 0.95 and 98.577 by checkpoint-120832, but task success was lower than the unchanged control at both measured checkpoints. On the shared seed-4600 panel, checkpoint-95232 lost four control successes with no reciprocal wins, and checkpoint-120832 lost six with no reciprocal wins; all failures ran to the 500-step limit. This is evidence against the tested transferred reward recipe, not a universal causal claim about every hold penalty or training seed. The task-reference failures were four episodes in a different, reused panel and clustered at target angles from -127.912 to -116.409 degrees; this cannot establish a causal geometry or generalization explanation. The evidence describes saved policies under the current evaluator and does not establish training-process reproducibility or official benchmark performance.

**Open questions:** It remains unresolved whether the residual long-horizon failures are primarily failures to preserve the hold or a broader reach/control limitation; the tested hold-stability reward change did not resolve that distinction. The source of the discrepancy between the reused task-reference panel and disjoint research panels is also unresolved, although the repeated 94.5% research result and the 95.5% matched-panel control make a one-panel fluctuation explanation insufficient. The campaign has not established learning-process reproducibility or official benchmark performance.

**Result:** The baseline produced a useful but not yet objective-satisfying policy. Checkpoint-100352 is selected as both the working and best-known lineage; the recipe is kept for the next experiment, and no final benchmark is requested.

**Observed behavior:** The candidate inventory records 120,832 completed steps. On research panel seed 4200, checkpoint-100352 achieved 189/200 (94.5%), checkpoint-120832 188/200 (94.0%), and checkpoint-86016 187/200 (93.5%); paired comparisons favored checkpoint-100352 by 1 and 2 discordant wins respectively. A disjoint research panel with seed 4400 again measured checkpoint-100352 at 189/200 (94.5%). The fixed task-reference panel measured the same checkpoint at 196/200 (98.0%), with four failures, all truncated at 500 steps and targeting angles from -127.912 to -116.409 degrees. Research-panel failures for the measured candidates also truncated at 500 steps.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The measurement question was whether late training-proxy improvements transferred to the complete reach-and-hold task and which checkpoint was useful for closure. Checkpoint-100352 was better than the two comparison checkpoints on the shared research panel and remained stable on a disjoint panel, supporting transfer of substantial task competence and selecting it as the most useful saved policy. The transfer did not reach 98% on either independent research panel, and continued training to checkpoint-120832 did not improve measured success. The 98.0% task-reference result is a promising but selection-contaminated and non-independent signal, so it strengthens evidence of progress without resolving the independent-panel shortfall. These observations do not identify which training component caused the result.

**Interpretation:** The baseline made meaningful progress toward the human objective, but the measured policy is not supported as meeting the objective across independent development panels. Checkpoint-100352 is the appropriate current reference for further development because it has the best measured independent performance and stable behavior across disjoint panels; the lower measured alternatives do not justify retention as competing lineages. Keeping the recipe preserves the tested baseline for the next ordinary experiment, while deferring terminal assessment avoids treating the task-reference panel as independent confirmation.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Weakened. The expected improvement over the working policy and reduction in timeouts were not observed; both measured modified-reward checkpoints were worse on the matched panel, and the later checkpoint degraded further alongside its training proxies. The hold-stability proposition is weakened under this transferred recipe and training run, but the evidence does not establish that all possible hold penalties or random seeds would fail.

**Interpretation:** The shared-panel control and paired outcomes make panel difficulty an insufficient explanation for the observed shortfall. The measured saved policies therefore do not support keeping the modified reward as the campaign recipe. Because only one transferred training run was tested and the development panels are not the official assessment, the result supports a lineage reversal rather than a universal causal conclusion or an official objective verdict. Further training can start from the unchanged working lineage with its original recipe.
