## 5d4e159f-7fae-4a25-b885-b127042e894e / Scientific strategy

**Direction:** Establish a reliable baseline for the reach-and-hold objective, then improve robustness from the strongest measured checkpoint with mechanisms that do not broaden failures. Experiment 7 provides the first positive development result: nearest-branch IK canonicalization improves both fixed panels while changing the residual failure locations. The immediate action is to benchmark its strongest measured checkpoint officially; subsequent training should target reproducibility or the relocated failures rather than repeat the already-tested radius, redundant direction-feature, lower-rate, action-cost, or smoothing interventions.

**Lessons and limits:** The fresh baseline learned the task late in training, reaching 97.0% on the research panel and 98.0% on the fixed task-reference panel at 100,352 steps; its later 120,832-step checkpoint declined, so checkpoint selection matters. Radius coverage, redundant direction features, a lower learning rate, stronger action regularization, and one-step actuator smoothing all failed as standalone interventions and often added broad reach failures. Experiment 7's fresh canonicalized policy reached 99.0% on research at both 100,352 and 120,832 steps, and 99.0% then 99.5% on task-reference. Against the incumbent, the 100,352-step candidate won 6 and lost 2 of 8 discordant research episodes, improving the panel by 4 episodes (99.0% versus 97.0%); the 120,832-step candidate had the same aggregate research result. Both candidates eliminated the incumbent's six research failures and four task-reference failures, but replaced them with new failures: research failures were at angles 24.1 and -40.4 degrees for 100,352, and 69.4 and 146.0 degrees for 120,832; task-reference failures were two episodes at -38.9 and 153.9 degrees for 100,352, and one episode at -41.3 degrees for 120,832. Thus canonicalization improved aggregate robustness and removed the original negative-angle cluster, but did not eliminate failure risk or prevent all failure relocation. The 120,832-step candidate is preferable among the measured canonicalized checkpoints because it preserves research success and has the strongest task-reference result. Its training success stayed at 1.0 while mean reward fell from 183.2 at 80,896 steps to 130.8 at 100,352 and 114.0 at 120,832, reinforcing that process metrics are not evaluation evidence. The development panels and one fresh training realization do not establish official success, causal attribution, or learning-process reproducibility.

**Open questions:** Whether canonicalization reaches the 98% objective on the official panel remains unknown. It is unresolved whether the development gain is caused by branch canonicalization or partly by the fresh training realization, whether the relocated failures are systematic control-limit failures, and whether another seed reproduces the gain. The two canonicalized checkpoints differ only weakly on the research panel and favor 120,832 on task-reference, so their late-training relationship is not established beyond these fixed panels. The development panels are repeated evidence rather than independent confirmation, and they do not resolve stochastic training variation. The incumbent's official result and the canonicalized policy's official result remain unmeasured.

**Conditional next steps:** Use the 120,832-step canonicalized checkpoint for the official benchmark because it is at least as strong on research and strongest on task-reference. If it passes the official threshold, stop this line with the benchmark verdict. If it fails, retain canonicalization as the working representation and investigate either a reproducibility seed or a narrowly isolated control mechanism for the relocated failures; request another measurement only when it can change that choice. Do not repeat the tested radius, redundant direction-feature, lower-rate, stronger action-cost, or factor-0.5 smoothing changes without a new mechanism.

## 5d4e159f-7fae-4a25-b885-b127042e894e / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh baseline produced a strong but not yet officially validated policy. Checkpoint 100,352 is the measured peak and is selected for continued work.

**Observed behavior:** Training success rose from 0 through the first 70,656 steps, reached 0.97 at 100,352 steps, and was 0.95 at 120,832 steps. On the same 200-episode research panel, checkpoint 100,352 succeeded on 194 episodes (97.0%) and checkpoint 120,832 on 193 (96.5%); the later checkpoint added one failure and did not recover any of the earlier failures. On the fixed task-reference panel, the checkpoints achieved 98.0% and 97.0%, respectively. The earlier checkpoint had four failures, all truncated at 500 steps with target angles between -116 and -128 degrees and target radii between 6.7 and 9.9 cm. The later checkpoint retained those four failures and added two more. Neither result is the official benchmark.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The baseline expectation of establishing a competent initial policy was supported: learning was substantial and the best checkpoint approached the objective. The contradicting observation is that the completed 120,000-step run did not improve monotonically and both development results remained below the objective on at least one panel. Because this was a baseline with no intervention, the evidence does not attribute the outcome to a method change or establish reproducibility.

**Interpretation:** The decline after the 100,352-step peak makes checkpoint selection consequential and argues against continuing from the final checkpoint by default. The paired research-panel comparison favors 100,352 by one episode, while the task-reference comparison favors it by two; together these are sufficient to choose the earlier policy for lineage, but not to claim generalization or explain causality. The persistent task-reference failures suggest a targeted angular/near-base robustness question for future training.

The paired results provide enough evidence to reject radius coverage as the next working intervention and to avoid retaining the candidate. The late-training decline also argues against selecting the final checkpoint merely because it was trained longer.

**Result:** Increasing the action-cost coefficient during transfer contradicted the control-regularization hypothesis. The candidate was materially worse on both development panels, so the experiment-1 checkpoint remains the working and best-known lineage and the action-cost change is reverted.

**Observed behavior:** The transferred checkpoint-100352 candidate used the baseline PPO learning rate and increased the action-cost coefficient from 0.01 to 0.05. **Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis that stronger action regularization would reduce excessive torque changes near difficult negative-angle targets while preserving incumbent success is contradicted. It neither preserved the incumbent's success nor improved the localized failure pattern: all incumbent research failures persisted and the task-reference candidate added 11 failures despite fixing one. This conclusion is limited to the tested coefficient 0.05, transferred initialization, PPO recipe, single training realization, and development panels; it does not establish that every control-aware reward or action strategy is ineffective, nor does it determine the official benchmark result.

**Interpretation:** The larger action penalty changed behavior broadly rather than repairing the suspected localized control problem.
