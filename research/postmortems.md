## dd6d401e-1c3b-46d0-8ecf-3b9304fbff2a / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The baseline learned strong reach-and-hold behavior, making `checkpoint-100352` the current working and best-known candidate, but the human objective remains unestablished. Two transfer interventions changed target-radius exposure and then target-geometry frequency without improving the selected lineage, and the fresh branch-feasibility observation intervention caused a large regression. Across five distinct research panels, working is 979/1000 (97.9%), including 195/200 on the latest panel; its latest failures were three complete missed reaches and two one-step holds. The experiment-4 challengers achieved only 147/200 and 146/200 on the same panel, with no challenger-only successes, and every recorded first reach for both challengers was still classified as open rather than folded.

**Lessons and limits:** Measured task success, rather than training reward or training success, supports the lineage choice. Changing target coverage alone did not change the recurring failure set, and explicit branch-feasibility indicators did not make the fresh policy select the folded branch; instead they coincided with broad reach-and-hold degradation. This contradicts the tested intervention's behavioral hypothesis, but does not establish whether the added features were ignored, poorly integrated, or insufficient to solve the residual control problem. Two historical failures had both ordinary branches feasible. Development measurements remain non-official and do not establish the human objective.

**Open questions:** The working policy's remaining failures may require a different controller, trajectory, settling, or hold-margin intervention rather than more target exposure or the tested observation extension. It remains unresolved whether a different integration of branch information could help without sacrificing broad behavior, and whether the close development results would meet the official panel's 98% requirement.

## dd6d401e-1c3b-46d0-8ecf-3b9304fbff2a / Experiment 2

**Result:** The full-radius training intervention produced no measured improvement. `checkpoint-100352` remains the working and best-known lineage.

The experiment-2 training success proxy peaked at 1.0, but this did not translate into higher measured task success.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Weakened. Expanding target-radius exposure did not reduce the targeted inner-workspace failures, although it also did not materially change aggregate success on this panel. Because the conclusion rests on one disjoint panel and no official benchmark was run, the intervention is not conclusively ruled out as a source of behavior change, but it is not supported as a useful recipe for the selected lineage.

**Interpretation:** Coverage of the omitted inner radii was insufficient by itself to fix the residual failures. The shared failure geometry and the occasional short holds indicate that the remaining deficit may involve posture selection, settling, or hold-margin control in addition to reach coverage; the available measurements do not identify the mechanism. The experiment-2 recipe should therefore be reverted while preserving the previously selected policy.

**Result:** The explicit inverse-kinematic branch-feasibility observation intervention was not useful. The existing `working` and `best_known` lineage remains selected, and the experiment-4 recipe should be reverted.

**Observed behavior:** On the new disjoint episodes 6000-6199 panel, `working` achieved 195/200 (97.5%), while `checkpoint-115712` achieved 147/200 (73.5%) and `checkpoint-120832` achieved 146/200 (73.0%). The working failures were three missed reaches and two episodes that reached tolerance for only one held step. The challengers produced 53 and 54 failures respectively, spanning inner and outer targets; all 153 and 151 recorded first reaches were classified as open, with none classified as folded.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted under the tested fresh training recipe. Adding explicit open- and folded-branch feasibility indicators did not improve complete reach-and-hold success or induce folded-branch first reaches; it instead caused a 24.0-24.5 percentage-point regression against the same-panel working reference. This establishes the intervention as unsuitable for the selected lineage in this form, but does not distinguish an unused representation from an inadequate control or training integration.

**Interpretation:** The frozen model identifies branch selection, bounded-torque trajectory control, settling, and the uninterrupted hold as separate behavioral requirements. The new indicators supplied branch feasibility information, but the measured challengers retained open-branch first reaches and lost broad task competence, so the evidence does not support replacing the mature working policy. The residual working-policy problem remains open, and the development evidence remains below what is needed to claim the official 98% objective.
