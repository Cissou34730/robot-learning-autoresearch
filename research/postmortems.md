# Research postmortems

## 768a395f-05a2-43c3-b1ef-449056fbf8ed / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign has a useful PPO reach-and-hold policy but no established official result: the frozen final assessment was rejected, and development panels remain distinct from the official panel. The experiment-1 best-known checkpoint reached 98% on task-reference-v1 and 98% pooled success across the two researcher panels. Experiment 2 transferred that policy while expanding training radii to the official 6-20 cm range; its selected checkpoint reached 98% on task-reference-v1 and 99% on the researcher panel, but aggregate success did not improve and the failure locations shifted. Experiment 3's transferred angle oversampling regressed both development measurements and did not remove the repeated negative-angle failures. Experiment 4's fresh replication of the unchanged full-radius recipe scored 96.5% on task-reference-v1 and 96% on the matched researcher panel at both measured checkpoints, below experiment 2. The experiment-2 working policy is therefore still the strongest measured working candidate, while run-to-run variability, the residual failure mechanism, and the official objective remain unresolved.

**Lessons and limits:** Training proxy success and reward peaked before the end of both runs and are not task-acceptance evidence. The experiment-1 task-reference failures clustered at small radii and negative angles. Experiment 2 corrected radius coverage, removed one small-radius failure, and preserved the task-reference score, but its researcher panel still failed at 13.9-18.2 cm and about -137 to -149 degrees; its task-reference failures still included -116 to -128 degrees and one -154.8-degree case. Experiment 3's selected checkpoint scored 95% on the researcher panel and 94% on task-reference-v1, versus 98% and 98% for its measured parent; the endpoint scored 86.5% and 93%. Experiment 4's fresh checkpoint-100352 and endpoint both scored 96% on the researcher panel; checkpoint-100352 scored 96.5% on task-reference-v1, with failures including four positive-angle targets around 144-149 degrees, two small-radius positive-angle targets, one outer negative-angle target, and no exact reproduction of experiment 2's four failures. On the matched researcher episodes, the experiment-2 parent won 8 comparisons to experiment 4's 2, with 10 discordant episodes; this supports a practical lineage choice but does not estimate the full distribution of run variance. The observation already contains continuous target-relative geometry and both inverse-kinematics branches, so the evidence does not justify treating missing angle coordinates or tensor size as the established cause. Fixed development panels, one fresh replication, and one transferred intervention cannot separate directional coverage from control, representation, inherited weights, or other learning variation, and neither development evidence nor the rejected final assessment establishes official success. Sources: `research/results.jsonl`, `research/research_state.json`, `research/postmortems.md`, the experiment-1 through experiment-4 detailed artifacts under `research/evaluations/768a395f-05a2-43c3-b1ef-449056fbf8ed/`, the experiment-4 training log, `robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`, and `robot_learning/scenario/observations.py`.

**Open questions:** It remains unknown whether the repeated negative-angle failures are primarily caused by a directional control or representation limitation, by run-to-run variation, or by another training dynamic; experiment 3 weakened but did not prove the alternative that sector exposure was the main cause. Experiment 4 weakens reproducibility of experiment 2's strong full-radius result from fresh learning, but its single fresh run cannot distinguish run variance from inherited representation or other learning dynamics. It is also unresolved whether a policy can maintain strong positive-angle and outer-radius behavior while improving the residual failures. The official 98% objective remains unresolved, and development evidence cannot substitute for the distinct final panel.

## 768a395f-05a2-43c3-b1ef-449056fbf8ed / Experiment 1

**Result:** The baseline produced a useful near-objective policy. Checkpoint-100352 is selected for closure because it achieved 98% on the protected task-reference panel; the final benchmark is requested to determine the official result.

**Observed behavior:** Training logs show proxy success increasing from 0.00 at step 70656 to 0.98 at step 99328, then declining to 0.95 at step 120832; mean reward similarly declined from 168 at step 87040 to 117 at step 100352 and 112 at step 120832. These are training signals, not task-acceptance measurements. On task-reference-v1, checkpoint-90112 scored 93% (186/200), checkpoint-100352 scored 98% (196/200), and checkpoint-120832 scored 97% (194/200). On researcher evaluation, checkpoint-100352 scored 97% on seeds 0-199 and 99% on seeds 200-399; checkpoint-120832 scored 99% on seeds 200-399. The paired comparison on seeds 200-399 found no discordant outcomes and no wins for either checkpoint. The four checkpoint-100352 task-reference failures were all small-radius targets (6.7-9.9 cm) at angles from -116.4 to -127.9 degrees; the endpoint retained those failures and added two failures, including one at +169.1 degrees. Twenty-two of the twenty-four saved checkpoints remain unmeasured.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Supported for the baseline's intended purpose of establishing a strong initial reference: the learned policy substantially improved measured task success and reached 98% on one independent development panel. This does not establish the official objective, and the endpoint's research-panel result does not overturn the protected-panel regression because the paired research outcomes were identical and the panels are distinct. The assessment is limited to this single training run and the measured checkpoints.

**Interpretation:** The saved policy demonstrates measured progress toward the human objective and is ready for the official frozen-policy assessment. Checkpoint-100352 is preferable to the endpoint on the protected development panel and has comparable independent research performance. The failure concentration suggests a residual directional/near-radius weakness, but the evidence does not justify attributing it to a particular reward, observation, or training mechanism.

## 768a395f-05a2-43c3-b1ef-449056fbf8ed / Experiment 2

**Result:** The full 6-20 cm training-radius intervention was partially supported as a task-level refinement but did not produce a measured aggregate improvement over the selected baseline policy. Checkpoint-100352 is selected as the working candidate; the experiment-1 best-known lineage remains unchanged.

**Observed behavior:** Experiment 2 was transferred from the experiment-1 best-known policy and trained for 120,832 local steps with the official 6-20 cm radius range. On the researcher evaluator, checkpoints 65,536, 100,352, and 120,832 each scored 99% on the same 200-episode seed-200 panel, with the same two failures (episodes 42 and 174). On task-reference-v1, all three checkpoints scored 98% (196/200). Checkpoint-100352 failed on episodes 10, 84, and 102 at 7.2-9.9 cm and negative angles, plus episode 175 at 18.2 cm and -154.8 degrees. Relative to experiment-1 checkpoint-100352, it removed the 6.7 cm failure at episode 0 but added the outer-radius episode-175 failure, leaving total success unchanged. The experiment-2 endpoint also scored 98%, compared with 97% for the experiment-1 endpoint, but its failures shifted to one small-radius case and three outer-radius cases rather than demonstrating a uniform improvement. Training proxy success reached 0.99 around steps 64,512-68,608 and was 0.97 at step 120,832; these proxy and reward signals were not used as task-acceptance evidence.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. Full-radius exposure removed one of the selected baseline's small-radius failures at checkpoint-100352 and preserved 98% task-reference success, while the endpoint recovered one percentage point relative to the experiment-1 endpoint. However, the selected candidate gained an outer-radius failure with no net success gain, the repeated negative-angle failure at episode 102 persisted, and researcher-evaluation outcomes were unchanged across measured checkpoints. The single transferred run and one fixed development panel do not establish that radius coverage caused the observed redistribution or that the intervention improves the official task distribution.

**Interpretation:** The intervention addresses a real training-distribution mismatch and is compatible with the learned policy, but the measurements do not show that radius coverage alone resolves the residual failures. Checkpoint-100352 is a reasonable working candidate because it matches the prior best-known task-reference score while avoiding the endpoint's measured task regression; the prior best-known remains the evidence-backed best-known designation because the new candidate has no superior comparable success result. The full-range recipe is kept as the working recipe for a later ordinary experiment, while the official objective remains unresolved and development panels remain distinct from the final assessment.

## 768a395f-05a2-43c3-b1ef-449056fbf8ed / Experiment 3

**Result:** The angle-coverage intervention was contradicted under the measured conditions. The selected checkpoint regressed relative to the experiment-2 working parent, and the endpoint regressed further; the prior working lineage remains the useful working policy and the angle-focused recipe is not retained.

**Observed behavior:** Experiment 3 transferred the experiment-2 working policy and trained for 120,832 local steps with the full radius range and 50% of training targets in the -170 to -90 degree sector. Training proxy success reached 0.97 at intermediate checkpoints, was 0.95 at checkpoint-100352, and was 0.93 at checkpoint-120832; recorded mean reward was 104.50 and 107.85 at those two checkpoints respectively. These are training signals, not task-acceptance measurements. On the matched researcher panel, the parent scored 98%, checkpoint-100352 scored 95%, and the endpoint scored 86.5%. On task-reference-v1, the parent scored 98%, checkpoint-100352 scored 94%, and the endpoint scored 93%. The selected checkpoint retained three of the parent's four researcher failures, fixed one, and added seven. Its twelve task-reference failures included six in the targeted negative-angle sector and six outside it; the repeated negative-angle cases were not selectively removed. The endpoint retained all four parent researcher failures and added 23 more.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted for this single transferred run and these fixed development panels. The expected selective improvement in negative-angle outcomes while preserving aggregate success was not observed: both measured checkpoints were below the parent on both panels, and the selected task-reference failures extended beyond the oversampled sector. This weakens the explanation that insufficient sector exposure was the primary residual cause. It does not establish that directional control or representation is causal, because the intervention was tested in one run and the remaining checkpoints were unmeasured.

**Interpretation:** Measured task behavior supports the experiment-2 parent over both measured experiment-3 candidates, so restoring the parent's complete scientific recipe is the appropriate closure decision. The divergence between the endpoint's higher recorded mean reward and its lower measured success further demonstrates that proxy training signals cannot select a policy for the human objective. The experiment-3 result is evidence against this focused-sampling recipe under the tested conditions, not evidence that no other angle, control, representation, or training intervention can help.

## 768a395f-05a2-43c3-b1ef-449056fbf8ed / Experiment 4

**Result:** The fresh replication did not reproduce experiment 2's strong full-radius development behavior. Checkpoint-100352 is not a useful replacement for the experiment-2 working lineage, and the endpoint provides no measured researcher-panel advantage.

**Observed behavior:** Experiment 4 replicated the unchanged full-radius PPO recipe from fresh initialization for 120,832 local steps. Training proxy success rose to 0.97 at steps 109,568-112,640, then declined to 0.93 at the endpoint; mean reward peaked at 156.63 at step 70,656 and was 116.01 at checkpoint-100352 and 115.22 at the endpoint. These are training signals, not task-acceptance measurements. On the matched 200-episode researcher panel, checkpoint-100352 and the endpoint each scored 96%, versus 99% for the experiment-2 working parent. The paired comparison had 10 discordant episodes, with 8 parent wins and 2 replication wins (net -6, exact p=0.109375). On task-reference-v1, checkpoint-100352 scored 96.5% (193/200). Its seven failures included targets at 18.0 cm/+148.0 degrees, 19.5 cm/-175.4 degrees, 16.5 cm/+149.1 degrees, 9.1 cm/+127.8 degrees, 18.0 cm/+144.4 degrees, 7.9 cm/+125.3 degrees, and 11.5 cm/+131.5 degrees. The endpoint retained six of checkpoint-100352's eight researcher-panel failures, fixed two, and added two, leaving its aggregate score unchanged. The endpoint was not measured on task-reference-v1. Twenty-two of twenty-four checkpoints remain unmeasured.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted for this fresh run and the measured development panels, with the conclusion scoped to this replication. The expected approximately 98% task-reference and near-99% matched researcher results were not observed: the measured checkpoint was 96.5% and both researcher measurements were 96%, while the parent was 99% on the same researcher panel. The broader shift in task-reference failure geometry and the parent's paired advantage support the alternative that the strong experiment-2 outcome was not a reproducible property of this fresh run. This does not establish whether run-to-run variation, inherited weights, or another learning dynamic caused the difference, and unmeasured checkpoints cannot be treated as failures.

**Interpretation:** Measured task behavior supports retaining experiment 2 checkpoint-100352 as the working lineage rather than selecting either experiment-4 checkpoint. The late proxy decline is consistent with no endpoint task improvement, but the endpoint's equal researcher score and the divergence between proxy signals and measured success do not establish a causal training explanation. The experiment-4 replication weakens confidence that the full-radius recipe alone reliably produces the near-objective result, while the experiment-2 candidate remains the strongest measured working policy and experiment 1 remains the evidence-backed best-known designation. The official objective remains unresolved, so terminal assessment is not requested.
