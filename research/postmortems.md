# Research postmortems

## e396d2fc-96c5-4690-8031-b9a7969d0c0c / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The campaign objective is at least 196 successes in the official 200-episode panel for the unchanged two-joint-arm reach-and-hold task. The unchanged PPO baseline learned effective reaching behavior, but its strongest measured checkpoint, `100352`, reached 97.0% on the independent research panel and 98.0% on the task-reference panel; this does not establish the official objective. Later checkpoints were no better on the independent panel, and the transferred hold-forfeit reward in experiment 2 did not improve the residual failures before late regression. The current evidence therefore describes a capable but not yet robust policy, with the experiment-1 `checkpoint-100352` lineage as the strongest measured candidate.

**Lessons and limits:** Training success and reward are useful for locating candidate checkpoints but are not task-performance claims. In experiment 1, logged success rose to 0.98 around 99,328 steps, then declined to 0.95 by 120,832 steps; independent research evaluation likewise declined from 97.0% at 100,352 to 96.0% at 120,832. The task-reference panel is development evidence rather than the official assessment. Its four failures at checkpoints `100352` and `110592` were the same fixed negative-angle cases, from about -116 to -128 degrees and radii from about 6.7 to 9.9 cm, with final distances around 0.99 to 1.33 cm. The independent panel had six failures for checkpoint `100352`, all truncated, and the matching experiment-2 checkpoint had the same six episode outcomes in the paired comparison. Experiment 2's later checkpoint instead had broad regression, so the changed reward did not isolate or repair the residual pattern. These are single-panel, single-seed development measurements; they do not establish seed variance, causal mechanism, or the official result.

**Open questions:** The prevalence of the negative-angle residual region on the official target distribution, the contribution of hold-continuity learning versus geometry-specific control limitations, and the variability of the near-98% result across training seeds remain unresolved. It is also unknown whether a fresh run learns a comparably useful checkpoint or whether the observed capability is seed- and checkpoint-specific. No next action is prescribed by this memory.

## e396d2fc-96c5-4690-8031-b9a7969d0c0c / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh baseline produced a useful but not yet official-objective-ready policy. `checkpoint-100352` is selected as both working and best-known lineage.

**Observed behavior:** Training logs show no successes through 70,656 steps, then a rapid rise in logged success from 0.06 at 75,776 to 0.97 at 100,352. Logged success subsequently fluctuated between 0.93 and 0.96 and ended at 0.95, while mean episode reward peaked at 163.85 around 86,016 and fell to 112.02 by 120,832. On the independent 200-episode research panel, checkpoints `100352`, `110592`, and `120832` achieved 97.0%, 96.5%, and 96.0%, respectively. On the fixed 200-episode task-reference panel they achieved 98.0%, 98.0%, and 97.0%, respectively. The two strongest task-reference checkpoints shared four failures at negative angles between -116.4 and -127.9 degrees; the final checkpoint added two failures. All task-reference failures ran to the 500-step limit.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. As an automatic fresh baseline, the experiment established that the unchanged method can learn a policy with high measured task performance and identified a useful checkpoint near 100,352 steps. It did not establish that the learned policy reliably satisfies the 98% objective: the strongest independent measurement was 97.0%, and the task-reference result is development evidence on a distinct fixed panel. The non-monotonic late training metrics and the lower independent-panel results weaken any inference that continuing this baseline recipe to the final checkpoint improves the task policy. These conclusions concern the saved checkpoints and measured panels, not unmeasured checkpoints or the official final panel.

**Interpretation:** The baseline made substantial progress toward the human objective, but residual failures and late-training degradation make `checkpoint-100352` preferable to the later saved checkpoints. The recipe itself is unchanged and should be kept for provenance; no alternative artifact is retained because the later candidates have weaker independent measurements and no distinct demonstrated advantage.

## e396d2fc-96c5-4690-8031-b9a7969d0c0c / Experiment 2

**Result:** The transferred hold-forfeit reward did not improve the measured policy over the existing working lineage. `checkpoint-95232` reached 97.0% on the independent 200-episode panel, tying the parent, and 97.5% on the task-reference panel, below the parent's 98.0%. `checkpoint-105472` reached 90.5% and 89.0% on those panels, respectively. The working and best-known lineage remain the experiment-1 `checkpoint-100352`.

**Observed behavior:** The experiment-2 logs began with high training-proxy success, reached 100% at 35,840 steps, and remained around 0.98-0.99 near checkpoints 90,112-109,568, while mean reward was about 103 at 35,840 and 105,472. Both proxies declined late, ending at 0.95 success and about 98 mean reward at 120,832. These are training signals, not task-performance claims. On the independent panel, `checkpoint-95232` had six failures, all truncated at 500 steps, matching the parent's six failures in the paired comparison with no discordant outcomes. On task-reference, `checkpoint-95232` had five truncated failures: the four previously observed negative-angle cases persisted, and one additional failure occurred at about -66 degrees. The later `checkpoint-105472` had 19 independent-panel failures and 22 task-reference failures, all truncated; its paired independent comparison lost 13 of 13 discordant episodes to the parent. The fixed task-reference panel is development evidence and neither panel is the official final assessment.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Weakened. The expected improvement in independent-panel success and reduction of negative-angle failures did not occur at the strongest measured experiment-2 checkpoint, although that checkpoint preserved the parent's independent-panel outcomes and most of the previously successful task-reference behavior. The later checkpoint showed broad regression rather than a focused hold improvement. This weakens the hold-continuity explanation under the tested transferred recipe and leaves geometry-specific control or representation limits as plausible alternatives; it does not identify a causal mechanism because this was one changed-reward training run and checkpoints also differ in training time.

**Interpretation:** The intervention supplied no measured progress toward the 98% objective and did not resolve the residual failure pattern. The high training proxies are orthogonal evidence that the transferred run learned and retained a capable policy, but they do not justify selecting its artifacts. Restoring the parent recipe preserves the strongest measured candidate while leaving future work free to address the persistent geometry and hold failures with a different investigation.
