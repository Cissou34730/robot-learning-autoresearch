# Research postmortems

## 614b8ee6-6a63-4ee9-b948-d4fb9064d6be / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The retained baseline is still the only broadly competent controller measured, with 1705/1760 successes across nine distinct research panels. Its latest panel scored 192/200, below the 196/200 objective threshold, and remains development evidence. The early experiment-5 checkpoint matched it episode-for-episode; the final checkpoint fell to 177/200. Residual failures cluster in a negative-angle sector but include both non-entry and interrupted holds, consistent with coupled approach, entry, settling, and regulation behavior.

**Lessons and limits:** Training reward and proxy success did not establish complete-task performance. Full-radius training, explicit branch-limit observations, a soft joint-limit reward, and focused negative-angle exposure did not improve complete behavior under their tested conditions, weakening those explanations without isolating the regressions. Joint-limit crossing remains a correlate rather than an established cause because successful episodes also approach or cross the measured boundary. The direct-torque dynamics, singular reset, alternative postures, and 100-step uninterrupted hold keep approach, braking, settling, and regulation physically coupled.

**Open questions:** The evidence does not distinguish posture choice, torque trajectory and braking, settling, local regulation, or their interactions with joint limits. It also does not establish how target angle and radius relate to tolerance entry versus sustained holding, or whether the negative-angle pattern reflects geometry, trajectory, or regulation history rather than exposure.

## 614b8ee6-6a63-4ee9-b948-d4fb9064d6be / Experiment 2

**Result:** The full official target-radius training distribution did not improve complete reach-and-hold success and is closed without selecting either challenger.

**Observed behavior:** On the fresh research panel covering episode seeds 4800-4999, checkpoint-100352 and checkpoint-120832 each achieved 118/200 successes (59.0%). The retained baseline achieved 197/200 (98.5%) on the same panel; each challenger lost all 79 discordant paired episodes to that reference, and the challengers were tied on every paired outcome. Training proxies increased from 0.49 to 0.60 success between the measured checkpoints, but this did not correspond to improved complete-task behavior. The detailed challenger traces contain both episodes that never enter tolerance and episodes that enter without completing the uninterrupted hold.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted under the tested fresh initialization, training recipe, checkpoints, and panel. Training on the full official radius interval did not produce the predicted overall complete-success improvement, and the available result does not show an inner-radius benefit; both measured challengers were 39.5 percentage points below the baseline reference. The conclusion is bounded by the single fresh evaluation panel and does not identify which coupled physical behavior caused the regression or rule out a different intervention.

**Interpretation:** The unchanged baseline remains the useful working and best-known lineage, while the experiment-2 radius change is not supported as a path toward the human objective. The unchanged result between matched and final challenger checkpoints indicates that the higher training proxy and additional late training did not recover the measured policy behavior. Because success couples approach, tolerance entry, settling, and sustained regulation, the 59% outcome should not be assigned to radius coverage alone as a single isolated stage failure.

**Observed behavior:** The intervention changed only training target-angle sampling, using a 50/50 mixture of full-circle angles and angles from -170 to -110 degrees while leaving the radius range, evaluation, and official task unchanged. On episode seeds 5800-5999, the early checkpoint achieved 192/200 successes, exactly matching `best_known`'s success outcomes with zero discordant episodes. The final degradation therefore extended beyond the targeted angle band and did not preserve complete-task competence.

Additional exposure did not improve the recurrent negative-angle behavior at the early checkpoint and was associated with broad complete-task regression at the final checkpoint. This conclusion is bounded to the tested training trajectory and panel; it does not establish that angle exposure caused the regression or identify whether geometry, posture, direct-torque trajectory, braking, settling, or sustained regulation is responsible for the residual failures.

**Interpretation:** The unchanged `best_known` policy remains the strongest available lineage, but its 1705/1760 aggregate development success and latest 192/200 panel do not establish the 196/200 human objective or justify an official assessment. The shared negative-angle failures and mixed entry/hold outcomes are consistent with the Scientific model's coupled approach, inverse-kinematic posture, braking, settling, and regulation constraints. Restore the baseline scientific recipe rather than carrying the sampler change forward; further training would be a separate future experiment, not part of this closure.
