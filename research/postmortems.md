# Research postmortems

## 8c345590-23d1-446d-b3de-14fca7c9df5d / Scientific strategy

**Current synthesis:** The tanh [64, 64] PPO recipe learns useful complete-task behavior: the selected policy reached 391/400 (97.75%) on the latest disjoint panel and 2144/2200 (97.5%) across six distinct research panels. Increasing action cost during transfer weakened the policy, broadening the training radius range produced two candidates tied with the parent and no paired advantage, and oversampling the observed negative-angle band produced no improvement followed by broad regression. The selected policy remains below the 98% objective in pooled development evidence, and the reused task-reference result is not independent confirmation.

**Lessons and limits:** Training reward and proxy success do not establish the uninterrupted 100-step hold. Checkpoint-100352 remains the strongest saved policy: the angle-focused checkpoint-5120 scored 390/400 versus 391/400 for the parent with zero candidate wins and one parent win, while checkpoint-120832 scored 364/400 with 27 parent wins and zero candidate wins. The early candidate retained the negative-angle failure pattern, and the final candidate added failures at inner radii and positive angles. The diagnostic panels show entry, brief-hold, saturation and high-speed behavior, but do not identify branch choice or sampled stabilization as the cause. The frozen model establishes that the official radii are within the annular workspace and that the arm is a bounded 50 Hz inertial task with a 2-second hold, not that angle-focused sampling caused or could prevent the failures.

**Open questions:** The residual negative-angle failures remain unresolved between angle-conditioned learning, branch and trajectory choice, and sampled stabilization. Experiment 4 does not justify selecting the angle-focused recipe, and the 22 unmeasured candidates remain unmeasured rather than failed. The 98% objective has not been established; further development would need a new intervention rather than treating the unresolved physical mechanism or training proxies as evidence.

## 8c345590-23d1-446d-b3de-14fca7c9df5d / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh PPO baseline learned a useful policy but did not meet the human objective. Checkpoint-100352 is the best measured candidate and is selected as both working and best-known for continued development.

**Observed behavior:** With the unchanged tanh [64, 64] PPO recipe, training success was zero through the early checkpoints, rose to 0.42 at 86016 steps, peaked at 0.97 at 100352 steps, and was 0.95 at 120832 steps. Checkpoint-100352 achieved 195/200 (97.5%) on research episodes 2000-2199 and 387/400 (96.75%) on disjoint episodes 2200-2599, for 582/600 (97.0%) pooled success. Checkpoint-120832 achieved 194/200 (97.0%) and 384/400 (96.0%) on those panels. The paired comparison over 600 distinct research episodes was 4-0 in discordant wins for checkpoint-100352. On the reused task-reference panel, checkpoint-100352 scored 98.0% and checkpoint-120832 scored 97.0%; this panel is not independent confirmation. The four task-reference failures of checkpoint-100352 were all 500-step truncations and occupied a narrow inner-target region.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline established substantial learned task performance, and the training-proxy peak identified the strongest of the measured candidates. It did not establish the required at-least-98% success, and the final checkpoint was weaker than the earlier selected checkpoint under both disjoint research panels. These conclusions apply to the measured checkpoints and panels; the 21 unmeasured candidates remain unmeasured rather than failed.

**Interpretation:** The baseline is a useful saved starting point, not a terminal policy. The failure cluster is consistent with a difficulty in the sampled stabilization problem, but the frozen model cannot identify the policy mechanism: the arm is a sampled 50 Hz inertial system, and success requires remaining within the 1 cm disk for 100 consecutive control steps. Because no trajectory diagnostics were recorded, branch selection, braking, overshoot, and sustained regulation remain competing explanations. The disjoint comparison is sufficient to select checkpoint-100352 over checkpoint-120832 for closure, while the sub-objective success rates argue against requesting the official benchmark now.

## 8c345590-23d1-446d-b3de-14fca7c9df5d / Experiment 2

**Result:** Continuing the selected policy with `ACTION_COST_COEFFICIENT` increased from 0.01 to 0.05 did not improve complete reach-and-hold behavior. On the shared episodes 3000-3399, the unchanged parent achieved 389/400 (97.25%), while checkpoints 75776 and 120832 achieved 357/400 (89.25%) and 360/400 (90.0%).

**Observed behavior:** The parent had 11 failures on this panel. Checkpoint-75776 had 43 failures, including all 11 parent failures plus 32 additional failures; checkpoint-120832 had 40 failures, including all 11 parent failures plus 29 additional failures. The paired comparisons therefore gave the parent 32 and 29 discordant wins, respectively, with zero wins for either changed checkpoint. The training log still showed proxy success of 0.99 at checkpoint-75776 and 0.97 at the final checkpoint, so those proxies did not predict complete-task success. The new candidate artifacts contain episode outcomes but no trajectory diagnostics.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Contradicted for the tested transfer recipe and measured checkpoints. The hypothesis predicted reduced persistent saturation, improved complete-task success, and no material degradation of successful behavior; both measured changed checkpoints instead regressed substantially against the unchanged parent on the same panel. This does not prove that action cost can never help, and the 22 unmeasured checkpoints remain unmeasured rather than failed. It also does not isolate the coefficient from the continued-learning package or identify the learned failure mechanism.

**Interpretation:** The action-cost continuation is not a useful replacement for the established parent under the tested conditions. The frozen model establishes bounded motor commands, 50 Hz zero-order-held control, a 1 cm tolerance, and a 100-step hold, while the official target radii remain inside the physical annulus; it does not establish why the changed policy regressed. Because the parent remains the strongest measured lineage and the measured challengers add broad failures rather than repairing parent failures, closure should restore the parent's recipe and retain the parent as working and best-known. The distinct unmeasured checkpoint-100352 is retained only to preserve a possible future comparison, not as evidence of progress.

## 8c345590-23d1-446d-b3de-14fca7c9df5d / Experiment 3

**Result:** Broadening the training target-radius sampler from 0.14-0.20 m to the full official 0.06-0.20 m range did not improve complete reach-and-hold success. The unchanged `best_known` and both measured transfer candidates scored 388/400 (97.0%) on the disjoint panel, so the parent remains working and best-known and the altered recipe is not retained.

**Observed behavior:** The experiment used the unchanged PPO configuration from `research/current_params.json`; the training log reached a proxy success of 1.0 at checkpoint-105472 and ended at 0.94 at checkpoint-120832. The detailed evaluations recorded 12 failures for each measured model, all truncated at 500 steps and concentrated at target angles from -156.4 to -120.1 degrees. The radius-panel diagnostics showed the candidates repaired the parent's failure at episode seed 3589 (radius 12.24 cm) but introduced a failure at seed 3701 (radius 19.97 cm); the low-radius failures near 7.1, 7.2, and 11.0 cm persisted. The paired comparisons were one candidate win and one parent win for each candidate.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Weakened for the tested transfer recipe and measured checkpoints. Full-radius exposure did not improve aggregate success, did not show a reduction of the negative-angle failure pattern, and did not remove the inner-radius failures; it also did not produce a broad regression on this panel. This does not prove that radius coverage can never help, because only two checkpoints and one disjoint panel were measured, but it provides no evidence to replace the established parent or continue this recipe.

**Interpretation:** The unchanged parent is the useful saved policy for subsequent development. The frozen model establishes that the newly covered radii are physically feasible and that success requires sampled inertial stabilization, while the measurements show only that this transfer under the changed sampler did not improve the realized policy. The failure exchange between one inner-radius episode and one outer-radius episode, together with persistent saturation and closest-branch diagnostics, does not identify branch choice or stabilization as the cause; another training intervention should therefore be chosen only after a decision-changing measurement or with its competing explanations stated.

## 8c345590-23d1-446d-b3de-14fca7c9df5d / Experiment 4

**Result:** The angle-focused transfer did not improve complete reach-and-hold behavior and is not selected. The working and best-known lineage remain checkpoint-100352 with the established recipe restored.

**Observed behavior:** The changed sampler retained radii 0.14-0.20 m and drew half of targets from angles -2.8 to -2.0 radians. On the new disjoint research panel, `best_known` achieved 391/400 (97.75%), checkpoint-5120 achieved 390/400 (97.5%), and checkpoint-120832 achieved 364/400 (91.0%). The paired comparisons gave `best_known` one discordant win and no wins for checkpoint-5120, and 27 discordant wins and no wins for checkpoint-120832. The parent had nine failures, all at negative angles from -150.9 to -127.2 degrees; checkpoint-5120 had ten failures and retained that pattern, while checkpoint-120832 had 36 failures including inner-radius and positive-angle failures. The run's training-success proxy declined from 0.973 at checkpoint-5120 to 0.77 at checkpoint-120832, but these proxies are not complete-task measurements.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Weakened for the tested transfer recipe and measured checkpoints. Oversampling the observed negative-angle band produced neither the predicted improvement in that band nor preservation of broad behavior through the continuation. The conclusion is limited to the measured checkpoints and panel; the 22 unmeasured candidates remain unmeasured rather than failed.

**Interpretation:** The measurements answer the lineage decision: the angle-focused recipe should not replace the parent. The frozen model establishes that the targets are inside the annular workspace and that the task requires bounded 50 Hz inertial control and a continuous 100-step hold, so the failures are not established as unreachable; it does not identify whether the learned shortfall is angle-conditioned learning, branch or trajectory choice, or sampled stabilization. No additional measurement is requested because the new disjoint panel already tested the planned intervention against its saved reference, while the fixed task-reference panel would be reused and training proxies do not make an unmeasured middle checkpoint a decision-ready replacement.
