# Research postmortems

## 9f0c1a9e-23c2-4c48-85a6-09cb6d902999 / Scientific strategy

**Direction:** Improve robustness on the unchanged official reach-and-hold task, with particular attention to the residual small-radius, negative-angle failures seen in the task-reference panel. The baseline establishes that the current PPO recipe can solve nearly all measured targets, but it does not establish that training proxy success is a reliable substitute for task success or that late training preserves the best policy.

**Lessons and limits:** The automatic baseline reached 98% task-reference success at checkpoint-100352 and 97% at the final checkpoint-120832 on the same 200-episode development panel (`research/results.jsonl` and the two task-reference artifacts). The four failures at checkpoint-100352 were all targets with angles from about -116 to -128 degrees and radii from 6.7 to 9.9 cm; checkpoint-120832 retained those four failures and added two more. This is an observed panel pattern, not proof of a general geometric blind spot. Training used radii 14-20 cm while the official task uses 6-20 cm (`robot_learning/scenario/environment.py`), making coverage mismatch a plausible explanation but not a demonstrated cause. Training proxy success rose to 0.97 at 100352 steps and was 0.95 at 120832, while the measured task result fell by one percentage point; reward and proxy metrics therefore guide diagnosis but do not support a task-progress claim by themselves. The two task-reference measurements reuse one development panel and are not independent confirmation. No researcher-authored expected_observation or contradicting_observation existed for the automatic baseline; it established a reference outcome rather than testing an intervention.

**Open questions:** Does expanding or rebalancing training coverage for the 6-14 cm region and the observed negative-angle sector improve task-reference success without causing regressions elsewhere? Is the late decline from checkpoint-100352 to checkpoint-120832 ordinary PPO variability, policy degradation, or a reward/proxy mismatch? Does the 98% result persist on a distinct evaluation panel, and does the selected policy meet the official benchmark?

**Conditional next steps:** Continue research from checkpoint-100352 as the strongest measured policy. A subsequent experiment should test a targeted coverage or curriculum change if it can address the residual failures while preserving the full official distribution; use fresh or transfer initialization according to the semantic compatibility of that change. If a new policy exceeds 98% on comparable task measurements, reassess it for best-known status; if it does not, retain the baseline recipe and investigate the failure geometry and measurement coverage before attributing causality.

## 9f0c1a9e-23c2-4c48-85a6-09cb6d902999 / Experiment 1

**Result:** The fresh automatic baseline produced a viable policy and reached 98% on the task-reference panel at checkpoint-100352, but its final checkpoint measured 97%. The experiment is closed with checkpoint-100352 selected as working and best-known; no official benchmark is requested yet.

**Observed behavior:** Training completed 120,832 steps. The training log's proxy success increased from 0.06 at 75,776 steps to 0.97 at 97,280-100,352 steps, then stayed between 0.93 and 0.96 through the final checkpoint; mean training reward peaked at 135 around 80,896 and was 117 at 100,352 and 112 at 120,832. On the fixed 200-episode task-reference panel (seed 7300), checkpoint-100352 succeeded on 196 episodes (98.0%) and checkpoint-120832 on 194 (97.0%). The earlier checkpoint had four failures, at episode seeds 7300, 7310, 7384, and 7402; the final checkpoint failed on those plus seeds 7421 and 7452. The recurring failures were near 6.7-9.9 cm and approximately -116 to -128 degrees, with the final checkpoint's additional failures at approximately -132 degrees and 169 degrees.

**Hypothesis assessment:** Partially supported. The baseline hypothesis was only to establish an initial baseline, and that objective was met: the recipe learned a policy with 98% measured task-reference success. The measurement does not show robust attainment of the human objective because the final checkpoint scored 97%, the best score is exactly at the threshold, and both measurements use the same development panel. Since this was not an intervention, the result does not support a causal claim about PPO settings, reward terms, or the training-range mismatch.

**Interpretation:** The selected checkpoint is preferable to the final checkpoint on measured task behavior, despite the final checkpoint's still-high training proxy. The baseline makes the current method a useful starting point for targeted improvement, while the persistent failure cluster and the 14-20 cm training range motivate coverage-focused investigation. The evidence does not distinguish coverage mismatch from policy optimization variability or another representation issue.
