# Scientific model: two-joint arm reach-and-hold

The system is a planar, fixed-base two-link arm controlled by direct joint
torque commands. Each episode begins from the same static arm configuration and
changes only the target. A policy must therefore solve a coupled problem:
select a feasible inverse-kinematic configuration, move there with bounded
torque over a sampled-data control loop, remove residual motion, and preserve
the end effector inside a small Cartesian ball for the full hold interval.
The final outcome is temporal, not a measure of the best distance reached.

In the arm plane, with shoulder angle \(q_1\) and relative elbow angle \(q_2\),
the end-effector position is

\[
x = 0.12\cos(q_1) + 0.10\cos(q_1+q_2), \qquad
y = 0.12\sin(q_1) + 0.10\sin(q_1+q_2).
\]

The plane is at \(z=0.02\) m. The target is a fixed, non-contacting mocap
point in that plane, so the physical task is completely described by the
two-dimensional position error even though success is computed as a
three-dimensional Euclidean distance.

# Established facts

### Morphology and geometry

- The base is fixed at the world origin. The shoulder and elbow are revolute
  joints whose axes are parallel to world \(z\), giving two planar degrees of
  freedom.
- The upper arm is 0.12 m long and the forearm is 0.10 m long. The end-effector
  site is at the distal end of the forearm. With both joint coordinates zero,
  the arm points along positive \(x\), and the end effector is at
  \((0.22, 0, 0.02)\) m.
- Both joints have declared ranges of -170 to 170 degrees. The unconstrained
  radial workspace is the annulus from 0.02 m to 0.22 m; the elbow range raises
  the smallest radius at its limit to approximately 0.0277 m.
- The official target radius is sampled between 0.06 and 0.20 m and its angle
  between -pi and pi. The implementation samples radius and angle
  independently and uniformly, rather than uniformly by area.
- The current training environment samples only radii from 0.14 to 0.20 m;
  the protected official task samples 0.06 to 0.20 m. This is a learning
  distribution distinction, not a change to the robot's physical workspace.

### Actuation, dynamics, and timing

- Each joint is driven by a MuJoCo motor with control range [-1, 1] and gear
  5. The environment passes the policy output directly to the two controls
  after clipping, so the nominal generalized torque command is proportional to
  five times the control value.
- The MuJoCo integration timestep is 0.002 s. Each policy action is held
  constant for 10 integration steps, giving a 0.020 s control interval.
  Consequently, the official 2 s hold is 100 consecutive control intervals and
  the 500-step episode limit is 10 s.
- Gravity is explicitly zero. Each joint has damping 0.5 and armature 0.01.
  No actuator position loop, actuator dynamics, or explicit joint stiffness is
  declared.
- Link mass and inertia are generated by MuJoCo from the capsule geometry and
  model defaults rather than explicitly specified in the XML. In the locked
  compiled model, the upper arm and forearm masses are approximately 0.09896 kg
  and 0.05248 kg; at a straight configuration the joint-space inertia,
  including armature, is approximately
  \(\begin{bmatrix}0.01210&0.000507\\0.000507&0.01019\end{bmatrix}\)
  kg m^2 and varies with elbow angle. The target, plane, and base visual
  geometry do not provide a task contact or grasp interaction, and the target
  is moved as a mocap body.

### Initial state and task semantics

- Reset sets both joint positions and both joint velocities to zero, forwards
  the model, then samples one target. The arm therefore always starts
  extended along positive \(x\), while the target can be anywhere in the
  official annulus.
- After each action interval, the simulator computes the end-effector-target
  distance. A distance at or below 0.01 m increments the current hold count;
  any distance above it resets that count to zero. Success terminates the
  episode only when the count reaches 100.
- The protected final assessment uses one frozen policy for 200 independently
  seeded episodes. It counts only complete uninterrupted holds; reaching the
  target briefly, minimizing the final distance, or accumulating shaped reward
  is not success.

### Observation and control interface

- The policy receives 11 float values: two joint positions, two joint
  velocities, the three-dimensional end-effector-to-target vector, and four
  wrapped angular residuals to the two analytic inverse-kinematic branches
  (positive and negative elbow solutions).
- Since the current joint state determines the end-effector position, the
  relative position vector also exposes the target position in the arm plane.
  There is no target orientation, force, torque, acceleration, sensor noise, or
  measurement delay in the declared observation.
- The observation does not expose the current hold count or whether a previous
  in-tolerance run was interrupted. The policy can observe current distance
  indirectly, but hold progress is available only through elapsed interaction
  or policy memory.
- In the research environment, reward combines distance progress, exponential
  closeness, hold progress, an action cost, and a small penalty after leaving
  the tolerance band. These terms shape learning but do not alter the
  protected success definition; the final benchmark itself returns zero
  reward.

# Physical consequences

- The target annulus lies inside the arm's radial workspace. For every sampled
  target there are generally two inverse-kinematic configurations:
  an elbow-positive and an elbow-negative configuration, with corresponding
  shoulder angles. Across the official annulus both branches fit the declared
  joint ranges, so branch choice is a real control alternative rather than
  redundant notation; the motion path can still approach a limit.
- Targets near 0.20 m require a relatively extended arm and targets near
  0.06 m require a strongly folded elbow. These configurations have different
  Jacobians, joint-limit margins, and transient torque demands even when their
  Cartesian errors are equal. The sampled angle changes which joint must move
  most and how close the shoulder is to a limit.
- Because there is no gravity or task contact, a stationary configuration does
  not require a holding torque against an external load. The difficult part of
  the hold is therefore convergence and suppression of residual joint motion:
  a nonzero velocity can carry the end effector outside a 1 cm ball even after
  its center has first entered the ball.
- The action is zero-order-held for 20 ms, so the policy cannot correct
  continuously. Its action changes affect position only through the arm's
  inertia and damping over several integration steps. Large or late torque
  changes can reduce position error while increasing velocity and causing a
  later tolerance exit; a successful strategy must coordinate translation,
  deceleration, and stabilization rather than optimize instantaneous distance.
- The end-effector Jacobian maps joint velocity into Cartesian velocity. Its
  conditioning changes with elbow angle, so identical joint-level motion can
  produce very different Cartesian motion across target radii and branches.
  The tolerance boundary is a Cartesian constraint, while actuation and joint
  limits are joint-space constraints.
- The complete causal chain is
  target and physical state -> 11-value observation -> clipped joint controls
  -> ten MuJoCo integrations -> new joint state and end-effector position ->
  distance threshold and hold-counter update. A failure can consequently arise
  from branch selection, insufficient reach time, overshoot, poor damping of
  residual motion, or a hold interruption, even when the arm has previously
  reached the neighborhood.
- Meaningful cross-episode physical quantities are target radius and angle,
  chosen inverse-kinematic branch, joint-limit margin, joint positions and
  velocities, commanded torque, Cartesian error and its radial/tangential
  components, time to first enter tolerance, longest uninterrupted in-band
  run, exit count, peak speed, and mechanical effort. These quantities
  distinguish geometric difficulty, transient reach behavior, and true hold
  stability; episode success alone does not.

# Unknowns

- The resulting discrete-time response under the 20 ms action hold is not
  known a priori: in particular, the reachable settling time, overshoot, and
  residual velocity differ by target geometry and inverse-kinematic branch.
  The compiled inertial quantities determine the response, but do not by
  themselves predict the behavior of a learned closed-loop policy.
- It is not known whether a learned policy will consistently select one branch,
  switch branches during motion, or exploit different branches for different
  target angles. It is also unknown whether any sampled target is practically
  difficult despite being kinematically reachable because of time, torque, or
  joint-limit margins.
- The relative-position and joint-state observations expose the instantaneous
  target and physical configuration, but the scientific effect of the missing
  hold counter is unresolved. A recurrent policy could infer elapsed in-band
  time, while a memoryless policy must succeed by maintaining a stabilizing
  state-dependent control law.
- Before campaign evidence exists, the distribution of time-to-tolerance,
  longest hold duration, hold interruptions, final error, effort, and failure
  geometry is unknown. In particular, it is not established whether the
  dominant failure class will be failure to reach, failure to settle, or
  failure to remain continuously inside the tolerance.
- The importance of simulator numerical details near the tolerance boundary,
  joint limits, and any possible arm self-contact is unresolved from the task
  specification alone. These effects matter only if trajectories enter those
  regimes, which cannot be inferred from the pre-campaign implementation.
