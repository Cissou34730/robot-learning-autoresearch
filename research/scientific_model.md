# Scientific model of the robot and task

## Embodied mechanism and kinematics

**Established fact.** The robot is a rooted, planar two-link serial arm. The
shoulder and elbow are revolute joints whose axes are both the world `z` axis.
The upper arm has length 0.12 m and the forearm has length 0.10 m. The
end-effector site is at the forearm tip, so the arm moves in the horizontal
plane `z = 0.02 m`. The shoulder angle is the first joint coordinate and the
elbow coordinate is relative to the upper arm. Both joints are limited to
[-170, 170] degrees. The base is fixed; there are no translational degrees of
freedom and the endpoint orientation is irrelevant to the task.

**Physical or scientific consequence.** With joint coordinates
\(q=(q_1,q_2)\), the endpoint position is

\[
x = 0.12\cos(q_1) + 0.10\cos(q_1+q_2),\qquad
y = 0.12\sin(q_1) + 0.10\sin(q_1+q_2).
\]

The ideal two-link workspace is bounded by 0.02 m and 0.22 m from the base.
The official target radii, 0.06--0.20 m, are inside these bounds, and the
joint limits still permit an inverse-kinematic solution over the full target
angle range. Each ordinary target generally has two configurations: an
elbow-open and an elbow-folded solution. The branches coalesce at kinematic
singularities, while the joint limits and the relative elbow coordinate make
some angular representations of a target inadmissible even when another
branch remains valid.

The endpoint Jacobian depends on \(q_2\), with its determinant proportional to
\(0.12\cdot0.10\sin(q_2)\). Consequently, the same Cartesian tolerance does
not represent the same joint-space tolerance at every configuration. Near
straight or folded configurations, Cartesian motion in a weak Jacobian
direction requires comparatively large joint motion, and joint velocity,
torque, and numerical tracking demands are coupled to the chosen branch.

**Unknown.** The implementation establishes geometric reachability and the
available branches, but not which branch a controller will use, whether it
will switch branches, or what transient path it will take.

## Actuation, timing, and dynamics

**Established fact.** The action is a two-element vector clipped to [-1, 1]
and passed unchanged to two MuJoCo motor actuators. Each motor is attached to
one joint with gear 5, direct unit gain, and no position or velocity servo.
Thus the physical command is a bounded generalized torque in the simulator's
actuator convention, with magnitude up to 5 per joint. An action is held
constant for ten physics steps. The MuJoCo timestep is 0.002 s, so the
control period is 0.020 s (50 Hz), while the physics is integrated at 500 Hz.
The compiled model uses the default Euler integrator.

Gravity is explicitly zero. Each joint has viscous damping 0.5 and armature
inertia 0.01; there is no specified joint friction loss. The link masses and
inertias are generated by MuJoCo from the capsule geometry and its defaults,
rather than supplied as independent joint parameters. The effective
generalized inertia therefore changes with configuration because the forearm
is carried through the shoulder. The plane, base, and target are explicitly
non-contacting; there is no environmental obstacle or physical target
interaction. The link capsules remain simulator geometry, so any self-contact
behavior is a property of the simulator's ordinary geom rules, not part of the
success definition.

**Physical or scientific consequence.** This is direct, sample-and-hold
torque control of a damped, coupled mechanism. Torque first changes joint
velocity and only then endpoint position; the forearm motion also changes the
shoulder's effective inertia and endpoint Jacobian. A fast approach can leave
velocity that must be dissipated before or during tolerance entry. Conversely,
the absence of gravity means a stationary configuration at rest does not
require a gravity-compensating torque; the controller mainly has to arrest
motion, correct errors, and avoid injecting motion during the hold. Saturation,
damping, configuration-dependent inertia, and the 20 ms action period jointly
bound the attainable transient response.

**Unknown.** The code does not determine the controller's actual rise time,
overshoot, residual endpoint velocity, torque saturation, self-contact
incidence, or numerical tracking behavior. Those are behaviors of a policy
interacting with the dynamics, not facts implied by the task definition.

## Initial state and target geometry

**Established fact.** Reset sets both joint positions and both joint velocities
to zero, performs forward kinematics, and then samples a target angle
uniformly over [-pi, pi] and a radius uniformly over [0.06, 0.20] m. The
target is a stationary mocap body at the endpoint's plane height. The initial
endpoint is (0.22, 0, 0.02) m. Radius is sampled uniformly, not area-weighted.

**Physical or scientific consequence.** The initial endpoint is at maximum
extension. Its initial distance from a target of radius \(r\) and angle
\(\phi\) is
\[
\sqrt{0.22^2+r^2-2(0.22)r\cos\phi}.
\]
The minimum possible initial distance is 0.02 m, so no official target is
initially inside the 0.01 m success tolerance. Every episode therefore
requires an approach from the same joint state, but the required direction,
radial displacement, and eventual inverse-kinematic configuration vary.
Targets near the inner radius demand a more folded posture; targets near the
outer radius demand a more extended posture.

**Unknown.** The target distribution fixes the physical variation but does not
establish the time or path by which any policy will reach a particular target.

## Task as one coupled control process

**Established fact.** At each control step, success geometry is the Euclidean
distance between the endpoint site and the target. It must be no greater than
0.01 m for 100 consecutive control steps, exactly 2 seconds. Leaving the
tolerance resets the consecutive counter. An episode is truncated after 500
control steps, equivalent to 10 seconds; completion of the uninterrupted hold
is the task success event. The official assessment uses 200 fixed-panel
episodes, and its objective threshold is at least 196 successes (98%).

**Physical or scientific consequence.** Approach, entry, settling, and hold
are not independent subproblems. The same torque sequence determines both
where the endpoint crosses the tolerance boundary and the velocity with which
it crosses it. A trajectory that reaches the target quickly can still fail if
its residual motion exits the 1 cm ball; a conservative approach can reduce
exit risk while consuming the finite horizon. During a successful hold, the
endpoint must remain inside a small Cartesian region despite discrete
sample-and-hold control and coupled joint dynamics. The required behavior is
therefore convergence to a target neighborhood followed by local stabilization,
not merely a single accurate pose.

The full target panel is physically reachable, but target geometry, branch
choice, Jacobian conditioning, joint limits, and the approach velocity can
change the relationship between joint motion and Cartesian error. Improving
one part of the process can consequently alter another: for example, a
different approach speed changes the settling burden, and a different
kinematic branch changes both the local Jacobian and the dynamic inertia.
These are physical couplings, not separate outcome causes.

**Unknown.** The implementation does not establish which episodes, if any,
will enter tolerance, settle, leave tolerance, or complete the hold. A label
such as interrupted hold describes the measured event but does not identify
whether its cause was kinematic, dynamic, control, or observability-related.

## Observation and control information

**Established fact.** The observation has 11 values:

* the two joint positions and two joint velocities;
* the three-dimensional endpoint-minus-target displacement;
* four wrapped angular errors from the current joint state to the two
  inverse-kinematic branches (shoulder and elbow for elbow-open and
  elbow-folded solutions).

There is no observation noise, delay, camera image, contact signal, actuator
state, acceleration, endpoint velocity, episode clock, or hold counter. The
target position and endpoint position are not separately supplied, but the
target displacement is supplied and endpoint position is deterministically
recoverable from the observed joint positions and known geometry. The target
is static, so target velocity is not a missing dynamic variable.

**Physical or scientific consequence.** In this deterministic model the
instantaneous task-relevant configuration is substantially observable: joint
position and velocity identify the arm state, and displacement identifies the
Cartesian error. Endpoint velocity can be derived from joint velocity and the
Jacobian, but is not directly represented. The four branch errors expose both
kinematic alternatives while preserving the distinction between the actual
configuration and the unused alternative. Wrapped angles are numerically
convenient but have a representation discontinuity at the -pi/pi boundary.
The environment also does not expose how long the current tolerance streak
has lasted, so identical physical states can occur at different points in the
required hold.

The observation-to-outcome loop is therefore: the policy maps measured
configuration, velocity, and target-relative geometry to two bounded torques;
MuJoCo integrates those torques for 20 ms; the resulting endpoint displacement
is measured; and the displacement both becomes the next observation and
updates the consecutive-hold state.

**Unknown.** Whether this observation is sufficient for a particular policy to
infer the needed phase of motion, select a stable branch, and maintain the
hold cannot be concluded from the representation alone. No current weakness
or failure cause is established without observing behavior.

## Scientifically meaningful physical quantities

**Established fact.** The implementation directly defines target radius and
angle, joint positions and velocities, endpoint position, endpoint-target
distance, tolerance membership, consecutive held steps, episode step count,
and applied action. These quantities connect state, control, and outcome.

**Physical or scientific consequence.** Across a complete behavior, meaningful
quantities also include time to first tolerance entry, longest uninterrupted
hold, exits and re-entries, endpoint and joint velocities at entry, residual
motion during the hold, torque magnitude and saturation, joint-limit or
self-contact events, branch identity, Jacobian conditioning, and mechanical
work or action variation. Together they distinguish geometric reachability,
trajectory control, convergence, stabilization, and sustained completion
without treating those stages as independent tasks.

**Unknown.** Their realized distributions over the official target panel,
their relationships for a learned controller, and the extent to which any
one quantity explains an observed outcome are not determined by the
human-authored implementation. The only fixed outcome contract is the binary
completion of the uninterrupted 2-second hold.
