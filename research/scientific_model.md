# Scientific model of the robot and task

## Embodied geometry and workspace

**Established fact.** The robot is a fixed-base planar two-revolute-joint arm.
The shoulder and elbow axes are parallel to the world z axis. The upper arm is
0.12 m long and the forearm is 0.10 m long, so the end effector is at

```text
x = 0.12 cos(q1) + 0.10 cos(q1 + q2)
y = 0.12 sin(q1) + 0.10 sin(q1 + q2)
z = 0.02
```

Both joint ranges are -170 to +170 degrees. The arm starts at
`q = (0, 0)` with zero velocity, which places the end effector at
`(0.22, 0, 0.02)`. The target is a kinematically prescribed mocap point
whose z coordinate is set to the arm plane. The target and the visual plane do
not provide a physical contact or manipulation interaction.

**Physical or scientific consequence.** With unrestricted joints, the
two-link workspace is the annulus from 0.02 m to 0.22 m. The official target
radii, 0.06--0.20 m, lie strictly inside this annulus. The joint limits
guarantee at least one legal inverse-kinematic configuration for every official
target, although they can remove one of the two branches in some angular
sectors. Thus the problem is planar point positioning, not orientation control,
obstacle avoidance, or forceful interaction. Starting fully extended means
every episode begins from the outer boundary of the nominal workspace and the
arm must generally shorten and/or rotate its reach.

For a target at polar angle `phi`, the inverse kinematics has the usual two
elbow branches:

```text
q2 = +/- acos((r^2 - 0.12^2 - 0.10^2) / (2 * 0.12 * 0.10))
q1 = phi - atan2(0.10 sin(q2), 0.12 + 0.10 cos(q2))
```

The positive and negative values of `q2` correspond to the two sides of the
target in joint space. At the target-radius limits, the elbow magnitudes are
approximately 150 degrees and 49 degrees respectively, rather than the
singular fully folded or fully extended values.

**Unknown.** The implementation does not determine which branch a controller
will use, whether it will remain on one branch, or what transient path it will
take between the initial and final configurations.

## Actuation, dynamics, and control timing

**Established fact.** MuJoCo integrates the model with a 0.002 s timestep and
zero gravity. Each policy action is held constant for 10 physics steps, so the
control interval is 0.020 s. There are two direct motor actuators, one per
joint. Each command is clipped to `[-1, 1]` and transmitted with gear 5; for a
hinge motor this gives a nominal joint torque command in the range -5 to +5 in
MuJoCo force units. Each joint has damping 0.5 and armature inertia 0.01.
Link masses and inertias are generated by MuJoCo from the capsule geometry,
sizes, and default density rather than specified as independent task
parameters. The current physical action mapping is the identity, so the policy
commands are the motor controls directly.

**Physical or scientific consequence.** The robot is a torque-driven,
second-order system with coupled two-link inertia. Commands are
piecewise-constant over 20 ms intervals, not instantaneous position
teleports. Inertia, Coriolis/centripetal coupling, damping, and actuator
saturation determine how joint velocities evolve and how accurately motion can
be arrested. Zero gravity removes gravitational loading: at rest, a
configuration does not require gravity compensation, but residual velocity
still has to be dissipated through control and the specified damping. Since
the target is stationary and there is no intended target or obstacle contact,
stabilization is a free-space regulation problem around a two-dimensional
position.

The official horizon is 500 control steps, or 10 s. The two-second hold
therefore consists of exactly 100 post-action distance checks. The dynamics
are sampled only at these control boundaries for success, even though MuJoCo
integrates ten finer steps between observations.

**Unknown.** The XML and task contract establish the equations and parameters,
but not the resulting acceleration, overshoot, settling time, steady-state
error, or amount of time spent at actuator saturation for any particular
policy.

## Task geometry and required capabilities

**Established fact.** At reset, the environment samples an angle uniformly
over `[-pi, pi]` and a radius uniformly over `[0.06, 0.20]` m, then places the
target at that planar position. An episode is successful only if the
end-effector distance is at most 0.01 m at 100 consecutive control steps. A
single check outside the tolerance resets the consecutive count to zero. The
episode can run for at most 500 control steps.

**Physical or scientific consequence.** Success requires a sequence of
distinct behaviors:

1. **Reachability and gross motion:** rotate and fold the arm from the
   straight initial pose toward the target's radial and angular coordinates.
2. **Trajectory control:** produce coordinated shoulder and elbow motion,
   accounting for the nonlinear forward kinematics and joint coupling.
3. **Convergence:** reduce Cartesian error below 1 cm, not merely approach the
   correct direction or radius.
4. **Stabilization:** keep the end effector inside a small Cartesian disk for
   2 s while the arm's velocity and actuator commands settle.

The local mapping from joint motion to end-effector motion is governed by the
two-link Jacobian. Its determinant is proportional to
`0.12 * 0.10 * sin(q2)`, so the two elbow branches have opposite local
motion orientations and different joint-space representations of the same
Cartesian target. The official radii avoid the exact outer and inner
two-link singular radii, but the Cartesian tolerance still corresponds to a
configuration-dependent region in joint space.

**Unknown.** No implementation-only conclusion identifies the actual limiting
part of an episode, the controller's preferred trajectory, or whether a
particular failure to complete the hold would arise from reaching,
convergence, velocity regulation, or perturbation across the tolerance
boundary.

## Sensing and the action-outcome relationship

**Established fact.** The observation has 11 floating-point values:

- two joint positions `q`,
- two joint velocities `qdot`,
- the three-dimensional vector from the end effector to the target,
- four wrapped angular errors to the two analytic inverse-kinematic
  configurations (open and folded).

The angular errors are wrapped to `[-pi, pi)`. The observation has no sensor
noise or delay in the implementation. The action has two values and is mapped
directly to the two clipped motor controls. After each 20 ms action interval,
MuJoCo advances the state, the new end-effector distance is computed, and the
hold counter is updated.

**Physical or scientific consequence.** The instantaneous planar mechanical
state needed for this task is observable: joint positions and velocities are
given, and the target position can be reconstructed from the known
end-effector kinematics plus the observed end-effector-to-target vector. The
two IK error pairs expose both available geometric target configurations
without forcing a single branch choice. The observation does not directly
contain acceleration, actuator torque after clipping, contact forces, or a
future trajectory; those must be inferred from successive state-action
relationships. There is no hidden target motion to estimate.

The causal chain is therefore: policy observes configuration, velocity, target
geometry, and branch errors; it selects two motor commands; the commands
produce joint accelerations and coupled motion over ten physics steps; the
resulting end-effector distance determines the next observation and whether
the uninterrupted hold continues.

**Unknown.** The observation contract alone does not reveal what internal
state, memory, or branch-selection rule a learned policy uses, nor how it
converts the available information into a stable control law.

## Scientifically meaningful physical quantities

The quantities that directly describe this embodied task are joint positions,
joint velocities, joint accelerations, commanded and clipped motor controls,
implied joint torques, end-effector position and velocity, target radius and
angle, Cartesian distance, distance margin to the 1 cm boundary, the longest
consecutive in-tolerance streak, and the joint-space branch used. Settling
time, peak speed, peak control, saturation duration, and distance variation
during the hold distinguish reaching from convergence and stabilization.

These are physical descriptors of behavior, not evidence about any particular
policy. Actual values, branch selection, transient behavior, and success
outcomes remain **Unknown** until motion is observed.
