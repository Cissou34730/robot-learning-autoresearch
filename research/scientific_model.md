# Scientific model of the robot and task

## Physical system and configuration

**Established fact.** The robot is a planar serial arm with two revolute joints.
The shoulder joint rotates about the world \(z\)-axis at the base, and the elbow
joint rotates about the same axis relative to the upper arm. The upper arm is
0.12 m long and the forearm is 0.10 m long. The end-effector site is at the
forearm tip. The arm therefore moves in the horizontal plane \(z=0.02\) m; its
planar forward kinematics are

\[
x = 0.12\cos q_1 + 0.10\cos(q_1+q_2),\qquad
y = 0.12\sin q_1 + 0.10\sin(q_1+q_2).
\]

Both joints have limits of \([-170^\circ,170^\circ]\). The base is a visual
cylinder, and the arm is rendered with capsule geometries. The plane, base, and
target are non-colliding; the target is a mocap body whose position is set by
the environment, not a freely moving physical object.

**Physical or scientific consequence.** With unrestricted joints, the
end-effector workspace is an annulus from \(|0.12-0.10|=0.02\) m to
\(0.12+0.10=0.22\) m. The elbow limit increases the minimum attainable radius
slightly, to about 0.0277 m at \(|q_2|=170^\circ\). The official radial interval
0.06--0.20 m is consequently inside the workspace. The joint-limit margin is
also sufficient for the full target angle range: for the official endpoints,
the positive elbow inverse-kinematic branch has approximately
\(q_2=150.1^\circ\) at radius 0.06 m and \(q_2=49.5^\circ\) at radius 0.20 m,
with corresponding shoulder offset magnitudes of about \(56.3^\circ\) and
\(22.3^\circ\). The opposite branch changes both signs. Thus every official
target position has at least one feasible configuration, and generally two
configuration branches.

**Unknown.** The implementation establishes geometric reachability, not which
branch a learned controller will select, whether it will switch branches, or
what path it will take between them.

## Actuation and dynamics

**Established fact.** Each action has two components and is used directly as
the two joint motor commands. The environment clips each component to
[-1, 1]. Each MuJoCo motor has gear 5, so the actuator command corresponds to
a bounded joint motor effort with magnitude up to 5 in MuJoCo's torque units.
There is no position or velocity servo in the XML: the policy commands effort,
not a desired joint angle.

Physics advances with a 0.002 s timestep. An action is held constant for
10 physics steps, giving a 0.020 s control interval and a 50 Hz policy/control
rate. The action-to-motion relationship is therefore a sampled torque-driven
dynamical system rather than an instantaneous kinematic placement.

**Established fact.** Gravity is explicitly zero. Each joint has damping 0.5
and armature 0.01. The instantiated model derives masses from the specified
geometries; the reported body masses are approximately 0.099 kg for the upper
arm and 0.0525 kg for the forearm (the base is approximately 0.201 kg).
The two joint coordinates are dynamically coupled through the serial
mechanism, and the effective inertia changes with configuration.

**Physical or scientific consequence.** In generalized-coordinate form, the
motion is governed by an inertia matrix and velocity-dependent coupling,
together with joint damping and commanded motor effort; the gravity term is
absent. A useful abstraction is
\[
M(q)\ddot q + C(q,\dot q)\dot q + D\dot q = \tau.
\]
The armature increases effective joint inertia, while damping removes energy
and supports eventual settling. Because the controller applies a constant
effort over each 20 ms interval, large or rapidly changing commands can create
overshoot and residual velocity rather than directly producing a desired
end-effector displacement. The endpoint velocity is \(J(q)\dot q\), and the
planar Jacobian loses rank at a straight or fully folded configuration when
\(\sin q_2=0\). Official goal radii avoid the straight-arm singularity, but the
reset state is exactly straight.

**Unknown.** The model specifies the deterministic physical response for any
given state and action, but the implementation alone does not reveal the
transient trajectory, overshoot, settling time, or steady-state motion
generated by a learned policy.

## Initial state and target geometry

**Established fact.** Reset sets both joint positions and velocities to zero,
then performs forward kinematics. The initial end effector is at
(0.22, 0, 0.02) m, the maximum-reach straight configuration. A target is then
sampled with angle uniform over \([-\pi,\pi]\) and radius uniform over
[0.06, 0.20] m, and its \(z\)-coordinate is set to 0.02 m. The target is
stationary for the episode. The official episode limit is 500 control steps.

**Physical or scientific consequence.** The initial arm is at a kinematic
singularity and is not generally near the target. Initial target distance
varies with target angle and radius, from 0.02 m for the nearest
same-direction target to 0.42 m for a 0.20 m target directly behind the arm.
The controller must first generate a coordinated departure from the
straight-arm state, then move to one feasible inverse-kinematic configuration.
Since there is no gravity, movement and stopping are governed primarily by
motor effort, configuration-dependent inertia, damping, and the discrete
20 ms command updates.

**Unknown.** Reset defines the distribution and physical state, but it does not
determine how quickly any policy leaves the singular configuration or whether
it approaches the open or folded solution.

## Sensing and observation

**Established fact.** The observation has 11 floating-point values:

1. the two raw joint positions \(q_1,q_2\);
2. the two joint velocities;
3. the three-dimensional end-effector-to-target displacement; and
4. four wrapped angular residuals, measuring the current shoulder and elbow
   against both the positive-elbow and negative-elbow inverse-kinematic
   solutions for the current target.

The target and end effector share the same plane, so the third displacement
component is physically zero apart from numerical representation. The
action mapping is the identity, with no additional control-space conversion in
the scenario interface.

**Physical or scientific consequence.** The observation contains the robot's
configuration and velocity, and enough relative target geometry to reconstruct
the planar target relationship. For this fixed mechanism, it is effectively a
state description of the physical reach problem: the target does not move and
there are no task-relevant external contacts or hidden objects. The two
inverse-kinematic residual pairs expose the alternative elbow branches and
provide a branch-relative error signal, while wrapping prevents angular error
from growing at the \(-\pi/\pi\) representation boundary.

The observation does not include motor effort, acceleration, contact forces,
the previous action, elapsed time, or the internal consecutive-hold counter.
It also does not directly encode which target sample was drawn, although the
relative displacement together with the known kinematics determines its planar
location.

**Unknown.** Whether the learned policy uses joint state, displacement, or the
branch residuals, and whether its internal memory (if any) compensates for the
omitted hold counter or action history, cannot be inferred from the interface.

## Task requirements and success dynamics

**Established fact.** A target is considered reached when the Euclidean
end-effector distance is at most 0.01 m. At the 50 Hz control rate, the
controller must satisfy this condition on 100 consecutive control observations,
which is 2 seconds. Any sample outside the tolerance resets the consecutive
counter. The episode succeeds on completion of that uninterrupted streak;
otherwise it can run for at most 500 control steps, or 10 seconds.

**Physical or scientific consequence.** Success is a trajectory property, not
merely an inverse-kinematic endpoint property. The policy must provide:

- coordinated two-joint reaching from the straight initial state;
- selection of a feasible configuration branch;
- convergence of position error into a 1 cm disk;
- dissipation or compensation of residual velocity so the endpoint does not
  leave that disk; and
- continuous stabilization with a sampling interval of 20 ms for the full
  2-second hold.

The tolerance is finite, but it is small relative to the 0.20 m arm reach.
Consequently, small endpoint motion caused by joint velocity or torque updates
can break the hold even after the nominal target configuration has been
reached. The benchmark checks the same distance and uninterrupted streak
semantics; its per-episode outcome is binary.

**Unknown.** The implementation alone cannot establish the policy's actual
success probability, preferred settling behavior, branch usage, or the
physical causes of any future unsuccessful episode.

## Scientifically meaningful physical quantities

The quantities that directly describe this embodied task are joint position and
velocity; commanded joint effort; end-effector position, velocity, and
distance-to-target; radial and angular target error; inverse-kinematic branch;
Jacobian rank or conditioning; peak effort and effort changes; overshoot;
settling time; residual endpoint velocity; and the longest uninterrupted
inside-tolerance interval. Together these distinguish geometric reachability,
dynamic motion, convergence, and stabilization. The decisive task quantity is
the consecutive number of control samples with distance at most 1 cm, because
that is the physical trajectory condition used for success.
